QUESTION 1 OF 5
Why does Rabbi Meir concede that a backup provisional guilt offering (designated as insurance alongside a first one) remains consecrated, even though he normally holds such offerings become non-sacred once innocence is confirmed?
A. Because two animals are always more valuable than one
B. Because designating a spare animal in advance reveals genuine, deep anxiety about sin, unlike the ordinary single-offering case
C. Because backup offerings are governed by different verses entirely
D. Because Rabbi Meir retracted his general position
Correct: B — Rabbi Meir reasons that the deliberate choice to designate a second, backup animal demonstrates real concern about sin ('his heart struck him with pangs of conscience'), which is precisely the psychological state that the Rabbis say keeps an offering consecrated.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to Rabbi Yoḥanan, why is a provisional guilt offering NOT comparable to a sotah's meal offering of jealousy, even when both cases involve later-discredited witnesses?
A. Because sotah offerings are never brought
B. Because the sotah's offering serves only to clarify guilt or innocence, while the guilt offering serves genuine atonement rooted in the person's real anxiety
C. Because provisional guilt offerings are always more expensive
D. Because witnesses are irrelevant to guilt offerings
Correct: B — Rabbi Yoḥanan distinguishes the two: the sotah's offering exists purely to resolve uncertainty about her guilt, so once resolved, its purpose vanishes; the guilt offering serves atonement, which depends on the person's sincere state of mind at consecration, unaffected by later developments.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to Rava, why does the owner of an ox accused of bestiality NOT relinquish ownership when the accusation is that the owner himself committed the act (rather than that the ox was a victim)?
A. Because such accusations are never taken seriously
B. Because the owner knows for certain he did not commit the act, so he does not give up — instead he actively seeks witnesses to disprove the charge
C. Because oxen cannot be involved in such cases
D. Because the law treats both accusations identically
Correct: B — Rava explains that when the accusation targets the owner's own actions, he has personal certainty of his innocence and therefore keeps fighting the charge rather than abandoning the animal, unlike a case where the ox itself is merely alleged to be the victim of another's act.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Rava's resolution of the mishna's apparent contradiction, why is a discovered-innocent DEFINITE guilt offering (before blood is sprinkled) buried rather than burned, while a provisional guilt offering in the same position is burned?
A. Because they are made from different animals
B. Because a definite guilt offering was consecrated only on the assumption of actual need, so once that need disappears it was effectively never sacred (like ordinary meat, hence buried); a provisional offering stays genuinely consecrated but disqualified (hence burned)
C. Because burial is always preferred over burning
D. Because the mishna contains a scribal error with no real explanation
Correct: B — Rava explains that the definite guilt offering's consecration depended entirely on actual need, so its cancellation renders it as though never sanctified (buried like ordinary slaughtered meat); the provisional offering's consecration reflects genuine anxiety and remains valid but disqualified, hence treated like other disqualified offerings (burned).
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to Rava's reading of Leviticus 5:18 ('and he knew it not, and he shall be forgiven'), why can the meat of a provisional guilt offering still be eaten if the blood was sprinkled before the person discovered he never actually sinned?
A. Because the meat is never actually eaten in such cases
B. Because the critical moment for determining 'lack of knowledge' is the moment of atonement (the blood's sprinkling), not any later discovery
C. Because priests can eat any meat regardless of its status
D. Because the verse has no bearing on this case
Correct: B — Rava derives from the verse's phrasing that what matters is whether the person lacked knowledge AT THE TIME of atonement (blood-sprinkling); since he was still uncertain at that critical moment, the offering functioned validly, permitting the meat to be eaten even though he later learned the truth.
