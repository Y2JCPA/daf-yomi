QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to Reish Lakish, when does the karet-bearing 'blood with which the soul departs' begin during slaughter?
A. Only once the blood spurts forcefully
B. From the last black drop onward, even before the blood begins spurting forcefully
C. Only after the animal is completely dead
D. Only from blood collected in a vessel
Correct: B — Reish Lakish holds the karet-bearing blood begins from the last black drop onward — earlier than Rabbi Yoḥanan's view, which requires forceful spurting.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to the Gemara's resolution of the rejected a fortiori argument, why is human milk not forbidden despite the seemingly compelling logic that humans (strict on contact-impurity) should also be strict about their milk?
A. Because human milk is never produced
B. Because the verse 'these are they which are impure to you' (Leviticus 11:29) specifically classifies only the enumerated animals as impure, excluding human milk from the comparison
C. Because milk is never forbidden under any circumstances
D. Because the a fortiori argument was never actually proposed
Correct: B — The Torah's explicit classification in Leviticus 11:29 overrides the logical inference, establishing that only the specified non-kosher animals' milk (and blood) are forbidden, not humans'.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to the mishna, what is Rabbi Akiva's position on whether uncertain misuse of consecrated property requires a provisional guilt offering?
A. He holds it never requires any offering
B. He holds it does require a provisional guilt offering, just as ordinary uncertain sin-offering-level transgressions do
C. He holds it always requires a definite guilt offering immediately
D. He agrees entirely with the Rabbis that no offering is needed
Correct: B — Rabbi Akiva rules that even uncertainty about misuse of consecrated property obligates a provisional guilt offering, disagreeing with the Rabbis who exempt such uncertainty entirely.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to the Gemara, what is the actual basis of the Rabbis' exemption of uncertain misuse from a provisional guilt offering?
A. A simple rejection of Rabbi Akiva's authority
B. A verbal analogy ('mitzvot'/'mitzvot') linking the provisional guilt offering to the sin offering, which requires the underlying transgression to carry karet for intentional violation — something misuse of consecrated property lacks
C. The fact that misuse offerings are always more expensive
D. A general rule that guilt offerings are never brought for uncertainty
Correct: B — The Rabbis derive via a verbal analogy that the provisional guilt offering parallels the sin offering, which requires the underlying sin to carry karet when intentional; since intentional misuse carries no karet, misuse is excluded from the provisional guilt offering's scope.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to Rabbi Tarfon's proposed stipulation for uncertain misuse, what does the person say when bringing his combined offering?
A. That he refuses to bring any offering at all
B. That if it is certain he misused property, this is his definite payment and guilt offering; if uncertain, the money is a donation and the offering is provisional
C. That the priest must decide the offering's status
D. That he will wait until Yom Kippur to bring any offering
Correct: B — Rabbi Tarfon's stipulation allows one offering and payment to serve both possible outcomes, avoiding the need for two separate offerings by making the offering's status conditional on whether the misuse turns out to be certain or remains uncertain.
