📖 Overview: The Blood That Carries the Soul, and the Guilt Offering of Doubt

Keritot 22a–22b
רַבִּי עֲקִיבָא מְחַיֵּיב עַל סְפֵק מְעִילָה אָשָׁם תָּלוּי, וַחֲכָמִים פּוֹטְרִים
The scene: The daf pins down exactly which blood counts as 'blood with which the soul departs' — the blood whose consumption carries karet — through a dispute between Rabbi Yoḥanan and Reish Lakish over whether only forcefully spurting blood qualifies, or also the very first red drops after the initial black ones. A rejected a fortiori argument about human milk, and Rav Sheshet's ruling on human blood, round out the chapter's treatment of blood's outer boundaries. The daf then turns to a new mishna launching a fresh debate: does uncertainty about misusing consecrated property (me'ila) require a provisional guilt offering, the way uncertainty about a sin-offering-level transgression does? Rabbi Akiva says yes; the Rabbis say no — and the Gemara traces their dispute to competing readings of a single connecting vav and a contested verbal analogy. The daf closes with two further mishnayot applying the same 'same species covers both certain and uncertain cases' principle to a woman's bird offering and to pieces of non-sacred and consecrated meat.

💉 Which Blood Carries the Soul?

Rabbi Yoḥanan and Reish Lakish define the boundary of karet-bearing slaughter blood
1️⃣ Rabbi Yoḥanan: only blood that spurts forcefully (מְקַלֵּחַ) counts as 'the blood with which the soul departs' — everything before or after is merely exudate
↓
2️⃣ Reish Lakish: the boundary starts earlier — from the last BLACK drop onward, even before forceful spurting begins
↓
3️⃣ Multiple baraitot seem to refute Reish Lakish (defining it strictly as 'while spurting'), but the Gemara reconciles them: they exclude only the black blood itself, not the first/last red drops, which still count even if merely flowing
↓
4️⃣ A further baraita appears to refute him again — resolved as reflecting a genuine tannaitic dispute: Rabbi Elazar agrees with Rabbi Yoḥanan's position, Rabbi Shimon with Reish Lakish's
Practical stakes: Rabbi Yirmeya's dilemma about blood collected in two separate cups (first cup: forceful spurt; second cup: mere flow) directly maps onto this dispute — Reish Lakish would obligate two sin offerings, Rabbi Yoḥanan only one.

🥛 Why Human Milk (and Blood) Escape the Prohibition

A rejected a fortiori argument, corrected by the Torah's own wording
StepContent
The tempting inferenceNon-kosher animals are LENIENT regarding contact-impurity but STRICT regarding their milk (forbidden) — so humans, who are STRICT regarding contact-impurity, should be even more STRICT regarding their milk
The correction'And these are they which are impure to you' (Lev. 11:29) — 'these' (the specified non-kosher animals) are impure; human milk is not impure, but pure/permitted
Extending the logicOne might still think blood — unlike milk, forbidden even from kosher animals — should apply to humans too; the same verse excludes human blood as well
Rav Sheshet's conclusionHuman blood carries not even a preference to abstain from it ab initio — the exclusion is complete
Why the a fortiori fails: Intuitive logical inferences about severity can be overridden by the Torah's own explicit classification — 'these are impure' draws a hard boundary no inference can cross.

⚖️ Uncertain Misuse — Does It Need a Guilt Offering?

A new mishna: does the provisional guilt offering (for sin-offering-level doubt) extend to misuse of consecrated property?
Rabbi Akiva
Yes — one who is uncertain whether he misused consecrated property still brings a provisional guilt offering, just like ordinary uncertain sins.
The Rabbis
No — a provisional guilt offering applies only to uncertainty about SIN-OFFERING-level transgressions, not misuse.
Rabbi Tarfon's proposal
Why bring two separate guilt offerings ever? Bring the misuse payment plus a two-sela guilt offering, and STIPULATE: 'if certain, this is my definite guilt offering; if uncertain, the money is a donation and this animal is my provisional offering.'
Rabbi Akiva's limit
That works for SMALL amounts of misuse. But if the uncertain misuse involves 10,000 dinars, isn't it better to bring just the cheap provisional offering now, rather than pay the full massive sum on mere uncertainty?
Resolution
Rabbi Akiva concedes to Rabbi Tarfon's method specifically for minor cases of misuse.
Underlying agreement: Both tanna'im accept that a certain guilt offering does not require prior knowledge from the very outset — it suffices that certainty eventually arrives.

🔗 Vav, Gezeirah Shava, and Juxtaposition

Tracing the Akiva/Rabbis dispute to its exegetical roots
📜 The provisional-guilt-offering verse (Lev. 5:17) begins with 'AND if anyone sin' — does this vav connect it backward to the misuse passage, teaching that misuse too gets a provisional offering?
↓
🔀 Rav Pappa clarifies: everyone agrees a vav CAN teach 'earlier from later' (proven elsewhere, e.g. northern slaughter for sheep from cattle) — so that's not the real dispute
↓
🧩 The REAL dispute: a separate verbal analogy ('mitzvot'/'mitzvot') links the provisional guilt offering to the ordinary SIN offering — which requires karet for intentional violation. Since intentional misuse carries no karet (only death-at-Heaven's-hand or lashes), the Rabbis conclude misuse is EXCLUDED
↓
⚖️ Rabbi Akiva rejects using that same analogy to also fix the OFFERING'S MINIMUM VALUE (2 shekels) — deriving that instead from 'this is the law of the guilt offering' (one law for all guilt offerings) — freeing him to read the vav literally and include misuse after all
The elegant symmetry: Each sage uses ONE derivation (the vav, or the gezeirah shava) for one purpose and the OTHER derivation for the other — precisely reversed between Rabbi Akiva and the Rabbis.
