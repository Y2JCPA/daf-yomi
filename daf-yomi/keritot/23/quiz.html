Keritot 23 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to the mishna, what offering(s) does one bring after eating a piece of forbidden fat followed by a piece of sacrificial permitted fat, when he doesn't know which piece he ate first?
A. Nothing at all
B. A provisional guilt offering after the first piece, then a sin offering and a definite guilt offering for misuse after eating the second piece too
C. Only a sin offering
D. Only a definite guilt offering
Correct: B — The mishna rules that after the first piece (uncertain), he brings a provisional guilt offering; once he eats the second piece too, he certainly ate both the forbidden fat and the sacrificial fat, requiring a sin offering plus a definite guilt offering for misuse.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to the Gemara's resolution, why can Rabbi Shimon hold that the notar prohibition takes effect atop the already-existing forbidden-fat prohibition, when he elsewhere holds that Yom Kippur's prohibition does NOT take effect atop a neveila's prohibition?
A. Because notar is always more severe than any other prohibition
B. Because specifically with regard to sacrificial food, the Torah reveals that prohibitions do take effect atop one another, unlike with non-sacred food
C. Because Rabbi Shimon actually contradicts himself with no resolution
D. Because neveila and notar are the same prohibition
Correct: B — The Gemara resolves this by showing that sacrificial food is a special case where the Torah explicitly allows layered prohibitions (proven from the impurity prohibition applying to already-forbidden sacrificial portions), unlike ordinary non-sacred food.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to Rav Naḥman, why doesn't eating notar (which is sacrificial food) also trigger a definite guilt offering for misuse of consecrated property in this mishna's case?
A. Because notar is never actually sacrificial food
B. Because this notar, having spoiled, is worth less than a peruta — the minimum value required for misuse liability
C. Because misuse liability never applies to notar under any circumstances
D. Because the person already brought enough offerings
Correct: B — Rav Naḥman explains that spoiled notar has lost enough value that it falls below the peruta threshold required for misuse liability, distinguishing it from the earlier clause involving fresh sacrificial fat.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to the new mishna, what happens to a provisional guilt offering (per the Rabbis' view) if it becomes known BEFORE slaughter that the person never sinned?
A. It is immediately released to graze as a non-sacred animal
B. It retains its consecrated status as a disqualified offering — it must graze until blemished, then be sold, with proceeds going to communal gift offerings
C. It is sacrificed anyway
D. It is buried immediately
Correct: B — The Rabbis (unlike Rabbi Meir) hold that the animal's consecration stands, since the owner's sincere concern at the time of designation was real; it becomes a disqualified offering that must graze until blemished before being sold.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to the mishna, what is unique about an ox sentenced to be stoned, compared to a provisional guilt offering, once the defining act (stoning) has already occurred and the ox is later found innocent?
A. It must still be buried like the guilt offering
B. Deriving benefit from its carcass becomes permitted, since its stoned status is fixed regardless of the later discovery
C. It is treated exactly like the provisional guilt offering
D. Nothing changes about its status
Correct: B — The mishna teaches that once the ox has actually been stoned, benefiting from its carcass becomes permitted — the outcome is locked in by the completed act, distinguishing it from the provisional guilt offering's more flexible rules before its blood is sprinkled.
