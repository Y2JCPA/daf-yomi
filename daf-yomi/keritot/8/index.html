📖 Overview: Closing One Chapter, Opening the Next

Keritot 8a–8b
אַרְבָּעָה מְחוּסְּרֵי כַּפָּרָה, וְאַרְבָּעָה מְבִיאִין עַל הַזָּדוֹן כַּשּׁוֹגֵג
The scene: The Gemara first extends Beit Hillel and Beit Shammai's dispute about a woman miscarrying on the night before her eighty-first day to a parallel case: a zav who experiences discharges on the night before his eighth day. It then turns to a new mishna addressing a woman with multiple uncertain or definite obligations to bring offerings after childbirth or zivah — a leniency that, according to a famous story, Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel forced through to crash runaway bird prices in Jerusalem. The chapter of thirty-six karet violations then concludes, and a new mishna opens: the four people whose purification process is complete except for the atonement offering that still bars them from sacrificial food, followed by a detailed look at the convert's own distinctive atonement offering.

🌙 A Zav's Night Sighting

Does a discharge on the night before the eighth day count toward a new offering?
❓ Rabbi Hoshaya to bar Kappara: does Beit Hillel's ruling on a woman miscarrying at night (derived from an extra word) extend to a zav who sees three discharges on the night before his eighth day?
↓
💡 Rav Huna bar Aḥa (R' Elazar): night is treated as 'lacking its time' for sacrifice — so three sightings entirely at night do NOT trigger a new offering
↓
1 at night + 2 by day
The lapsed night sighting still combines with the two day sightings — Rabbi Yoḥanan teaches this explicitly, since the first sighting was seen at a 'time of potential liability'
2 at night + 1 by day
Does not combine — according to the view that night lacks its time, the two night sightings can't anchor liability
↓
✅ Rabbi Yoḥanan's own view (elsewhere, re: an impure nazirite) is that night does NOT lack its time — his zav ruling here reflects the other position, cited for completeness
Why it matters: Whether 'night' counts as a valid time for an obligation to take effect recurs across multiple purification cases in the Gemara.

🐦 One Offering for Five Uncertain Births

The mishna's leniency — and the crisis it was designed to solve
CaseRequirement
Uncertainty about five births or five zivot (discharges)Brings one offering total, may eat sacrificial food; no further obligation
Five definite births or five definite zivotBrings one offering to permit eating sacrificial food, but still owes the remaining four offerings
Historical incidentBird-pairs (kinim) for these offerings spiked to a gold dinar in Jerusalem from demand
Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel's oathVowed not to sleep until the price dropped; entered court and taught this very leniency — the price fell to a quarter-silver-dinar that same day
Compare with daf 7: A separate baraita adds: mixed definite-and-uncertain obligations require two nests — one eaten (for the definite), one not (for the uncertain) — with the same asymmetry in remaining liability.

📣 Declaring Which Offering Is For Which

When bringing one offering to cover multiple obligations, what must she say?
R' Yoḥanan b. Nuri
For the definite obligations, she must say: 'This is for the last one.' For the uncertain ones, she names a definite one if present, or else any one of them.
R' Akiva
Whether definite or uncertain, she need only say: 'This is for one of them' — no need to specify which.
Rav Pappa's analysis
Both actually compare her offerings to immersions (one immersion purifies regardless of which obligation prompted it) rather than to sin offerings (which require every one to be brought).
The real dispute
Whether we are concerned for negligence: Rabbi Yoḥanan ben Nuri worries she'll wrongly think future births need no offering unless reminded via 'the last one'; Rabbi Akiva is not concerned.
Why not a sin-offering model? A baraita teaches that liability divided among sin offerings is likewise divided among provisional guilt offerings — ruling out treating her offering like a sin offering, where only the final one atones.

🧍 Four Who Lack Atonement

The new mishna opens Perek 2: purification is complete but sacrificial food is still off-limits
IndividualWhy counted separately (or not)
Zav (male discharge)Counted apart from zava — impurity is triggered by number of sightings, not consecutive days, and excludes accidental causes
Zava (female discharge)Counted apart from zav — impurity depends on consecutive days, and includes accidental causes
Woman after childbirth (yoledet)A distinct halakhic category entirely
Leper (metzora)Male and female lepers are not split into two cases — their impurity's essence (a mark of minimum size) is identical for both
Convert (per Rabbi Eliezer ben Ya'akov)Added by this one opinion — lacks atonement until his offering's blood is sprinkled, though this offering enables entry to the congregation, not eating sacrificial food
Also excluded: The nazirite is left off the main list too, since his offering permits wine-drinking, not sacrificial eating — though Rabbi Eliezer ben Ya'akov includes a pure nazirite for the same reason he includes the convert.

🕊️ The Convert's Unusual Offering

A baraita details what the convert may bring to end his lack of atonement

Standard: a bird nest

  • Two birds — but unlike every other bird-nest offering in the Torah, both are burnt offerings, not one sin and one burnt
  • Bringing just the first bird in the morning already permits eating sacrificial food that evening

Valid substitutes

  • A single animal burnt offering — fulfills the obligation (an a fortiori inference from the lenient bird option)
  • A burnt offering plus a peace offering — mirrors Israel's own offerings at Sinai ('as you do, so he shall do')

Invalid substitute

  • A meal offering plus a peace offering — does not fulfill the obligation, since the verse requires matching Israel's Sinai offerings, which did not include a meal offering
Source verse: 'As you do, so he shall do' (Numbers 15:14) ties the convert's offering to what the Jewish people brought entering the covenant at Sinai — burnt and peace offerings, from animal or, leniently, bird.
