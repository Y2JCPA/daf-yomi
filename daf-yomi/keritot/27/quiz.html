QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to the baraita that settles Rav Menashya bar Gadda's dilemma, if an animal was worth one sela when designated as a guilt offering but appreciated to two sela by the time of sacrifice, has the owner fulfilled his obligation?
A. Yes, since the offering is now worth the required amount
B. No — passive appreciation in value does not count toward fulfilling the obligation
C. Yes, but only if he actively cared for the animal
D. The mishna leaves this unresolved
Correct: B — The baraita explicitly rules that one has NOT fulfilled his obligation in this case, establishing that mere passive appreciation in a consecrated animal's value does not count toward the required offering value.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
What was Rabbi Yoḥanan's reaction each time Rabbi Elazar raised one of his dilemmas?
A. He praised Rabbi Elazar's originality
B. He expressed surprise that his own student hadn't already learned the answer from him after so many years of study
C. He refused to answer
D. He said the questions were unimportant
Correct: B — Rabbi Yoḥanan responded each time with astonishment, noting how many years Rabbi Elazar had studied with him without having already absorbed the relevant halakha.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to the mishna and its derivation, why can a son never fulfill his own sin-offering obligation using an animal his deceased father had already designated, even for an identical transgression?
A. Because sons are exempt from all sin offerings
B. Because the verse 'his offering' (repeated in Leviticus 4:23 and 4:28) teaches that one fulfills his obligation only with his OWN offering, never his father's, regardless of matching severity
C. Because animals cannot be inherited
D. Because only money, never animals, can be inherited for this purpose
Correct: B — The doubled phrase 'his offering' is expounded to exclude even the case of matching severity between father's and son's sin, establishing that one can never fulfill his obligation with an offering designated by someone else, even a parent.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to the Gemara's explanation, what is the key difference between redirecting an already-designated ANIMAL to a different sin versus redirecting designated MONEY to a different sin?
A. There is no difference between them
B. Redirecting an animal fails entirely (no misuse occurs and no atonement results), while redirecting money constitutes actual misuse of consecrated property and can achieve atonement through that mechanism
C. Money can never be redirected under any circumstances
D. Animals can always be redirected freely
Correct: B — The Gemara distinguishes that an already-designated animal simply cannot be repurposed for a different sin at all, whereas money, if misdirected, triggers the halakhot of misuse of consecrated property, which can itself provide a path to atonement.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to the new mishna, what happens if someone designates a bird pair as a sliding-scale sin offering and the bird then develops a blemish?
A. He redeems the bird and brings flour instead
B. He may not bring one-tenth of an ephah of flour with the bird's value, since birds cannot be redeemed at all
C. He simply brings another bird for free
D. The blemish has no effect on the offering
Correct: B — The mishna specifically states that a blemished bird cannot be converted toward a flour offering, since unlike animals, birds have no mechanism of redemption — the money designated for that bird simply cannot be repurposed this way.
