Keritot 28 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to Rav Huna, son of Rav Yehoshua, what three halakhot are derived from the ruling about a formerly-wealthy person's disqualified bird-pair?
A. That birds are always exempt from consecration
B. That living consecrated animals can be permanently disqualified, that value-based sanctity can also be disqualified, and that disqualification from the very outset counts the same as later disqualification
C. That only blemished animals can be disqualified
D. That priests alone can disqualify offerings
Correct: B — The three halakhot are: consecrated living animals can be permanently disqualified even without blemish; sanctity that inheres only in monetary value can also be disqualified; and disqualification from the outset is equivalent to later disqualification.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to Rabbi Shimon's ruling about a Paschal offering, what happens if a woman's designated female animal (unfit for the Paschal offering) gives birth to a male?
A. The offspring must also graze until blemished and be sold
B. The offspring itself may be sacrificed as the Paschal offering, proving that Rabbi Shimon holds disqualified living animals are not permanently disqualified
C. The offspring is forbidden from any use
D. The mother becomes valid instead
Correct: B — Rabbi Shimon rules the male offspring may itself be sacrificed as the Paschal offering, demonstrating his view that consecrated living animals are not permanently disqualified, unlike the Rabbis' position.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to Rav Ḥisda, at which moments can a pair of birds be designated as one sin offering and one burnt offering?
A. At any time the owner chooses
B. Only at the time of purchase by the owner or at the time of slaughter by the priest
C. Only during the Temple service itself
D. Only on Yom Kippur
Correct: B — Rav Ḥisda restricts the valid moments for designation to either the owner's purchase or the priest's slaughter — no intermediate declaration establishes the designation.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to the closing mishna, why does the Torah sometimes list 'lamb' before 'goat' and sometimes reverse the order?
A. Because lambs are inherently more valuable
B. To teach that the two are equal in status, despite the usual order of mention — the same pattern used for doves/pigeons and father/mother
C. Because of scribal error
D. Because goats were rare in the desert
Correct: B — The mishna teaches that when the Torah's usual order (lamb before goat) is reversed in another verse, this reversal itself teaches that the two are of equal status — the same exegetical pattern applied to doves/pigeons and father/mother.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to Rav Ashi's closing comment, what was the High Priest Yissakhar of Kfar Barkai's fundamental error at the king's dinner debate about goat versus lamb meat?
A. He gave the wrong species entirely
B. He did not know that this very mishna teaches lambs and goats are equal in status, rather than lamb being superior, and his confident wrong answer (with a contemptuous gesture) led to his hands being severed
C. He refused to answer the question
D. He insulted the queen directly
Correct: B — Rav Ashi explains that Yissakhar had never properly learned the mishna teaching that lambs and goats are equal, leading him to assert lamb's superiority; combined with his disrespectful gesture, this cost him dearly when the king ordered his hands severed.
