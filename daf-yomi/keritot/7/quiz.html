QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to the Rabbis (as opposed to Rabbi Meir), what happens to a priest's daughter who married a man permanently disqualifying her from returning to the priesthood, if she then unwittingly eats teruma?
A. She pays principal and an added fifth, and her death penalty is strangulation
B. She pays only the principal, with no added fifth, and her death penalty is burning
C. She is entirely exempt from any payment
D. She must divorce her husband immediately
Correct: B — The Rabbis hold that regardless of whom she married, she pays only the principal without the added fifth, and her death penalty remains burning, as she retains a conceptual tie to her priestly lineage.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to Reish Lakish, why doesn't the mishna list 'one whose sin occurred before Yom Kippur' among its exceptions to the provisional guilt offering, the way it lists the Temple-defiler?
A. Because Yom Kippur never atones for unwitting sins
B. Because after Yom Kippur has passed, there is no longer any sin remaining to require exclusion — Yom Kippur already atoned for it
C. Because the Temple-defiler case is unrelated to sin offerings
D. Because provisional guilt offerings are brought only on Yom Kippur
Correct: B — Reish Lakish explains that the mishna's stated exceptions involve genuine unresolved sins that the Torah exempts from an offering; after Yom Kippur, the sin itself is already atoned for, so there is nothing left to exclude.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to Rava's final resolution, for which transgression does even Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi agree that Yom Kippur cannot atone, regardless of repentance?
A. Idol worship
B. Forbidden relations
C. Transgressing the prohibitions of Yom Kippur itself (such as eating or working on it)
D. Theft
Correct: C — Rava explains that Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi must concede that Yom Kippur cannot atone for the desecration of Yom Kippur itself, since the sinner might die before the day's atoning power can take effect.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Rabbi Akiva, why is an unwitting blasphemer liable to bring a sin offering despite committing no physical action?
A. Because blasphemy always involves striking someone
B. Because the karet stated for blasphemy (Numbers 15:30) is written in a passage that discusses bringing an offering
C. Because the Rabbis agree with him on this point
D. Because blasphemy is punished the same as idol worship
Correct: B — Rabbi Akiva reasons that since the Torah could have stated karet for blasphemy anywhere, its placement specifically within a passage about offerings teaches that an offering is required.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to the new mishna, which of the following miscarriages requires NO sin offering at all?
A. A fetus with the form of a domesticated animal
B. A 'sandal' fetus (flat, fish-like form)
C. An amniotic sac full of water
D. A fetus that emerged cut into pieces
Correct: C — The mishna lists an amniotic sac full of water (or blood, or discolored matter) among the cases requiring no sin offering at all, along with fetuses shaped like fish, locusts, or vermin, and a miscarriage on day forty.
