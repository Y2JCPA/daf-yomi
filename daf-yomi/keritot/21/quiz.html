Keritot 21 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to Rav Adda bar Avin, what does the phrase 'so too every item' in the generalization-detail-generalization reading come to include, beyond birds and animals?
A. Fish blood
B. The blood of a koy, since it is a genuinely distinct category of creature, not merely an uncertain case
C. Human blood
D. The blood of creeping animals
Correct: B — Rav Adda bar Avin explains that this phrase includes the koy's blood, understanding the koy as a distinct entity in its own right (not merely an animal of uncertain status), which is why a special verse was needed to include it.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
How does the Gemara resolve the apparent contradiction between the baraita (claiming human flesh has no 'light' impurity) and the mishna in Oktzin (which describes flesh cut from a living person becoming impure as food)?
A. The mishna is rejected as unreliable
B. The baraita's claim applies only after death — a living person's flesh can become impure as food, but a corpse carries only severe impurity
C. Human flesh never becomes impure under any circumstances
D. The mishna is discussing animal flesh, not human flesh
Correct: B — The Gemara resolves the contradiction by limiting the baraita's claim to a person's status after death; while alive, flesh cut from a person can indeed become susceptible to the 'light' impurity of food, given proper intent and contact with liquid.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to Rav, when does fish blood become prohibited for consumption despite normally being entirely permitted?
A. Whenever a person touches it
B. When it is collected in a receptacle without any visible identifying marks like scales, since it then resembles forbidden animal or bird blood
C. Fish blood is never prohibited under any circumstances
D. Only on Yom Kippur
Correct: B — Rav rules that fish blood collected in a container becomes suspect and prohibited unless it contains visible scales confirming its fish origin, since otherwise it could be mistaken for forbidden blood.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Rabbi Zeira, under what condition is one flogged for consuming the blood of a creeping animal?
A. Never, under any circumstances
B. Only if warned specifically about the prohibition against eating a creeping animal (which encompasses its blood), not if warned about the general blood prohibition
C. Only if warned about the general blood prohibition
D. Always, regardless of the warning given
Correct: B — Rabbi Zeira explains that lashes apply only when the forewarning specifies the creeping-animal prohibition, which includes its blood; the general blood prohibition does not actually cover creeping animals' blood at all.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to Rabbi Ḥiyya's explanation, why doesn't a sliver of animal carcass (less than an olive-bulk) combined with less than an egg-bulk of food require contact with liquid to become susceptible to impurity, unlike ordinary food items?
A. Because carcasses are never susceptible to any impurity
B. Because since this sliver could eventually combine to reach a full olive-bulk (triggering severe impurity on its own), it is treated differently from ordinary food that never carries severe impurity
C. Because liquid never affects susceptibility to impurity
D. Because dough always eliminates the need for liquid contact
Correct: B — Rabbi Ḥiyya explains that because this carcass sliver has the potential to eventually reach the threshold for severe impurity on its own, it is exempted from the general rule requiring liquid contact for susceptibility, unlike ordinary food that could never carry severe impurity.
