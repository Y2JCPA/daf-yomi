Keritot 2 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to the opening mishna, how many transgressions in the Torah carry a punishment of karet for intentional violation?
A. Twenty-four
B. Thirty-nine
C. Thirty-six
D. Forty
Correct: C — The mishna opens by listing thirty-six categories of transgression punishable by karet.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
Why, according to Rabbi Yoḥanan, does the mishna state the number 'thirty-six' instead of simply listing the cases?
A. To help students memorize the list
B. To teach that one who transgresses all of them in a single lapse of awareness is liable for each one separately
C. To exclude a case not really punishable by karet
D. To indicate the order in which they occurred at Sinai
Correct: B — Rabbi Yoḥanan explains that the tally teaches that one who violates all thirty-six unwittingly in one lapse of awareness must bring thirty-six separate sin offerings.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
What halakha does Rabbi Eliezer ben Ya'akov's dissenting view about 'five' mechusrei kappara (rather than four) add?
A. That the leper needs two offerings
B. That a convert is also considered lacking atonement until the blood of his offering is sprinkled
C. That the zav is exempt from an offering
D. That the woman after childbirth is like a zava
Correct: B — Rabbi Eliezer ben Ya'akov holds a convert is a fifth person who lacks atonement, needing the sprinkling of his offering's blood before eating consecrated food; the mishna's 'four' excludes this view.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
Why, according to Rabbi Yoḥanan, is karet for intercourse with one's sister mentioned in its own separate verse (Leviticus 20:17)?
A. To teach that this transgression alone bears karet
B. To exempt it from a sin offering
C. To separate this and all other forbidden relations, teaching a distinct liability for each one
D. To teach that only lashes apply, not karet
Correct: C — Rabbi Yoḥanan holds the singling-out of the sister teaches that every forbidden relation generates a separate liability rather than all being punished as one.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
How does Rabbi Yitzḥak understand the purpose of singling out karet for one's sister in Leviticus 20:17?
A. To teach that she alone carries karet while all others carry only lashes
B. To teach that she is judged by karet rather than lashes, since forbidden relations were already generally included under karet
C. To exempt an unwitting sinner entirely
D. To teach that a sister requires two sin offerings
Correct: B — Rabbi Yitzḥak holds that all forbidden relations were already covered by the general karet verse (Lev. 18:29); the sister-verse instead establishes that her punishment is specifically karet rather than lashes.
