5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to Rabbi Yoḥanan's resolution, why does the impurity verse uniquely treat uncertain knowledge as equivalent to full knowledge, unlike the rest of the Torah?
A. Because impurity is always more severe than other sins
B. Because the impurity verse (Leviticus 5:2) uses the phrase 'it was concealed FROM him,' while the general Torah formula requires it to be 'known TO him'
C. Because impurity has no minimum measure
D. Because Rabbi Yoḥanan simply preferred a stricter ruling
Correct: B — Rabbi Yoḥanan derives the distinction from the specific wording of Leviticus 5:2 ('concealed from him'), which uniquely includes even uncertain awareness, unlike the standard formula elsewhere requiring definite knowledge.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
In the new mishna's case of eating either forbidden fat or notar without knowing which, what is Rabbi Eliezer's reasoning for liability?
A. He believes uncertainty never matters
B. Either way, the person definitely sinned — one of the two prohibitions was certainly violated
C. He requires two witnesses regardless
D. He holds notar and forbidden fat are identical prohibitions
Correct: B — Rabbi Eliezer's argument is that regardless of which item was eaten, the person certainly transgressed one prohibition or the other, so he should be liable either way.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to Rabbi Yosei, do Rabbi Eliezer and Rabbi Yehoshua actually disagree about someone who performed labor during the twilight period between Shabbat and Yom Kippur, not knowing which day it was?
A. Yes, this is their central disagreement
B. No — Rabbi Yosei says they both agree he is exempt, since the labor can be understood as partly performed on each day
C. Yes, but only regarding Yom Kippur specifically
D. No, they both agree he is liable
Correct: B — Rabbi Yosei holds that Rabbi Eliezer and Rabbi Yehoshua do not actually disagree about the twilight case — both agree the person is exempt, since the labor can be conceptually split between the two days, meaning no full measure was performed on either single day.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Rabbi Shimon and Rabbi Shimon Shezuri, in which scenario do Rabbi Eliezer and Rabbi Yehoshua actually disagree?
A. When the uncertainty involves two different categories ('two names') of prohibited labor, such as not knowing if fruit was picked from a grapevine or a fig tree
B. When the uncertainty involves the same category ('one name'), such as not knowing which of two vines the grapes came from
C. They never disagree about anything
D. Only when three or more categories are involved
Correct: A — Rabbi Shimon and Rabbi Shimon Shezuri hold the dispute applies only when the uncertainty spans two distinct categories of prohibited labor; when it's the same category (one name), both agree he is liable.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to Rav Naḥman (in the name of Shmuel), why is one who acts entirely without intent (mit'asek) still liable for eating forbidden fat or engaging in forbidden relations, but exempt for performing labor on Shabbat?
A. Because Shabbat labor is never prohibited without full intent
B. Because he derives pleasure from forbidden fat or forbidden relations regardless of intent, while the Torah requires purposeful, constructive intent (melekhet maḥshevet) specifically for Shabbat labor
C. Because forbidden fat carries no minimum measure
D. Because Shabbat labor always requires two witnesses
Correct: B — Rav Naḥman explains that pleasure-based prohibitions like forbidden fat or forbidden relations create liability regardless of intent, since the physical pleasure occurred regardless; Shabbat labor, by contrast, requires genuinely purposeful action (melekhet maḥshevet), so accidental labor without intent is exempt.
