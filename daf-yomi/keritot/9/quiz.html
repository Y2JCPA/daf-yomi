QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to Rav Aḥa bar Ya'akov, how can converts be accepted today, when there is no Temple to bring the atonement offering that Rebbi says is required for entering the covenant?
A. Converts cannot be accepted today at all
B. The verse 'throughout your generations' teaches that converts are accepted in every era, even without an offering
C. A substitute animal is always available
D. Only the immersion is truly required
Correct: B — Rav Aḥa bar Ya'akov derives from 'throughout your generations' (Numbers 15:14) that the acceptance of converts continues across all eras, regardless of whether the Temple offering can currently be brought.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to the mishna, which of the following is NOT one of the four who bring an offering for an intentional transgression the same as for an unwitting one?
A. One who has relations with an espoused maidservant
B. A nazirite who became ritually impure
C. One who eats forbidden fat
D. One who falsely swears the oath of testimony
Correct: C — Eating forbidden fat is not among these four; ordinarily, an unwitting sin brings a sin offering while an intentional one brings a harsher penalty. The four exceptions are the maidservant case, the impure nazirite, and the two false oaths (testimony and deposit).
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to Rabbi Yoḥanan, why is one liable to bring five separate offerings for having relations with five different espoused maidservants in one lapse of awareness, while multiple acts with a single maidservant require only one offering?
A. Because five maidservants is always a capital case
B. Because five separate maidservants constitute separate 'bodies,' each requiring its own offering, unlike repeated acts with one body
C. Because the Torah specifically counts to five
D. Because maidservants are treated like forbidden animals
Correct: B — Rabbi Yoḥanan explains that liability divides according to separate bodies (individuals); repeated acts with the same maidservant share one body and thus one offering, while five different maidservants are five separate bodies requiring five offerings.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
Whose opinion does Rav Ḥisda say the mishna (that a nazirite impure multiple times brings only one offering) must follow, and why?
A. Rabbi Meir, because he is lenient generally
B. Rabbi Yosei, son of Rabbi Yehuda, because he holds the naziriteship of purity begins on the seventh day, so repeated seventh-day impurities never reach an offering-eligible moment
C. Rabbi Akiva, because he requires no action for liability
D. Rabbi Shimon, because of his general leniency toward nazirites
Correct: B — Rav Ḥisda explains that only according to Rabbi Yosei, son of Rabbi Yehuda (naziriteship of purity begins on day seven), can repeated seventh-day impurities combine into one offering; according to Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi, the case wouldn't work this way.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
In the case of a woman who miscarries three times within overlapping 80-day purification periods, how does Rabbi Yehuda's view differ from the Rabbis' view?
A. Rabbi Yehuda says she brings no offering at all
B. The Rabbis anchor her single offering to the most recent (second) fetus; Rabbi Yehuda brings an offering for the first miscarriage and a separate one for the third, which fell outside the first's purification period
C. Rabbi Yehuda requires a separate offering for every single miscarriage
D. There is no actual disagreement between them
Correct: B — The Rabbis hold that the woman's purification period counts from the second (most recent) fetus, requiring one offering total; Rabbi Yehuda holds the first fetus anchors the period, so the second is absorbed into it, but the third — falling after that period ends — requires its own separate offering.
