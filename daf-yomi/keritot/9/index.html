📖 Overview: Converts, Repeated Sins, and Multiple Births

Keritot 9a–9b
חֲמִשָּׁה מְבִיאִין קׇרְבָּן אֶחָד עַל עֲבֵירוֹת הַרְבֵּה
The scene: The daf finishes analyzing the convert's atonement offering — modeled, per Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi, on how the Jewish people themselves entered the covenant at Sinai through circumcision, immersion, and the sprinkling of blood — and explains how conversion continues even without a Temple. A new mishna then lists four transgressors who bring the very same offering whether they sinned intentionally or unwittingly (the espoused maidservant's partner, the impure nazirite, and two false oaths), followed by a mishna listing five people who bring only a single offering no matter how many times they repeated the same transgression. The daf closes with a mishna on a woman who gives birth or miscarries repeatedly in overlapping periods of purification, where the Rabbis and Rabbi Yehuda dispute exactly which pregnancy anchors her single obligatory offering.

🕊️ Entering the Covenant Without a Temple

Rebbi models the convert's rite on Israel's own entry into the covenant at Sinai
📜 Rebbi: 'As you are, so shall the stranger be' (Num. 15:15) means as your ancestors were — entry required circumcision, immersion, and sprinkled blood
↓
❓ Objection: if all three are required by Torah law, how can converts be accepted today, when there is no Temple for the blood-offering?
↓
✅ Rav Aḥa bar Ya'akov: the verse itself says 'throughout your generations' — converts are accepted in every era, offering or not
↓
💰 Practical follow-up: a baraita required a convert today to set aside a quarter-dinar for a future offering
↓
🚫 Rabbi Shimon: Rabban Yoḥanan ben Zakkai's court already nullified this practice, fearing accidental misuse of the set-aside money
Ruling: The halakha follows Rabbi Shimon — no money need be set aside.

⚖️ Four Who Bring an Offering for Intentional as for Unwitting Sin

A new mishna: these four owe the identical offering regardless of intent
TransgressorTextual source for equal liability
One who has relations with an espoused maidservant'For his sin that he has sinned' (Lev. 19:22) — the doubled phrase equates unwitting and intentional
A nazirite who became impureThe word 'suddenly' (פִּתְאוֹם) can mean intentional or beyond-control; paired with 'unexpectedly' (פֶּתַע, unwitting) to show the offering covers all three
One who falsely swears the oath of testimonyUnlike other sliding-scale cases, the Torah omits 'and it be hidden from him' — showing intent doesn't change liability
One who falsely swears the oath on a depositDerived by verbal analogy ('shall sin' / 'shall sin') from the oath of testimony
Why these four? In every other case, only an unwitting sin brings a sin offering; intentional sin brings a harsher penalty instead. These four are exceptions where the same offering covers both.

👩‍👩‍👧 Five Maidservants vs. One, Five Times

Rabbi Ḥanina of Tirna'a questions Rabbi Yoḥanan on separate bodies
R' Ḥanina
If one has relations with five different espoused maidservants in a single lapse of awareness, is he liable for one offering or five?
R' Yoḥanan
Liable for each and every one — five separate offerings.
R' Ḥanina
But the mishna says multiple acts with one maidservant require only one offering — what's the difference?
R' Yoḥanan
One maidservant means the acts share a single body (גּוּפָא); five maidservants means five separate bodies — and separate bodies always divide liability.
R' Ḥanina
Where is 'separate bodies divide liability' derived for a maidservant specifically?
R' Yoḥanan
Just as forbidden relations divide by 'a woman' (Lev. 18:19), the maidservant verse (Lev. 19:20) likewise says 'a woman, and she is a maidservant' — dividing liability by each woman.
Principle: Repetition with the same body collapses into one offering; the same act with different people never does.

🔄 Five Who Bring One Offering for Many Transgressions

A mishna: repeated violations of the same prohibition, by the same person against the same target, still cost only one offering

Repeated acts, one body

  • Many acts of intercourse with the same espoused maidservant
  • A nazirite impure many times — but only works per Rabbi Yosei b. Rabbi Yehuda's view that purity-naziriteship starts on day 7, so repeated 7th-day impurity never reaches the offering-eligible 8th day

Repeated warnings / afflictions, one legal event

  • A husband's jealousy warnings against several men — 'law of jealousies' (plural) teaches one law for many warnings
  • A leper afflicted with leprosy multiple times before completing purification — 'this is the law of the leper' covers all relapses
The fifth case: A woman who gives birth to multiple offspring — introduced by the next mishna, and the subject of the daf's final debate.

🤰 Multiple Births, One Offering — Which Birth 'Causes' It?

A woman who miscarries repeatedly within overlapping 80-day purification periods
Case
A woman miscarries a female fetus, then miscarries again within that fetus's 80-day purification period, then miscarries a third time within the second period.
The Rabbis
She brings one offering total ('this is the law of the woman who bears,' Lev. 12:7) — and she counts her purification period from the second (most recent) fetus.
Rabbi Yehuda
She brings an offering for the first miscarriage, none for the second (absorbed into the first's period), then a separate offering for the third (since it fell after the first period ended).
Extended even to twins
The Gemara stresses Rabbi Yehuda's position applies even to a woman miscarrying twins from a single pregnancy — one might have thought he'd concede there, but he does not.
Underlying question: Does the obligation 'reset' with each new pregnancy event (Rabbis: the most recent triggers it) or 'anchor' to the first (Rabbi Yehuda: the earliest triggers it, with later ones assessed against its own clock)?
