QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to the initial answer in the Gemara, why can't karet for intercourse with a full sister (both father's and mother's daughter) be derived by a fortiori inference from the half-sister cases?
A. Because a full sister is not really forbidden
B. Because punishment is never administered on the basis of a fortiori inference alone
C. Because the half-sister cases do not carry karet either
D. Because Rabbi Yitzḥak forbids all logical inferences
Correct: B — The Gemara's rule is that one does not administer punishment (karet) based on an a fortiori inference — an explicit verse is required, even though Rabbi Yitzḥak himself disagrees with this principle.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
What is Rabbi Elazar's (in the name of Rabbi Hoshaya) rule about two prohibitions that share a single stated karet?
A. The transgressor is entirely exempt from any offering
B. One who unwittingly transgresses both owes only one sin offering
C. One who unwittingly transgresses both owes a separate sin offering for each
D. The rule applies only to the anointing oil and nowhere else
Correct: C — Rabbi Elazar teaches that wherever two distinct prohibitions are covered by one karet verse — like blending and applying the anointing oil — the sin offering is split, yielding two separate offerings.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
How does Rabbi Yoḥanan resolve the problem that the mishna's 36 cases mix acts only a man could do with an act only a woman could do?
A. He says the mishna is simply inconsistent and unreliable
B. He removes the case of a woman bringing an animal upon herself and substitutes a male who brings another male upon him, per Rabbi Yishmael
C. He says women are also included in every one of the 36 cases
D. He deletes the case of male homosexual intercourse entirely
Correct: B — Rabbi Yoḥanan reads the mishna as describing a man's possible transgressions, replacing the female bestiality case with the case of a male passively brought into intercourse by another male, following Rabbi Yishmael's view that this is an independent prohibition.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Rav's explanation (via Rabbi Elazar), what are the two categories that together make up the mishna's 36 cases?
A. 18 cases for men and 18 for women
B. 33 cases that carry a sin offering for unwitting violation, plus 3 more added only to complete the count of karet violations
C. 36 identical cases with no distinction
D. 30 cases of forbidden relations plus 6 unrelated cases
Correct: B — Rav's structure: 33 genuine sin-offering cases, plus three more (female bestiality partner, failure to bring the Paschal offering, failure to circumcise) added purely to round out the total of 36 karet violations.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
Why, according to Rabbi Yoḥanan, does the mishna's karet list mention the necromancer but not the sorcerer?
A. Because sorcery is not actually forbidden
B. Because both are stated together in a single prohibition (Leviticus 19:31), so only one need be listed
C. Because the sorcerer's punishment is not karet but stoning
D. Because sorcery was added later and postdates the mishna
Correct: B — Rabbi Yoḥanan explains that since the necromancer and sorcerer are both included in one verse's prohibition, listing one covers both; Reish Lakish instead explains the omission as due to sorcery lacking any physical action.
