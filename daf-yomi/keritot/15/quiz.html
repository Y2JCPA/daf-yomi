Keritot 15 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
At what event does Rabbi Akiva recall asking his three unresolved questions to Rabban Gamliel and Rabbi Yehoshua?
A. A funeral
B. A meat market in Emmaus, while shopping for an animal for Rabban Gamliel's son's wedding feast
C. A Sanhedrin session
D. A Yom Kippur service
Correct: B — Rabbi Akiva recalls asking these questions at the meat market (itlis) of Emmaus, where the group had gone to purchase an animal for the wedding feast of Rabban Gamliel's son.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
How do Rabban Gamliel and Rabbi Yehoshua resolve Rabbi Akiva's question about whether an animal's dangling limb transmits ritual impurity?
A. By citing an explicit verse
B. By analogy to a documented medical practice in Jerusalem, where a dangling human limb was shown to cause no impurity
C. By majority vote
D. They declare the question unanswerable
Correct: B — They cite the practice of people afflicted with boils who had their nearly-severed limbs removed by a doctor on Passover eve without either party becoming impure, proving a dangling limb causes no impurity — and infer the same for animals a fortiori.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
What rebuttal does Rabbi Akiva offer to Rabbi Yehoshua's a fortiori inference from misuse of consecrated property (me'ila) to slaughtering five offerings outside the Temple?
A. That me'ila does not actually require multiple offerings
B. That me'ila carries unique stringencies (treating feeding others like eating, and combining benefits over time) that don't apply to ordinary sin offerings
C. That slaughtering offerings outside the Temple carries no penalty at all
D. That Rabbi Yehoshua's testimony was unreliable
Correct: B — Rabbi Akiva argues that misuse of consecrated property has special stringencies not found in ordinary transgressions, so an inference from its rules to ordinary sin-offering liability is invalid.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to the resolving baraita, how many sin offerings does one owe for eating notar from ONE offering, cooked in five different pots, all within a single lapse of awareness?
A. Five
B. One
C. Zero
D. Ten
Correct: B — The baraita rules that eating notar from a single offering, even prepared in five separate pots, within one lapse of awareness, results in only one sin offering, since it remains one 'body' (one offering).
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to the first tanna in the baraita, how many sin offerings does one owe for eating notar from FIVE separate offerings within a single lapse of awareness — and how does Rabbi Yosei, son of Rabbi Yehuda, disagree?
A. The first tanna says one; Rabbi Yosei says five
B. The first tanna says five, since the offerings are differentiated bodies; Rabbi Yosei says only one, since it is still one lapse of awareness
C. Both agree on one offering total
D. Both agree on five separate offerings
Correct: B — The first tanna holds that five separate offerings (differentiated bodies) generate five sin offerings even within one lapse; Rabbi Yosei, son of Rabbi Yehuda, dissents, holding that even eating from five separate offerings within one lapse of awareness yields only one sin offering.
