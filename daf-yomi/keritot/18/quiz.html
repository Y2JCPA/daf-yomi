QUESTION 1 OF 5
In the test case where a gentile eats the first of two pieces of fat (one forbidden, one permitted) and then a Jew eats the second, not knowing which it is, what does Rav Naḥman rule?
A. The Jew is exempt, since only one piece remained
B. The Jew is liable to bring a provisional guilt offering, since a prohibition was definitely established at some point
C. The Jew must bring a sin offering
D. The gentile is liable instead
Correct: B — Rav Naḥman's theory focuses on whether a prohibition was ever genuinely 'established' — since there definitely was a forbidden piece present at some point, the Jew is liable, unlike under Rava's or Rabbi Zeira's competing theories.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to the baraita, what happens when two different Jews each unwittingly eat one of two pieces of fat (one forbidden, one permitted), each eating only one piece?
A. Neither is liable to any offering
B. Only the first Jew is liable
C. Both are liable to a provisional guilt offering, with the second Jew's liability serving as a precaution against wrongly obligating the first Jew in a sin offering
D. Both must bring sin offerings
Correct: C — The baraita rules both Jews liable — the second one not strictly by the letter of the law, but as a precaution: if he were exempt, it would effectively establish that the first Jew definitely ate the forbidden piece, wrongly obligating him in a sin offering.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to Rav Ashi, why does Rav frame his foundational ruling around a case where someone actually ate a piece of uncertain status, rather than citing Rabbi Eliezer's broader rule that one may volunteer a guilt offering any day?
A. Because Rabbi Eliezer's rule doesn't actually exist
B. Because Rav's case reflects Bava ben Buta's specific practice, which required entering an actual situation of potential uncertainty before volunteering an offering
C. Because eating is required by Torah law for any offering
D. Because Rav rejected Rabbi Eliezer's opinion entirely
Correct: B — Rav Ashi explains that Rav's case tracks Bava ben Buta's practice: even he, who volunteered offerings daily, needed to enter a genuine situation of potential uncertainty first, which is what Rav's eating scenario provides.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
What is the core disagreement between Rabbi Zeira/Reish Lakish and Rava/Rabbi Yoḥanan regarding knowledge of uncertainty?
A. Whether uncertainty ever requires any offering at all
B. Whether learning of mere uncertainty (rather than definite knowledge) before a second unwitting act divides the two acts into separate liabilities
C. Whether Yom Kippur atones for any sins
D. Whether misuse of consecrated property requires an offering
Correct: B — The dispute centers on whether gaining knowledge of a mere possibility (not confirmed) before a subsequent unwitting act functions like confirmed knowledge to divide liability into separate sin/guilt offerings for each act.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to the Gemara's resolution, why does misuse of consecrated property (me'ila) still combine into one liability even when a Yom Kippur falls between the two acts, unlike an ordinary uncertain transgression?
A. Because misuse of consecrated property is never atoned for
B. Because Yom Kippur atones for ritual prohibitions but not for the monetary restitution obligation that misuse also requires
C. Because misuse always requires two separate offerings regardless
D. Because Yom Kippur does not exist for priests
Correct: B — The Gemara explains that Yom Kippur's atonement covers ritual violations but not the monetary component of misuse of consecrated property (repayment plus a fifth), so the two acts of misuse still combine despite the intervening Yom Kippur.
