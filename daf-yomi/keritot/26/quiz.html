QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to the Gemara, why does Yom Kippur NOT exempt a woman with an uncertain miscarriage from bringing her sin offering afterward, despite this seeming to be 'a sin known only to God'?
A. Because women are never included in Yom Kippur's atonement
B. Because her offering is brought for purification (to permit eating sacrificial food), not for atonement — Yom Kippur atones for 'all their sins' but not 'all their impurity'
C. Because miscarriage is always a definite, known event
D. Because Yom Kippur only atones for men
Correct: B — The Gemara distinguishes her offering as serving a purification function rather than an atonement function, and Yom Kippur's atonement covers sins specifically, not impurity-related purification requirements.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to the Gemara's elimination process regarding the three 'knowing' verses (individual, Nasi, community sin offerings), why can't the individual's requirement be derived from the Nasi's?
A. Because individuals never bring sin offerings
B. Because a Nasi is uniquely exempt from the sin offering for the 'hearing a voice' oath category, while individuals are not exempt from it
C. Because Nasi offerings are always more expensive
D. Because there is no difference between them at all
Correct: B — The Gemara shows that a Nasi's sin offering obligations are more lenient in that a Nasi is excluded from the 'hearing a voice' oath's sin offering, a leniency that doesn't apply to an individual, blocking direct derivation.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to Rava's final explanation, what is the actual purpose of bringing a provisional guilt offering, if a sin offering is still required once the sin becomes definitively known?
A. It has no purpose at all
B. It protects the person from suffering and anguish during the period of genuine uncertainty, not final atonement
C. It replaces the need for a sin offering entirely
D. It is required only for financial reasons
Correct: B — Rava concludes that the provisional guilt offering's function is to spare the person from ongoing suffering while the uncertainty remains unresolved, since a sin offering is still ultimately required once the sin is confirmed.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
What is the core disagreement between Rav and Rabbi Yoḥanan regarding an uncertain bird sin offering when certainty of birth arrives after the bird's neck has already been pinched?
A. Whether the bird achieves any atonement at all
B. Whether the meat may be eaten afterward — Rav permits it since atonement was achieved, while Rabbi Yoḥanan forbids it as a rabbinic decree to prevent confusion with ordinary uncertain offerings
C. Whether the bird must be a specific species
D. Whether the woman must bring a second bird
Correct: B — Both agree the sprinkling achieves genuine atonement/purification, but they disagree on eating the meat: Rav permits it, while Rabbi Yoḥanan prohibits it as a protective rabbinic decree against people mistakenly eating ordinary unresolved uncertain bird offerings.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to the Gemara's challenge in the new mishna, why can't 'his misuse' (me'ilato) in the mishna's first clause refer to the guilt-offering ram itself?
A. Because guilt offerings are never made from rams
B. Because the Torah (Leviticus 5:16) requires the added one-fifth payment to accompany the repayment for the STOLEN value, not the offering animal itself
C. Because the mishna never mentions any payment at all
D. Because rams cannot be valued in sela
Correct: B — The Gemara demonstrates that if 'his misuse' referred to the guilt-offering animal, it would wrongly imply the one-fifth payment travels with the offering animal, contradicting the Torah's requirement that the one-fifth accompanies the restitution for the stolen value itself.
