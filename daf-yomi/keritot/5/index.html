📖 Overview: From Legal Analogy to the History of the Sacred Oil

Keritot 5a–5b
אַל תְּהִי גְּזֵירָה שָׁוָה קַלָּה בְּעֵינֶיךָ
The scene: After closing out the discussion of lashes for general prohibitions (bread, parched grain, and fresh stalks before the omer), the Gemara pauses to stress how much halakhic weight can rest on a single verbal analogy (gezeirah shava): karet for piggul, for notar, for a daughter born of rape, and stoning for those liable to it are all derived this way. The daf then returns to the mishna's case of the anointing oil, detailing who is liable for blending or applying it and why blending it in partial amounts is only sometimes permitted. A lengthy aggadic excursus follows on the miracles that let the original twelve log of oil anoint the Tabernacle, priests, and kings across the generations, which kings of the house of David were anointed and why, and the touching story of Moses's and Aaron's worry that they had misused consecrated oil.

📜 Never Take a Verbal Analogy Lightly

Four Sages each cite a foundational halakha that rests entirely on a gezeirah shava
SageHalakha derived only by verbal analogy
Rabbi YannaiKaret for piggul is derived from the karet stated for notar, via the shared phrase 'shall bear his iniquity'
Rabbi SimaiKaret for notar itself is derived from a second gezeirah shava, linking 'sacred' (קֹדֶשׁ) in Leviticus to 'sacred' in Exodus
AbayeKaret for intercourse with one's daughter born of rape is derived from the word 'henna' (הֵנָּה) shared between two verses about granddaughters
Rav AshiDeath by stoning for those liable to it in cases of forbidden relations is derived from 'their blood shall be upon them,' shared with the necromancer and sorcerer
The lesson: A gezeirah shava may look like a minor technical tool, but entire halakhot with no other textual basis depend on it.

🫗 Blending and Applying the Anointing Oil

A baraita defines exactly who becomes liable
1️⃣ Blending the oil to learn how it is made, or to prepare it for communal Temple use — exempt
↓
2️⃣ Blending the oil in order to apply it to one's own skin — liable
↓
3️⃣ Applying oil that someone else blended (not the original Mosaic formula) — exempt, since liability requires Moses's own oil specifically
↓
4️⃣ Blending only half the specified amount: for the anointing oil this is exempt ('like it' implies the full exact composition); for the incense, by contrast, even a half-batch is liable, since incense is routinely burned in two half-portions (morning and afternoon)
Precision matters: A baraita even specifies the exact weights — myrrh, cassia, and cinnamon at 500 shekels each, calamus at 250 — totaling 1,750 shekels, with each ingredient weighed independently rather than balanced against another.

✨ The Miracle of the Twelve Log

Rabbi Yehuda: many miracles accompanied the anointing oil from beginning to end
שֶׁמֶן מִשְׁחַת קֹדֶשׁ יִהְיֶה זֶה לִי לְדֹרֹתֵיכֶם
The account: Moses prepared only twelve log of anointing oil in the wilderness — despite absorption by the cooking pot, by the spice roots, and loss to the fire — and that same amount anointed the Tabernacle and its vessels, Aaron and his sons throughout the seven days of inauguration, and every High Priest and king afterward, yet remains intact for future use. Rabbi Yehuda finds this hinted in the word 'zeh' ('this'), whose gematria is twelve. The oil was later sequestered together with the Ark, the jar of manna, and Aaron's staff — a linkage the Gemara derives through three separate verbal analogies on the words 'there,' 'generations,' and 'kept.'

👑 Which Kings Were Anointed?

Anointing confirms a contested succession, not a routine one

House of David — anointed only amid dispute

  • Solomon: due to Adonijah's rival claim to the throne
  • Joash: due to the usurper Athaliah
  • Jehoahaz: due to competition from his older brother Jehoiakim

Kings of Israel — never anointed with the sacred oil

  • Jehu was anointed by Elisha only due to his rebellion against Joram
  • But the oil used was pure balsam, not the sacred anointing oil — since Israelite kings never qualify for the real thing
  • By the time of Jehoahaz the sacred oil had already been sequestered with the Ark, manna, and Aaron's staff — confirming only balsam was used even for Davidic kings anointed after that point
The rule: A king inherits the throne without need for anointing — Deuteronomy 17:20 ('he and his sons') teaches succession is automatic — except where a dispute requires public confirmation.

😟 Moses and Aaron's Worry

Did the oil overflowing onto Aaron's beard count as misuse of consecrated property?
Scene
Two drops of anointing oil, shaped like pearls, hung miraculously in Aaron's beard whenever he spoke, never falling — evidence of extra oil poured beyond what was needed.
Moses
Worried: perhaps I misused consecrated property, God forbid, by pouring more oil than necessary.
Bat kol
'Like the dew of Hermon that comes down upon the mountains of Zion' (Ps. 133:3) — just as dew is not subject to misuse, the oil on Aaron's beard was not consecrated property misused.
Aaron
Still worried: perhaps Moses did not misuse it, but I did — since I am the one benefiting from the extra oil on my own body.
Bat kol
'How good and how pleasant it is for brothers to dwell together' (Ps. 133:1) — just as Moses did not misuse it, neither did you.
Also disputed: Tanna'im disagree whether pouring oil on the High Priest's head or anointing between his eyelashes came first, and kings were anointed in the shape of a crown while priests in the shape of the Greek letter chi (Χ).
