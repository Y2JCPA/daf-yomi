Keritot 14 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to Rabbi Meir, under what circumstances can a new prohibition take legal effect on something already forbidden by an earlier prohibition?
A. Never, under any circumstances
B. Only when the new prohibition is 'inclusive' (extending to new people/contexts) or 'expanded' (adding new severity)
C. Only on Yom Kippur
D. Always, without exception
Correct: B — Rabbi Meir holds that a prohibition generally cannot take effect where one already exists, except for an inclusive prohibition (issur kolel) or an expanded prohibition (issur mosif), both of which add a genuinely new legal dimension.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
Why can't the prohibitions of notar and piggul both apply to the very same olive-bulk of meat, according to the Gemara?
A. Because notar and piggul are the same prohibition
B. Because once meat is rendered piggul, its piggul status prevents it from ever additionally becoming notar
C. Because piggul only applies to blood
D. Because notar cannot exist on Yom Kippur
Correct: B — The Gemara explains that piggul status, once it applies to a piece of meat, precludes that same piece from also becoming notar — the two statuses are mutually exclusive on the identical olive-bulk.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
Why does the Gemara reject the possibility of a sixth sin offering (for blood) in the mishna's scenario of maximum liability from a single act of eating?
A. Because blood carries no punishment at all
B. Because the human throat physically cannot hold more than two olive-bulks at once during a single act of swallowing
C. Because blood is always kosher
D. Because Yom Kippur exempts blood consumption
Correct: B — The Gemara reasons that since the mishna describes a single continuous act of eating, and the throat holds no more than two olive-bulks at a time, there is a physical cap on how many distinct forbidden foods can combine within that one act.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to the mishna, how many sin offerings can one be liable to bring for a single act of intercourse with one's daughter's daughter (granddaughter)?
A. Four
B. Five
C. Six
D. Seven
Correct: D — The mishna lists seven categories violated simultaneously: granddaughter, daughter-in-law, brother's wife, father's brother's wife, wife's sister, married woman, and niddah.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
How do Rabbi Abbahu and Rabbi Yoḥanan reconcile Rabbi Yosei's ruling in this mishna (adding 'wife of father' liability) with his position elsewhere that a later prohibition does not create additional liability?
A. They say Rabbi Yosei retracted his other position entirely
B. They explain that Rabbi Yosei concedes specifically for an expanded prohibition (issur mosif), which is the case here
C. They say the two rulings are unrelated and cannot be reconciled
D. They say this mishna is not really attributed to Rabbi Yosei
Correct: B — Rabbi Abbahu and Rabbi Yoḥanan explain that even though Rabbi Yosei generally holds a later prohibition creates no additional liability, he concedes this is different for an expanded prohibition (issur mosif), which is what applies in this case.
