📖 Overview: How Prohibitions Stack on One Another

Keritot 14a–14b
אִיסּוּר חָל עַל אִיסּוּר
The scene: The daf opens with a foundational question: does a new prohibition take legal effect on an item already forbidden by an earlier prohibition? Rabbi Meir generally says no — except when the new prohibition is either 'inclusive' (issur kolel, extending the forbidden scope to new people or contexts) or 'expanded' (issur mosif, adding a new dimension of severity). Using this distinction, the Gemara explains exactly how the previous daf's case of one bite generating four sin offerings and one guilt offering actually works, then fends off several attempts to push the count even higher. The daf then pivots to a series of escalating mishnayot: one act of forbidden intercourse that violates six, then seven, forbidden-relation prohibitions simultaneously, built through the same logic of expanding and inclusive prohibitions layering onto a single relationship.

🔗 A Prohibition Landing on a Prohibition

Rabbi Meir's general rule, and its two exceptions
נְהִי דְּאִיסּוּר חָל עַל אִיסּוּר לֵית לֵיהּ, אִיסּוּר כּוֹלֵל וְאִיסּוּר מוֹסִיף אִית לֵיהּ
The principle: Rabbi Meir holds that, as a rule, a second prohibition cannot take effect on an object or person already forbidden by an earlier one — the first prohibition already covers the case, so nothing new is added. But he recognizes two exceptions. An inclusive prohibition (issur kolel) does take effect, because it extends the forbidden scope to new people or circumstances beyond the original prohibition's reach. An expanded prohibition (issur mosif) also takes effect, because it adds an entirely new dimension of severity (such as forbidding benefit, not just consumption) that the first prohibition never covered. These two exceptions are the engine behind the extraordinary multi-offering cases explored on this daf.

🍽️ Building the Five Liabilities

Each new prohibition in the chain is inclusive or expanded — never a flat repeat
StageWhy the new prohibition takes effect
Pure person eats chelevBaseline: forbidden only due to the fat itself
He becomes impureInclusive — being impure adds prohibition even against pure pieces of the meat, so it lands atop the fat prohibition too
The animal is consecratedExpanded — adds a benefit-prohibition beyond mere eating, so it also attaches to the chelev
It becomes leftover (notar)Expanded — a prohibition against the altar itself is added, which then extends to the ordinary consumer too
It is eaten on Yom KippurInclusive — the day's universal eating-ban extends even to consecrated leftover food
Result: Five distinct legal 'reasons' stack on the very same bite — four sin offerings and one guilt offering (for misusing consecrated property), exactly as the previous daf's mishna described.

❓ Why Not Five, Six, or Even More?

The Gemara tests — and rejects — several ways to push the liability count higher
🤔 Why not add piggul (improperly-intended offering) for a 5th sin offering?
↓
🚫 Notar and piggul can never overlap on the very same olive-bulk — piggul, once it applies, blocks the meat from ever becoming notar
↓
🤔 What about adding blood (dam) for a 6th sin offering?
↓
🚫 The mishna describes one continuous act of swallowing — and the throat physically holds no more than two olive-bulks at once, capping how many prohibited foods can combine in a single act
↓
✅ Two competing solutions instead reach 5 sin offerings validly: eating a kidney with its fat (reaching Yom Kippur's larger 'date-bulk' minimum), or eating an olive-bulk of piggul from a separate animal alongside the chelev
Side note: A claim that Yom Kippur lacks the Shabbat-style prohibitions of 'joining courtyards' and carrying is raised (via Rafram) but ultimately rejected as a mistaken transmission (beruta).

👨‍👩‍👧 One Act, Six or Seven Sins

A series of mishnayot: forbidden relationships that compound through inclusive and expanded prohibitions
RelationshipSin offerings owedCategories violated
Intercourse with one's daughter6Daughter, sister (if she was born of his mother), brother's wife, father's brother's wife, married woman, niddah
Intercourse with daughter's daughter7Granddaughter, daughter-in-law, brother's wife, father's brother's wife, wife's sister, married woman, niddah
Intercourse with wife's daughter6Same structure as the daughter case
Intercourse with wife's daughter's daughter7Same structure as the granddaughter case
Intercourse with mother-in-law7Mother-in-law, daughter-in-law, brother's wife, father's brother's wife, wife's sister, married woman, niddah
How the categories accumulate: Each additional prohibition attaches only because the woman's status changes in a way that is inclusive or expanded relative to the man — e.g., she later marries his brother, or his father's brother, extending the forbidden scope without ever simply repeating an existing prohibition.

⚖️ Rabbi Yosei's Added Case

Reconciling his 'wife of father' addition with his stance elsewhere on competing prohibitions
R' Yosei
If the grandfather 'transgressed' and married the granddaughter, one is also liable for 'wife of his father' when engaging in relations with her.
Challenge
But how could the grandfather even marry her — she'd already be forbidden to him as his brother's former wife?
R' Yoḥanan
The marriage happened through levirate marriage (yibum), when the grandfather's brother died childless.
Further challenge
If it was valid yibum, why call it a 'transgression'? And separately — doesn't Rabbi Yosei elsewhere (Sanhedrin) hold that a later-arising prohibition does NOT create additional liability, sentencing only by the first prohibition to apply?
Resolution
Rabbi Ya'akov: he 'transgressed' a rabbinic secondary-relative restriction (his son's daughter-in-law). And Rabbi Abbahu/Rabbi Yoḥanan: Rabbi Yosei concedes specifically for an expanded prohibition — here, the added prohibition against another of the grandfather's own sons marrying her extends to him too.
Consistent thread: Even a tanna who generally rejects layered liability still concedes the issur mosif exception — the same principle driving every multi-offering case on this daf.
