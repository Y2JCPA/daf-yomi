Keritot 11 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to Rabbi Yehuda, who qualifies as the 'espoused maidservant' discussed in the mishna?
A. Any fully free woman
B. A woman who is half-maidservant and half-free, owned by two masters one of whom freed his share
C. Any gentile woman regardless of status
D. A widow who remarried
Correct: B — Rabbi Yehuda reads 'redeemed and not redeemed' (Leviticus 19:20) literally, defining the espoused maidservant as a woman partially freed — half-maidservant, half-free.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to the Gemara's derivation from the verse's word order, what determines whether the man brings a guilt offering for relations with an espoused maidservant?
A. His own intent alone
B. Whether the woman herself is flogged — if she is not flogged, he brings no offering
C. The season of the year
D. Whether he is married
Correct: B — Because 'there shall be an inspection' (her flogging) is written before 'he shall bring his guilt offering,' the Gemara derives that his liability to an offering depends entirely on whether she is flogged.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
In the maidservant case, what happens according to the mishna if one partner is a minor or one partner is asleep?
A. Only the minor or sleeping partner is exempt; the other remains liable, as in ordinary forbidden relations
B. Both partners are exempt entirely, since their liabilities are juxtaposed to one another in the verse
C. Both partners are always liable regardless of age or wakefulness
D. The law is silent on this scenario
Correct: B — Unlike ordinary forbidden relations (where only the minor or sleeping partner is exempt while the other remains liable), the maidservant case links both partners' liability together, so a minor or sleeping partner on either side exempts both.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Rav Sheshet's explanation of the seemingly corrupted baraita, what does 'they equated completion with the initial stage' mean with regard to the maidservant?
A. Both are always liable
B. Atypical (non-procreative) completed intercourse is exempt, just as typical initial-stage contact is exempt — both fail to meet 'lies carnally' (seed-bearing intercourse)
C. The baraita is meaningless and should be deleted
D. Completion always creates liability regardless of manner
Correct: B — Rav Sheshet explains the baraita is not corrupted: it equates atypical (non-procreative) completed intercourse, which is exempt, with typical initial-stage contact, which is also exempt — both fail the Torah's requirement of 'lies carnally.'
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to the new mishna, what is the outcome if one witness says a person ate forbidden fat, and the person himself denies it?
A. He is liable to a sin offering
B. He is liable to a provisional guilt offering
C. He is exempt entirely, since his own denial overrides a single witness
D. He receives lashes
Correct: C — The mishna rules that when a single witness's testimony is directly contradicted by the person's own denial, he is exempt — a lone witness's word does not override his own statement.
