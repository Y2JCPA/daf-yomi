Tamid 29 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to the mishna, which two types of wood were NOT fit for the altar's wood arrangement?
A. Fig and nut wood
B. Olive wood and vine (grape) wood
C. Pine and oak wood
D. Cedar and palm wood
Correct: B — The mishna states that all wood is fit for the arrangement except wood from the olive tree and the vine; fig, nut, and pine were the preferred choices.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to Rabbi Ami, which categories of sacred texts sometimes employ exaggerated (havai) language?
A. Only the Sages
B. The Torah, the Prophets, and the Sages all employ exaggerated language at times
C. Only the Torah
D. None of these sources ever exaggerate
Correct: B — Rabbi Ami states that exaggerated language appears in the Torah (e.g., 'cities fortified to heaven'), in the Prophets (e.g., 'the earth split' at Solomon's coronation), and in statements of the Sages.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to Shmuel, which THREE things did the Sages describe using exaggeration?
A. The golden cup, the ash heap, and the Curtain
B. The ash heap, the golden vine, and the Curtain
C. The altar, the Menorah, and the Ark
D. The wood arrangement, the incense, and the daily offering
Correct: B — Shmuel specifies exactly three instances of rabbinic exaggeration: the massive ash heap, the golden vine (300 priests to move it), and the Curtain (300 priests to immerse it).
QUESTION 4 OF 5
Why does Shmuel insist that the golden cup used to give the daily lamb water was NOT an exaggeration, contrary to Rava's view?
A. Because gold cups were common household items
B. Because 'there is no poverty in a place of wealth' — the Temple, as a place of abundant wealth, genuinely used an actual gold cup
C. Because the mishna about the golden cup is a later, unreliable addition
D. Because Rava later retracted his own opinion
Correct: B — Shmuel excludes the golden cup from his list of exaggerations, explaining that the Temple's genuine wealth meant an actual gold vessel was entirely plausible and real.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
What challenge is raised against Rav Aḥa bar Ya'akov's explanation that olive and vine wood are excluded to conserve the Land of Israel's resources?
A. That olive trees don't actually grow in Israel
B. That palm trees are also a praised species of the Land, yet palm wood is considered fit for the arrangement — requiring the answer that the mishna refers to non-fruit-bearing varieties
C. That the reasoning applies equally well to Rav Pappa's explanation
D. That the mishna never mentions palm trees at all
Correct: B — The Gemara challenges that if conservation were the reason, praised species like the palm (and even the preferred fig) should also be excluded — resolved by explaining that the wood used comes from non-fruit-bearing varieties of these trees.
