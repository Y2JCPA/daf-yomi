📖 Overview: A King's Questions, and the Chapter's Close

Tamid 32a–32b
אֵיזֶהוּ חָכָם – הָרוֹאֶה אֶת הַנּוֹלָד
The scene: Continuing from the previous daf's mention of sunlight and the altar, the Gemara records a lengthy exchange between Alexander of Macedon and the Elders of the Negev — ten probing questions about the cosmos, wisdom, wealth, and how to be accepted by others. The narrative follows Alexander on a fantastical journey through the Mountains of Darkness, an encounter with a city ruled entirely by women, and a visit to the very gate of the Garden of Eden, where a single eyeball outweighs all his treasure until covered with dust. The chapter then closes with teachings praising nighttime Torah study, before a new mishna resumes the Temple service: the priests' recitation of Shema and blessings, followed by two more lotteries — for burning the incense, and for carrying the limbs up to the altar.

👑 A Selection of Alexander's Questions

Wisdom the Elders of the Negev offered the Macedonian king
Alexander's QuestionThe Elders' Answer
Who is truly wise?'The one who sees and anticipates the consequences of his behavior' (הָרוֹאֶה אֶת הַנּוֹלָד)
Who is truly mighty?'The one who masters his own desire' (הַכּוֹבֵשׁ אֶת יִצְרוֹ)
Who is truly wealthy?'The one who is content with his own portion' (הַשָּׂמֵחַ בְּחֶלְקוֹ)
Is it better to live at sea or on dry land?Dry land — sailors' minds are never truly settled until they reach shore
Who among you is wisest?'We are all equal — every question you ask, we answer as one'
A tense exchange: When Alexander threatened execution for their 'defiance,' the Elders replied that a king's power is real, but falsehood is unbecoming of a king who had pledged them no harm — and Alexander, impressed, honored them with royal garments and golden chains instead.

🗺️ Through the Mountains of Darkness

A journey ending at the gate of the Garden of Eden
1️⃣ Wanting to campaign in Africa, Alexander is advised to bring Libyan donkeys (suited to darkness) and coils of rope to mark his path through the Mountains of Darkness
↓
2️⃣ He reaches a town ruled entirely by women, who decline battle (a loss either way would shame him) and instead serve him bread made of GOLD — teaching him he came not for food, but for wealth
↓
3️⃣ Later, resting by a fragrant spring, he suspects it flows from the Garden of Eden and follows it to its gate, demanding entry
↓
4️⃣ Denied entry as unrighteous, he asks for a gift instead and receives a single eyeball — which, when weighed, outweighs ALL his gold and silver combined
↓
5️⃣ The Sages explain: it's a mortal eye, never satisfied while it can see — proven when covering it with a bit of dirt instantly balances the scale, per Proverbs 27:20: 'the eyes of man are never satiated'

📖 Closing the Chapter: The Value of Nighttime Torah Study

Praise for Torah scholars, marking the end of this section
וְכׇל בָּנַיִךְ לִמּוּדֵי ה׳ וְרַב שְׁלוֹם בָּנָיִךְ
Two teachings: Rabbi Ḥiyya taught: anyone who occupies himself with Torah study at night is accompanied by the Divine Presence, drawing on Lamentations 2:19's call to 'arise, cry out in the night... pour out your heart before the Lord.' Rabbi Elazar ben Azarya added: Torah scholars increase peace in the world, reading Isaiah 54:13's 'your children (בָּנַיִךְ)' as 'your builders (בּוֹנַיִךְ)' — scholars literally build peace for the world through their study.

🎲 The Third and Fourth Lotteries

Incense, limb-carrying, and preparing the incense-bearer
🎲 Third lottery: only priests who had NEVER burned the incense before could participate — a lifetime honor given just once
↓
🎲 Fourth lottery (new AND experienced priests together): who carries the limbs from the ramp's lower half up onto the altar itself
↓
👕 Priests who didn't win any lottery — still dressed for potential service — were handed to attendants, who removed their sacred vestments (four labeled storage compartments: trousers, tunic, belt, mitre) down to their trousers alone
↓
🥄 The incense winner took the ceremonial spoon (like a large gold vessel holding 3 kav) with a smaller vessel nestled inside it, ready to carry the incense to the golden altar
