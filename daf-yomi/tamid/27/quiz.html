QUESTION 1 OF 5
How does the Gemara ultimately reconcile the biblical verse (I Chronicles 26:17–18), which seems to describe 24 watch posts, with the mishna's count of 21 Levite posts?
A. By declaring the verse an error
B. One approach: minor recount adjustments (the Parbar had only one actual watchman) bring it to 21; an alternative approach accepts 24 as accurate, with 3 of them being priestly posts described using the term 'Levites,' following Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi's principle that priests are sometimes called Levites in Scripture
C. By assuming the mishna is mistaken and adopting 24 as the true number for Levites alone
D. There is no reconciliation offered
Correct: B — The Gemara offers two paths: adjusting the verse's count down to 21, or accepting 24 while attributing 3 posts to priests referred to by the term 'Levites' elsewhere in Scripture.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
Why were the Levites' watch posts on the Temple Mount located INSIDE its wall, while their watch posts for the Temple courtyard were located OUTSIDE its wall?
A. It was purely random placement
B. Sitting is permitted on the Temple Mount, so watchmen could rest inside; sitting in the Temple courtyard is forbidden to all but Davidic kings, so watchmen were placed outside where they could sit if tired
C. The Temple Mount had no wall at all
D. Courtyard watchmen were considered more important and needed more space
Correct: B — The Gemara explains the placement is based entirely on where a tired watchman would be permitted to sit — inside the Mount's wall, but only outside the courtyard's wall, since courtyard sitting is reserved for Davidic royalty.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
What is Rav Ashi's resolution to the problem of priests placing vestments containing wool and linen (kilayim) beneath their heads while sleeping?
A. He says this never actually happened
B. He explains that priestly vestments, especially the belt, are stiff, and the diverse-kinds prohibition applies only to soft garments that provide warmth and comfort, not stiff material
C. He says kilayim never applies to consecrated items
D. He says the vestments were made of a single material only
Correct: B — Rav Ashi's resolution is that the kilayim prohibition targets garments that give warmth and comfort, and stiff material like the priestly belt doesn't fall into that category.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to the Gemara's discussion of Shimon HaTzaddik meeting Alexander the Great while wearing priestly vestments outside the Temple, how is this reconciled with the rule forbidding wearing such vestments 'in the country'?
A. It cannot be reconciled; the story is rejected as false
B. Either his garments only resembled (but weren't actually) consecrated priestly vestments, or the extraordinary circumstance of averting the Temple's destruction justified overriding the halakha under 'it is time to act for the Lord'
C. The rule only applied after Shimon HaTzaddik's time
D. Alexander the Great was considered part of the Temple precincts
Correct: B — The Gemara offers two resolutions: the garments may not have been technically consecrated, or the extreme circumstances permitted overriding normal halakha for the sake of the Temple's survival.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
Why, according to Rav and Rav Huna's advice to their sons, should a person relieve themselves at dawn and dusk even without urgent need?
A. Because it is considered more respectful to do so at those times
B. Because the streets are typically empty then, allowing privacy near one's home, so one won't be forced to hold it in during the busy daytime, which risks health
C. Because Jewish law requires it at those specific times
D. There is no such advice in the text
Correct: B — The advice reflects practical wisdom: relieving oneself at quiet times avoids the health risks of holding it in later when streets are crowded and privacy is harder to find.
