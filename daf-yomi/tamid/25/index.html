📖 Overview: Guarding the Temple Overnight

Tamid 25a–25b
בִּשְׁלֹשָׁה מְקוֹמוֹת הַכֹּהֲנִים שׁוֹמְרִים בְּבֵית הַמִּקְדָּשׁ
The scene: Tractate Tamid opens its Gemara here on 25b, since the Bavli's discussion of this tractate exists only from this point through 33b. The mishna describes the nightly guard duty priests performed in the Temple courtyard, much like guards stationed in a royal palace — a mark of honor for the sacred space. Three chambers served this purpose: Beit Avtinas (where the incense was prepared), Beit HaNitzotz (with its small perpetual fire), and Beit HaMoked (the Hearth, where priests warmed themselves). The mishna details who slept where, how the young priests handled their sacred vestments overnight, and what protocol governed a priest who became ritually impure during the night.

🛏️ Inside the Chamber of the Hearth

Architecture built for both warmth and rest
1️⃣ The chamber's ceiling was rounded like a cupola (כִּיפָּה) — no upper floor, unlike Avtinas and HaNitzotz
↓
2️⃣ It was a large hall, ringed by rows of stone (רוֹבְדִין) protruding from the walls to serve as benches
↓
3️⃣ The elders of that day's serving priestly patrilineal family slept there overnight
↓
4️⃣ They kept the keys to the Temple courtyard in their own possession while they slept
A position of trust: Holding the courtyard keys overnight marked these elders as responsible for the Temple's overall security, distinct from the younger priests' separate sleeping arrangements.

🌙 Who Slept Where, and How

Different arrangements for different ranks of priests

Elders of the Serving Family

  • Slept in the Chamber of the Hearth
  • Used the stone bench-rows
  • Held the Temple courtyard's keys overnight

Young Priests Eligible to Serve

  • Also slept in the Chamber of the Hearth
  • Each on the ground with his own garment, NOT on the stone benches
  • Removed sacred vestments before sleeping

Young Trainee Priests (not yet eligible)

  • Kept watch in the UPPER stories of Beit Avtinas and Beit HaNitzotz
  • Separate posts from the main sleeping hall

👕 Never Sleeping in the Sacred Vestments

How the eligible young priests handled their garments overnight
פִּרְחֵי כְהוּנָּה אִישׁ כִּסְתּוֹ בָּאָרֶץ. לֹא הָיוּ יְשֵׁנִים בְּבִגְדֵי קֹדֶשׁ
The protocol: The young priests eligible to serve did not sleep on the stone benches, and — critically — they never slept while wearing the sacred priestly vestments. Instead, each removed his sacred garments, folded them, and placed them beneath his own head as a pillow. He then covered himself for warmth with his own personal, non-sacred clothing. This distinction between the sacred vestments (treated with careful respect, never worn casually or during sleep) and personal garments (used freely for practical needs like warmth) reflects the broader halakhic principle that sacred items are reserved strictly for their designated purpose.
