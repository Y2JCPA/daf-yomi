QUESTION 1 OF 5
How did the priest suspend the daily lamb in order to flay it, according to the mishna?
A. By tying a rope around its neck
B. By puncturing the hind legs at the knee and hanging the animal from two hooks
C. By breaking its leg and hanging it by the bone
D. By using a special sling
Correct: B — The mishna specifies that the priest would not break the leg in the ordinary manner but would puncture it at the knee joint and suspend the animal from hooks placed through those holes.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to the baraita, what does the binding of the lamb's foreleg to its hind leg evoke?
A. The Exodus from Egypt
B. The binding of Isaac, son of Abraham (Akeidat Yitzchak)
C. The revelation at Mount Sinai
D. The dedication of the Tabernacle
Correct: B — The baraita explicitly describes this binding method as being 'like the binding of Isaac, son of Abraham,' invoking that foundational moment of sacrifice.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
What are the two explanations offered by Rav Huna and Rav Ḥisda for why the lamb's legs were not all tied together?
A. Cost and practicality
B. One says it would be a degradation of consecrated items; the other says it would resemble a pagan ritual practice, violating 'you shall not walk in their statutes'
C. Both agree it was simply for convenience
D. Neither offers any halakhic reason
Correct: B — Rav Huna attributes the practice to avoiding degradation of sacred items, while Rav Ḥisda attributes it to avoiding imitation of gentile ritual customs.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
Why were eight of the Temple's thirteen tables made of marble rather than gold or silver, despite the principle that 'there is no poverty in a place of wealth'?
A. Marble was simply cheaper
B. Metal tables would become scalding hot in the sun and could damage the sacrificial limbs, while marble stayed cool
C. Gold and silver were reserved only for the Holy of Holies
D. There is no explanation given in the text
Correct: B — The Gemara explains that metal heats up excessively in the sun, which could harm the meat, whereas marble remains cool — a practical reason overriding the general preference for lavish materials.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to the Gemara's derivation from 'two by day' (Numbers 28:3), why were the morning and afternoon daily offerings slaughtered at different corners of the altar?
A. To commemorate the two tablets of the Ten Commandments
B. Each was slaughtered directly opposite the sunlight — the morning offering at the northwest corner facing the rising sun, the afternoon offering at the northeast corner facing the setting sun
C. It was purely a matter of convenience for the priests
D. There is no halakhic reason; it was random
Correct: B — The Gemara derives from the extra phrase 'two by day' that each offering must be slaughtered in direct sunlight, which — given the sun's path — requires different altar corners for morning versus afternoon.
