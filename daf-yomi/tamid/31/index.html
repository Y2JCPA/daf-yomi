📖 Overview: Anatomy, Symbolism, and Sunlight

Tamid 31a–31b
יָד וָרֶגֶל, כַּעֲקֵידַת יִצְחָק בֶּן אַבְרָהָם
The scene: The mishna walks through the daily lamb's flaying and dismemberment in exacting anatomical detail — which limb comes off in which order, which priest carries which piece, and even how each priest holds his portion as the line of nine forms. The Gemara then explains why the lamb's legs were bound only in pairs (foreleg to hind leg) rather than all four together, comparing it to the binding of Isaac, and debates whether the reason is dignity or avoiding pagan custom. A description of the Temple's thirteen specialized tables follows, along with the practical reason marble was preferred over precious metal for handling meat. The daf closes by explaining why the morning and afternoon daily offerings were slaughtered at different corners of the altar — always 'opposite the sun.'

🔪 Flaying and Dismembering the Lamb

A precise sequence, hook by hook, cut by cut
1️⃣ The priest punctures the hind legs at the knee joints (never breaking a bone) and hangs the animal from two hooks to begin flaying
↓
2️⃣ He flays downward to the breast, then severs the head and the lower legs, handing each to its designated carrier
↓
3️⃣ He completes the flaying, cuts open the heart to drain its blood, then removes the forelegs and the right hind leg (with both testicles)
↓
4️⃣ With the animal now hanging only from its left hind leg, he tears open the midsection, exposing all the innards
↓
5️⃣ He carefully separates the lung from the liver and the liver's lobe — WITHOUT moving any organ from its natural position — then removes breast, flanks (leaving the spine untouched), the cud (with windpipe/heart/lung attached), and finally the haunch and remaining leg
Result: All nine priests assigned to carry limbs now stand in a line, each holding his designated piece, ready to ascend to the altar.

🐏 Why Not Tie All Four Legs Together?

A dispute over dignity vs. avoiding pagan custom
Baraita
The foreleg and hind leg are bound together — 'like the binding of Isaac, son of Abraham' — evoking that foundational moment of sacrifice.
Rav Huna
Tying all four legs together is avoided because it would constitute a DEGRADATION of consecrated items — an undignified way to handle a sacred offering.
Rav Ḥisda
Rather, it's avoided because that specific method of binding was associated with pagan ritual practice — following it would violate 'you shall not walk in their statutes' (Leviticus 18:3).
Practical difference
If one tied the animal with SILK or a thread of GOLD — not degrading, but still resembling pagan custom — the two opinions would disagree on whether it's permitted.

🍽️ Thirteen Tables, Each With Its Purpose

Marble, silver, and gold — chosen deliberately, not by default
TablesMaterialLocation & Use
8 tablesMarbleIn the slaughterhouse, north of the altar — for rinsing the innards
2 tablesMarble & SilverWest of the ramp — marble held the limbs before ascent; silver held the 93 daily service vessels
2 tablesSilver & GoldInside the Entrance Hall — silver received the incoming shewbread, gold received it on its way OUT (since sanctity only rises, never descends)
1 tableGoldInside the Sanctuary itself — the permanent Table for the shewbread
Why marble at all, given Temple wealth? Rav Ḥinnana (citing Rabbi Asi, citing Rabbi Shmuel bar Rav Yitzḥak): metal tables would get scalding hot in the sun and could damage the sacrificial limbs — marble stays cool, protecting the meat.

☀️ 'Two by Day' — Slaughtered Opposite the Sun

Deriving why morning and afternoon offerings use different altar corners
שְׁנַיִם לַיּוֹם – כְּנֶגֶד הַיּוֹם
The derivation: Numbers 28:3 says the daily offerings are 'two by day' — since the daily-obligation aspect is already covered by the next verse ('one lamb in the morning, the other in the afternoon'), this extra phrase must teach something more: that each lamb is slaughtered directly opposite the sunlight. Since the sun tracks from east to west across the southern sky (Israel's latitude), the MORNING offering is slaughtered at the altar's NORTHWEST corner (facing the rising eastern sun), and the AFTERNOON offering at the NORTHEAST corner (facing the setting western sun) — always positioned in full daylight, never in the altar's shadow.
