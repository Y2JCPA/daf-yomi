📖 Overview: Completing the Night, Beginning the Day

Tamid 26a–26b
מִי שֶׁטָּבַל – יָבֹא וְיָפִיס
The scene: The daf completes the account of the ritually impure priest's nighttime journey — through a lamplit underground passage to a chamber with an immersion pool, a warming fire, and a bathroom designed for privacy — before he rejoins his fellow priests until dawn. A new mishna then opens the day's service: whoever wishes to remove the ashes from the altar must immerse before the appointed officer arrives, at an unpredictable hour tied to the rooster's crow, after which a lottery determines who wins the privilege. The Gemara spends the rest of the daf grounding the earlier mishna's details in verses — deriving the three separate priestly guard posts from Numbers 3:38, resolving whether Beit Avtinas and Beit HaNitzotz were true upper stories, and explaining how Beit HaMoked's stone benches were built without violating the prohibition on iron tools in the Temple.

📜 Deriving Three Guard Posts From One Verse

Numbers 3:38, read word by word
1️⃣ Abaye's source: 'those camping before the Tabernacle...were Moses and Aaron and his sons, keeping the watch of the Sanctuary' (Num. 3:38)
↓
2️⃣ Objection: the verse groups Moses (a Levite) WITH Aaron and his sons (priests) — but the mishna has priests and Levites watching in DIFFERENT places (3 vs. 21)!
↓
3️⃣ Resolution: read it as two separate clauses — 'those camping...were Moses' (the Levites' watch), THEN 'Aaron and his sons, keeping the watch' (the priests', separately)
↓
4️⃣ Since Moses keeps watch in ONE discrete place, and the verse juxtaposes Aaron/sons the same way, each of THEM also keeps watch in a discrete place — Aaron in one, his two sons together accounting for the total of three priestly posts
↓
5️⃣ Rav Ashi's alternative: the verse repeats the root ש-מ-ר three times ('שֹׁמְרִים מִשְׁמֶרֶת...לְמִשְׁמֶרֶת') — hinting directly at three separate watches

🏢 Were Avtinas and HaNitzotz Real Upper Stories?

A dilemma, resolved by a parallel mishna and a verse
The dilemma
Were the chambers of Avtinas and HaNitzotz genuinely upper stories (with a separate floor below), or were they simply very tall halls — perhaps built on pillars — that merely resembled upper stories?
Resolution (Middot)
A parallel mishna about the Temple gates describes the Gate of the Spark as a portico with a true upper story built ABOVE it — priests watched from above, Levites from below. This confirms: real upper stories.
Why do Levites watch from BELOW?
A baraita derives this from Numbers 18:2 and 18:4: the Levites' role is to 'accompany' the priests in the priests' OWN watch-service — meaning they join as a secondary presence, not as equals stationed at the same height.
The structural result
Priests keep watch from the upper story; Levites keep watch from the level below — physically expressing their distinct, subordinate role in this particular shared duty.

🪨 Building Stone Benches Without Hammer or Ax

Reconciling Beit HaMoked's construction with the Temple's famous silence
וְהַבַּיִת בְּהִבָּנוֹתוֹ אֶבֶן שְׁלֵמָה ... וְלֹא נִשְׁמַע בַּבַּיִת בְּהִבָּנֹתוֹ
The question and answer: The mishna describes Beit HaMoked's protruding stone rows (רוֹבְדִין) — steps of chiseled stone by which priests climbed up to a higher row where they slept. But 1 Kings 6:7 states the Temple was built of finished stone, with 'no hammer, ax, or iron tool heard' during construction — so how were these stones shaped? Abaye answers: the prohibition applied only to cutting stone WITHIN the Temple precincts. Builders instead brought pre-shaped stones — some small, some large ('stones of ten cubits and stones of eight cubits,' 1 Kings 7:10) — already finished at the quarry, using larger stones for the lower tier and smaller ones above. Separately, the Gemara explains the elders slept on these stone benches rather than bringing in beds, since bringing beds into the Temple was simply not proper conduct.
