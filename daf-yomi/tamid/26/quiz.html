QUESTION 1 OF 5
How could a ritually impure priest travel from the Chamber of the Hearth to the immersion chamber without passing through the Temple courtyard?
A. He was carried by other priests
B. Through a circuitous underground passage beneath the Temple, lit by lamps on both sides
C. He waited outside the Temple entirely
D. There was no accommodation; he simply left the Temple grounds
Correct: B — The mishna describes a lamplit underground passage specifically built so an impure priest could reach the immersion chamber without entering the sacred courtyard.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to the new mishna, when does the appointed officer arrive to begin the ash-removal lottery?
A. Always precisely at midnight
B. At an unpredictable time — sometimes at the rooster's first crow, sometimes shortly before or after
C. Only on Shabbat
D. Exactly at sunrise every day
Correct: B — The mishna explicitly states that 'not all times are equal' — the officer's arrival time varies, tied loosely to the rooster's crow.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
How does the Gemara derive from Numbers 3:38 that priests kept watch in three separate locations?
A. It cites a separate, explicit verse naming three locations
B. By reading the verse as describing Moses (Levites) separately from 'Aaron and his sons' (priests), with Aaron keeping watch in one place and his two sons together accounting for two more — three total; Rav Ashi alternatively notes the threefold repetition of the root for 'watch'
C. By counting the number of Temple gates
D. The three locations are not derived from any verse at all
Correct: B — The Gemara parses the verse's structure and repeated terminology to derive that Aaron and his two sons occupied three distinct guard posts, separate from the Levites' watch.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
How does the Gemara resolve the dilemma about whether Beit Avtinas and Beit HaNitzotz were genuine upper stories?
A. It leaves the question completely unresolved (teiku)
B. By citing a parallel mishna (Middot) describing the Gate of the Spark with a true upper story where priests watched from above and Levites from below
C. By saying they were definitely not upper stories
D. By comparing them to the Holy of Holies
Correct: B — The Gemara resolves the dilemma by citing the mishna in Middot, which explicitly describes a genuine upper story at the Gate of the Spark, with priests above and Levites below.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
How does Abaye reconcile Beit HaMoked's chiseled stone benches with the verse stating the Temple was built without any iron tool being heard during construction?
A. He says the verse doesn't apply to Beit HaMoked
B. He explains that the prohibition applied only to cutting stone WITHIN the Temple precincts; the stones were pre-shaped elsewhere at the quarry before being brought in
C. He says the benches were actually made of wood, not stone
D. He says the verse is not to be taken literally
Correct: B — Abaye clarifies that stones were shaped outside the Temple grounds at the quarry, so no iron tool was ever used or heard within the sacred precincts itself, even though the finished stones were chiseled.
