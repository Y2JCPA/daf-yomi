📖 Overview: The Morning Service Reaches Its Climax

Tamid 33a–33b — mishnayot 5:5 through 7:4
וְכׇל אָדָם לֹא יִהְיֶה בְּאֹהֶל מוֹעֵד בְּבֹאוֹ לְכַפֵּר בַּקֹּדֶשׁ עַד צֵאתוֹ
The scene: Tamid has walked through the morning service in order, and this daf carries it to its end. Coals are moved from the outer altar to the golden pan, the shovel is hurled to signal the whole city, the lamps are tended, and the incense is offered in a Sanctuary that everyone else must leave. Then the priests descend the twelve steps and bless the people — in the Temple's own form, with one blessing, hands raised overhead, and the explicit Name. The tractate closes with the High Priest at the altar, the trumpets, ben Arza's cymbals, and the list of the psalm sung on each day of the week.

🔥 Moving the Coals — Silver to Gold

Mishna 5:5, and what was done on Shabbat
1️⃣ The priest takes the silver coal pan up the outer altar and clears the extinguished coals aside
↓
2️⃣ He shovels four kav of consumed coals from the depths of the flame
↓
3️⃣ He descends and empties them into the gold coal pan, which holds only three kav — so about one kav spills onto the courtyard floor
↓
4️⃣ On a weekday the spilled coals are swept into the canal; on Shabbat, when extinguishing is forbidden, a pesakhter is overturned upon them
↓
5️⃣ The pesakhter — a half-kor vessel with two chains, one to pull it down the ramp and one to steady it — served three purposes: covering spilled coals, covering a creeping animal's carcass found on Shabbat, and carrying ashes down from the altar
Why gold is smaller: The deliberate mismatch of vessels means the transfer always spills — the mishna records the practice, and the Shabbat workaround it required, rather than treating the spill as a mishap.

🔔 The Shovel Thrown Between the Hall and the Altar

Mishna 5:6 — one sound, three separate messages
🥄 One of the two priests hurls the shovel down between the Entrance Hall and the outer altar
↓
📣 No one in Jerusalem could hear another person speak over the noise
↓
A priest hears it
His brethren are entering to prostrate in the Sanctuary — he runs to join them
A Levite hears it
His brethren are taking their places on the platform to sing — he runs to sing with them
The watch head hears it
He stations the ritually impure priests at the eastern gate
Function: A single unmistakable noise coordinates three different groups at once, without a word spoken — the Temple's public clock.

The warning and the clearing: Experienced priests would caution him — 'Be careful, do not begin scattering in front of you and get burned.' And from 'there shall be no man in the Tent of Meeting… until he comes out' (Lev. 16:17), the area between the Hall and the altar was emptied. To a High Priest the appointed one said deferentially: My master, the High Priest, burn the incense.

🙌 The Priestly Benediction: Temple vs. Everywhere Else

Mishna 7:2 — the priests stand on the twelve stairs with five vessels

In the Temple

  • Recited as one blessing
  • The people answer: 'Blessed are You Lord, God of Israel, from everlasting to everlasting'
  • Hands raised above the head
  • The explicit Name is used

Elsewhere in the country

  • Recited as three blessings
  • The listeners answer amen after each
  • Hands raised only opposite the shoulders

The High Priest's exception

  • He does not raise his hands above the frontplate, which bears God's name
  • Rabbi Yehuda: even he raises them above it, as Scripture says of Aaron's blessing that he lifted his hands
The five vessels: Basket of inner-altar ashes, jug of Candelabrum ashes, the coal pan, the incense bowl, and the spoon — set on the ground before the blessing was recited.

🎺 The Libation, the Trumpets and the Psalm

Mishnayot 7:3–7:4 — the close of the tractate
1️⃣ The High Priest ascends the ramp with the Deputy at his right, who takes his hand at the halfway point; three priests support him when he enters the Sanctuary, and the bells on his robe signal his exit
↓
2️⃣ The nine priests hand him the limbs in turn — head and hind leg, then the two forelegs, and so on — each slipping away after his turn
↓
3️⃣ He circles the altar from the southeast to the northeast to the northwest, reaching the southwestern corner, where the wine is given him
↓
4️⃣ Two priests at the table of the fats sound tekia–terua–tekia and take their places beside ben Arza at the cymbals
↓
5️⃣ He stoops to pour; the Deputy waves the cloths; the cymbals crash; the Levites sing the day's psalm in three sections, a tekia at each break and the people prostrating themselves — Sunday Psalm 24, Monday Psalm 48, Tuesday Psalm 82
Signalling: Cloths, trumpets and cymbals are all cues, not ornament: the libation, the song and the people's prostration have to be synchronised across a crowded courtyard.
