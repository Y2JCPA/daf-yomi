QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to the new mishna, what areas of a consecrated field are excluded from its measurement for valuation purposes?
A. Any rocky area regardless of size
B. Crevices or boulders that are at least ten handbreadths deep or high, since they are unfit for sowing
C. Only areas containing water year-round
D. The entire field if any part is unusable
Correct: B — The mishna excludes crevices ten handbreadths deep or boulders ten handbreadths high from the measured area, since land at that scale of unevenness is not fit for sowing; smaller irregularities are still counted as part of the field.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
Why does the Gemara conclude that the phrase 'to another man' (Lev. 27:20) excludes a son from triggering the field's transfer to the priests, but not a brother?
A. Because the Torah never mentions brothers
B. Because a son stands in his father's place for two other halakhot (designating a Hebrew maidservant, inheriting a Hebrew slave), while a brother's only parallel role — levirate marriage — applies only when there is no son
C. Because brothers cannot legally redeem land
D. Because sons always inherit more than brothers
Correct: B — The word 'man' already includes a brother, so 'another' must exclude someone more specific — the son, who is shown elsewhere to stand more fully in his father's place than a brother does (since levirate marriage only occurs when there is no son).
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to the new mishna, what happens to an ancestral field at the Jubilee if the owner's SON redeemed it (rather than the owner himself)?
A. It goes to the priests
B. It never returns to anyone, remaining with the Temple treasury
C. It returns to the father, the original owner
D. It is divided between the son and the priests
Correct: C — The mishna specifies that when the son redeems the field, it returns to his father (the original owner) at the Jubilee — unlike redemption by another person or relative, which sends it to the priests.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
What is Rami bar Ḥama's dilemma to Rav Ḥisda, and how is it resolved?
A. Whether a field can be consecrated at all near the Jubilee; resolved that it cannot
B. Whether an unredeemed field consecrated less than two years before the Jubilee (redeemable only at full price, no discount) still transfers to the priests; resolved that it does
C. Whether priests can redeem their own fields; resolved that they cannot
D. Whether the sela-and-pundeyon payment applies to purchased fields; resolved that it does not
Correct: B — Rami bar Ḥama asks whether a field consecrated too close to the Jubilee to qualify for discounted redemption still goes to the priests if unredeemed. Rav Ḥisda concludes it does, since the relevant verse's condition is simply whether the field was fit for redemption at all, which it was.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
In the three-way dispute over a field that was never redeemed by the time the Jubilee arrives, what does Rabbi Eliezer rule?
A. The priests take it for free, exactly like Rabbi Shimon
B. The priests take it but must pay the Temple treasury, exactly like Rabbi Yehuda
C. The priests do not enter the field at all — it remains with the Temple treasury as a 'forsaken field' until someone eventually redeems it
D. The field returns automatically to the original owner
Correct: C — Rabbi Eliezer holds that the priests never take possession of an unredeemed field on their own; it stays with the Temple treasury, termed a 'forsaken field,' potentially through multiple Jubilee cycles, until someone actually redeems it.
