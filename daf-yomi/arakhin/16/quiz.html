5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to Rabba bar Rav Huna, why does information said in the presence of three people lose its status as forbidden malicious speech when repeated?
A. Because three witnesses make any statement legally true
B. Because it can be assumed the information will spread regardless, as 'your friend has a friend, and your friend's friend has a friend'
C. Because Jewish law requires three witnesses for any testimony
D. Because the original speaker becomes liable instead
Correct: B — The reasoning is practical: once said before three people, the information is effectively already public and will spread on its own.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to Rabbi Shmuel bar Naḥmani, which biblical figure's household was cursed with leprosy specifically for the sin of bloodshed?
A. Pharaoh's household, over Sarai
B. Joab's house, for the murder of Avner
C. King Uzziah, for his arrogance
D. Gehazi, for a vain oath
Correct: B — The verse in II Samuel 3:29 curses Joab's house with affliction, including leprosy, as punishment for his murder of Avner.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
How does the Gemara reconcile Rabbi Anani bar Sason's claim that the High Priest's robe atones for malicious speech with Rabbi Shmuel bar Naḥmani's claim that malicious speech causes leprosy?
A. It rejects one of the two teachings entirely
B. If the malicious words caused actual harm, leprosy still results; if they caused no real damage, the robe atones instead
C. The robe only atones for men, leprosy only afflicts women
D. Malicious speech is redefined so the two teachings never overlap
Correct: B — The Gemara distinguishes based on outcome: harmful, effective malicious speech brings leprosy, while ineffective, harmless malicious speech is atoned for by the robe.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Rav, Shmuel, and Rabbi Yochanan's dispute, what marks the outer limit of the obligation to keep rebuking someone?
A. Rav: until struck; Shmuel: until cursed; Rabbi Yochanan: until merely reprimanded — all derived from Shaul's escalating reaction to Yonatan's rebuke
B. There is no limit; rebuke must continue indefinitely
C. The obligation ends after exactly three attempts, according to all three
D. The dispute concerns only who may give rebuke, not how far it goes
Correct: A — All three positions are drawn from the same verse describing King Shaul's response to his son Yonatan's rebuke over his treatment of David, each reading a different point in that escalation as the limit.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to the school of Rabbi Yishmael, what is notable about someone who passes forty days without experiencing any suffering at all?
A. He is considered especially righteous and will be rewarded further
B. He has, in effect, already received his reward in this world, with nothing further owed in the World-to-Come
C. He is suspected of having sinned secretly
D. The teaching applies only to Torah scholars
Correct: B — The baraita teaches that a life entirely free of even minor suffering for forty days is itself remarkable, suggesting the person has already collected his due reward in this world.
