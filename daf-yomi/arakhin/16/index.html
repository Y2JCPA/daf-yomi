📖 Overview: Speech, Atonement, and Rebuke

Arakhin 16a–16b
עַל שִׁבְעָה דְּבָרִים נְגָעִים בָּאִין
The scene: Continuing yesterday's discussion of malicious speech, the daf opens with practical guidance on when repeating information does not count as lashon hara, and warns that even public praise can curdle into harm. Rabbi Shmuel bar Naḥmani then lists seven sins that bring leprous marks — malicious speech among them — each proven from a biblical episode, from Joab's house to King Uzziah. Rabbi Anani bar Sason's teaching that the High Priest's garments atone for these same sins seems to contradict the leprosy list, and the Gemara works out how both can be true, alongside a further view that bloodshed and malicious speech alone are atoned outside the normal sacrificial system entirely. The daf then turns to the mitzva of rebuke — how insistently one must correct another, and where that obligation ends — before closing with rulings on how long a guest should tolerate poor treatment and just how minor a hardship still counts as genuine suffering.

👔 The Priestly Garments Atone — A Contradiction?

Reconciling atonement-by-vestment with punishment-by-leprosy
R' Anani b. Sason
Each priestly garment atones for a specific sin: the tunic for bloodshed, the trousers for forbidden relations, the mitre for arrogance, the belt for wrongful thoughts, the breastplate for corrupt judgment, the ephod for idolatry — and the ROBE, with its bells, for malicious speech.
Objection
But doesn't that contradict Rabbi Shmuel bar Naḥmani's claim that lashon hara causes LEPROSY, not atonement by a garment?
Resolution
No contradiction: if the malicious words actually caused harm or discord, leprosy still comes. If they caused no real damage, the robe atones instead.
Further objection
Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi also taught that bloodshed and malicious speech have NO atonement through ordinary offerings at all — bloodshed through the broken-necked heifer, lashon hara through the INCENSE (Numbers 17:12)!
Resolution
Also reconcilable: the heifer atones when the murderer is unknown, the tunic when he is known but cannot be executed; the incense atones for PRIVATE malicious speech, the robe for PUBLIC malicious speech.

⚖️ How Far Must Rebuke (Tochacha) Go?

The obligation to correct another — and its outer limit
1️⃣ Seeing a fault in another obligates rebuking him — and rebuking again if the first attempt fails — but never to the point of humiliating him
↓
2️⃣ Rabbi Tarfon: 'I doubt anyone in this generation can even RECEIVE rebuke' — tell someone to remove a splinter, and he answers, 'remove the beam from your own eyes'
↓
3️⃣ Three views on the outer limit of the obligation itself: Rav — until he might be struck; Shmuel — until he might be cursed; Rabbi Yochanan — until mere reprimand, all drawn from Shaul's escalating anger at Yonatan
↓
4️⃣ Rabbi Yochanan ben Nuri: complaining about Akiva to their teacher, again and again, only deepened Akiva's love for him — proving 'rebuke a wise man and he will love you'

🏠 A Guest's Patience, and the Measure of 'Suffering'

How much should one tolerate before reacting?

How Long Should a Guest Stay Despite Poor Treatment?

  • Rav: until the host actually strikes him
  • Shmuel: until the host packs his belongings and puts them on his back

What Counts as Real 'Suffering'?

  • Rabbi Elazar: a robe woven for you that simply doesn't fit right
  • Even smaller: wine mixed with the wrong-temperature water, or reaching for three coins and getting only two
Why it matters: The school of Rabbi Yishmael: anyone who passes forty days without any suffering at all has, in effect, already received his reward in this world — a life entirely free of minor troubles is itself remarkable.
