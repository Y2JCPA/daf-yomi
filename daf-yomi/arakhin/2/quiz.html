Arakhin 2 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to the opening mishna, why can a tumtum and a hermaphrodite (androginos) vow to donate another's valuation but not themselves be valuated?
A. They lack the mental competence required to vow
B. Only a definite male or a definite female can be valuated
C. They are entirely exempt from Temple-related obligations
D. Their market value as slaves cannot be assessed
Correct: B — The mishna states they are not valuated 'since only a definite male or a definite female are valuated' — their sex is not definite.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
Why are a deaf-mute, an imbecile, and a minor the object of a vow of valuation but cannot themselves vow to donate someone's valuation?
A. They lack the presumed mental competence (da'at) to make a commitment
B. They have no monetary value on the open market
C. The Torah exempts them from all Temple gifts
D. They are considered legally dead for these purposes
Correct: A — The mishna gives the reason explicitly: 'because they lack the presumed mental competence,' so they cannot vow, though others can vow about them.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
The Gemara includes a repulsive person or one afflicted with boils among those who are valuated, despite their having no market value. What word in the verse teaches this?
A. 'According to your valuation' (בְּעֶרְכְּךָ)
B. 'Persons' (נְפָשֹׁת) — anyone with any amount of life
C. 'A vow' (נֶדֶר)
D. 'To the Lord' (לַה')
Correct: B — The Gemara explains that 'nefashot' teaches that anyone possessing any amount of life is subject to valuation, even without market value.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
What is Rabbi Yehuda's reasoning for ruling that an heir cannot place hands on, or effect substitution for, an offering he inherited?
A. He derives it from the doubled form 'hamer yamir'
B. He expounds 'his offering' (קׇרְבָּנוֹ) as excluding the father's offering, and derives the earlier stage (substitution) from the later stage (placing hands)
C. He holds that heirs are considered ritually impure
D. The Rabbis agree with this position
Correct: B — Rabbi Yehuda reads 'korbano' as 'his own offering, not his father's,' and reasons from the final stage of consecration (placing hands) to the initial stage (substitution) — an heir does neither.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
How was the situation of one who is half-slave and half-freeman ultimately resolved, according to Beit Hillel's retracted position?
A. He remained a half-slave permanently
B. The court forces the master to free him fully, and he writes a bill of debt for half his value
C. He was sold to a non-Jew
D. He was declared entirely a slave
Correct: C — Beit Hillel retracted to agree with Beit Shammai: for the betterment of the world, the master is forced to free him, and the freed man commits by document to repay half his value.
