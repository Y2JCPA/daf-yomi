5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to Rabbi Meir, if one says 'the leg of this animal is a burnt offering,' what becomes sacred?
A. The entire animal
B. Only the leg — the animal is sold for burnt-offering use, with proceeds non-sacred except for that limb's value
C. Nothing at all, since the declaration is invalid
D. Only if the leg happens to be a vital organ
Correct: B — Rabbi Meir derives from 'of it that one gives...it shall be sacred' that only the specified part becomes sacred, not the whole animal — unless the named part is one the animal's life depends on.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
What is the outcome of the three dilemmas about consecrating a head's value, one's valuation, or a field specifically 'for the altar'?
A. All three are resolved in favor of full inclusion of the whole item
B. All three are resolved in favor of exclusion
C. All three are left unresolved as teiku
D. The Gemara rejects all three questions as illegitimate
Correct: C — Rabba's, Rava's, and Rav Ashi's dilemmas each end with the Gemara's formula 'teiku' — the question stands unresolved.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
Why does Rabbi Meir rule that one who vows the valuation ('erech') of a child less than a month old must pay the child's market value?
A. Because Jewish courts impose a monetary penalty for meaningless vows
B. Because 'a person does not make his words for naught' — knowing there is no erech for such a child, he must have intended its assessment (damim)
C. Because the Torah explicitly fixes a market-value sum for infants
D. Because such a vow is treated as a false oath requiring atonement
Correct: B — Rabbi Meir's principle is that a person's statement is never meaningless; since he knew erech doesn't apply, his true intent must have been damim.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
Why did Rav need to teach separately that one who vows the 'erech' of a utensil, and one who vows the 'erech' of a child under a month, both owe damim, rather than deriving one ruling from the other?
A. Because a utensil and a child are governed by completely unrelated Torah verses
B. Because with a utensil there's no room for genuine error at all, while with the child one could mistakenly believe erech applies (as it does for a one-month-old), so each situation needed its own ruling
C. Because Rabbi Meir disagreed with himself between the two cases
D. Because only the child case involves the Temple treasury
Correct: B — The Gemara explains that the utensil case has no possibility of error, while the child's case does (since erech applies to a slightly older child) — so the identical conclusion had to be taught twice, for two different reasons.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to the mishna, what do Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yehuda agree about regarding a gentile, despite their opposite rulings on erech?
A. A gentile is excluded from every Temple-related law
B. A gentile can vow to donate another's assessment (damim) and can himself be the object of such an assessment vow
C. A gentile can never contribute anything to the Temple treasury
D. A gentile is treated identically to a minor in every respect
Correct: B — The mishna states that both tannaim agree gentiles vow to donate another's assessment and are the object of such vows — their dispute is limited to erech (valuation).
