📖 Overview: Ending Chapter One, Opening Chapter Two

Arakhin 7a–7b
אֵין נֶעֱרָכִין פָּחוֹת מִסֶּלַע, וְלֹא יָתֵר עַל חֲמִשִּׁים סֶלַע
The scene: The daf finishes the Gemara's discussion of one led to execution, resolving how an injured party's damages claim can reach his heirs — only where a court had already rendered judgment before his death, so the Torah's debt is then treated like a written loan. A mishna then rules on a pregnant woman condemned to death, executed at once unless she has already gone into labor, and the Gemara adds Shmuel's directive to strike her womb so the fetus dies first, alongside the law permitting one to cut open the abdomen of a woman who died in labor on Shabbat to try to save her child. The mishna's further ruling — that one may benefit from an executed woman's hair but not a slain animal's — is explained by the different moment each is rendered forbidden. The daf then closes the first chapter (הדרן עלך הכל מעריכין) and opens the second with a new mishna: a vow of valuation is never less than a sela nor more than fifty, with a dispute over what a poor man with limited funds actually owes.

⚖️ Collecting Damages From a Condemned Man's Heirs

Continuing yesterday's dispute between the tanna kamma and Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar
❓ If one led to execution injured another, can the victim collect from his heirs after the execution?
↓
Rav Yosef
The dispute is whether an ORAL loan is collectible from heirs at all
Rabba
Rather, everyone agrees an oral loan is NOT collectible — the dispute is whether a Torah-imposed debt (like damages) counts as a WRITTEN loan
↓
📜 Objection: a baraita rules that if a pit-digger dies from an ox's fall, his heirs must pay the ox's owner — proof that Torah-debts ARE collected from heirs!
↓
✅ Resolved: that baraita describes a case where judges already sat and found him liable BEFORE he died — once a court renders judgment, the debt becomes like a written loan

💔 Which Dies First — the Mother or the Fetus?

Shmuel's precaution, and a Shabbat exception to try to save a newborn
Shmuel
Strike the pregnant woman opposite her womb before executing her, so the fetus dies first — sparing her the public disgrace of going into labor as she dies.
Gemara
But we hold that in NATURAL death the fetus dies first anyway — proven from the law that a day-old infant can inherit his mother. Why the extra step?
Resolution
That principle applies only to natural death, where the fetus's weak vitality lets it be 'cut' first. In EXECUTION, the mother dies first — hence Shmuel's precaution.
Rav Naḥman/Shmuel
Separately: if a woman in labor dies on Shabbat, cut open her abdomen and remove the fetus — even desecrating Shabbat on the mere chance he is alive, though he never had a prior presumption of life at all.

💇 Hair of an Executed Woman vs. Hair of a Slain Animal

Same mishna, opposite rulings — why?

A Woman's Hair

  • Permitted to derive benefit from it after her execution
  • Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak: her DEATH is what forbids her — hair, unaffected by that moment, stays permitted
  • Alternate view (Rav): only a wig she designated for her daughter is permitted; her natural hair itself is not

An Animal's Hair

  • Forbidden to derive any benefit from it
  • The court's VERDICT itself renders the animal forbidden, even before it is killed
  • So the hair attached at the moment of the verdict is forbidden along with the rest

💰 New Mishna: The Floor and Ceiling of a Valuation Vow

Chapter one closes, chapter two opens
1️⃣ No valuation is less than a sela — from 'your valuation shall be according to the shekel of the Sanctuary' — nor more than fifty sela, the Torah's maximum, fixed for a man aged 20–60
↓
2️⃣ A poor man with only 5 sela vows his own erech, which the Torah fixes far higher: Rabbi Meir says he pays just 1 sela; the Rabbis say he must pay all 5
↓
3️⃣ Rabbi Meir's reasoning: the verse gives only 'fifty' OR 'a shekel,' no in-between; the Rabbis read 'according to the means of the vower' as requiring payment of whatever he can actually afford
↓
4️⃣ Rav Adda bar Ahava's case: a man with 5 sela vows his erech twice; paying the SECOND vow with 4 sela and the FIRST with the remaining 1 fulfills both — 'a later creditor who collects first keeps what he collected'
