QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to the baraita, what happens to a field that a PRIEST himself received as a dedication (ḥerem) and then consecrated, if it remains unredeemed at the Jubilee?
A. The priest keeps the entire field for himself
B. It is divided among all the priests of the currently serving watch, just like an ordinary Israelite's unredeemed ancestral field
C. It automatically reverts to the original donor
D. It becomes property of the Levites instead of the priests
Correct: B — The baraita teaches that even though a priest already holds this field as his own dedicated property, it is still divided among his priestly brethren at the Jubilee — he may not claim it entirely for himself.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
What is the priest's kal vaḥomer (a fortiori) argument for why he should be allowed to keep his own dedicated field, and why does the Gemara initially question whether this comparison is even valid?
A. He argues the field is worthless; the Gemara agrees it's a fair comparison
B. He argues that since he already acquires a SHARE of fields dedicated by others, he should keep his OWN field entirely — but the Gemara notes acquiring one share among many priests is not comparable to claiming an entire field alone
C. He argues that priests never lose any property; the comparison is deemed perfectly valid
D. He cites a completely different verse about tithes
Correct: B — The priest reasons that since he shares in fields dedicated by others (albeit only a fractional share, as one of many priestly brothers), he should certainly keep his own field, which is entirely in his possession — a much larger claim that the Gemara initially questions as not truly parallel.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
Why does the Gemara reject Rami bar Ḥama's suggestion that a priest's dedicated field should be compared to 'his hallowed things' (Numbers 5:10), which a priest may keep for himself?
A. Because that verse doesn't exist
B. Because a priest's sacrificial portions were never actually 'in his possession' to begin with — he receives them as compensation, unlike a field he genuinely holds
C. Because dedicated fields are never actually owned by anyone
D. Because the comparison was accepted, not rejected
Correct: B — The Gemara distinguishes: a priest's portions from offerings he sacrifices are compensation he receives 'from the table of the Most High,' never truly his personal possession beforehand — while a dedicated field genuinely sits in his own hands, making the comparison invalid.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Rav Naḥman's accepted resolution, what is the key textual distinction that explains why a priest's OWN INHERITED ancestral field stays with him forever, but his DEDICATED field does not?
A. There is no distinction; both are treated identically
B. The verse's own wording distinguishes 'his ancestral field' (achuzato), which remains his perpetual possession, from 'his dedicated field' (ḥermo), which is divided among his priestly brethren at the Jubilee
C. The distinction is based purely on the field's monetary value
D. The distinction depends on which tribe originally owned the field
Correct: B — Rav Naḥman explains that the verse's precise language separates a priest's genuinely inherited ancestral field (achuzato), protected as perpetual possession, from a field merely dedicated to him by someone else (ḥermo), which does not receive that same protection.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
What Hebrew phrase marks the conclusion of Masechet Arakhin at the end of this daf, and what does it mean?
A. 'Baruch Hashem' — a simple blessing with no special significance
B. 'Hadran alach' ('we shall return to you') followed by 'u'slika lah Masechet Arakhin' ('and thus concludes Tractate Arakhin')
C. 'Ein od milvado' — an unrelated theological statement
D. There is no closing formula; the daf simply stops mid-discussion
Correct: B — The daf closes with the traditional formula 'Hadran alach ha-mokher sadeihu, u'slika lah Masechet Arakhin' — 'we shall return to you, [the chapter] "One who sold his field," and thus concludes Tractate Arakhin' — marking the formal end of the tractate.
