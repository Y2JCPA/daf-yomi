📖 Overview: Defining a Walled City

Arakhin 32a–32b
כׇּל שֶׁהוּא לִפְנִים מִן הַחוֹמָה — הֲרֵי הוּא כְּבָתֵּי עָרֵי חוֹמָה, חוּץ מִן הַשָּׂדוֹת
The scene: Having established the one-year redemption rule for a walled-city house, the daf now asks exactly what structures and which cities qualify. A new mishna extends the category to olive presses, bathhouses, towers, dovecotes, and other structures within the wall (but not fields), debates a house built directly into the wall itself, and then lists specific cities recognized as walled since the days of Joshua. A sharp contradiction emerges over whether Jerusalem itself is included, and the daf closes with a sweeping question: did the sanctity Joshua's conquest gave the land ever lapse during the Babylonian exile, requiring Ezra's generation to renew it?

🏰 What Structures Count as a 'House'?

New mishna: the category extends well beyond literal houses — almost

Included (All Opinions)

  • Olive presses, bathhouses, towers, dovecotes, pits, ditches, caves
  • Derived from 'that is in the walled city' — anything located within the city

Disputed

  • Fields inside the wall: R' Yehuda excludes them ('house' implies containing items); R' Meir includes them too
  • A quarry or sand-bed (cholsit u-metzula): R' Meir treats it like a house; R' Yehuda treats it like a field

🧱 A House Built INTO the Wall

Rabbi Yehuda and Rabbi Shimon read the same biblical verse two ways
R' Yehuda
A house built directly into the city wall is NOT treated as a walled-city house — it isn't really 'in the city.'
R' Shimon
It IS included — the wall's outer face counts as the house's own wall, making it fully part of the city.
R' Yoḥanan
Both derive their view from the same verse about Rahab in Jericho: 'her house was upon the side of the wall, and she dwelt upon the wall' (Joshua 2:15).
The split
R' Shimon reads it plainly — her house was attached to the wall, inside the city. R' Yehuda reads 'she dwelt upon the wall' as meaning she lived IN the wall itself, not in the city it enclosed.

🤔 Does Jerusalem Actually Qualify?

The mishna lists it — but another baraita seems to say otherwise
עֲשָׂרָה דְּבָרִים נֶאֶמְרוּ בִּירוּשָׁלַיִם, אֵין הַבַּיִת חָלוּט בָּהּ
The contradiction: The mishna names Jerusalem among the cities walled since Joshua's day. But a baraita elsewhere teaches 'ten things were said about Jerusalem,' including that houses there are never sold in perpetuity after a year — directly contradicting the mishna! Two resolutions are offered: Rabbi Yoḥanan says the mishna means a city like Jerusalem (equally walled since Joshua) but not Jerusalem itself, which has its own unique exemption; Rav Ashi suggests instead — as with the two places called Kadesh — that there were simply two different towns named Jerusalem in Judea, and only one carries the special exemption.

⏳ Did the Land's Original Sanctity Lapse?

A sweeping dispute traced through Ezra's return from Babylon
1️⃣ One view (R' Yishmael b. R' Yosei): Joshua's conquest sanctified the land only 'for its time' — exile nullified it, so Ezra's generation had to sanctify walled cities anew, which is why the mishna's list is so short
↓
2️⃣ Proof from Nehemiah 8:17: the returnees made sukkot 'as they had not done since the days of Joshua' — implying centuries of lapsed practice, renewed only under Ezra
↓
3️⃣ The opposing view: the original conquest sanctified the land forever — 'since the days of Joshua' in Nehemiah is a comparison of national merit, not a literal gap in sukka-building
↓
4️⃣ Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak's resolution: after the return, they DID count Jubilee cycles — but only to keep the Sabbatical Year's timing accurate, not because the Jubilee's full halakhot were truly in effect (since some tribes remained in exile)
