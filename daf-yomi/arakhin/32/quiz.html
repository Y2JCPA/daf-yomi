QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to the new mishna, which structures inside a walled city are subject to the same redemption rules as houses, and what is universally excluded?
A. Only literal houses qualify; nothing else is included
B. Olive presses, bathhouses, towers, dovecotes, pits, and caves are included; fields are excluded (though Rabbi Meir disputes this)
C. Only fields qualify, not any buildings
D. Everything inside the wall is excluded except houses
Correct: B — The Gemara derives from 'that is in the walled city' that structures like olive presses, bathhouses, towers, and dovecotes are included, while 'house' itself is used to exclude fields — though Rabbi Meir disagrees and includes fields too.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
What is the basis of the dispute between Rabbi Yehuda and Rabbi Shimon regarding a house built directly into a city's wall?
A. They disagree about the wall's construction materials
B. Both interpret the same verse about Rahab's house in Jericho differently — Rabbi Shimon reads it as inside the city, Rabbi Yehuda as literally part of the wall and thus not 'in the city'
C. They disagree only about the house's monetary value
D. The dispute concerns houses outside city walls entirely
Correct: B — Rabbi Yoḥanan explains both opinions derive from Joshua 2:15's description of Rahab's house 'upon the side of the wall' — Rabbi Shimon reads this as being inside the city, while Rabbi Yehuda reads 'she dwelt upon the wall' as living in the wall structure itself, separate from the city.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
Why does the mishna exclude Tiberias from the category of cities 'walled since the days of Joshua,' even though it has protective boundaries?
A. Because Tiberias was built after Joshua's era entirely
B. Because the verse requires the wall to fully surround ('saviv') the city, and Tiberias's sea served as its wall on one side instead of an actual wall
C. Because Tiberias never had any protective boundary at all
D. Because Tiberias's population was too small
Correct: B — The Gemara derives from 'saviv' (surrounding) that a city needs to be fully encircled by an actual wall; Tiberias, whose sea substituted for a wall on one side, does not meet this requirement.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
What is the contradiction the Gemara raises about Jerusalem's inclusion in the mishna's list of walled cities?
A. The mishna never actually mentions Jerusalem
B. The mishna lists Jerusalem as qualifying, but another baraita states that among 'ten things said about Jerusalem,' houses there are never sold in perpetuity, seemingly excluding it from this very rule
C. Jerusalem is said to have no wall at all
D. The contradiction concerns Jerusalem's Jubilee status, not walled cities
Correct: B — The baraita's list of Jerusalem's ten unique features includes that its houses are never forfeited permanently after a year, directly contradicting the mishna's inclusion of Jerusalem among ordinary walled cities — resolved by positing either a different, comparable city or two distinct towns named Jerusalem.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak, why did the returnees from Babylonian exile count Jubilee cycles even though the tribes of Reuben, Gad, and half of Manasseh remained in exile (which should nullify the Jubilee)?
A. They counted Jubilees to fully reinstate every halakha of the Jubilee Year
B. They counted Jubilee cycles solely to keep the timing of the Sabbatical Year accurate, not because the full halakhot of the Jubilee Year were actually in effect
C. They did not count Jubilee cycles at all after the return
D. They counted Jubilees only for houses in walled cities
Correct: B — Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak resolves the difficulty by explaining that counting to the fiftieth year after the return was done purely to keep Sabbatical Year cycles properly timed, even though the Jubilee Year's own halakhot did not actually apply, since not all tribes had returned to the land.
