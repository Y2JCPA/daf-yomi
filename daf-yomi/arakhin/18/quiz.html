5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to the baraita's principle, when is a witness who became temporarily unfit (for example, went deaf or blind) between seeing an event and testifying about it still considered valid?
A. Never — any interim disqualification permanently invalidates him
B. If he was fit both at the beginning (when he saw the event) and at the end (when he testifies), regardless of any interim period of disqualification
C. Only if he was unfit for less than one day
D. Only if a court explicitly reinstates him
Correct: B — The baraita's stated principle is that fitness at the beginning and end of the process suffices, even with disqualification in between.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
Why is a leper still considered 'poor' even though his dying father is about to leave him a fortune?
A. Because inheritances are never counted toward wealth
B. Because he does not legally inherit the money until his father actually dies, and a majority of moribund people eventually dying does not change his status early
C. Because the leper renounced his inheritance
D. Because Jewish law forbids inheriting while a parent is alive under any circumstance
Correct: B — The Gemara clarifies the case refers to a father who is dying but has not yet died — the son has no legal claim to the money until the death actually occurs.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to the new mishna, whose age and sex determine the sum owed in a valuation vow?
A. The person being valuated (the subject), not the one who takes the vow
B. The one who takes the vow (the vower), not the subject
C. Whichever party is older
D. The Temple treasurer decides case by case
Correct: A — The mishna states that the age-based sum and the male/female distinction both follow the person being valuated, in contrast to affordability, which follows the vower.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to the mishna's ruling about the age brackets (under 5, under 20, under 60) and its accompanying verbal analogy, how is the transition point at each threshold determined?
A. By comparing the word 'year' used for the explicit 'sixty years and upward' bracket to the same word used for the 5- and 20-year brackets, applying the rule both leniently and stringently
B. By simple majority vote among the Sages
C. By counting from the person's birthday on the Jewish calendar only
D. There is no fixed rule; it varies case by case
Correct: A — The Gemara derives via gezeira shava that the same 'incomplete year counts as the lower bracket' rule from age 60 applies equally to ages 5 and 20, in both directions.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to the baraita, how is the Torah's 'one year' period calculated for a sacrificial animal, a walled-city house redemption, and similar cases?
A. According to the fixed calendar year (Rosh Hashanah to Rosh Hashanah)
B. As an exact anniversary from the specific starting event (birth, sale, etc.), not according to the calendar year
C. According to the lunar month only
D. There is no consistent method; each case is unrelated
Correct: B — The baraita establishes that these Torah-stated year periods run 'from time to time' — an exact anniversary from their own starting point — rather than aligning with the calendar year.
