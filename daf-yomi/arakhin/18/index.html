📖 Overview: Fixing Every Boundary Precisely

Arakhin 18a–18b
וְהָעֵרֶךְ בִּזְמַן הָעֵרֶךְ
The scene: The daf closes the discussion of a witness's fitness with the principle that one who was fit both when he saw the event and when he testifies remains valid even if temporarily disqualified in between. It then finishes clarifying why a leper is still considered 'poor' even with a fortune technically coming his way: his dying father hasn't yet died, and his cargo ship is leased to others whose rent isn't yet due. A new mishna states that a valuation's age- and sex-based sum follows the person being valuated rather than the vower, fixed permanently at the moment the vow is spoken — paralleled to how a pearl is assessed by its immediate local market. The next mishna maps out exactly how the Torah's three age brackets transition, learned via a verbal analogy, with Rabbi Eliezer requiring a full extra month past each threshold rather than a single day. The daf closes with a baraita cataloguing several other Torah time periods, each calculated as an exact anniversary rather than the calendar year.

🧑‍⚖️ When Is a Witness Disqualified Mid-Way?

A 'beginning and end' principle, applied to testimony
❓ A witness became temporarily unfit (deaf, blind, incompetent, or too closely related) between seeing an event and testifying about it, but was fit at both the START and the END — is his testimony valid?
↓
✅ Yes: 'anyone whose beginning and end are in a state of fitness is fit,' even if disqualified in the interim
↓
Why different from valuation vows?
Testimony depends only on valid SEEING and valid TELLING (Leviticus 5:1) — both occurred, regardless of the interim
So what does 'and he is a witness' exclude?
Not interim fitness — rather, that the witness must be explicitly SINGLED OUT by name, not merely one of an anonymous crowd addressed collectively

💰 Still Counted as 'Poor' — Two Edge Cases

A leper's offering tracks only what he actually possesses right now
ScenarioRulingReason
His father is moribund (gosess) and will leave him a fortune, but hasn't died yetStill counted as poorHe does not legally own the inheritance until his father actually dies
His ship is at sea loaded with valuable cargo, but the ship itself is leased to othersStill counted as poorThe cargo belongs to the lessee, and rent isn't owed until the lease ends
Underlying view: This follows Rabbi Eliezer's broader position that business assets used for one's livelihood — a plow-team, a rental donkey — aren't seized to satisfy Temple debts.

⚖️ New Mishna: Age and Sex Follow the Person Valuated

Locked in permanently at the moment the vow is spoken

Age

  • A youth who valuates an elder pays the ELDER's rate
  • An elder who valuates a youth pays the YOUTH's rate

Sex

  • A man who valuates a woman pays the WOMAN's rate
  • A woman who valuates a man pays the MAN's rate

Timing

  • The rate LOCKS IN at the moment the vow is spoken — even if the subject's age bracket changes before payment
The Gemara's analogy: Just as a pearl is assessed by its immediate, local market rather than a distant one, a valuation is fixed at that moment — the same principle that values a vital limb by the dignity of the whole person.

📅 Where Exactly Do the Age Brackets Switch Over?

A verbal analogy, and Rabbi Eliezer's stricter reading
Tanna Kamma
Day 30 counts as 'under a month'; likewise, year 5 and year 20 count as 'under' the next bracket — derived by comparing the word 'year' used for age 60 ('and upward') to the same word used for ages 5 and 20.
Objection
But that comparison could cut the WRONG way — using it to make ages 5 and 20 more LENIENT, unlike 60, which is more stringent!
Resolved
The verbal analogy applies both ways, for leniency AND stringency alike, since both mentions of 'year' are otherwise superfluous ('free') in their verses.
Rabbi Eliezer dissents
Not just one day — a FULL EXTRA MONTH is needed past each threshold, derived instead from a comparison to the census of the Levites, 'from a month old and upward.'
