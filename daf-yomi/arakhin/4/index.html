📖 Overview: From 'Priests, Too' to the Verse's Every Word

Arakhin 4a–4b
נֶדֶר בְּעֶרְכְּךָ נְפָשֹׁת לַה׳
The scene: The daf opens by continuing the 'isn't it obvious?' pattern for priests: they must cancel their Temple service to hear the Megilla, and they form a zimun even while eating sacrificial meat meant for atonement, since the Torah still calls it 'eating and being satisfied.' The mishna's own inclusion of priests in arakhin is then debated through ben Bukhri's position that priests are exempt from the half-shekel — could that exempt them from valuations too? From there the Gemara turns to the verse of arakhin itself (Leviticus 27:2, 'a vow of persons... according to your valuation') and mines it for a cluster of derashot: an unspecified vow's minimum payment, valuation of the whole body rather than a limb, inclusion of vital organs and of women as valuators and valuated, exclusion of the dead and the dying, and — spilling into the next daf — whether a tumtum who consecrates only his head thereby consecrates his whole self.

⚖️ Must Priests Give the Half-Shekel?

Ben Bukhri's testimony at Yavne, and why it matters for arakhin
Ben Bukhri
I testify: any priest who contributes the half-shekel is not considered a sinner — implying priests are not obligated to give it at all.
R' Yoḥanan b. Zakkai
Not so! Any priest who does not give the half-shekel is a sinner. Priests only excuse themselves by reading 'every meal offering of the priest shall be wholly consumed' as applying to the omer, the two loaves, and the showbread they would then partly own.
Rava
The mishna's inclusion of priests in arakhin is needed only per ben Bukhri — one might think 'your valuation... shekel of the Sanctuary' links arakhin to the half-shekel obligation.
Abaye
That verse isn't about priests at all — it teaches that no valuation may be assessed below a sela! The real concern is elsewhere: since priests are not redeemed as firstborns, maybe they're excluded from arakhin too.
Rava/R' Ashi
Rather, the concern was from 'he shall be set before the priest' — implying a priest is not set before another priest for valuation. The mishna teaches: priests ARE included nonetheless.
Bottom line: Every possible verse that could have excluded priests from arakhin gets raised and dismissed in turn — the mishna's plain inclusion of 'priests' turns out to answer several distinct doubts at once.

❤️ What Does the Plural 'Persons' (נְפָשׁוֹת) Teach?

One word, read for several different halakhot
❓ The verse reads 'a vow of PERSONS' — why the plural, and what does it add beyond 'valuation'?
↓
A vital organ
One might exclude valuing a limb the soul depends on — 'nefashot' teaches: pay the FULL valuation regardless
The dying (גוֹסֵס)
'He shall be set...and valued' — only one who CAN stand is valuated; the dead and the dying are both excluded
↓
💡 The same plural also teaches: one who valuates a hundred people pays for each of them; a woman may valuate or be valuated exactly as a man; and a repulsive man or one with boils is included despite having no market value

📜 Two Halakhot Hiding in 'According to Your Valuation'

בְּעֶרְכְּךָ is read twice, for two separate rulings

Unspecified Valuation (עֵרֶךְ סָתוּם)

  • One who vows 'a valuation is upon me' without naming anyone pays the SMALLEST fixed sum — three shekels (a girl under five)
  • Not fifty (the largest): 'if you grasped a lot, you grasped nothing; if a little, you grasped it'
  • Dispute: is that three-shekel sum reduced for a poor person, or fixed regardless of means?

Valuation of the Whole Self

  • One gives the value of his ENTIRE body, never of an isolated limb
  • If one vows 'the valuation of my hand is upon me,' he owes nothing — the vow attaches only to a whole person

🐴 Consecrating 'The Head' — Whose Body Is Really Sanctified?

Rava and Abaye debate a tumtum's limb-vow against parallel cases
1️⃣ Rava: a tumtum, though excluded from arakhin, IS included in 'assessment' (דָּמִים) via 'then your valuation shall be' — and if he consecrates one limb, he is 'judged by its dignity': his whole self becomes hekdesh
↓
2️⃣ Abaye objects from a baraita: saying 'this SLAVE's head is hekdesh' makes the treasury a partner in the whole slave — but 'this DONKEY's/COW's head is hekdesh' sanctifies only the head itself
↓
3️⃣ Abaye's proof: a donkey and cow aren't included in arakhin at all, yet their 'head' case still isn't governed by 'judged by its dignity' — so the rule can't track inclusion in arakhin
↓
4️⃣ Rava counters: by that logic, a slave — who IS included in arakhin — should also be 'judged by dignity,' yet only his head becomes hekdesh too! Resolution: it depends on whether the consecration is for the altar (head alone) or for Temple upkeep (the whole)
