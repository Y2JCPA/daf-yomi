QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to the new mishna, how do houses of unwalled courtyards combine the rules of fields and walled-city houses?
A. They follow only the rules of fields, with no borrowing from walled-city houses
B. They may be redeemed immediately like walled-city houses, but they always return to the seller at the Jubilee like fields — never becoming permanently the buyer's
C. They may never be redeemed under any circumstances
D. They follow only the rules of walled-city houses
Correct: B — The mishna gives courtyard houses the walled-city house's immediate redeemability while keeping the ancestral field's Jubilee-return protection — the best (for the seller) of both categories.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to Rav Huna, what happens when someone consecrates a courtyard-house, ANOTHER person redeems it from the Temple treasury, and the Jubilee Year then arrives unredeemed by the original owner?
A. It permanently belongs to the person who redeemed it from hekdesh
B. It goes to the priests, exactly like an unredeemed ancestral field
C. It returns to the original owner at the Jubilee — neither locked to the redeemer nor lost to the priests
D. It remains with the Temple treasury forever
Correct: C — Rav Huna teaches that this specific case is why the verse needed to add 'it shall go out in the Jubilee' — the house returns to its original owner, unlike either a walled-city house (which locks to the buyer) or a field (which passes to the priests).
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to the new mishna, how small can a settlement be and still qualify as 'houses of unwalled courtyards' — even if it happens to have an ancient wall from Joshua's era?
A. It must have at least ten houses
B. As few as two courtyards, each containing two houses
C. Any single house counts, regardless of courtyards
D. It must have no wall of any kind, ever, under any circumstance
Correct: B — The Gemara derives from the plural terms 'houses' and 'courtyards' in Leviticus 25:31 that a minimum of two courtyards of two houses each qualifies as an unwalled-courtyard settlement — even overriding the presence of an actual ancient wall.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Rebbi (Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi) in the new mishna, what two conditions must BOTH be met for a house to be redeemable perpetually under the Levite's special right?
A. The house must be expensive and located in Jerusalem
B. The seller must be a Levite AND the house must be located in a city of the Levites
C. The house must be a courtyard house and consecrated
D. Only that the buyer must be an Israelite
Correct: B — Rebbi derives from the verse's wording that the perpetual redemption right applies only when both conditions hold together — a Levite seller and a Levite city — so an Israelite who merely inherited such a house from a Levite grandfather does not get the perpetual right.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
Why does Rabbi Elazar limit the rule against converting fields into open lots (or vice versa) to Levite cities specifically?
A. Because Israelite cities have no open lots at all
B. Because in Israelite cities such conversions ARE permitted in the direction of city growth (field to lot, lot to city), so the cities are not stunted
C. Because Levite cities never had any fields to begin with
D. Because the rule applies equally everywhere with no distinction
Correct: B — Rabbi Elazar explains that in ordinary Israelite cities, conversions that allow the city to expand (field to lot, lot to city) are permitted specifically so that Israelite settlements are not prevented from growing — only Levite cities keep the strict, symmetric prohibition in both directions.
