Arakhin 28 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to Rabbi Eliezer in the new mishna, what happens if a person dedicates ALL of one category of his property, such as his entire flock?
A. The dedication takes full effect as usual
B. The dedication does not take effect at all
C. Only half of the flock becomes dedicated
D. He must add an additional animal to make it valid
Correct: B — Rabbi Eliezer rules that dedicating an entire category of property — all of one's flock, slaves, or field — is invalid; the dedication simply does not take effect, derived from the word 'notwithstanding' (akh) in Leviticus 27:28.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
Why does the Torah need to separately exclude 'of man,' 'of animal,' and 'of his ancestral field,' rather than relying on the general phrase 'of all that he has'?
A. Because each word is purely stylistic with no legal significance
B. Because without each separate exclusion, one might think an entire single category (like all his slaves, or all his movable goods) could still be fully dedicated
C. Because the Torah always repeats phrases for emphasis alone
D. Because different categories require different priests to receive them
Correct: B — The Gemara explains that each exclusion is independently necessary: without 'of man,' one might think an entire category could still be wholly dedicated as long as the general rule was satisfied elsewhere, and so on for each category, including movable goods generally, via 'animal.'
QUESTION 3 OF 5
How does Rebbi (Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi) reconcile the mishna's dispute between Rabbi Yehuda and Rabbi Shimon over whether Levites may dedicate their property?
A. He rules that neither opinion is correct
B. He says Rabbi Yehuda's view fits land (Levite land is inalienable) and Rabbi Shimon's view fits movable property (which Levites may freely dedicate)
C. He says both sages agree completely and there is no practical difference
D. He says the dispute applies only to priests, not Levites
Correct: B — Rebbi explains that Rabbi Yehuda's ruling that Levites cannot dedicate applies most accurately to land, since Levite fields are their inalienable perpetual possession, while Rabbi Shimon's more lenient ruling applies to movable property, which even Rabbi Shimon agrees Levites may dedicate.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to the Gemara, who receives a field that a person dedicates (as opposed to movable property he dedicates)?
A. Any priest the donor personally chooses, exactly like movable property
B. Only the priestly watch that is currently serving in the Temple, derived by verbal analogy from the law of stolen property of a deceased convert
C. It must be sold and the proceeds split among all priests equally
D. It reverts automatically to the Temple treasury for maintenance
Correct: B — Unlike movable dedications, which go to any priest the donor wishes (per Numbers 18:14), a dedicated field goes specifically to the priestly watch currently serving in the Temple, derived via a gezeirah shava ('to the priest'–'to the priest') from the law of a convert's stolen property (Numbers 5:8).
QUESTION 5 OF 5
What is the difference in how one dedicates a VOW-offering versus a GIFT-offering that is already consecrated, according to the new mishna?
A. There is no difference; both require the same payment
B. For a vow-offering he must give its full value (since he's obligated to replace it); for a gift-offering he gives only its 'benefit' — what someone would pay to sacrifice it
C. Vow-offerings cannot be dedicated at all
D. Gift-offerings require double payment compared to vow-offerings
Correct: B — Since a vow-offering (like 'a burnt-offering is incumbent upon me') obligates the person to replace the animal if lost, dedicating it requires paying its full value; a gift-offering (like 'this bull is a burnt-offering') carries no such replacement obligation, so only the assessed 'benefit' of sacrificing it is owed.
