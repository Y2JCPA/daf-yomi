QUESTION 1 OF 5
What is the core disagreement between Rav Huna and Ulla about the mishna's phrase 'not more than eight'?
A. Rav Huna holds it caps the number of FULL (thirty-day) months at eight; Ulla holds it caps the number of DEFICIENT (twenty-nine-day) months at eight
B. They disagree about whether the number is seven or eight
C. Rav Huna says the cap applies only to leap years; Ulla says it applies to every year
D. They disagree about how many months are in a year altogether
Correct: A — Rav Huna reads the mishna's ceiling as limiting full, thirty-day months to eight per year, while Ulla reads it as limiting deficient, twenty-nine-day months to eight.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
Why does the Gemara say the Sages avoided establishing nine full (or nine deficient) months in a single year?
A. Because nine months is astronomically impossible
B. Because it would push the new moon of Tishrei three days out of alignment with Rosh HaShana, inviting the public to accuse the court of fixing dates arbitrarily
C. Because nine months would make the year too short for all the festivals
D. Because the Torah explicitly forbids more than eight such months
Correct: B — The Gemara's concern throughout is the court's credibility: a three-day gap between the calculated new moon and its actual appearance would be conspicuous and invite derision.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to the baraita of Aḥerim, what is the fixed weekday gap between one year's festivals (such as Shavuot or Rosh HaShana) and the next?
A. Four weekdays in an ordinary year, five in a leap year
B. Always exactly seven weekdays
C. Two weekdays regardless of the year type
D. There is no fixed gap at all
Correct: A — Aḥerim teach that an ordinary 354-day year is exactly 50 weeks and 4 days, and a leap year of 383 days is 54 weeks and 5 days, producing those fixed weekday shifts.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Shmuel, what is the range of days in a lunar year?
A. Always exactly 354 days, with no variation
B. No fewer than 352 days, no more than 356 days
C. No fewer than 350 days, no more than 360 days
D. Between 300 and 400 days
Correct: B — Shmuel states the lunar year ranges from 352 days (both variable months deficient) to 356 days (both variable months full), with 354 as the ordinary case.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
How does the Gemara resolve the challenge that a Nazirite who vows 'for the days of a lunar year' always counts exactly 354 days, despite Shmuel's range reaching up to 356?
A. It rejects Shmuel's ruling entirely
B. Vow language follows common, everyday usage, and since most lunar years run 354 days, that figure is the vow's default meaning
C. 356 days is used only for leap years
D. The Nazirite must ask a court to determine the exact count each time
Correct: B — The Gemara answers that halakhic vows are interpreted according to how ordinary people use the term, and the common lunar year is 354 days, regardless of the technical possible range.
