📖 Overview: The Calendar Behind the Mishna

Arakhin 9a–9b
אֵין שְׁנַת לְבָנָה פְּחוּתָה מִשְּׁלֹשׁ מֵאוֹת וַחֲמִשִּׁים וּשְׁתַּיִם יוֹם, וְלֹא יְתֵירָה עַל שְׁלֹשׁ מֵאוֹת וַחֲמִשִּׁים וְשִׁשָּׁה יוֹם
The scene: The daf continues the calendrical sugya opened by yesterday's mishna, that a year has no fewer than four full (thirty-day) months and no more than eight. Rav Huna and Ulla disagree about which figure — full or deficient months — the mishna's 'no more than eight' actually caps, each explaining why nine would embarrass the court by making the new moon of Tishrei visibly out of step with Rosh HaShana. A baraita citing Aḥerim's fixed four- (or five-, in a leap year) weekday gap between one year's festivals and the next is examined, together with Rava's point that it shows the calendar need not wait strictly on eyewitness testimony. Shmuel then fixes the range of a lunar year at 352 to 356 days, a claim tested against how a Nazirite vow 'for the days of a lunar year' is counted, and the daf closes with Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi's own puzzling case of nine deficient months whose new moon nonetheless appeared exactly on time.

🌕 Rav Huna vs. Ulla — Full Months or Deficient Months?

Two readings of the mishna's cap: 'not more than eight'
Rav Huna
The mishna's cap of eight applies to FULL, thirty-day months — no more than eight can be established in a single year.
Ulla
No — the cap of eight applies instead to DEFICIENT, twenty-nine-day months. The mishna's earlier floor of four full months is explained by THIS reason.
Gemara
To each: why not allow a ninth such month?
Both answer
Either way, a ninth would push the new moon of Tishrei three days out of alignment with Rosh HaShana — inviting the public to accuse the court of fixing the calendar arbitrarily.
Underlying source: The dispute traces to a baraita on how many days the intercalated (leap) month adds: the first tanna says thirty; Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel says twenty-nine. Rav Huna follows the latter, Ulla the former.

📅 The Baraita of Aḥerim — a Fixed Weekday Gap

How far does Rosh HaShana or Shavuot shift on the weekly calendar from one year to the next?
Year typeTotal daysWeekday gap to next year
Ordinary year (alternating months)354 days = 50 weeks + 4 days4 weekdays later
Leap year (extra month added)383 days = 54 weeks + 5 days5 weekdays later
Why it matters (Rava): This fixed, calculable gap teaches that the calendar need not wait strictly on eyewitness testimony of the new moon — a predictable calculation can stand in its place.

🔢 Shmuel's Range for a Lunar Year — and the Nazirite Challenge

352 to 356 days, tested against a real halakhic case
1️⃣ Shmuel: a lunar year is never fewer than 352 days, nor more than 356
↓
2️⃣ How: both 'variable' months full → 356 days; both deficient → 352 days; the ordinary alternation of full and deficient → 354 days
↓
3️⃣ Challenge: a baraita says a Nazirite who vows 'for the days of a lunar year' always counts exactly 354 days — so why not sometimes 356, per Shmuel's own range?
↓
4️⃣ Resolution: vow language follows common, everyday usage, and most lunar years run 354 days — that is the default meaning of the phrase, whatever the technical range

🤔 Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi's Puzzling Year

A real incident that seems to defy the whole system
The incident: Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi once established nine consecutive deficient (twenty-nine-day) months in a single year — a count that, by the Gemara's own logic, should have pushed the new moon three days out of alignment with Rosh HaShana. Yet the new moon was sighted exactly on time. Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi expressed his own astonishment at this. His son, Rabbi Shimon, offered the resolution: perhaps the preceding year had itself been a leap year, whose extra month absorbed the discrepancy — the same principle Rav Mesharshiyya used earlier to explain why an apparent gap does not always surface.
