📖 Overview: The Levites' Song

Arakhin 11a–11b
מִנַּיִן לְעִיקַּר שִׁירָה מִן הַתּוֹרָה?
The scene: This daf explores the requirement that the Levites accompany Temple offerings with song. It reframes the mishna's dispute over the musicians' identity — slaves, Levites, or Israelites — as really being about whether time on the musical platform earns a presumption of pure lineage for marriage and Levitical tithes. A baraita then debates whether the song is truly indispensable to the offering, followed by a long chain of proposed biblical sources establishing that song is a Torah-level requirement at all. The Gemara also weighs whether a Levite who performs another Levite's role — a singer gatekeeping, or the reverse — is liable to death or merely violates a prohibition, illustrated by a real incident involving Rabbi Yehoshua bar Ḥananya. It closes with Rabbi Avin's dilemma over whether a communal voluntary burnt offering requires song, tested against the dedication of Ḥizkiyahu's Temple service and the mournful song sung as the First and Second Temples fell.

🎶 Slaves, Levites, or Israelites? The Real Question

Reframing the mishna's dispute over who the Temple musicians were
First guess
Maybe the dispute is whether SINGING or INSTRUMENTS is the primary Temple 'song' — mere accompaniment could be left to slaves.
Objection
But then what does Rabbi Yosei (who says Israelites) hold? Either view of 'what's primary' collapses his middle position entirely!
True dispute
Actually everyone agrees singing is primary. They disagree instead about whether serving on the musicians' platform earns PRESUMPTIVE PURE LINEAGE for marriage and Levitical tithes.
Three views
Slaves — no elevation at all; Levites — elevation for both lineage and tithes; Israelites — elevation for lineage only, not tithes.

📜 Sources for Song as a Torah Requirement

A chain of Amora'im, each reading a different verse to establish the same halakha
❓ Where in the Torah is Levitical song actually required?
↓
📖 Shmuel: 'he shall serve with the NAME of the Lord' (Deut. 18:7) — service tied to God's name means song
↓
📖 Rav Mattana: 'you did not serve...with joy and goodness of heart' — the joyful service is song; the same word 'goodness' also proves first fruits require song
↓
📖 Ḥizkiyah reads 'master of lifting' (יָשׂוֹר) as 'he shall SING' (יָשִׁיר); Rabbi Yonatan compares Levites to priests in altar service
↓
✅ Rav Ashi: trumpeters and singers were 'as one, to make one sound' — song is bound to the same requirement as the trumpets

⚰️ Death Penalty for Swapping Levitical Jobs?

A singer who gatekeeps, or a gatekeeper who sings
Abaye
We hold: a singer who performs a gatekeeper's role alone is liable to death — 'the stranger who draws near shall be put to death' (Numbers 3:38) includes a Levite who is a 'stranger' to that particular service.
Objection
But a baraita says explicitly: a singer who gatekeeps, or a gatekeeper who sings, violates only a PROHIBITION — not a capital offense!
Resolved as a dispute
This tracks an incident: Rabbi Yehoshua bar Ḥananya (a singer) offered to help close the Temple gates. Rabbi Yoḥanan ben Gudgeda sent him back: 'my son, you are of the singers, not the gatekeepers.'
Narrower reading
Perhaps both agree it's only a prohibition — they disagree only about whether merely ASSISTING with another role is also forbidden.

🐂 Does a Voluntary Communal Burnt Offering Need Song?

Rabbi Avin's dilemma, tested against two dramatic Temple episodes
1️⃣ Rabbi Avin asks: does 'your burnt offerings' (Numbers 10:10) include a VOLUNTARY communal offering, or only one the nation is obligated to bring?
↓
2️⃣ First attempt: Ḥizkiyahu's Temple dedication, where song began together with a burnt offering — but the offering's identity (New Moon? the Omer lamb?) is disputed and each option runs into objections
↓
3️⃣ Second attempt: as the First and Second Temples fell, the Levites sang even though the daily offering had already ceased — but the Gemara shows the verse they sang wasn't even that day's assigned psalm, so it proves nothing about an actual offering
↓
4️⃣ Resolved: a verbal analogy links 'your burnt offerings' to 'your peace offerings' — since the one peace offering requiring song (the Shavuot lambs) has a FIXED time, only a burnt offering with a fixed time requires song; a voluntary one does not
