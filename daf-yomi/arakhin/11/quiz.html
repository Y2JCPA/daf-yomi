Arakhin 11 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to the Gemara's reframing, what is the true point of dispute among Rabbi Meir, Rabbi Yosei, and Rabbi Ḥanina ben Antigonus about the Temple musicians?
A. Whether instruments or vocal singing is more important
B. Whether serving on the musical platform grants a presumption of pure lineage for marriage and/or eligibility for Levitical tithes
C. Whether the musicians must be circumcised
D. Whether women could serve as musicians
Correct: B — The Gemara concludes all three agree singing is primary, and instead dispute whether platform service confers presumptive pure lineage (for marriage, for tithes, for both, or for neither).
QUESTION 2 OF 5
What is Rabbi Meir's source for ruling that the Levites' song is indispensable to the offering?
A. A verse comparing the Levites' service to the atonement achieved by the blood (Numbers 8:19) — just as atonement is indispensable, so is song
B. A verse that explicitly states 'song is indispensable'
C. An unbroken oral tradition with no biblical source
D. A comparison to the daily incense offering
Correct: A — Rabbi Meir derives indispensability from the verse's comparison between the Levites' service (song) and the atoning function of the sacrificial blood.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to Abaye, from which verse is it derived that a Levite singer who performs a gatekeeper's role alone may be liable to death?
A. 'The stranger that draws near shall be put to death' (Numbers 3:38), read as including a Levite who is a 'stranger' to that particular service
B. A verse about the death of Nadav and Avihu
C. There is no biblical source; it is a rabbinic decree
D. A verse forbidding gentiles from Temple service
Correct: A — Abaye applies the term 'stranger' in Numbers 3:38 not only to a non-Levite but to a Levite who is a stranger to a role not assigned to him.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
What resolves Rabbi Avin's dilemma about whether a voluntary communal burnt offering requires song?
A. A direct verse stating that voluntary offerings never require song
B. A verbal analogy linking 'your burnt offerings' to 'your peace offerings' — since the peace offering requiring song has a fixed time (the Shavuot lambs), only a burnt offering with a fixed time requires song
C. The testimony of Rabbi Avin's teacher
D. A resolution by lottery, as with other unresolved dilemmas
Correct: B — The Gemara resolves the question by comparing burnt offerings to peace offerings in Numbers 10:10, concluding that song accompanies only offerings — of either kind — that have a fixed, obligatory time.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to the baraita about the destruction of the Temple, what happened as the Levites sang on the day of destruction?
A. They completed the entire psalm without interruption
B. They did not manage to finish the verse 'He will cut them off' before enemies came and conquered them
C. They refused to sing at all that day
D. They sang a psalm of joy instead of lament
Correct: B — The baraita relates that the Levites were singing 'and He brought upon them their own iniquity, and He will cut them off' when they were interrupted by the conquest, unable to finish the verse.
