📖 Overview: Protected Possessions and the Jubilee Field

Arakhin 24a–24b
וְאִם מָךְ הוּא מֵעֶרְכֶּךָ — הַחֲיֵיהוּ מֵעֶרְכֶּךָ
The scene: The daf finishes explaining why a debtor keeps food, clothing, and trade tools even as the Temple treasury repossesses his other property, then turns to what even a person who consecrates literally everything must still give up (his tefillin) and what may never be seized from anyone — his family's clothing and anything not yet worth its later, appreciated price. With that, the chapter on proclaiming sales closes. A new mishna opens a fresh topic: the special two-year and one-year windows around the Jubilee Year that govern when an ancestral field may be consecrated or redeemed, and a sharp Rav-and-Shmuel dispute over what happens if someone consecrates his field during the Jubilee Year itself.

🧰 Why the Debtor Keeps His Tools

Scripture, and a very practical worry, behind the mishna's leniency
1️⃣ Source verse: 'if he be too poor for your valuation' (Lev. 27:8) is read as הַחֲיֵיהוּ מֵעֶרְכֶּךָ — 'sustain him' out of his own valuation debt
↓
2️⃣ 'Him' — but explicitly not his wife or children, who are not mentioned in the verse
↓
3️⃣ If he had many of one tool and few of another (e.g. three adzes, one saw), the treasury does not sell the extras to buy him a matching pair
↓
4️⃣ Why is this not obvious, since one saw always sufficed before? Because until now he could borrow a second tool if needed — now, newly poor, no one will lend to him

📿 Even Tefillin Are Not Safe From a Full Consecration

A real case brought before Rav Yeimar
הַמַּקְדִּישׁ אֶת נְכָסָיו מַעֲלִין לוֹ תְּפִילִּין
The incident: A man had sold all his property. When the case came before Rav Yeimar, he ruled: remove even his tefillin and give them to the buyer. This might seem obvious from the mishna's own rule that consecrating all one's property includes the tefillin — but the Gemara explains the case teaches something new: perhaps one might think tefillin are excluded only from a straightforward sale (since a person would not silently sell an item used for a mitzva performed with his own body without saying so explicitly) even while they're included in a mitzva-driven consecration. Rav Yeimar's ruling shows tefillin are swept into an unqualified sale of 'everything' just as they are into an unqualified consecration.

👕 What the Temple Treasury May Never Touch

New mishna: absolute protections, plus a 'here and now' valuation rule

His wife's or children's garmentsBoth one who consecrates property and one who valuates himselfNever part of his own liened property

New sandals bought for themSameSame
Full future/appreciated value (fine garments on a slave, a cow held for market day, a pearl brought to the city)Any item collected by hekdesh'Give your valuation on that day' (Lev. 27:23) — no delaying a sale to chase a higher price elsewhere
Bonus teaching: The same verse's phrase 'holy to the Lord' teaches that unspecified consecrations (simply 'my property is consecrated') go to Temple maintenance (bedek habayit), not to the priests.

🌾 New Mishna: Fencing Off the Jubilee Year

Why 'less than two years before' and 'less than one year after' the Jubilee are off-limits
❓ Mishna: one may not consecrate an ancestral field less than 2 years before the Jubilee, nor redeem one less than 1 year after it. But a baraita says fields may be consecrated any time except during the Jubilee Year itself — contradiction?
↓
💡 Rav and Shmuel together: the mishna's '2 years' bars only redemption with a deduction (gerua, prorated by years left) — not consecration itself, which always remains valid
↓
✅ Practical effect: consecrate less than 2 years out, and you (or your heirs) simply pay the full 50-sela valuation rather than a discounted, year-prorated sum — 'let a person be concerned for his property'

🎉 Consecrating a Field DURING the Jubilee Year Itself

Rav and Shmuel read the same verse two different ways
Rav
It is consecrated — and one redeems it for the full fifty sela per beit kor. The verse 'if he sanctifies his field from the Jubilee Year' (Lev. 27:17) includes the Jubilee Year itself in that opening word.
Shmuel
It is not consecrated at all! 'From the Jubilee Year' means starting the year after — the Jubilee Year itself is excluded.
Rav Yosef's challenge
For a sale, Shmuel's logic makes sense by an a fortiori: even an already-sold field reverts at the Jubilee, so an unsold one surely can't be newly sold then. But for consecration, doesn't a parallel mishna show a field consecrated earlier does NOT simply revert at the Jubilee — the priests receive its value?
Resolution
Shmuel follows Rabbi Shimon, who holds the priests enter the field without any payment — so the a fortiori does hold after all. Rav disputes even this, since the field still leaves hekdesh (to priests, 'from the Table of the Most High'), not back to the original owner.
