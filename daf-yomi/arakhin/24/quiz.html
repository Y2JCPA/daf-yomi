QUESTION 1 OF 5
Why does the mishna's list of protected items (food, clothing, bed, tools) apply only to the debtor himself and not to his wife or children?
A. Because wives and children have no legal standing in Temple matters
B. Because the source verse, 'if he be too poor for your valuation,' is read as an instruction to sustain specifically 'him,' not his wife and children
C. Because wives and children are always independently wealthy
D. Because this rule applies only to unmarried debtors
Correct: B — The Gemara reads Leviticus 27:8's 'he' as teaching that the treasurer sustains the debtor himself from his valuation debt — his wife and children are not included in that verse.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
What did Rav Yeimar's ruling about a man who sold all his property come to teach, given that the mishna already states that one who consecrates his property has his tefillin taken?
A. That the mishna's ruling was mistaken
B. That even in an ordinary sale (not consecration) of 'everything,' tefillin are included too, despite the concern that people don't casually sell items used for a bodily mitzva without saying so
C. That tefillin can never be sold under any circumstances
D. That the buyer must return the tefillin voluntarily
Correct: B — The novel teaching is that even a plain sale of 'all his property' includes tefillin, even though one might have thought a person wouldn't silently include a mitzva item like tefillin in a sale unless he consecrated it (with clear mitzva-intent).
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to the new mishna, why doesn't the Temple treasury collect based on an item's potential future or appreciated value (like a fine garment increasing a slave's sale price, or a pearl sold in the city)?
A. Because such items cannot be sold to the Temple at all
B. Because the verse 'give your valuation on that day' teaches that value is fixed to its current location and time, not a later, higher price
C. Because the Temple treasury only accepts cash, never goods
D. Because appreciated value is always given to the poor instead
Correct: B — The baraita derives from 'and he shall give your valuation as of that day' (Lev. 27:23) that the Temple must value and collect an item based on its present location and moment, not by waiting for it to fetch a higher price.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Rav and Shmuel's joint statement, what does the mishna's rule 'one may not consecrate an ancestral field less than two years before the Jubilee' actually restrict?
A. It bans consecrating the field entirely during that window
B. It bans only redeeming the field with a year-prorated deduction during that window — consecration itself remains valid, just redeemed at full price
C. It applies only to fields owned by priests
D. It has no practical effect at all
Correct: B — Rav and Shmuel explain the mishna is about redemption 'with a deduction' (a discount based on years remaining) — consecration is still effective within two years of the Jubilee, but redemption must then be at the full fifty-sela valuation.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
What is the core dispute between Rav and Shmuel regarding a field consecrated DURING the Jubilee Year itself?
A. Rav says it is not consecrated at all; Shmuel says it is consecrated at full price
B. Rav says it is consecrated and redeemed for the full fifty sela; Shmuel says it is not consecrated at all, reading 'from the Jubilee Year' as excluding that year
C. Both agree it is not consecrated
D. Both agree it is consecrated but only redeemable with a deduction
Correct: B — Rav holds the verse 'if he sanctifies his field from the Jubilee Year' includes the Jubilee Year itself, so consecration is valid at full price; Shmuel reads 'from the Jubilee Year' as beginning only the following year, so consecration during the Jubilee Year itself has no effect.
