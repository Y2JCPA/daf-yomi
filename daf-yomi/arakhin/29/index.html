📖 Overview: Dedications, Their Destination, and a New Chapter on Sales

Arakhin 29a–29b
חֶרְמֵי כֹהֲנִים אֵין לָהֶן פִּדְיוֹן וְנִותָּנִין לַכֹּהֵן. חֲרָמִים כׇּל זְמַן שֶׁהֵן בְּבֵית בְּעָלִים הֲרֵי הֵן כְּהֶקְדֵּשׁ
The scene: The Gemara continues examining ḥerem (dedication for the priests), tracking exactly when it behaves like ordinary consecrated property and when it becomes fully non-sacred, and revisiting the dispute over where an unspecified dedication defaults — Temple maintenance or the priests directly — through a real incident in Pumbedita. A baraita then raises the question of whether the laws of ḥerem even apply in an era without an active Jubilee. The daf closes its chapter and opens a new one on selling — not consecrating — a field, governed by its own Torah-mandated minimum holding period, and a sharp Rav-and-Shmuel dispute about a sale made during the Jubilee Year itself.

🌊 The Pumbedita Dedication: Redeem It, or Give It All Away?

A real case tests where an unspecified dedication really belongs
The case
A man in Pumbedita dedicated his property without specifying its purpose, then came to Rav Yehuda asking what to do.
Rav Yehuda
Take four dinars, transfer the property's sanctity onto them, then throw the dinars in the river (since no benefit may be taken from them) — and your property is redeemed and permitted to you.
Implication
This only works if unspecified dedications go to Temple maintenance, which can be redeemed — dedications to the priests directly could never be redeemed this way.
Ulla
'Had I been there, I would have given it ALL to the priests' — meaning unspecified dedications go straight to the priests, with no possibility of redemption at all.
Why only 4 dinars? Post-Temple, one may redeem hekdesh even for less than its true value — but Rav Yehuda still specified a real sum (not a single peruta) simply to publicize that a valid redemption had taken place.

🏛️ Does Ḥerem Still Apply Without an Active Jubilee?

A baraita seems to tie the whole institution to the Jubilee cycle
❓ Baraita: the Hebrew slave, the dedicated ancestral field, and houses in walled cities all 'apply only in an era when the Jubilee is observed' — so does ḥerem stop functioning entirely today?
↓
💡 Resolution: that baraita speaks only of land — dedicated fields, tied intrinsically to the Jubilee's return-cycle
↓
✅ Movable property dedications remain fully in force always — which is exactly why the Pumbedita case (about property) still needed real redemption
Related mishna: Rabbi Yishmael reconciles two verses on firstborn animals — 'you shall consecrate' (Deut. 15:19) and 'a man shall not consecrate it' (Lev. 27:26) — by distinguishing a 'consecration of value' (the right to give it to a chosen priest) from consecration for the altar, which is impossible since a firstborn already carries its own sanctity.

🌾 New Chapter: Selling (Not Consecrating) a Field

New mishna: a Torah-mandated waiting period before redemption
1️⃣ Base rule: a field sold while the Jubilee cycle is active cannot be redeemed less than two years after the sale — from the plural 'years' in Lev. 25:15
↓
2️⃣ A year of blight, mildew, or the Sabbatical Year doesn't count toward that tally, since the buyer got no real benefit from it
↓
3️⃣ But a year the buyer merely plowed (without sowing) or left fallow DOES count — the field was still fit to produce
↓
4️⃣ Rabbi Eliezer: if sold full of produce just before Rosh Hashana, the buyer may end up eating three crops in those two years — payment is reckoned by years, not by harvests
It's a prohibition, not just advice: The Gemara reads the mishna's 'not permitted to redeem' as a real prohibition — one may not even 'rattle coins' at the buyer to pressure an early sale. Both seller and buyer are bound by their own positive mitzvot from the same verse.

🔄 Selling a Field During the Jubilee Year Itself

Rav and Shmuel disagree sharply about whether such a sale has any effect

Rav: Sold, Then Reverts

  • The sale IS valid in principle
  • But the field immediately leaves the buyer's possession
  • The buyer's money is NOT refunded — he simply loses it

Shmuel: Never Sold At All

  • Kal vachomer: if a field already sold before the Jubilee reverts at the Jubilee, surely a field NOT yet sold cannot be newly sold during it
  • The sale has no effect whatsoever
  • The buyer's money IS refunded
A parallel dilemma: Rav Anan recalled two similar rulings from Shmuel — this one, and a case of selling a Canaanite slave abroad — but couldn't remember which involved a refund. Rav Yosef resolves it from a baraita's own wording.
