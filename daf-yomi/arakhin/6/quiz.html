Arakhin 6 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to Rabbi Yoḥanan, when may a gentile's donation for Temple maintenance be accepted?
A. At the very beginning of construction, to encourage goodwill
B. Only at the end of construction, and only if the item cannot be identified as coming from him
C. Never, under any circumstances
D. Only if the donation is food, such as water and salt
Correct: B — The Gemara rules that nothing is accepted at the beginning (not even water and salt), and at the end only an unidentifiable, undefined item may be accepted.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
Why must a gentile's separated teruma be interred (geniza) when his intent is unclear?
A. Because gentile produce is always forbidden
B. Because we are concerned his heart may have been directed toward Heaven, i.e., he may have intended to consecrate it rather than give it to a priest
C. Because teruma may never be separated by a gentile
D. Because the produce might be diseased
Correct: B — Rav Yehuda in the name of Rav rules that without clarity on his intent, the doubtful possibility of a consecration to the Temple requires the produce to be interred rather than used.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
Once a coin pledged 'for charity' reaches the hand of the charity collector, what is the rule?
A. It may no longer be exchanged for a different coin
B. It may always be freely exchanged
C. It must be doubled before being given
D. It becomes ownerless property
Correct: A — The baraita states that a coin designated for charity may be changed only before it comes into the possession of the charity collectors; afterward it is fixed.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
Why may a gentile's donated synagogue lamp not be redirected to another use, while a Jew's donated lamp may be redirected to a different mitzva?
A. Gentile donations are inherently invalid
B. A gentile donor would protest and demand its return if he saw it changed, while a Jew would not object to a shift toward another mitzva
C. Only Jews are permitted to donate synagogue items
D. Lamps donated by gentiles must always remain lit
Correct: B — The Gemara explains the distinction is behavioral: a gentile donor is expected to protest ('where is the lamp I gave?'), so his gift may not be touched until his name as donor is forgotten.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to the mishna, what does Rabbi Yosei say about one who has been sentenced to execution?
A. He is fully exempt from all halakhic obligations
B. He can still vow to donate another's assessment, take vows of valuation, and consecrate his own property, and is liable for damage he causes to others
C. He may no longer own any property at all
D. He is treated exactly like an ordinary living person in every respect
Correct: B — Rabbi Yosei holds that such a person retains the capacity to vow, valuate, and consecrate, and remains liable for damage he causes — a position the Gemara ties to whether such a debt is collectible from his estate.
