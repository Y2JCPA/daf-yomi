📖 Overview: Whose Means Determine the Payment?

Arakhin 17a–17b
הֶשֵּׂג יָד בַּנּוֹדֵר, וְהַשָּׁנִים בַּנִּידָּר
The scene: The daf closes the discussion of rebuke with Rabbi Eliezer the Great's teaching that even the patriarchs could not withstand God's strict judgment, and a debate over whether a generation's character follows its leader or the reverse, resolved as a question of temperament rather than righteousness. A new mishna then opens the tractate's next chapter with four governing principles for a valuation vow: affordability follows the one who vows, while the age- and sex-based sum follows the person being valuated, fixed at the moment the vow is spoken. The Gemara contrasts this with vowing to provide a leper's purification offering, which follows the leper's own means rather than the vower's — except according to Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi, who applies the 'follow the original subject's obligation' principle to valuations too. A further mishna addresses a change in financial status between the vow and its payment, ruling that the more stringent, wealthy rate always applies — in sharp contrast to a leper's own offering, which tracks only his actual possessions at the moment of payment.

⚖️ Could the Patriarchs Withstand Strict Judgment?

Closing the discussion of rebuke
Rabbi Eliezer the Great teaches: Even Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob could not have withstood God's rebuke had He entered into strict judgment with them — proven from Samuel's own words, 'that I may plead with you...concerning all the righteous acts (i.e., mercies) of the Lord.' A separate debate asks whether a generation's level follows its leader, or a leader's level follows his generation — resolved not as a claim about righteousness (since righteous Zedekiah led an unrighteous generation, and wicked Jehoiakim an upright one) but about harshness versus gentleness of temperament.

📜 New Chapter, New Mishna: Four Governing Principles

Whose circumstances set the terms of a valuation vow?

Follows the VOWER

  • Affordability (heseg yad) — a poor person valuating a rich person pays only the poor rate, and a rich person valuating a poor person pays the rich rate

Follows the ONE VALUATED

  • The age-based fixed sum — set by the subject's own age
  • The male/female distinction — follows the subject, not the vower

Fixed at the Moment

  • The valuation amount locks in based on the subject's age and status AT THE TIME the vow is spoken

💰 Poor-Then-Rich, or Rich-Then-Poor — Which Rate Applies?

The mishna's rule, and Rabbi Yehuda's extension
Tanna Kamma
If one was poor when he vowed and became rich by payment time, OR was rich when he vowed and became poor by payment time — either way, he pays the RICH rate.
Gemara
Derived from two phrases: rich-then-poor from 'according to the vower's means' (rich at the vow); poor-then-rich from 'al pi asher tasig' — the moment he speaks it with his mouth, or ultimately affords it.
Rabbi Yehuda
Even poor-then-rich-then-poor AGAIN — he still pays the rich rate, since 'if he is poor' requires unbroken poverty from start to finish.
Objection
But shouldn't that same logic apply to a leper's OWN offering too? Yet a mishna in Nega'im says his status is fixed by only ONE of his offerings (sin, guilt, or the birds — disputed), regardless of fluctuation in between!
Resolution
The leper's own case rests on a distinct verse and is a special Torah edict — it does not require unbroken poverty. Only laws like the valuation vow demand consistency throughout.

🩹 Arakhin vs. a Leper's Offering — Whose Means Count?

Two vows that sound alike but follow opposite rules

Valuation Vow (Erech)

  • Follows the VOWER'S means — a poor person who valuates a rich person pays only the poor rate

Vowing to Provide a Leper's Offering

  • Follows the LEPER'S OWN means, not the vower's — a poor person who vows for a WEALTHY leper still brings the wealthy offering
Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi dissents: Even for arakhin he holds 'we follow the original subject's obligation' — so if a wealthy person vows his own valuation and a poor bystander adopts that vow ('what he said, upon me'), the poor bystander must still pay the WEALTHY rate.

💸 Windfalls Don't Count for a Leper's Offering

Current possession, not anticipated wealth, determines the sum
ScenarioEffect on payment
