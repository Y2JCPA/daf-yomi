Arakhin 17 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to Rabbi Eliezer the Great, what would have happened had God entered into strict judgment with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob?
A. They would have easily withstood it, since they were entirely righteous
B. They would not have been able to withstand the rebuke, since God actually dealt with them through mercy rather than the strict letter of the law
C. God would never have created the world
D. The question is left unresolved in the Gemara
Correct: B — Rabbi Eliezer the Great derives from Samuel's words about God's 'righteous acts' (i.e., mercies) toward the patriarchs that they were treated leniently, implying that strict judgment would have been unbearable even for them.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to the new mishna, whose financial means determine 'affordability' (heseg yad) in a valuation vow?
A. The person being valuated (the subject of the vow)
B. The one who takes the vow (the vower), not the person being valuated
C. The Temple treasurer
D. Both parties equally, split down the middle
Correct: B — The mishna states plainly that affordability is determined by the vower's means, derived from the verse 'according to the means of him who vowed.'
QUESTION 3 OF 5
How does vowing to provide a leper's purification offering differ from a standard valuation vow, according to the mishna?
A. There is no difference; both follow the same rule
B. It follows the LEPER's own means, not the means of the person who vowed to provide it — the opposite of a valuation vow, which follows the vower's means
C. It always requires the wealthy-tier offering regardless of anyone's means
D. Only priests may vow to provide a leper's offering
Correct: B — The mishna explicitly contrasts the two: valuations track the vower's means, but a leper's offering tracks the leper's own actual financial status.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to the mishna, if one was poor when he took a valuation vow and became wealthy by the time of payment (or vice versa), which rate does he pay?
A. The poor rate, in either case
B. The wealthy rate, in either case
C. An average of the two rates
D. Whichever rate is lower at each stage separately
Correct: B — The mishna rules that in both directions of change — poor-to-rich or rich-to-poor — the more stringent, wealthy rate applies.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
Why does a leper's offering NOT increase even if he is about to inherit a large fortune or receive valuable cargo before actually bringing the offering?
A. Because leprosy offerings are always fixed regardless of wealth
B. Because his offering is determined solely by his actual, current possessions at the time he brings it, not by anticipated future wealth
C. Because the Temple treasury refuses windfall-based payments on principle
D. Because such windfalls are considered stolen property
Correct: B — The mishna states that even known future wealth — an inheritance or incoming cargo — has no bearing on the leper's offering, since only his present actual possessions determine what he brings.
