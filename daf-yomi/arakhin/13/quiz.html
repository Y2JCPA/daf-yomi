QUESTION 1 OF 5
What did the prophets decree about the precedence of the priestly watch of Jedaiah?
A. That Jedaiah's watch would be dissolved once Yehoyariv's line returned
B. That even if Yehoyariv's original watch later returned to Eretz Yisrael, Jedaiah would retain its place as first, and Yehoyariv would be subordinate to it
C. That the two watches would alternate service annually
D. That only Yehoyariv's watch could ever serve as first
Correct: B — The prophets protected the newly reconstituted watch of Jedaiah's precedence permanently, even against the possibility that the original leading watch of Yehoyariv might later return.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to Rav Ashi's resolution, why does the 420-year span of the Second Temple still work out to a destruction in the year after Shmita?
A. Because the Second Temple actually stood for a different number of years
B. The six years between the start of construction and Ezra's arrival, when he resanctified the land, are not counted toward the Sabbatical cycle at all
C. Because Rabbi Yehuda's Jubilee-counting method is applied throughout
D. Because the calculation ignores leap years entirely
Correct: B — Rav Ashi excludes the six years before Ezra resanctified the land (since the Sabbatical cycle was not in effect during that period), which shifts the final total to land in the year after Shmita.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to ben Bag Bag, from where is it derived that the daily offering requires inspection four days before its slaughter?
A. A direct verse stating the exact number of days
B. A shared use of the word 'safeguard' (מִשְׁמֶרֶת/תִּשְׁמְרוּ) links the daily offering's verse to the Paschal offering's, which explicitly requires four days of inspection
C. An oral tradition with no textual basis
D. A comparison to the inspection required for a blemished animal
Correct: B — Ben Bag Bag derives the four-day requirement through a verbal analogy based on the shared root of 'safeguard' in the verses about the daily offering and the Paschal offering.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to the mishna, what is unique about the cymbal in the Temple orchestra compared to the trumpets or harps?
A. There is exactly one, and none may ever be added — unlike the flexible minimums and unlimited maximums for trumpets and harps
B. The cymbal has no fixed number at all
C. The cymbal could be played by anyone, unlike other instruments
D. The cymbal was banned from Temple use entirely
Correct: A — Unlike trumpets (2 to 120) and harps (9 and unlimited), the mishna fixes the cymbal at exactly one — played by a single person striking the paired cymbals together.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to the new mishna's example of leniency and stringency in arakhin, what is true of someone who vows the fixed valuation (erech) of both the most beautiful and the most unsightly Israelite?
A. The payment varies significantly based on actual appearance
B. Both cases require exactly the same fixed sum of fifty sela, regardless of actual appearance or market worth
C. Only the valuation of the beautiful person is valid
D. Neither valuation is ever collected
Correct: B — The mishna's example shows that the Torah's flat fixed valuation of fifty sela applies identically to both the most attractive and the least attractive person, unlike a vow based on actual market price (damim).
