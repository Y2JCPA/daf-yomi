📖 Overview: Closing One Chapter, Opening the Next

Arakhin 13a–13b
יֵשׁ בַּעֲרָכִין לְהָקֵל וּלְהַחְמִיר
The scene: The daf closes out the discussion of the priestly watches with the prophets' ruling that Jedaiah's watch keeps precedence even if Yehoyariv's line later returns, and offers Rav Ashi's own resolution to the Second Temple dating puzzle: the six years before Ezra resanctified the land for the Sabbatical cycle simply don't count. A new mishna then sets more Temple minimums and maximums — six inspected lambs kept on hand, examined four days before slaughter per ben Bag Bag, a floor of two trumpets and nine harps with generous ceilings, and exactly one cymbal, never more. Another mishna fixes twelve Levites as the minimum on the singers' platform, with a dispute over whether minor Levites, who join only during the actual singing to sweeten the sound, count toward that number. The daf then closes the tractate's opening chapters entirely and opens a new one: a mishna listing four areas of halakha with both lenient and stringent rulings, starting with the observation that the Torah's flat fifty-sela valuation applies identically to the most beautiful and the most unsightly person, in sharp contrast to a vow of one's actual market price.

⛓️ Finishing the Priestly-Watch Puzzle

Two more resolutions to yesterday's dating question
1️⃣ The prophets decreed: even if Yehoyariv's original watch later returned to Eretz Yisrael, Jedaiah's watch — already reconstituted — keeps its precedence as first; Yehoyariv would be subordinate
↓
2️⃣ So the baraita's claim that both Temples fell 'the same way' refers only to shared details (the 9th of Av, a Sunday) — not to which watch was actually on duty
↓
3️⃣ Rav Ashi's separate answer: the six years between the start of the Second Temple's construction and Ezra's arrival, when he resanctified the land, don't count toward the Sabbatical cycle at all
↓
4️⃣ Excluding those six years, the remaining count of years lands the destruction squarely in the year after Shmita — matching the baraita after all

🎺 Trumpets, Harps, and the Solitary Cymbal

A floor and ceiling for each instrument — except one

Trumpets

  • Minimum: 2
  • Maximum: 120
  • Source: '120 priests sounding trumpets' (II Chronicles 5:12)

Harps

  • Minimum: 9
  • Maximum: unlimited
  • Matches the minimum size of the Temple orchestra

Cymbal

  • Exactly 1 — never more, never fewer
  • 'Asaph with the cymbals' — one person plays the paired cymbals as a single instrument

🎵 Do Minor Singers Count Toward the Twelve Levites?

Rabbi Eliezer ben Ya'akov's dissent
Tanna Kamma
No fewer than twelve Levites stand on the platform. Minors may join in SINGING (not instruments) only while the adults sing, adding sweetness with their pure, high voices.
R' Eliezer b. Ya'akov
Minors are NOT counted among the twelve, and they don't even stand on the platform — they stand on the ground, their heads reaching only to between the Levites' feet.
Naming (Tanna Kamma)
Our mishna calls them tza'arei — 'those who cause distress' — since their pure high voices made the grown Levites sound inferior by comparison.
A baraita disagrees
It instead calls them so'adei — 'those who assist' — a gentler name for the same young singers.

⚖️ New Chapter: Leniency and Stringency in a Valuation Vow

The same person, two different ways of being valuated

Fixed Torah Valuation (erech)

  • Vowing the fixed valuation of the most beautiful Israelite, OR of the most unsightly
  • BOTH pay exactly 50 sela — the Torah's flat sum ignores actual appearance or worth

Market Assessment (damim)

  • Vowing instead 'his price is upon me,' as if he were sold as a slave
  • Payment tracks his ACTUAL market worth — more for the beautiful, less for the unsightly
Leniency and stringency: The flat 50-sela erech is lenient for someone actually worth more on the market, and stringent for someone actually worth less — exactly the reverse of the damim-based vow, which is always precise to the person's real value.
