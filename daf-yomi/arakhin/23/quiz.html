QUESTION 1 OF 5
Why does Rabbi Eliezer require a man who consecrated his property and then divorces his wife to vow that benefit from her is forbidden to him?
A. To punish him for consecrating his property
B. To prevent collusion: she could collect her ketubah from the consecrated property, and then he could remarry her, effectively recovering Temple funds for the family
C. Because Jewish law always requires such a vow after any divorce
D. To ensure she never remarries
Correct: B — Rabbi Eliezer is concerned about a scheme where the divorced wife collects her ketubah debt from the consecrated property and the couple then remarries, having effectively extracted Temple treasury funds for themselves.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
How does the Gemara ultimately explain the basis of the Rabbi Eliezer/Rabbi Yehoshua dispute?
A. They disagree about whether ketubah debts are valid at all
B. They disagree about whether a vow made regarding consecrated property can later be dissolved (annulled) by a halakhic authority
C. They disagree about the value of the consecrated property
D. They disagree about whether women may collect ketubah payments
Correct: B — The Gemara settles on explaining that Rabbi Eliezer holds a vow concerning consecrated property cannot be dissolved (so the anti-collusion vow is truly effective), while Rabbi Yehoshua holds it can be dissolved, making the vow pointless as a safeguard.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
What was revealed about Rav Huna (son of the guarantor Moshe bar Atzrei) that undermined Abaye's proposed scheme for him to gain money?
A. He had no wife to divorce
B. He was discovered to be a priest (kohen), who is forbidden to remarry a woman he divorced
C. He had already paid off the debt
D. He was not actually a Torah scholar
Correct: B — It emerged that Rav Huna was a kohen, who by Torah law may never remarry a woman he divorced — making Abaye's divorce-and-remarry scheme impossible, prompting Abaye's remark 'poverty follows the poor.'
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Rabbi Abbahu, why does the mishna require that consecrated property burdened by debts be formally 'redeemed' rather than simply handed over to the creditor or wife?
A. Redemption always yields a higher price for the Temple
B. So that people should not say that consecrated property left its sacred status without any redemption
C. Because direct transfer is halakhically impossible for any consecrated item
D. Because the creditor prefers cash to property
Correct: B — Rabbi Abbahu explains the requirement to formally redeem the property is to avoid the appearance (and reality) that hekdesh property exited consecration without a proper act of redemption.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
What is left for a debtor whose property is repossessed for an unpaid valuation (arakhin), according to the mishna, and how does this differ from someone who consecrates ALL his property?
A. Both cases leave the debtor with nothing at all
B. The arakhin debtor keeps food for 30 days, clothing for a year, a bed, sandals, and tefillin; but one who consecrated everything has even his tefillin taken
C. The arakhin debtor loses his tefillin but keeps everything else
D. There is no difference between the two cases
Correct: B — The mishna leaves an arakhin debtor basic sustenance items including tefillin, based on the verse 'sustain him.' But someone who explicitly consecrated 'all his property' has even his tefillin taken, since his declaration excluded nothing.
