QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi's reading of the new mishna, if a field was sold to a first buyer for 100 dinars, who then resold it to a second buyer for 200 dinars, at what price is the field redeemed?
A. 200 dinars, the most recent price
B. 150 dinars, an average of the two
C. 100 dinars, the price of the FIRST sale
D. The field cannot be redeemed once resold
Correct: C — Rebbi derives from 'to the man to whom he sold it' (Lev. 25:27) that when the price decreased with resale, redemption is calculated according to the first buyer's original price of 100 dinars.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
What underlying principle does the Gemara prove via a gezeirah shava between a field's redemption and a Hebrew slave's redemption?
A. That both must be redeemed with borrowed money
B. That both are always calculated using whichever value is LOWER, favoring the seller/slave
C. That both require exactly two witnesses
D. That both can only be redeemed during the Jubilee Year
Correct: B — The Gemara links the word 'redemption' in both contexts to show that just as a Hebrew slave's redemption price is always calculated at the lower of his original or current value, so too is a field's redemption price.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
In Rabbi Yosei bar Ḥanina's sermon, what marks the escalating stages of a person's financial and moral decline?
A. He immediately sells himself into slavery without warning signs
B. He progresses from selling movable goods, to fields, to his house, to borrowing with interest, to selling his daughter, and finally to selling himself into slavery
C. He only ever loses his house and nothing else
D. The stages are unrelated to Sabbatical Year or Jubilee violations
Correct: B — Rabbi Yosei bar Ḥanina traces a step-by-step decline, each tied to an adjacent Torah verse, from trading in Sabbatical-year produce all the way down to selling oneself into servitude — even to idol worship.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
How does Abaye respond to the challenge that a self-sold slave (who even sold himself to idol worship) should face STRICTER redemption terms because of his own sins?
A. He agrees and imposes stricter terms
B. He cites that the Torah requires the slave be treated as an equal ('with you') and that the verse itself guarantees him a perpetual right of redemption so he isn't assimilated among the nations
C. He says the slave has no right to redemption at all
D. He argues the question is irrelevant since slavery no longer exists
Correct: B — Abaye points to the Torah's explicit gentleness toward a Hebrew slave and to the verse promising him 'a perpetual right of redemption,' explaining that leniency stands specifically so he won't be lost among the gentiles, regardless of how he got there.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to Rabbi Shimon in the new mishna, why can one redeem a Temple-treasury (hekdesh) field by borrowing money or paying in installments, when neither is allowed for a privately sold field?
A. Because hekdesh fields are worth less money
B. Because a privately sold field already has the built-in advantage of automatically returning to the seller at the Jubilee if unredeemed, so its redemption process is restricted; a consecrated field lacks that advantage (it goes to the priests instead), so its redemption is eased
C. Because Temple treasurers are permitted to lend money
D. There is no real reason; it is a fixed decree with no logic given
Correct: B — Rabbi Shimon explains the Torah balances the scales: since an unredeemed privately-sold field reverts to the seller anyway at the Jubilee, its redemption is deliberately restricted; since an unredeemed consecrated field is LOST to the priests instead, its redemption is deliberately made easier.
