Arakhin 19 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to the mishna, if one vows 'my weight is incumbent upon me' without specifying silver or gold, what determines what he must pay?
A. He is entirely exempt
B. He must always pay in gold
C. He may pay in any commodity that merchants in his locale sell by weight
D. He must pay in silver by default
Correct: C — The Gemara infers from the mishna's 'if silver, silver; if gold, gold' that only a specified metal binds him — otherwise any locally-weighed commodity (even pitch or onions, per Raḥava and Rav Pappa) suffices.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
How does the Gemara reconcile the incident of the mother of Yirmatya, who paid her daughter's weight in gold despite not specifying a metal in her vow?
A. She had actually specified gold and the mishna omitted it
B. The mishna is incomplete: a distinguished, wealthy person must pay according to their honor even without specifying
C. Gold is always the default when nothing is specified
D. The incident does not really concern a weight-vow
Correct: B — The Gemara answers that the mishna is missing text and really teaches that a person of stature pays 'according to their honor' — she was wealthy, so gold was expected of her even absent a specification.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
What is Rabbi Yosei's objection to Rabbi Yehuda's water-barrel method for weighing a forearm-vow?
A. Water cannot be used in a barrel for Temple purposes
B. Displacement measures volume, not weight, so donkey flesh, bones, and sinews cannot be precisely matched to the person's own flesh, bones, and sinews
C. Donkey flesh is forbidden to bring near the Temple treasury
D. The vow can only be fulfilled with money, never with physical measurement
Correct: B — Rabbi Yosei points out that the barrel measures volume by displacement, while weight is a different quantity, so matching donkey parts to human parts by volume cannot be precise — better to estimate the arm's weight directly.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
Why does Rav Yehuda rule that 'my full height' (as opposed to just 'my height') permits donating a thin, bendable rod?
A. Because 'full' always means the largest possible payment
B. Following Rabbi Akiva's principle that extra, unnecessary language in a statement comes to add something new
C. Because thin rods are cheaper and therefore the default
D. Because the word 'full' cancels the vow entirely
Correct: B — The Gemara traces this to Rabbi Akiva's method (seen regarding a house sale 'with its depth and height') of deriving added meaning from superfluous wording — since 'full' need not have been said, it adds the leniency of a thin rod.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
Why is the new mishna's law of 'the assessment (damim) of my forearm' described as more stringent than a Torah valuation (erekh) of a forearm?
A. Because damim vows are punished with lashes while arakhin vows are not
B. Because arakhin values a whole person only, so 'the valuation of my forearm' means nothing, whereas a damim vow lets the court directly assess the forearm's worth
C. Because damim vows apply only to gold, never silver
D. Because arakhin vows can never be paid by heirs
Correct: B — Torah valuations (arakhin) are fixed sums that apply only to an entire person, so a vow on a single limb 'says nothing' under arakhin. A vow of assessment (damim), by contrast, lets the court appraise that limb's value directly — hence the stringency.
