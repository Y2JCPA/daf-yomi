📖 Overview: The Power of the Tongue

Arakhin 15a–15b
כׇּל הַמְסַפֵּר לָשׁוֹן הָרָע כְּאִילּוּ כָּפַר בָּעִיקָּר
The scene: The daf finishes the mishna's treatment of a rapist and seducer, establishing through Abaye and Rava's readings of Deuteronomy 22:29 that the flat fifty-sela fine covers only the violation itself, leaving room for separately assessed payments for humiliation and degradation. A new mishna on a defamer, who falsely claims his bride was not a virgin, sets his fine at a flat one hundred sela — exactly double — from which the Gemara concludes that malicious speech is graver than a physical act, proven from the spies' evil report about the land, which alone sealed the decree that their generation would die in the wilderness. This launches an extended series of teachings, largely from Reish Lakish and Rabbi Yoḥanan in the name of Rabbi Yosei ben Zimra, on the severity of lashon hara: it is compared to denying God, it brings leprous marks, it kills at any distance like an arrow, it destroys three people at once, and the school of Rabbi Yishmael equates it with the three cardinal transgressions of idolatry, forbidden relations, and bloodshed combined.

💰 Why Doesn't the 50-Sela Fine Cover Everything?

Rav Zeira, Abaye, and Rava on humiliation and degradation
Rav Zeira
Had two men raped her — one atypically (leaving her technically intact) and one typically — would we really say both cases involve the same fifty-sela payment? That can't be the WHOLE story.
Abaye
By that logic, a healthy slave and one afflicted with boils should also differ in value — yet the ox's fine is a flat thirty sela regardless!
Abaye's answer
Rather: 'because he tormented her' (Deuteronomy 22:29) — the fifty sela covers only the ACT itself. Humiliation and degradation are separate, additional payments.
Rava
A parallel proof from 'lying with her': the fifty sela covers only the pleasure of the act, implying that something more — humiliation and degradation — is owed besides.

🕵️ Was It Really the Spies' Speech That Sealed the Decree?

Proving malicious speech, not accumulated sin, caused the harsh verdict
❓ How do we know the decree that the wilderness generation would die there was sealed specifically for the spies' malicious report, rather than an accumulation of sins?
↓
Possible alternative
Rav Hamnuna: God punishes only once a person's full measure of sin is reached — perhaps the report was merely the LAST straw
Reish Lakish's proof
The verse's extra word — 'they have tested Me THESE ten times' — pins the sealed decree to THIS specific sin, the report itself
↓
💡 Further objection: perhaps it was really their veiled BLASPHEMY ('stronger than Him') that was punished. Resolved: the verse says they died 'for bringing an evil report of the land' — pinning the punishment to the malicious speech itself

☠️ The Severity of Malicious Speech

A chain of teachings, mostly from Reish Lakish and Rabbi Yosei ben Zimra
1️⃣ The tongue is uniquely built for restraint — lying flat, hidden inside, walled by teeth and lips — yet still betrays; speaking lashon hara is like denying God entirely
↓
2️⃣ Malicious speech brings leprous marks (tzara'at) — linked by a shared root, 'I will destroy him' (אַצְמִית) and 'in perpetuity' (לַצְּמִיתֻת)
↓
3️⃣ It 'reaches the heavens' while the tongue 'walks the earth' — its harm has no distance limit, killing like an arrow, but from any range at all
↓
4️⃣ 'Third-party speech kills three': the one who speaks it, the one who accepts it, and the one it is spoken about (a teaching from the Sages of the West)

⚖️ Malicious Speech vs. the Three Cardinal Sins

The school of Rabbi Yishmael's striking equation

Lashon Hara

  • Described with the PLURAL 'great things' (גְּדוֹלוֹת)
  • The school of Rabbi Yishmael: equivalent to ALL THREE cardinal transgressions together, since none of the three could be singled out as lesser

The Three Cardinal Sins

  • Idolatry — called 'a great sin' (Exodus 32:31)
  • Forbidden relations — 'this great wickedness' (Joseph, Genesis 39:9)
  • Bloodshed — 'my sin is too great to bear' (Cain, Genesis 4:13)
The remedy: A Torah scholar should immerse himself in Torah study; an unlearned person should cultivate humility — both practices diminish the impulse to speak maliciously.
