QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to Abaye, what does the verse 'because he tormented her' (Deuteronomy 22:29) prove about the rapist or seducer's fifty-sela fine?
A. That the fine may be waived entirely under certain circumstances
B. That the fine covers only the act itself, implying separate additional payments exist for humiliation and degradation
C. That the fine applies only to seduction, not rape
D. That the fine must always equal one hundred sela
Correct: B — Abaye derives from this phrase that the fixed fifty-sela sum addresses only the torment of the act, leaving room for further, variable payments for humiliation and degradation.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to the new mishna, how does the fine for a defamer (motzi shem ra) compare to that of a rapist or seducer?
A. It is exactly half as much
B. It is exactly the same amount
C. It is exactly double: 100 sela versus 50 sela
D. There is no fixed fine for a defamer
Correct: C — The mishna sets the defamer's fine at 100 sela, precisely double the rapist/seducer's 50 sela, from which the Gemara concludes that speech is judged more severely than action.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to Reish Lakish, what proves that the harsh decree against the wilderness generation was sealed specifically because of the spies' malicious report, rather than an accumulation of unrelated sins?
A. A direct verse stating this explicitly with no ambiguity
B. The verse's extra word 'this' ('they have tested Me THESE ten times') pins the sealed decree to that specific sin
C. The fact that the spies were priests
D. There is no such proof; it remains a matter of speculation
Correct: B — Reish Lakish reads the extra word 'zeh' (this/these) in Numbers 14:22 as specifically identifying the spies' sin as the trigger for the sealed decree.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Rabbi Yochanan in the name of Rabbi Yosei ben Zimra, what physical affliction does one who speaks malicious speech bring upon himself?
A. Blindness
B. Leprous marks (tzara'at), derived from a shared root linking 'I will destroy him' to 'in perpetuity'
C. Deafness
D. Paralysis of the tongue
Correct: B — The Gemara links the verse 'whoever defames his neighbor in secret, I will destroy him' to the language used for tzara'at's permanent status, teaching that malicious speech brings leprous marks.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to the school of Rabbi Yishmael, to how many of the three cardinal transgressions (idolatry, forbidden relations, bloodshed) is malicious speech equated?
A. Only idolatry
B. Two of the three
C. All three together, since the verse uses the plural 'great things' and none of the three could be excluded as lesser
D. None — the comparison is only rhetorical
Correct: C — The school of Rabbi Yishmael reasons that since malicious speech is described with the plural 'great things' and each cardinal sin is equally 'great,' none can be excluded — so it is equated with all three combined.
