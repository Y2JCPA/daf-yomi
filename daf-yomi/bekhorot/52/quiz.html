Bekhorot 52 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
From which parent's property does a firstborn son take a double portion?
A. Both parents equally
B. Only the mother's property
C. Only the father's property
D. Neither — firstborns get no special share
Correct: C — The mishna states that the firstborn takes a double portion only from his father's property, deriving from 'the law of primogeniture is his' (Deuteronomy 21:17) that this law applies to a man, not a woman.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
Why can't the firstborn claim double the increase in the value of property that appreciated after his father's death?
A. Because appreciation is never inherited by anyone
B. Because the verse specifies 'all that he HAS' — the appreciation was not in the father's possession at death
C. Because only real estate can appreciate
D. Because the firstborn forfeits all rights after the father's death
Correct: B — The Gemara derives this from the phrase 'all that he has' (Deuteronomy 21:17): since the enhanced value did not exist in the father's possession at the time of his death, it falls outside the double portion.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to Rabbi Meir, why don't the firstborn's extra portion, a husband's inheritance from his wife, and a yavam's levirate share need to return to original owners in the Jubilee Year?
A. Because they are all considered sales
B. Because they are all considered inheritances (or in the firstborn's case, a Torah-called 'gift' treated like inheritance), not sales, and only sold land returns
C. Because the Jubilee Year law doesn't apply to any of them
D. Because they were never legally acquired
Correct: B — Rabbi Meir holds that only land acquired through sale must return in the Jubilee Year; these cases are each rooted in inheritance (or gift-like inheritance), which the Torah does not require to be returned.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
What does Rabbi Elazar (as clarified by Ravin's report) mean by saying the firstborn's portion 'returns' in the Jubilee Year?
A. The firstborn simply gets an equal share alongside his brothers again next cycle
B. The firstborn forfeits the extra portion entirely, for naught, when the Jubilee arrives
C. The land physically changes location
D. Nothing changes at all
Correct: B — Rav Ḥama cites the tradition that Rabbi Elazar's position means the firstborn's double portion 'returns for naught' — he genuinely loses that extra share when the Jubilee Year comes, according to this opinion.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
Why must a husband who inherited his wife's family graveyard deduct part of its monetary value when returning it to her family, according to the baraita cited?
A. Because he must pay a fine for delaying the return
B. Because he should deduct the cost of his wife's own burial there, since a husband is obligated to pay for his wife's burial
C. Because graveyards are always worth less than their sale price
D. Because the family owes him for maintenance costs
Correct: B — The baraita explains that since a husband bears the financial obligation to bury his wife, when he returns the family plot he first deducts the value of his wife's grave from what he owes.
