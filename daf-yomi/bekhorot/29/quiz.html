QUESTION 1 OF 5
Why was Ila permitted to charge his fee regardless of whether he ruled the firstborn blemished or unblemished?
A. Because the Sages wanted him to profit either way
B. To prevent suspicion that he would only get paid by declaring an animal blemished, which could create a financial incentive for false leniency
C. Because unblemished rulings required more effort
D. Because this was a requirement of Roman law at the time
Correct: B — If Ila were only paid when he ruled an animal blemished, people would suspect he declared animals blemished dishonestly just to collect his fee — paying him either way removes that incentive.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to Abaye, what distinguishes the mishna here (which forbids payment for red heifer water/ash rituals) from the mishna in Kiddushin (which permits betrothal using purification water's value)?
A. There is no real distinction; the two mishnayot directly contradict each other
B. The Kiddushin mishna concerns payment for fetching and filling the water, a legitimate service, while this mishna forbids payment for the actual sprinkling and sanctifying
C. The Kiddushin mishna applies only to priests
D. The distinction is based on the gender of the person being betrothed
Correct: B — Abaye resolves the apparent contradiction by explaining that payment for logistics (bringing and filling the water) is permitted, while payment for performing the actual sacred act of sprinkling or sanctifying is forbidden — the precise wording of each mishna supports this reading.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
How is an elderly examiner, judge, or witness compensated for his service, without technically receiving a forbidden 'wage'?
A. He receives nothing at all
B. He is transported by donkey and paid the rate of an idle laborer from his own specific trade
C. He is given a permanent exemption from all future service
D. He receives double the normal wage as compensation
Correct: B — The mishna and Gemara describe compensating real costs — transportation for the elderly, and pay calculated as what an idle laborer in his specific trade would accept — rather than paying a direct fee for the ruling or service itself.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
Why does the mishna forbid buying untanned hides from someone suspected of selling firstborn meat, but permit buying tanned hides?
A. Untanned hides are worth more money
B. If the hide were truly from a firstborn, the seller would not risk investing the effort to tan it, since the Sages could confiscate it as forbidden
C. Tanned hides are never subject to firstborn law
D. There is no real distinction between the two
Correct: B — The Gemara reasons that a seller with a genuinely forbidden firstborn hide wouldn't bother tanning it, since doing so risks greater loss if the Sages later discover and confiscate it — so a tanned hide is more likely to be legitimately non-sacred.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
What is the difference between Rabbi Yehuda's and Rabbi Shimon's positions on what may be purchased from someone suspected of selling teruma disguised as ordinary produce?
A. They hold identical positions with no practical difference
B. Rabbi Yehuda forbids purchasing even water and salt from him; Rabbi Shimon permits water and salt but forbids anything relevant to teruma or tithe law
C. Rabbi Yehuda permits everything; Rabbi Shimon forbids everything
D. The dispute concerns only the price, not what may be purchased
Correct: B — Rabbi Yehuda takes the stricter position, forbidding even items like water and salt that have no connection to teruma law; Rabbi Shimon permits those specific items since teruma and tithe obligations never apply to them, but forbids anything genuinely relevant to those laws, including items like fish innards mixed with oil.
