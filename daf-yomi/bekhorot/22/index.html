📖 Overview: Nullification in a Majority, From Blood to Brine

Bekhorot 22a–22b
מִשּׁוּם בִּיטּוּל בְּרוֹב נָגְעוּ בָּהּ
The scene: The daf resolves the open question from the end of 21b: the mass of congealed blood must be buried simply to publicize that the mother is now exempt from a future firstborn — implying it really is a full-fledged offspring. Rabbi Yoḥanan then explains why it still doesn't impart ritual impurity: it is nullified within the majority of the surrounding mass, the same principle he finds underlying a parallel ruling of Rabbi Shimon about a disintegrated fetus in an afterbirth. A technical digression defines the precise size — a 'skein' (פִּיקָּה) of thread — at which a fetus's head begins to impart impurity, differing between a woman and an animal. The Gemara then pivots to a wider exploration of nullification in a majority through the case of purchasing fish brine from an unreliable seller, and finally through the dispute over whether a se'a of impure teruma that fell into non-sacred produce can ever have its impurity 'revive.'

🩸 Why Bury Something That Isn't Impure?

Resolving the question left open at the end of 21b
❓ The mass of congealed blood must be buried, yet doesn't impart impurity by touch or carrying — so is it a real offspring or not?
↓
⚖️ Answer: it is buried simply to publicize that the mother is exempt from a future firstborn — meaning it IS a full-fledged offspring
↓
🔍 So why doesn't it impart impurity like a real fetus should?
↓
✅ Rabbi Yoḥanan: it is nullified within the majority of the surrounding congealed mass — the Sages 'touched upon' it only enough to exclude it from tumah by contact or carrying
Key concept: בִּיטּוּל בְּרוֹב — a minority substance loses its independent halakhic identity when submerged within a larger majority.

🧶 How Big Must a Fetus's Head Be to Impart Impurity?

The skein (פִּיקָּה) measures — three tannaitic opinions, then a final ruling
OpinionMeasure
Rabbi MeirA skein of warp threads (שְׁתִי)
Rabbi YehudaA skein of woof threads (עֵרֶב) — coarser and thicker
Rabbi Eliezer b. R' TzadokFrom when tefifiyot appear — folds like those seen when a mule crouches to urinate
Rabbi Yoḥanan's final ruling (via Ravin)A woman's fetus: the finer warp-thread skein; an animal's fetus: the coarser woof-thread skein
A third, unrelated skeinThe larger sack-makers' skein — used to measure a clod of earth from a doubtful gravesite, not fetal size
Transmission chain: Rav Huna knew of two skeins but couldn't identify them; Rav Dimi added a third in Rabbi Yoḥanan's name; only when Ravin arrived was the ruling finally clarified.

👥 Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov and Rabbi Shimon 'Said the Same Thing'

Rabbi Yoḥanan finds one underlying principle behind two very different rulings

Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov

  • A mass of congealed blood from a large animal is a real offspring — hence buried, and the mother is exempt from a future firstborn
  • Yet it does not impart impurity, since it is nullified within the majority of the mass

Rabbi Shimon

  • When a woman's afterbirth is found in a house, the house becomes impure — since there is no afterbirth without an offspring inside it
  • But if the fetus disintegrated before emerging, the house stays pure — the disintegrated fetus is nullified within the majority afterbirth
Shared principle: In both cases, a real fetal presence is halakhically real, yet vanishes from legal significance once submerged in the majority of surrounding, non-fetal material.

🐟 Purifying Fish Brine From an Unreliable Seller

Reish Lakish, in the name of Rabbi Yehuda Nesia, applies bittul berov
R' Lakish
One who buys brine from an am ha'aretz may bring it into contact with a ritual bath's water, and it becomes pure.
Reasoning
מִמָּה נַפְשָׁךְ — whichever way you look at it: if water is the majority, contact purifies it; if brine is the majority, brine itself can't contract impurity anyway, and the minority water is nullified within it.
R' Yirmeya
This works only for dipping bread. For cooking in a pot with other water, it does not — the once-nullified water 'finds its own kind' and revives its impurity.
Abaye
Can nullified impurity really return and revive?
Rav Dimi
Yes — and there is a proof from the teruma mishna, about a se'a of impure teruma that fell into one hundred se'a of pure produce.
Setting up the next question: This exchange launches the Gemara into the teruma dispute over whether nullified impurity can ever be reactivated.

🌾 Does Nullified Impurity Ever Revive?

A se'a of impure teruma falls into one hundred se'a of pure, non-sacred produce
1️⃣ Case: the impure teruma is now a tiny minority within a huge quantity of ordinary produce
↓
2️⃣ Rabbi Eliezer: remove one se'a from the mixture and let it rot as impure teruma — 'the se'a that fell is the se'a that arose,' as if the impurity never truly vanished
↓
3️⃣ The Rabbis: let the teruma be fully nullified — all 101 se'a become non-sacred and may be eaten
↓
4️⃣ But even the Rabbis add a caution: keep the mixture away from water, since contact with water could make it susceptible to new impurity
↓
5️⃣ Workarounds: eat it dry or roasted, knead it with fruit juice instead of water, or divide it into small batches so no single batch holds an egg-bulk of the original teruma
Connection to Bekhorot 22: This debate over whether nullification is permanent or reversible is the same conceptual question raised by the mass of congealed blood and the fish brine — is bittul a true erasure, or just a temporary suppression?
