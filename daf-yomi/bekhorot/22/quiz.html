Bekhorot 22 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
Why must the mass of congealed blood be buried, according to the Gemara's resolution at the start of the daf?
A. Because it definitely imparts ritual impurity
B. To publicize that the mother is now exempt from having a future offspring counted a firstborn
C. Because it is halakhically identical to an ordinary firstborn animal
D. Because burial is required for all animal remains
Correct: B — The Gemara explains that burial serves to publicize the mother's new exemption from firstborn status for future offspring — implying that the mass really is treated as a full-fledged offspring.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to Rabbi Yoḥanan, why doesn't the mass of congealed blood impart ritual impurity through contact or carrying, if it is a real offspring?
A. Because congealed blood is never impure
B. Because it is nullified within the majority of the surrounding mass
C. Because only human fetuses impart this type of impurity
D. Because the mother's status protects it
Correct: B — Rabbi Yoḥanan explains that although the offspring is real, it is nullified (בִּיטּוּל בְּרוֹב) within the larger congealed mass, so it does not impart impurity by touch or carrying.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
What did Rabbi Yoḥanan mean when he said Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov and Rabbi Shimon 'said the same thing'?
A. They disagreed about everything except the definition of a firstborn
B. Both rulings rest on the principle that a real fetal presence is nullified when submerged within a majority of non-fetal material
C. Both rabbis discussed only human pregnancies
D. They both ruled that fetuses never impart impurity under any circumstances
Correct: B — Rabbi Yoḥanan connects Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov's ruling on the congealed mass to Rabbi Shimon's ruling on a disintegrated fetus in an afterbirth — both apply the same principle of nullification in a majority.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Reish Lakish, why is brine purchased from an unreliable seller (am ha'aretz) pure after contact with a ritual bath, regardless of the mixture's composition?
A. Brine can never become impure under any circumstances
B. If water is the majority, contact purifies it; if brine is the majority, brine itself cannot contract impurity and the minority water is nullified
C. The seller's unreliability is irrelevant to purity law
D. Ritual baths only purify solid objects, not liquids
Correct: B — Reish Lakish's 'מִמָּה נַפְשָׁךְ' argument covers both possibilities: whichever substance is the majority, the result is purity — either through direct purification of the water, or through nullification of the minority water within the brine.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
What is the practical difference between Rabbi Eliezer's and the Rabbis' positions on a se'a of impure teruma that fell into one hundred se'a of pure, non-sacred produce?
A. Rabbi Eliezer says the whole mixture is permanently forbidden; the Rabbis say it is always fully permitted with no restrictions
B. Rabbi Eliezer says one se'a must be removed and left to rot as impure; the Rabbis say the teruma is nullified but caution against contact with water
C. There is no practical difference between their positions
D. Rabbi Eliezer permits eating the mixture freely, while the Rabbis forbid it entirely
Correct: B — Rabbi Eliezer holds that a se'a must be removed and treated as impure teruma, while the Rabbis hold the teruma is nullified and the whole mixture becomes non-sacred — though they still caution against exposing it to water, which could make it newly susceptible to impurity.
