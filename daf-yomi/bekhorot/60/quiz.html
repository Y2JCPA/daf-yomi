QUESTION 1 OF 5
How does Rav Huna bar Seḥora resolve the apparent contradiction between two baraitot about nineteen lambs?
A. He says one baraita is simply incorrect
B. He explains the second baraita involves a pen with two openings, where nine exit each side and the last remaining lamb is 'fit' to exit either way, creating total exemption for all nineteen
C. He claims lambs and sheep are treated differently
D. He says the contradiction cannot be resolved
Correct: B — Rav Huna bar Seḥora interprets the second baraita as describing a two-gated pen where the structure of the exits makes all nineteen animals part of a 'fit to reach ten' count on each side, explaining the total exemption rather than partial combination.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
In the mishna's scenario where the owner mistakenly calls the 9th 'tenth,' the 10th 'ninth,' and the 11th 'tenth,' what is the halakhic status of the 11th animal according to Rabbi Meir?
A. It has no sanctity at all
B. It is sacrificed as a peace offering
C. It must be left to die
D. It becomes the new animal tithe, replacing the 10th
Correct: B — Rabbi Meir rules that since the true tenth's name was 'removed' by mistakenly calling it 'ninth,' the eleventh (mistakenly called 'tenth') picks up sanctity and is sacrificed as a peace offering.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
What is Rabbi Yehuda's objection to sacrificing the eleventh animal as a peace offering in this scenario?
A. He believes the eleventh animal has no value
B. He questions whether a substitute (the type of status the eleventh would carry) can itself generate another substitute
C. He says only priests may raise this objection
D. He agrees completely with Rabbi Meir
Correct: B — Rabbi Yehuda challenges whether a 'substitute of a substitute' is even a valid halakhic category — since the tenth (or its substitute) already occupies that role, can the eleventh become yet another layer of substitution?
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Rav Mari's understanding of Rabbi Yoḥanan's rule, if someone counts animals in pairs (calling the first pair 'one,' second pair 'two,' etc.), which animal is sanctified as the tithe?
A. The actual tenth animal by physical count, regardless of naming
B. Whichever pair the owner explicitly designates as 'ten' — i.e., animals 19 and 20
C. The first animal that exits
D. None of the animals are sanctified in this scenario
Correct: B — Rav Mari holds that sanctity follows the owner's own designated count — so if he calls the fifth pair 'ten,' that pair (the 19th and 20th animals) receives the tithe sanctity, not necessarily the physically tenth animal.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to Rav Kahana, why does the normal rule requiring the 'name of the tenth' to be removed before an eleventh animal can gain sanctity NOT apply when two animals emerge from the pen simultaneously?
A. Because simultaneous emergence never has any halakhic significance
B. That rule applies only when animals emerge one after another in sequence; when two emerge together and are both called 'tenth,' both become sanctified together as a special case
C. Because animals that emerge together are always non-sacred
D. Because the Torah only discusses animals emerging one at a time
Correct: B — Rav Kahana explains to Rav Ashi that the requirement to 'remove the name of the tenth' before an eleventh can gain sanctity applies specifically to sequential emergence; when two animals emerge simultaneously and are named together, they are both sanctified as an exception to that rule.
