📖 Overview: The Mechanics of Miscounting

Bekhorot 60a–60b
יָצְאוּ שְׁנַיִם כְּאַחַת — מוֹנֶה אוֹתָם שְׁנַיִם שְׁנַיִם
The scene: The daf opens by resolving an apparent contradiction between two baraitot about nineteen lambs, through Rav Huna bar Seḥora's clever reading of a two-gated pen — a solution Rava himself praises. A new mishna then details what happens when two animals emerge simultaneously (counted as a pair) or when the owner misnames animals near the tenth position: calling the ninth 'tenth,' the tenth 'ninth,' and the eleventh 'tenth' sanctifies all three with different statuses, a ruling Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yehuda debate in terms of substitution law. The Gemara explores whether Rabbi Yoḥanan's rule — that the tenth is sanctified 'according to his number' when counting by pairs or hundreds — refers to the owner's own count or the animals' actual count, and closes by examining what happens when two animals emerge at the exact same moment, testing whether 'removing the name of the tenth' is truly required in every case.

🔢 Resolving the Nineteen-Lambs Puzzle

Two baraitot seem to contradict — Rav Huna bar Seḥora's answer
❓ One baraita: 19 lambs → remainder combines forward. Another: 19 lambs → remainder is simply exempt. Contradiction!
↓
🚪 Rav Huna bar Seḥora: the second baraita involves a pen with TWO openings — 9 exit each side, and the 19th (remaining) animal is fit to exit either way
↓
🔟 So all 19 (not just 10) are part of one 'fit to reach ten' count on EACH side — hence total exemption, not partial combination
↓
🎉 Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak: 'Blessed is the mother of Rav Huna bar Seḥora' — he matched Rava's own halakha exactly, unprompted

😵 Three Animals, Three Different Statuses

The mishna's core scenario of consecutive misnaming
AnimalWhat owner called itResulting status
9th'Tenth' (mistake)Eaten only once blemished — not fully sacred
10th'Ninth' (mistake)Full animal-tithe status — sanctified regardless of the naming error
11th'Tenth' (mistake)Sacrificed as a peace offering, per Rabbi Meir; disputed by Rabbi Yehuda
The governing principle: As long as the name 'tenth' is eventually removed from the true tenth animal (by mistakenly calling it 'ninth'), the eleventh can pick up the misapplied 'tenth' designation and become sanctified too.

🔁 Substitute of a Substitute?

Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yehuda on the eleventh animal's status
R' Meir
The eleventh, mistakenly called 'tenth,' is sacrificed as a peace offering — like an ordinary substitute for tithe.
R' Yehuda
But can a substitute (the tenth already has that role) itself generate ANOTHER substitute? That shouldn't be possible!
R' Meir's camp responds
If the eleventh were truly a 'substitute of a substitute,' it wouldn't be sacrificed at all — the fact that it IS sacrificed proves it's not viewed that way.
Bottom line
The mishna's principle stands: 'as long as the name of the tenth is not removed, the eleventh cannot become sacred' — R' Yehuda's substitute framing doesn't override this.

🔁 Whose Count Is It — His, or the Animals'?

Rabbi Yoḥanan's rule tested against the mishna's own language

Rav Mari: 'His Own Count'

  • If counting by pairs, the pair he CALLS 'ten' is sanctified — regardless of actual animal number
  • Fits the mishna: calling the 10th 'ninth' and 11th 'tenth' creates flawed status, since the DESIGNATION shifted

Rav Kahana: 'Animal Count'

  • The animal that is numerically the tenth is sanctified, regardless of what the owner calls it
  • Reconciled by R' Yoḥanan: this applies only when animals emerge naturally one by one, not when the owner planned to count in pairs from the start

⚖️ Two Emerge Together — Does 'Removing the Name' Still Apply?

Rava's rulings on simultaneous emergence
1️⃣ Two animals emerge together AS THE NINTH, both called 'ninth': the true tenth (whichever one it is) is sacred; the actual ninth is not — but since they're mixed up, BOTH graze until blemished
↓
2️⃣ Two emerge together AS THE TENTH, both called 'tenth': tenth and eleventh are intermingled — since he called them BOTH 'tenth' simultaneously
↓
3️⃣ Rav Ashi's challenge to Rav Kahana: doesn't this violate the rule that the true tenth's name must be 'removed' first? Rav Kahana: that rule applies only when they emerge ONE AFTER THE OTHER, not simultaneously
↓
4️⃣ When two emerge as one, BOTH become sanctified together — a special case outside the general naming-removal principle
