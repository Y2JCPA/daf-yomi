5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to the Gemara's resolution of two contradictory baraitot, when is a cataract (dak) considered a blemish?
A. Sunken cataracts are always a blemish regardless of color
B. A sunken BLACK cataract is a blemish and a floating one is not; a sunken WHITE cataract is not a blemish and a floating one is
C. Floating cataracts are never a blemish
D. Color makes no difference at all
Correct: B — The two baraitot are reconciled by color: for a black cataract, sunken is a blemish and floating is not; for a white cataract, the rule is reversed.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
Why was Rabbi Shimon ben Yosei Lakonya permitted to rule that a snail-shaped growth was a blemish, even though it applied to his own firstborn?
A. Because a person may always rule on his own animal's blemishes
B. Because he had taught this halakha to his students before the matter became personally relevant, satisfying Rav Huna's condition for self-interested rulings
C. Because his students overruled him instead
D. Because snail-shaped growths are never disputed
Correct: B — Rav Huna's principle allows a scholar's ruling to stand even when it benefits him, provided he stated the halakha before the case arose — which Rabbi Shimon ben Yosei Lakonya had already done.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to Rabbi Yosei, why does a black thread crossing into the white of the eye NOT count as a tevallul blemish?
A. Because tevallul only applies to eyelid damage
B. Because the white of the eye is called 'the fat of the eye' rather than 'the eye' itself, and 'there are no blemishes in the white'
C. Because Rabbi Meir already ruled it is not a blemish
D. Because black threads are always invisible
Correct: B — Rabbi Yosei derives from Psalms 73:7 that the white portion is termed 'fat,' not 'eye,' so Leviticus 21:20's phrase 'tevallul in his eye' excludes blemishes located in the white.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to the mishna (following Rabbi Yehuda), how long must pale spots (chavarvar) persist to count as a permanent, disqualifying blemish?
A. 40 days
B. 80 days
C. One year
D. Three days
Correct: B — The mishna follows Rabbi Yehuda's opinion that pale spots must persist for 80 days, as opposed to Rabbi Meir's baraita, which requires only 40 days.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
In the fodder test for 'constant tears,' why does the order in which the animal is fed moist and dry fodder matter?
A. It doesn't matter at all
B. The animal must be fed moist fodder first and dry fodder afterward without improvement, to prove the tearing is a genuine permanent blemish rather than a temporary condition
C. Dry fodder must always be fed first
D. The animal must be fed only one type of fodder
Correct: B — The mishna specifies that only feeding moist fodder followed by dry fodder — with the eyes remaining unhealed — proves the condition is a true, permanent blemish.
