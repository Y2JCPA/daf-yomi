QUESTION 1 OF 5
What is the practical difference between Rabbi Yehuda's and Rabbi Yosei b'Rabbi Yehuda's views on a human tumtum's levirate marriage status?
A. There is no practical difference
B. Rabbi Yehuda treats him as a certain eunuch (so his ḥalitza is meaningless), while Rabbi Yosei treats him as uncertain (so his ḥalitza is still required, just in case)
C. Both agree completely on all cases
D. Only Rabbi Yosei allows levirate marriage at all
Correct: B — Since Rabbi Yehuda considers the tumtum a definite eunuch, his ḥalitza has no legal effect; Rabbi Yosei's view that the tumtum might be a valid male means his ḥalitza is still necessary to properly release the widow.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
How does the Gemara derive that animal blemishes like a wart (yabelet) also disqualify a priest, even though the Torah doesn't explicitly write 'wart' regarding a person?
A. By simple logical inference with no verse needed
B. Through a verbal analogy (gezeira shava) using the shared terms 'garav' and 'yalefet,' which appear in both the human and animal blemish lists
C. It is not derived at all; the mishna's ruling is rejected
D. By counting the total number of blemishes in each list
Correct: B — The Gemara identifies the words garav and yalefet as appearing in both contexts, establishing a verbal analogy that transfers each list's unique blemishes to the other.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to Rava, why couldn't the Torah's rule that blemishes disqualify a firstborn be derived from the parallel rules for a person and for sacrificial animals combined?
A. Because a firstborn cannot have blemishes at all
B. Because both a person and sacrificial animals have a stringency a firstborn lacks — a person is obligated in many mitzvot, and other offerings encompass many types with voluntary consecration, while a firstborn's sanctity is singular and automatic from birth
C. Because firstborns are never actually sacrificed
D. Because the three cases are entirely unrelated
Correct: B — Rava shows that each of the three categories (person, sacrifices, firstborn) has some unique stringency not shared by the other two, so none can be fully derived from the others, requiring all three verses.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Rava's ruling on baldness (kere'ach), when is a priest disqualified from Temple service?
A. Whenever he has any hair loss anywhere
B. Specifically when he lacks a row of hair at the BACK of his head (even if he has hair in front)
C. Only if he is completely bald all over
D. Baldness never disqualifies a priest
Correct: B — Rava clarifies that the disqualifying condition is lacking hair specifically at the back of the head; having hair only in the back but not the front is actually considered fit, as is typical for older men.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to the mishna and the Gemara's clarification, what does Rabbi Ḥanina ben Antigonus mean by a gibben having 'two backs and two spines'?
A. A person literally born with two separate spines
B. A person whose spine is crooked, making it appear as though there are two spines, though he can survive normally
C. A fictional condition that doesn't exist
D. Someone with an extra vertebra only
Correct: B — Rav clarified to his grandson that this refers to a crooked spine that merely appears doubled, not a literal second spine, which would be incompatible with survival.
