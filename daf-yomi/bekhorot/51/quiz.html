Bekhorot 51 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
In the story of Ḥanan the wicked, why did he end up paying a full dinar rather than just half a dinar?
A. He struck the victim twice, incurring two half-dinar fines
B. The fine doubled automatically for repeat offenders
C. He was fined extra for using a damaged coin
D. Rav Huna increased the fine as punishment
Correct: A — Ḥanan first offered a damaged dinar which the victim refused, then struck him a second time (incurring a second half-dinar fine), after which he paid the full dinar covering both strikes.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to the mishna, which of the following CANNOT be used to redeem a firstborn son?
A. Silver coins
B. Gold jewelry
C. Land
D. Wine of significant value
Correct: C — The mishna explicitly excludes redemption via slaves, promissory notes, land, and consecrated items — only movable property with genuine intrinsic value qualifies.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
What is the key difference between Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi's ruling and that of the Rabbis (first tanna) regarding redemption?
A. They fully agree on everything
B. Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi excludes only promissory notes, while the Rabbis also exclude slaves and land
C. Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi excludes everything the Rabbis permit
D. They disagree only about consecrated items
Correct: B — Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi, reading the verses through amplification and restriction, permits redemption with anything except promissory notes, while the Rabbis, reading through generalization and detail, also exclude land and slaves.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Ulla, if a son's redemption should technically work by Torah law once the father pays the promissory note, why does the mishna say the son is NOT redeemed?
A. Because Ulla disagrees with the Torah law entirely
B. It's a rabbinic decree, lest people mistakenly think a note can itself effect redemption
C. Because promissory notes are inherently invalid documents
D. Because the priest must always refuse notes
Correct: B — Ulla explains that by Torah law, once the money is actually paid the son would be redeemed; the Sages decreed otherwise so people would not come to think a mere note (without actual payment) could redeem a son.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
If a father designates five sela for his son's redemption and then loses the money before giving it to the priest, what is he obligated to do?
A. Nothing — the mitzva was already fulfilled by designation
B. He must pay again, as designation alone doesn't complete the redemption
C. The son becomes permanently unredeemable
D. The priest must absorb the loss
Correct: B — The mishna rules that the father bears financial responsibility for the loss and must pay again — mere designation of the money does not fulfill the mitzva until it actually reaches the priest.
