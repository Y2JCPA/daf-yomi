📖 Overview: The Mechanics of Redemption

Bekhorot 11a–11b
עַיִן יָפָה בְּסֶלַע, עַיִן רָעָה בְּשֶׁקֶל, בֵּינוֹנִית בְּרִגְיָא
The scene: The daf works through the practical mechanics of redeeming a firstborn donkey: how little the redeeming lamb may be worth, and what to do when one has no lamb at all. Reish Lakish permits redeeming with cash equal to the donkey's value, prompting a search for whose opinion this follows and why it holds even for the stricter view. The Gemara then asks whether redeeming a neighbor's donkey without being asked is even valid, works through a multi-generation inheritance case involving priests and Israelites, and closes by pinpointing the exact moment a designated lamb legally becomes the priest's.

💰 How Much Must the Redeeming Lamb Be Worth?

The letter of the law, sage guidance, and practical halakha
SourceRuling
Rav Yosef / Rava (letter of the law)Any value works — even a lean lamb worth a single ma'ah (a small fraction of a shekel)
Rabbi Tarfon's guidance (by disposition)Generous: a sela (4 dinars); miserly: a shekel (2 dinars); average: a ragya (3 dinars)
Rava's practical rulingOne who comes to ask a sage is told to give a ragya — but one who redeems on his own with less has still fulfilled the mitzva
No real contradiction: The strict letter of the law permits any value; someone who consults a sage first is simply held to the recommended middle standard.

🥬 Redeeming With Money Instead of a Lamb

What if you have a firstborn donkey but no lamb?
Reish Lakish
One who has a firstborn donkey but no lamb may redeem it with money equal to its value.
Objection
Whose view is this? Not Rabbi Yehuda — he insists the Torah is particular that redemption be specifically with a lamb! It must be Rabbi Shimon.
Ravina
But the halakha follows Rabbi Yehuda, and an unattributed mishna already follows him too — so how can Reish Lakish rule like Rabbi Shimon?
Resolution
Even Rabbi Yehuda agrees: a firstborn donkey shouldn't be more stringent than ordinary consecrated property, redeemable with any valuables. 'With a lamb' was stated to be lenient — a lamb costs less than the donkey — not to restrict the medium.
In practice: Rav Naḥman, son of Rav Yosef, redeemed a firstborn donkey with boiled vegetables worth its full value.

🐴 Redeeming Someone Else's Firstborn Donkey

Is it valid, and who owns the donkey afterward?
❓ If I redeem my neighbor's firstborn donkey without being asked, is the redemption valid? And whose donkey is it afterward?
↓
✅ Rav Huna: the redemption is valid — but a dilemma remains about ownership afterward
↓
According to R' Shimon
No dilemma — since benefit was already permitted beforehand, the donkey was always the original owner's property
According to R' Yehuda
Real dilemma — like redeeming consecrated property (redeemer acquires it), or does the owner keep rights since a lamb costs less than full value?
↓
✅ Resolved from theft law: a thief who steals it pays the ORIGINAL OWNER double — proving he retains rights, unlike ownerless consecrated property (no double payment for stealing from the Temple)

👪 Three Generations, Ten Donkeys

Inherited uncertainty across a priest-Israelite lineage
1️⃣ An Israelite has ten donkeys of uncertain firstborn status. He designates ten lambs and keeps them all — the priest can't prove any specific one is owed
↓
2️⃣ Trickier case: ten certainly firstborn donkeys, inherited from a priest grandfather — who himself inherited them, still unredeemed, from HIS Israelite grandfather
↓
3️⃣ Ruling: the Israelite grandson designates ten lambs and keeps them all — treated as if his priest grandfather had already separated and acquired the lambs himself
↓
4️⃣ A parallel ruling applies to inherited untithed produce passed the same priest-then-Israelite route — both cases had to be stated separately, since each could otherwise be explained away

☠️ When Does the Priest Acquire the Redeeming Lamb?

Pinpointing the exact legal moment
❓ The mishna: 'if the lamb dies, one may derive benefit from it.' At what point does the lamb become the priest's?
↓
🤔 Obvious cases dismissed: if it died already in the priest's house, of course he may benefit from his own property
↓
✅ Real novelty: from the very moment of designation by the owner — even before it physically reaches the priest's hand — the lamb is already legally his
