QUESTION 1 OF 5
How does Shmuel's opinion explain the baraita where five sheep in one village and five in another (32 mil apart) require an additional sheep in the middle village to combine?
A. It refutes Shmuel entirely
B. The baraita really means nine and nine sheep, with one in the middle bridging each group to ten
C. It only applies to goats, not sheep
D. The baraita is not about animal tithe at all
Correct: B — Shmuel reinterprets the baraita as involving nine sheep on each side, so the single sheep in the middle can combine with either group of nine to reach the required ten — consistent with his 'even one' rule.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to Rabbi Ḥiyya bar Abba citing Rabbi Yoḥanan, why does the Jordan River specifically (rather than any water barrier) divide flocks for animal tithe?
A. Because it is simply too wide to cross
B. Because a verse (Joshua 18:20) renders the Jordan a formal border in its own right, not because of mere physical difficulty
C. Because fish live in it
D. Because it is the only river mentioned in the Torah
Correct: B — The Gemara concludes that Rabbi Meir's ruling rests on the Jordan being explicitly called a border in Joshua 18:20, making it a formal halakhic boundary — distinguishing it from Rabbi Ami's earlier physical-obstacle reasoning, which would apply to any uncrossable river.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to Rabbi Ami, what happens to Rabbi Meir's rule that the Jordan divides flocks if there is a bridge across the river?
A. The rule still applies fully
B. The bridge reunites the two banks, so the flocks combine
C. Bridges are irrelevant to this halakha
D. A bridge makes the separation even stronger
Correct: B — Rabbi Ami teaches that Rabbi Meir's ruling applies only where there is no bridge; where a bridge exists, it functionally reunites the two sides, allowing the flocks to combine for tithing.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Rabbi Yehuda ben Beteira's view (cited in the dispute about whether the Jordan is part of the land of Canaan), what is the implication of the phrase 'when you pass over the Jordan into the land'?
A. The Jordan River itself is part of the land of Canaan
B. The Jordan River is NOT part of the land of Canaan, since one 'passes over' it to reach the land
C. The verse has no bearing on the Jordan's status
D. The Jordan is holier than the land itself
Correct: B — Rabbi Yehuda ben Beteira reads 'into the land' as indicating that crossing the Jordan gets you INTO the land of Canaan, implying the river itself stands outside that category.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to the new mishna at the end of the daf, is a person obligated to separate animal tithe from an animal they purchased or received as a gift?
A. Yes, exactly as with animals born into their own herd
B. No — such a person is exempt from animal tithe on that animal
C. Only if it was a gift, not a purchase
D. Only outside Eretz Yisrael
Correct: B — The mishna states plainly that one who purchases an animal or receives one as a gift is exempt from separating animal tithe on it — the obligation applies only to animals born into one's own flock.
