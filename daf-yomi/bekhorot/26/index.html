📖 Overview: Reconciling Sources, Then a New Chapter

Bekhorot 26a–26b
עַד כַּמָּה יִשְׂרָאֵל חַיָּיבִין לִיטַּפֵּל בִּבְכוֹר?
The scene: The daf completes the sugya on shed and plucked firstborn wool by having Rava reconcile three different tannaitic readings of exactly when Akavya ben Mahalalel and the Rabbis disagree — over a slaughtered animal, a naturally deceased one, or both. A parallel dispute over what counts as wool 'not part of the fleece' follows, before the chapter closes. A new mishna then opens fresh territory: how many days must an Israelite personally tend a firstborn animal before handing it to a priest, with the Gemara tracing that requirement back to a dense verse also used to derive the redemption age of a firstborn son. The daf ends with a related concern — priests, Levites, and the poor may not be paid for ordinary labor with teruma or tithes, a practice serious enough to invoke a biblical death penalty, even though the Sages stopped short of imposing an additional penalty of their own.

🧶 Three Ways to Read the Wool Dispute

Rava unifies the tanna kama, Rabbi Yehuda, and Rabbi Yosei versions of the same baraita

Tanna Kama

  • Akavya and the Rabbis disagree in BOTH cases — a slaughtered animal and one that died naturally
  • The baraita mentions 'died' specifically to show how far Akavya's leniency extends

Rabbi Yehuda

  • If the animal died, everyone agrees the wool is forbidden
  • They disagree only in the case of a slaughtered animal

Rabbi Yosei — the accepted halakha

  • If the animal was slaughtered, everyone agrees the wool is permitted
  • They disagree only in the case of a naturally deceased animal
  • Rav Naḥman rules the halakha follows this version, based on the parallel mishna in tractate Eduyyot
Common thread: All three agree slaughter and natural death are treated differently — they only disagree about which case is actually disputed.

🐑 Wool Dangling From a Firstborn: Two Definitions

What exactly counts as wool that 'does not appear to be part of the fleece'?
AuthorityDefinition
Reish Lakish (via Rabbi Elazar)Any strand whose root is turned outward, facing away from the body
Rav Natan bar OshayaAny strand not compressed together with the rest of the fleece — one that sticks out when the fleece is pressed down
Why Reish Lakish differs (Rabbi Ile'a)No fleece is ever entirely free of loose, dangling hairs — Rav Natan's stricter standard would make the lenient ruling meaningless in practice
Practical effect: Reish Lakish's more workable definition ensures the mishna's leniency for dangling-but-attached wool actually applies in real cases.

New mishna, new chapter
❓ How long must an Israelite personally tend a firstborn before giving it to the priest?
↓
Small animal
30 days (Rabbi Yosei: 3 months, due to the extra effort of raising it)
Large animal
50 days
↓
🚫 If the priest demands it early, the owner may not give it to him — this looks like paying the priest for his labor with a sacred gift
↓
Blemished firstborn
If the priest wants it early to EAT it — permitted
Unblemished, Temple standing
If the priest wants it early to SACRIFICE it — permitted
Underlying concern: Handing over the animal early, without a legitimate reason, would look like an improper trade of labor for sacred goods.

📜 Deriving Thirty and Fifty Days From One Verse

Exodus 22:28–29 links firstborn sons and firstborn animals
1️⃣ The verse juxtaposes 'you shall give Me your firstborn sons' (redeemed at 30 days) with 'so shall you do with your oxen and your sheep'
↓
2️⃣ Rav Kahana's first attempt: match each animal to its nearby phrase in the verse — but the Gemara challenges why the correspondence should run one way and not the other
↓
3️⃣ That attempt is set aside as inconclusive
↓
4️⃣ Rava's alternative: the extra word 'תַּעֲשֶׂה' (you shall do) teaches an ADDITIONAL act of care specifically for oxen — both sheep and oxen start from the son's 30-day baseline, but oxen require more
↓
5️⃣ Why not double to 60 days, then? The Torah handed the precise figure to the Sages to determine — they set it at 50
Structure: A single verse anchors both time periods, but only the Sages' own judgment fixes the exact additional number of days.

💰 Paying Temple Workers With Sacred Produce

Why the priest can't simply take the firstborn early
PointDetail
Rav Sheshet's reasoningA priest who takes the firstborn early looks like one who 'helps at the threshing floor' just to be given teruma — an improper labor-for-sacred-goods trade
The parallel baraitaPriests, Levites, and the poor who help at a shepherd's home, threshing floor, or slaughterhouse may not be paid with teruma or tithe as wages
Consequence if violatedThe produce is considered profaned; Numbers 18:32 even threatens death for desecrating sacred items in this way
Why no added rabbinic penaltyThe Sages feared people would then mistakenly separate tithes again from produce already validly tithed, over-tithing exempt produce
Bigger picture: The same concern — sacred status used as informal payment for labor — underlies both the firstborn-timing rule and the teruma/tithe wages prohibition.
