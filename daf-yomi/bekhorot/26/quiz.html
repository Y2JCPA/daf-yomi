Bekhorot 26 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to the version of the baraita that Rav Naḥman rules is halakha (Rabbi Yosei's version), in which case do Akavya ben Mahalalel and the Rabbis actually disagree?
A. Only when the animal was slaughtered
B. Only when the animal died naturally, not slaughtered
C. In both cases equally
D. They never actually disagree
Correct: B — According to Rabbi Yosei's version, which Rav Naḥman rules as halakha, everyone agrees the wool is permitted if the animal was slaughtered; Akavya and the Rabbis disagree only in the case of an animal that died by other means.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to Rabbi Ile'a, why does Reish Lakish reject Rav Natan bar Oshaya's stricter definition of wool 'not part of the fleece'?
A. Because Rav Natan's definition contradicts a verse
B. Because no fleece is ever entirely free of loose, dangling hairs, so that stricter standard would make the mishna's leniency meaningless
C. Because Reish Lakish disagreed with Rav Natan on unrelated matters
D. Because the stricter definition only applies to blemished animals
Correct: B — Rabbi Ile'a explains that if wool sticking out when pressed were always forbidden, since some dangling hairs are unavoidable in any fleece, the mishna's leniency would never actually apply — so Reish Lakish adopts a more workable definition.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to the new mishna, how long must an owner tend a small-animal firstborn before giving it to the priest, and what is one exception allowing earlier transfer?
A. 15 days; no exceptions exist
B. 30 days; the priest may take it earlier if the firstborn is blemished and he wants to eat it
C. 60 days; the priest may never take it earlier
D. 30 days; an exception only applies to large animals
Correct: B — The mishna sets 30 days for a small animal (Rabbi Yosei says 3 months). One clear exception: if the firstborn is blemished and the priest wants to eat it, the owner may give it to him earlier.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Rava, what does the extra word 'תַּעֲשֶׂה' (you shall do) in Exodus 22:29 teach about a firstborn ox?
A. That oxen are entirely exempt from the firstborn mitzva
B. That an additional period of care, beyond the 30-day baseline shared with a firstborn son, is required specifically for oxen
C. That oxen must be tended for exactly 30 days like sheep
D. That oxen may be given to the priest immediately
Correct: B — Rava explains that the extra word 'you shall do' adds an additional act of caretaking specifically required for oxen, beyond the 30-day baseline derived from the redemption of a firstborn son — with the exact additional amount (bringing the total to 50 days) left to the Sages to determine.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to Rav Sheshet, why may a priest not take a firstborn animal from its owner before the required tending period has ended, even if the owner is willing?
A. Because the animal would not yet be old enough to be kosher
B. Because it would look like the priest is being paid for labor (like helping at a threshing floor) with a sacred gift
C. Because the Torah explicitly forbids any early transfer under all circumstances
D. Because the priest is not yet permitted to own animals
Correct: B — Rav Sheshet explains that a priest collecting the firstborn early would appear similar to a priest who 'assists at the threshing floor' specifically to receive teruma — an improper arrangement where sacred goods function as payment for labor.
