Bekhorot 49 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
What happens if a firstborn son dies within 30 days of birth, after the father has already given five sela to the priest?
A. The priest keeps the money as a fine
B. The priest must return the five sela
C. The father owes an additional five sela
D. Nothing changes — the money stays with the priest by default
Correct: B — Since the redemption obligation had not yet taken effect (it applies only after 30 days), the priest must return the money if the son dies within that period.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
Why is Rabbi Akiva uncertain about the halakhic status of the 30th day itself?
A. He was unsure whether firstborns exist at all
B. Because the derivation from the wilderness census might be a 'two verses that come as one' that doesn't generalize
C. Because he disagreed with the Rabbis on principle
D. Because the Torah never discusses a firstborn son
Correct: B — Rabbi Akiva questions whether the 'and upward' language, needed independently for valuations, means the wilderness census and valuations are linked verses that don't teach their common lesson to unrelated cases like ongoing redemption.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to the practical halakha, if a father gives five sela within 30 days of his son's birth intending it to take effect after 30 days, and the money is spent by then, is the son redeemed?
A. Yes, following Rav's opinion
B. No, following Shmuel's opinion, which is accepted as halakha
C. It depends on the priest's consent
D. The question is left unresolved (teiku)
Correct: B — The Gemara states the halakha follows Shmuel: redemption of a son is not 'in the father's power' to effect from now the way betrothal is, so if the money is spent before 30 days elapse, the son is not redeemed.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Rabbi Yirmeya, when do Rabbi Yehuda and the first tanna actually disagree about whether one's own redemption precedes his son's?
A. Whenever there is any money at all available
B. Only when there is liened property worth five sela and separate unsold property worth five sela
C. They never truly disagree
D. Only on Shabbat
Correct: B — Rabbi Yirmeya clarifies that with only five sela available, everyone agrees one's own mitzva comes first; the dispute is limited to a case with both liened and unsold property, where Rabbi Yehuda's view about collecting from liened property lets both redemptions happen.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
In what coinage does the mishna say the five sela for a firstborn son's redemption must be measured?
A. Roman denarii
B. Provincial (medina) coinage
C. Tyrian maneh
D. Persian dinars
Correct: C — The mishna specifies that the five sela of redemption are calculated using the high-value Tyrian maneh, as further defined by Rabbi Asi, Rabbi Ami, and Rabbi Ḥanina.
