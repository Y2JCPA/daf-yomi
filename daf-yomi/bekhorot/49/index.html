📖 Overview: Timing and Value in Redemption

Bekhorot 49a–49b
מֵת בְּתוֹךְ שְׁלֹשִׁים יוֹם, אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁנָּתַן לַכֹּהֵן — יַחְזִיר
The scene: The daf continues the theme of timing: if a firstborn son dies within 30 days, any redemption money already given must be returned, but if he dies after 30 days the father must still pay even without prior redemption. Rabbi Akiva's uncertainty about the 30th day itself opens a window into how the Sages derive halakhot from the wilderness census of firstborns. The Gemara then asks whether redeeming a son within the first 30 days actually works once the deadline passes — Rav and Shmuel disagree — before turning to a new mishna on whose redemption takes precedence, one's own or one's son's, and finally to the precise value of the five sela of redemption money.

🌓 Why Is Rabbi Akiva Uncertain About Day Thirty?

A verbal analogy that may or may not generalize
Rabbis
We derive 'month' from the wilderness census of firstborns (Numbers 3:40) — there it says 'and upward,' meaning strictly after 30 days, so the same applies to redemption in every generation.
R' Akiva's doubt
But 'and upward' also had to be written separately for valuations (Leviticus 27:7) — suggesting the wilderness census and valuations are 'two verses that come as one,' which don't teach outward to other cases.
Or maybe...
Two verses only fail to teach general, unrelated halakhot — but for a similar case like ongoing redemption, they might still teach directly.
Result: Because Rabbi Akiva can't resolve which reading is correct, he treats the 30th day as a genuine case of uncertainty rather than ruling definitively.

🔓 Can You Redeem a Son Within 30 Days?

Rav says the redemption takes effect; Shmuel disagrees
❓ A father gives five sela within 30 days, saying it should take effect once the 30 days pass — is the son redeemed?
↓
🤝 Everyone agrees: if the money is still with the priest after 30 days, the son is certainly redeemed
↓
Rav
Redeemed even if the coins were spent — like betrothal money spent during a 30-day delay, which still validates the marriage
Shmuel
Not redeemed if spent — betrothal is 'in his power' to effect now; redemption of a son is not yet 'in his power' within 30 days
↓
⚖️ Halakha follows Shmuel here, even though monetary disputes usually favor Shmuel anyway — his view is confirmed as the ruling

👨‍👦 Who Gets Redeemed First — Father or Son?

The mishna's dispute, with Rabbi Yirmeya's key qualification

First Tanna

  • One's own redemption takes precedence over his son's
  • Applies whenever both need redeeming

Rabbi Yehuda

  • The son's redemption takes precedence
  • Reason: the mitzva to redeem the father falls on the grandfather; the mitzva to redeem the son falls on him

Rabbi Yirmeya's Nuance

  • With only 5 sela available, all agree: one's own mitzva comes first
  • They truly disagree only when there's liened property (5 sela) plus unsold property (5 sela) — R' Yehuda uses the liened land for himself, freeing the unsold land for his son

🪙 Pinning Down the Value of Five Sela

The new mishna and its amoraic definitions
1️⃣ Mishna: the five sela for redeeming a son are measured in Tyrian maneh — a high-value coinage
↓
2️⃣ Rabbi Asi: simply, the maneh minted in the city of Tyre
↓
3️⃣ Rabbi Ami: equal to a golden Arabian dinar
↓
4️⃣ Rabbi Ḥanina: a Syrian sela — eight of which sell for one large golden dinar; give five of these for redemption
Why it matters: The same standard also governs the 30 shekels for a slave killed by an ox, and the 50 and 100 shekels for a seducer, rapist, and defamer — all pegged to the same Sanctuary-shekel value.
