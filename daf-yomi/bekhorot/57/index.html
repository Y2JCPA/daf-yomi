📖 Overview: Exclusions, Exaggerations, and Deadlines

Bekhorot 57a–57b
הַכֹּל נִכְנָסִין לַדִּיר לְהִתְעַשֵּׂר, חוּץ מִן כִּלְאַיִם, וּטְרֵפָה, וְיוֹצֵא דּוֹפֶן, וּמְחוּסַּר זְמַן, וְהַיָּתוֹם
The scene: The daf opens by confirming why Rabbi Yoḥanan's rulings — that divided brothers are like purchasers of land, and that purchased fetuses still enter the pen — both needed to be stated independently. A new mishna then lists which animals are excluded from animal tithe altogether: a crossbred (kilayim), a tereifa, one born by caesarean section, one too young, and an 'orphan' whose mother died in childbirth — defining that last category precisely via Rabbi Yehoshua's view about the mother's hide. The Gemara derives these exclusions from the same 'under' verbal analogy used for sacrificial animals, then notes that certain disqualified-for-the-altar animals (from illicit unions or idol worship) are nonetheless included in tithe, since a blemish alone doesn't disqualify tithe. A colorful digression records outsized testimonies about the natural world before the daf turns to a new mishna: Rabbi Akiva's three yearly gathering times for animal tithe.

🐕 Why Two Separate Teachings Were Needed

וּצְרִיכִי, דְּאִי אַשְׁמְעִינַן הָכָא...
Rabbi Yoḥanan's rulings, and why neither alone would suffice: Rabbi Yoḥanan taught both that brothers who divide an inheritance are considered purchasers from each other (obligating them to return shares in the Jubilee), and separately that purchased fetuses still enter the pen for animal tithe. Had only the Jubilee ruling been stated, one might think it applies uniquely because land must physically 'return' to its original state. Had only the animal-tithe ruling been stated, one might think brothers are treated as receiving their own designated share retroactively. Both statements together close off each possible limitation.

🚫 Five Categories Excluded from Animal Tithe

The mishna's list and its scriptural source
Excluded animalSource phrase (Leviticus 22:27)
Crossbred (kilayim)'A bull OR a sheep' — excludes a mixed-species offspring
One resembling another species (nidmeh)'OR a goat' — excludes a lamb that looks like a goat, or vice versa
Born by caesarean section'When...is BORN' — excludes an unnatural birth
Too young (under 8 days)'Then it shall be SEVEN DAYS' — excludes one whose time hasn't arrived
Orphan (mother died in birth)'UNDER its mother' — excludes an animal with no mother present
Verbal analogy: Rabbi Yishmael, son of R' Yoḥanan ben Beroka, links 'under the rod' (tithe) to 'under its mother' (offerings) — each category excludes the other's cases too, including a tereifa on both sides.

🐄 Disqualified for the Altar, Yet Still Tithed

The mishna's expansive 'all' includes surprising cases

Included Despite Disqualification

  • Rove'a/nirva (involved in bestiality)
  • Mukzeh/ne'evad (idol-related)
  • Etnan/mechir (prostitute's payment, dog's price)
  • Tumtum/androginus (per most opinions)

Why the Difference?

  • 'Corruption' (licentiousness/idolatry) disqualifies wherever a BLEMISH disqualifies
  • Animal tithe is never disqualified by a mere blemish ('he shall not inspect between good and bad')
  • So corruption-based disqualifications don't apply to tithe either

The Exception

  • Rabbi Shimon ben Yehuda: tumtum and androginus ARE excluded
  • Reason: their SEX is uncertain, and sacrificial animals require a definite male or female — like a tereifa, excluded by 'under the rod' itself

🗣️ Testimonies from the Edge of the Empire

בִּמְקוֹמֵנוּ מַפְשִׁיטִין אֶת הַמֵּתָה וּמַלְבִּישִׁין אֶת הַחַי
Rabbi Yishmael ben Satriel's reports to Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi: In our locale, they flay a dead mother's hide to clothe her newborn — explaining why the mishna's 'orphan' exemption depends on whether the hide survives. Our lettuce has 600,000 leaves in its stalk. Once a cedar fell so wide that sixteen wagons passed over its trunk side by side. Once an unfertilized egg of the bar yokhani bird fell, drowning sixty cities and shattering three hundred cedars.

📅 Three Times a Year to Gather and Tithe

The new mishna's calendar, per Rabbi Akiva
1️⃣ Adjacent to Passover — animals born since the last gathering are counted and tithed
↓
2️⃣ Adjacent to Shavuot — a second gathering time
↓
3️⃣ Adjacent to Sukkot — a third and final gathering time for the year
↓
4️⃣ Alternative views follow (ben Azzai; R' Elazar and R' Shimon) with different exact dates, to be explored next
Why three? This sets up the next daf's explanation: so that untithed, permissible-to-eat animals remain available for those making pilgrimage to Jerusalem for each festival.
