Bekhorot 57 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
Why does the mishna exclude an 'orphan' animal — one whose mother died while giving birth to it — from animal tithe?
A. Because orphans are always weaker animals
B. The exclusion is derived from 'under its mother' (Leviticus 22:27), applied to tithe via a verbal analogy of 'under'
C. Because such animals cannot survive to be tithed
D. There is no such exclusion in the mishna
Correct: B — Rabbi Yishmael, son of Rabbi Yoḥanan ben Beroka, derives all the tithe exclusions — including the orphan — through a verbal analogy between 'under the rod' (tithe) and 'under its mother' (sacrificial offerings).
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to Rabbi Yehoshua's definition in the mishna, when is a newborn animal NOT considered an 'orphan' despite its mother having been slaughtered during birth?
A. Never — any animal whose mother died is always an orphan
B. When the mother's hide still exists (survives) at the time of birth
C. Only if the father is still alive
D. Only during the summer months
Correct: B — Rabbi Yehoshua rules that even if the mother was slaughtered, the newborn is not considered an orphan as long as the mother's hide is present — as later testimony reveals, the hide could be used to clothe and effectively 'replace' the mother for the newborn.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
Why are animals disqualified from being sacrificed due to licentiousness (e.g., bestiality) or idol worship nevertheless included in animal tithe?
A. Because tithe has no disqualifications at all
B. Because animal tithe is never disqualified by a mere physical blemish, and 'corruption'-based disqualifications only apply wherever a blemish would also disqualify
C. Because the Torah explicitly commands their inclusion by name
D. Because these animals are considered more valuable
Correct: B — The Gemara reasons that the disqualifications for licentiousness or idolatry are linked textually to blemish-disqualification; since animal tithe is exempt from blemish-disqualification ('he shall not inspect between good and bad'), it is likewise exempt from these corruption-based disqualifications.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Rabbi Shimon ben Yehuda (in the name of Rabbi Shimon), why are a tumtum and an androginus excluded from animal tithe, unlike other disqualified animals?
A. They are considered too valuable to tithe
B. Their sex is uncertain, and sacrificial animals by definition require a definite male or female — similar to how a tereifa is excluded
C. They are excluded only outside Eretz Yisrael
D. There is no such exclusion — they are always included
Correct: B — Rabbi Shimon ben Yehuda holds that a tumtum (indeterminate sex) or androginus (both sexes) represents a genuine uncertainty about the animal's category, which sacrificial law excludes just as it excludes a tereifa — via the same 'under' verbal analogy.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to Rabbi Akiva in the new mishna, how many times per year are animals gathered and tithed, and around which festivals?
A. Once a year, before Passover only
B. Three times: adjacent to Passover, Shavuot, and Sukkot
C. Twelve times, once each month
D. Two times: Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur
Correct: B — Rabbi Akiva's mishna establishes three yearly gathering times for animal tithe, timed adjacent to each of the three pilgrimage festivals: Passover, Shavuot, and Sukkot.
