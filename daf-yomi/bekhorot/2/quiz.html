Bekhorot 2 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to the mishna, what verse explains why a firstborn donkey owned partly by a gentile is exempt from firstborn status?
A. "I sanctified to Me all the firstborn in Israel, both man and animal" (Numbers 3:13)
B. "Six days shall you labor" (Exodus 20:9)
C. "An eye for an eye" (Exodus 21:24)
D. "You shall not muzzle an ox" (Deuteronomy 25:4)
Correct: A — The mishna reads 'in Israel' as limiting the obligation to animals owned entirely by Jews — 'but not others,' i.e., not when a gentile has any share.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
Why does the Gemara say the mishna needs to state the case of the seller (מוֹכֵר), even though selling a large animal to a gentile is generally forbidden?
A. To teach that the sale is actually permitted
B. To teach that the seller is not penalized with firstborn obligation despite acting improperly
C. To teach that gentiles may never own donkeys
D. To teach that the buyer must return the fetus
Correct: B — One might think the Sages would penalize the seller for removing the fetus from sanctity by imposing firstborn status anyway; the mishna teaches he is not penalized.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
Why does the mishna need to mention the case of a partnership (מִשְׁתַּתֵּף) with a gentile?
A. To teach that partnerships with gentiles are always forbidden
B. To exclude Rabbi Yehuda's opinion that an animal owned in partnership with a gentile is obligated in firstborn status
C. To teach that the gentile must be paid first
D. It is unnecessary and redundant
Correct: B — Rabbi Yehuda holds that a gentile partnership animal remains obligated; the mishna's case teaches that, according to the accepted view, it is exempt.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
In Rabbi Yehuda's baraita, what is the penalty when a Jew improperly gives his own donkey to a gentile in receivership (rather than properly receiving one from a gentile)?
A. No penalty at all
B. He gives half its value to the priest
C. He must buy out the gentile's share for up to ten times its value and give the entire value to the priest
D. The gentile keeps the whole animal
Correct: C — Properly receiving an animal from a gentile costs only half its value to the priest; improperly giving one's own animal to a gentile is penalized at up to ten times its value, given entirely to the priest.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to Rav Ashi's proof, why does Rabbi Yehuda forbid selling a fetus to a gentile even though he permits selling a damaged (broken) animal?
A. Because fetuses are worth more money
B. Because he permits a broken animal only since it 'cannot be cured,' implying a curable animal is forbidden — and a fetus will eventually be able to work
C. Because gentiles are not allowed to own fetuses under any circumstances
D. Because the mishna explicitly says so
Correct: B — Rabbi Yehuda's stated reason for permitting the sale of a broken animal is that it cannot be cured to work again; a fetus, which will eventually be capable of work, is therefore treated as curable and forbidden to sell.
