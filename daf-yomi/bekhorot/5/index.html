📖 Overview: From Wilderness Accounting to a New Mishna

Bekhorot 5a–5b
פֶּטֶר חֲמוֹר... פֶּטֶר חֲמוֹר, שְׁנֵי פְּעָמִים
The scene: The daf closes out the wilderness-sanctity debate with a twist: different academies transmitted the Rabbi Yoḥanan–Reish Lakish dispute in opposite directions. It then records the Roman officer Kontrokos's two sharp arithmetic challenges to Rabban Yoḥanan ben Zakkai, and Rabbi Ḥanina's question to Rabbi Eliezer about why only donkeys, among non-kosher animals, require redemption. A new mishna then opens fresh territory: when a cow gives birth to a donkey-like creature, or a donkey to a horse-like one, is the offspring subject to firstborn redemption, and may it be eaten?

🔄 Whose Opinion Is It? A Tradition Reversed

Rav Mordekhai tells Rav Ashi they learned the dispute backward
Rav Mordekhai
You have it backward! We teach: Rabbi Yoḥanan says firstborns were not sanctified in the wilderness, and Reish Lakish says they were.
Rav Ashi
Then do you also reverse Rabbi Elazar's proof — the one he attributed to Rabbi Yoḥanan after seeing him in a dream?
Rav Mordekhai
No — in my version, 'not sanctified' means they didn't need separate consecration, since they were already holy. The practical outcome matches your version exactly.
Gemara
So why report it in reversed language at all? To teach: a student must state a ruling in his teacher's own precise wording.
Takeaway: Two schools reached identical halakha through mirror-image terminology — a lesson about transmitting Torah faithfully, word for word.

🔢 Kontrokos's Two Puzzles for Rabban Yoḥanan ben Zakkai

A Roman officer challenges the wilderness census and Temple accounting
PuzzleRabban Yoḥanan ben Zakkai's answer
Individual Levite families total 22,300, but the collective count is only 22,000 (Numbers 3:39) — where did 300 go?They were themselves firstborn Levites; a firstborn cannot abrogate another firstborn's sanctity — it suffices for him to abrogate his own
Silver collected totaled 201 talents + 11 maneh, but only 100 talents were used for casting the sockets — 'was Moses a thief?'Moses was a trustworthy treasurer: the Sanctuary's maneh was double a standard maneh (50 shekels, not 25), so the sums match exactly
Underlying principle: A community may increase standard measures, but never by more than one-sixth — calculated from the final, increased total.

🐴 Why Donkeys, and Not Horses or Camels?

Rabbi Ḥanina's questions to Rabbi Eliezer 'in the great study hall'
1️⃣ Question: why are firstborn donkeys redeemed with a lamb, while firstborn horses and camels are not redeemed at all?
↓
2️⃣ Answer: it is a Torah decree — and besides, donkeys assisted Israel at the Exodus, each family leaving Egypt with ninety superior donkeys laden with silver and gold
↓
3️⃣ Follow-up: what does the place-name 'Rephidim' mean? Rabbi Eliezer: simply its name. (Rabbi Yehoshua elsewhere: it alludes to Israel growing lax [ריפיון] in Torah study there)
↓
4️⃣ Follow-up: and 'Shittim'? Rabbi Eliezer: simply its name. (Rabbi Yehoshua: it alludes to Israel's involvement in 'foolish' [שְׁטוּת] matters — idolatry and licentiousness there)
Pattern: Rabbi Eliezer consistently reads place-names literally; Rabbi Yehoshua elsewhere reads them as moral allusions to what happened there.

🐮 New Mishna: When Mother and Offspring Differ in Species

Redemption and kashrut rules for cross-species births

Cow → Donkey-like Offspring

  • Exempt from firstborn-donkey redemption
  • 'Firstborn of a donkey...firstborn of a donkey' stated twice (Exodus 13:13, 34:20) requires both mother and offspring to be a donkey
  • Permitted to eat — a kosher mother's offspring is kosher regardless of appearance

Donkey → Horse-like Offspring

  • Also exempt — same doubled-verse requirement
  • Neither animal is eaten, so no separate kashrut question arises here

General Kashrut Rule

  • Kosher animal births a non-kosher-looking creature: permitted to eat
  • Non-kosher animal births a kosher-looking creature: forbidden to eat
  • Status always follows the mother, never the offspring's appearance

📜 Two Sources for 'Must Resemble Its Mother'

The mishna's tanna and Rabbi Yosei HaGelili derive the same rule from opposite directions
Our tanna
I learn 'donkey mother, donkey offspring' from the doubled donkey verses. Since the Torah revealed this for a donkey's value-sanctity, the same applies to a kosher animal's inherent sanctity.
R' Yosei HaGelili
I learn it the other way: from the doubled 'firstborn...firstborn' by ox, lamb, and goat (Numbers 18:17). Since the Torah revealed it for inherent sanctity, the same applies to a donkey's value-sanctity.
Our tanna
Then what do I do with the ox/lamb/goat repetition? I use it to teach that sacrificial portions are burned on the altar for each of the three species separately — none could be derived from the other two.
R' Yosei HaGelili
And I use the doubled donkey verse differently: to exclude firstborn horses and camels from redemption altogether — only a donkey's firstborn is redeemed with a lamb.
Same rule, opposite proofs: Both agree the offspring must match its mother's species; they disagree only about which case — donkey or kosher animal — is the primary source.
