Bekhorot 16 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to Rav Huna's defense, why doesn't the baraita's wording actually prove that blemished offspring of a disqualified-consecration animal may be redeemed?
A. Because the Gemara rejects the baraita entirely
B. Because the phrasing is purely symmetrical/stylistic, mirroring the lenient clause's language without teaching an actual new leniency
C. Because blemished offspring were never discussed in the baraita
D. Because Rav Huna later retracted his opinion
Correct: B — Rav Huna explains that the baraita's parallel wording in both clauses is for stylistic symmetry, not to teach that blemished offspring can actually be redeemed.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to Abaye, why is a substitute made for a disqualified consecrated animal (after its redemption) not eaten, unlike a firstborn's or tithe's substitute, which is eaten even blemished?
A. Because such substitutes are always destroyed immediately
B. Because it inherits its mother's requirement of redemption first, and its own sanctity is too weak to ever be redeemed
C. Because only firstborn animals can have valid substitutes
D. Because the Torah forbids all substitutes without exception
Correct: B — Abaye explains that while both types of substitutes bear their 'mother's name,' this one inherits the requirement that its source be redeemed before consumption — but its sanctity cannot be transferred to money, leaving it permanently ineligible.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to the new mishna, what is the basic pattern of firstborn-status exemption for animals received from a gentile as a guaranteed investment (tzon barzel)?
A. No offspring are ever exempt
B. Direct offspring are exempt; the offspring of those offspring are obligated (absent formal substitution)
C. All offspring forever are exempt with no limit
D. Only female offspring are exempt
Correct: B — The mishna's basic rule exempts the direct offspring from firstborn status, while the next generation (offspring of offspring) is obligated, unless the Jew formally substituted the offspring for their mothers.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Rava, why are even the offspring of a guaranteed-investment animal exempt from firstborn status, though the gentile does not currently own them?
A. Because gentiles automatically own all animals in Israel
B. Because if the gentile isn't paid, he will seize the offspring as payment instead, giving him a live financial claim — 'his hand in the middle'
C. Because the Jew never truly owns any guaranteed-investment animal
D. Because the mishna provides no reasoning at all
Correct: B — Rava explains that the gentile's potential claim on the offspring as collateral for his payment is sufficient to exempt them, since ownership need not be current — only a live financial stake matters.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
What is the dispute between Rav Huna and Rav Yehuda regarding animals where the Jew did NOT formally substitute the offspring for their mothers?
A. Rav Huna exempts all generations; Rav Yehuda exempts none
B. Rav Huna exempts only the direct offspring; Rav Yehuda extends the exemption one generation further
C. Both agree exemption never applies without formal substitution
D. The dispute concerns only female offspring
Correct: B — Rav Huna limits exemption to direct offspring even without substitution, while Rav Yehuda holds the exemption reaches the next generation as well, regardless of formal substitution.
