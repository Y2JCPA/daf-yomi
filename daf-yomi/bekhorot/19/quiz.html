Bekhorot 19 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
In the mishna, why does Rabbi Akiva say that neither the caesarean-born animal nor the one that follows it is a firstborn?
A. Both were born to different mothers
B. The first never itself opened the womb, and the second was preceded by the first
C. Neither animal is male
D. The mother animal is disqualified
Correct: B — Rabbi Akiva rules the caesarean-born animal lacks firstborn status because it did not itself open the womb (פֶּטֶר רֶחֶם), and the one born after it lacks firstborn status because it was not truly first.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to the Gemara, what is the core of the Tarfon/Akiva dispute in this mishna?
A. Whether the priest may ever receive a blemished animal
B. Whether an animal that is a firstborn in only one respect retains any firstborn status
C. Whether a caesarean birth is valid at all
D. The proper way to sacrifice a firstborn
Correct: B — Rabbi Tarfon is uncertain whether 'firstborn in one respect' (בְּכוֹר לְדָבָר אֶחָד) counts as a firstborn, while Rabbi Akiva is certain that it does not — that is the basis of their disagreement.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
What does the phrase 'פֶּטֶר רֶחֶם' (that which opens the womb) exclude, according to the baraita's exegesis?
A. A female animal
B. A male animal born after a female had already emerged
C. An animal born to a gentile
D. A blemished firstborn
Correct: B — The baraita teaches that 'that which opens the womb' excludes a male that was not itself first to emerge — specifically a male born after a female had already come out first.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
Why does Rabbi Akiva object to Rabbi Yishmael's rule that a goat under one year old certainly never gave birth before?
A. Because goats cannot give birth before one year in any case
B. Because physical signs like discharge can indicate a completed pregnancy even within that age window
C. Because he holds all animals purchased from a gentile are automatically exempt
D. Because the age of the goat is irrelevant to firstborn law
Correct: B — Rabbi Akiva points out that physical indications of a prior birth — discharge in small animals, afterbirth in large animals — can occur even within Rabbi Yishmael's supposed age-based window of certainty, so age alone cannot establish that a birth never happened.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
What distinguishes the majority the Rabbis followed for meat found before nine kosher shops from the claim that 'most animals give birth within their first year'?
A. There is no real distinction between them
B. The shops example is an evident, examinable majority; the animal-birth claim is a non-evident, statistical majority
C. The shops example only applies to non-kosher food
D. The animal-birth claim was accepted by all opinions without dispute
Correct: B — The Gemara distinguishes a 'majority that is present before you' (like nine kosher shops, or the Sanhedrin's vote) from a purely statistical majority like 'most animals give birth within a year' — the Rabbis rely on the former but not, in this context, on the latter.
