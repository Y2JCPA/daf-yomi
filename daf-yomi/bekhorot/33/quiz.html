5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
What verse does Rabbi Akiva use to permit even a gentile to eat a blemished firstborn?
A. "You shall not redeem" (Numbers 18:17)
B. "Like the gazelle and like the deer" (Deuteronomy 15:22)
C. "The impure and the pure may eat it alike" (Deuteronomy 15:22)
D. "It shall not be redeemed" (Leviticus 27:33)
Correct: B — Rabbi Akiva reasons that just as a gentile may eat a gazelle or deer, he may eat a disqualified consecrated animal, since the verse compares them.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
How does Beit Hillel justify permitting a menstruating woman (niddah) to eat a blemished firstborn?
A. They forbid it just like Beit Shammai
B. The Torah writes 'impure' without qualification in Deuteronomy 15:22, unlike the Paschal offering where 'impure through a corpse' is specified
C. Only priests may eat it, so the question is irrelevant
D. It applies only to unblemished firstborns
Correct: B — Beit Hillel note that the unqualified term 'impure' includes all forms of impurity, unlike the Paschal offering's law which specifically names impurity from a corpse.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to Rav Ḥisda, whose opinion underlies the baraita's ban on skinning a firstborn 'from the feet'?
A. Rabbi Akiva's
B. Beit Shammai's, who hold a blemished firstborn retains sacrificial sanctity
C. Beit Hillel's
D. Rabban Gamliel's
Correct: B — Rav Ḥisda attributes the ban to Beit Shammai, who hold that a blemished firstborn must still be treated as sacrificial meat, making the feet-skinning technique inappropriate.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
In the new mishna, what does Rabbi Yehuda rule about letting the blood of a firstborn congested with excess blood?
A. One may always let blood freely
B. One may never let its blood, even if the animal will die, because the incision might cause a blemish
C. One may let blood only on a Festival
D. One must let blood only in a place that will not blemish it
Correct: B — Rabbi Yehuda takes the strictest position: bloodletting is forbidden entirely, even at the cost of the animal's life, since it risks causing a blemish.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
Why does the Gemara ultimately question the parallel Rabbi Elazar draws between the bloodletting dispute and the barrel-of-teruma dispute?
A. Because the two disputes are recorded by different tannaim entirely
B. Because each sage's position in one case might differ in the other, due to differences like direct versus indirect action or certain versus possible loss, and the two disputes rest on different verses
C. Because the barrel of teruma has nothing to do with impurity
D. Because Rabbi Elazar later retracted the comparison
Correct: B — The Gemara repeatedly shows that a sage's ruling in one case need not carry over to the other case, since the underlying considerations (direct action vs. indirect cause, certain death vs. possible rescue) differ, and concludes the disputes stem from different verses.
