📖 Overview: From Penalties to the Verse Itself

Bekhorot 3a–3b
כׇּל בְּכוֹר
The scene: The daf opens by resolving yesterday's dilemma: it is indeed permitted to sell a gentile only the rights to an animal's future fetuses, so long as the animal is not yet pregnant. The Gemara then compares penalties for improperly selling an animal or a slave to a gentile, before turning to the theoretical heart of the mishna's rule: what does the phrase 'sanctify to Me all the firstborn' (Exodus 13:2) actually teach, and how much gentile ownership is enough to exempt an animal? Two real cases test the limits of the leniency, and the daf closes with a new mishna: priests and Levites need not redeem their own firstborn donkeys.

💰 Selling to a Gentile: Two Versions of the Penalty

Reish Lakish vs. Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi, and the amount they set
VersionSells an animal (Reish Lakish)Sells a slave (R' Yehoshua ben Levi)
First versionBuy it back for up to 10× its valueBuy him back for up to 100× its value — a slave loses mitzvot every day he serves a gentile
Second versionBuy it back for up to 100× its valueBuy him back for up to 10× its value — a slave is uncommon, so the Sages did not decree as severely for him
Why receivership is lighter: A sale severs the animal entirely from the Jew (פָּסְקָה מִינֵּיהּ); giving it in receivership does not — the Jew still owns it — so that penalty stays at ten times, regardless of which version of the sale-penalty is correct.

📜 'All the Firstborn' — One Verse, Two Readings

Rabbi Yehoshua: both the Rabbis and Rabbi Yehuda interpret the same verse (Exodus 13:2)
Rabbis
Plain 'the firstborn' would mean even a partial firstborn is obligated. So the Torah writes 'all' — teaching it is obligated only when the entire animal belongs to a Jew.
R' Yehuda
Plain 'the firstborn' would mean only a fully Jewish-owned firstborn is obligated. So the Torah writes 'all' — teaching it is obligated even if any amount belongs to a Jew.
Alt. read
Or say: everyone agrees plain 'the firstborn' means a majority-owned firstborn is obligated.
Alt. read
And they disagree only whether 'all' comes to fill the requirement up to the whole animal, or to reduce it down to any share at all.
Stakes: This dispute is the theoretical engine behind the mishna's rule that a firstborn donkey is exempt whenever a gentile has any share in it.

🐑 How Much Gentile Ownership Is Enough to Exempt?

Three opinions on the threshold, per the Rabbis' view

Rav Huna

  • Even the animal's ear alone, if gentile-owned, exempts it
  • Rav Naḥman objects: then let the priest tell the gentile, 'take your ear and go'

Rav Ḥisda

  • Only a limb that, if removed, would immediately render the animal a carcass (נְבֵלָה)
  • A merely important limb is not enough

Rava

  • Only a limb that, if removed, would eventually render it a tereifa
  • Broader than Rav Ḥisda's standard
Rav Pappa's harmonization: No dispute at all — Rav Huna's lenient 'even an ear' speaks about the fetus itself (which needs 'all the firstborn' to be Jewish); Rav Ḥisda and Rava speak about the mother.

📖 Two Case Stories Testing the Leniency

One ends in a simple ruling; the other ends badly
1️⃣ A female convert's gentile brothers gave her animals to fatten, splitting the profits. She asked Rava about firstborn status.
↓
2️⃣ Rava ruled: exempt — no one is concerned for Rabbi Yehuda's stricter view that a gentile-partnership animal is obligated
↓
3️⃣ Rav Mari bar Raḥel transferred ownership of just his firstborns' ears to a gentile, yet still barred shearing or labor and gave them to priests
↓
4️⃣ His animals ultimately died — either because he abrogated their sanctity unnecessarily, or because others copied his transfer without completing it properly and came to mishandle their own firstborns
Lesson: A leniency that works only through a precise, completed legal transaction is dangerous to rely on in practice — others may imitate the result without the mechanism.

🍼 New Mishna: Priests and Levites Need Not Redeem Their Firstborn Donkeys

An a fortiori (קַל וָחוֹמֶר) argument from the census in the wilderness
❓ Why are priests and Levites exempt from redeeming their own firstborn donkeys?
↓
📜 In the wilderness: 'Take the Levites in exchange for all the firstborn among the children of Israel, and the animal of the Levites in exchange for their animals' (Numbers 3:45)
↓
✅ If the Levites' substitution exempted the firstborns and donkeys of all Israel in the wilderness, all the more so it exempts their own firstborn donkeys now
