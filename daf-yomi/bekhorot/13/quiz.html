5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to Rava's resolution of the 'immediately' vs. 'after thirty days' contradiction, whose opinion requires waiting until the donkey is thirty days old, and why?
A. The Rabbis, because they compare it to consecrated property
B. Rabbi Eliezer, because he compares redeeming a firstborn donkey to redeeming a firstborn son, which occurs after thirty days
C. Rabbi Yehuda, because of the prohibition on deriving benefit
D. No one requires waiting; it is always immediate
Correct: B — Rava explains that the baraita requiring a thirty-day wait follows Rabbi Eliezer, who compares the donkey's redemption to a firstborn son's (Numbers 18:16); the Rabbis reject this comparison and require immediate redemption.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to the mishna closing the first chapter, which takes precedence: redeeming a firstborn donkey or breaking its neck?
A. Breaking its neck always comes first
B. Redemption takes precedence, derived from 'if you will not redeem it, then you shall break its neck'
C. They are performed simultaneously
D. It depends on the owner's preference alone, with no textual basis
Correct: B — The mishna derives from Exodus 13:13's wording — redemption named before neck-breaking — that redemption is the preferred, primary option.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to the new mishna opening the second chapter, are priests and Levites obligated in the firstborn status of a kosher animal such as a cow?
A. No, they are exempt just as with a firstborn donkey
B. Yes — unlike their exemption from a firstborn son or firstborn donkey, they are fully obligated for a kosher animal's firstborn
C. Only Levites are obligated, not priests
D. The mishna does not address this question
Correct: B — The mishna explicitly states that priests and Levites were exempted only from redeeming a firstborn son and a firstborn donkey, but remain obligated regarding a firstborn kosher animal.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Abaye, from what verse is it derived that a Jew can acquire an animal from a gentile through payment of money alone, without pulling?
A. 'I sanctified to Me all the firstborn' (Numbers 3:13)
B. 'Buy of your counterpart's hand' (Leviticus 25:14) — implying pulling is needed specifically from a fellow Jew, while money alone suffices from a gentile
C. 'And you shall redeem the firstborn of a donkey with a lamb' (Exodus 13:13)
D. There is no verse; it is a purely rabbinic enactment
Correct: B — Abaye reads 'of your counterpart's hand' as limiting the requirement of pulling to transactions between Jews, implying that money alone completes an acquisition from a gentile.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to Rava's explanation of the idol-in-scrap-metal baraita, what is the key difference between pulling the idol before versus after paying for it?
A. There is no real difference; the rulings are identical
B. It is a matter of appearances — before payment, the idol doesn't yet look like it belongs to the Jew; after payment, it does
C. Pulling before payment is always invalid under Jewish law
D. The difference concerns only the idol's monetary value
Correct: B — Rava explains both clauses involve a mistaken transaction, but the practical ruling differs based on how it appears to onlookers: after payment, the idol appears to be in the Jew's possession and so cannot simply be returned.
