QUESTION 1 OF 5
What does the Gemara conclude about hearsay testimony ('a witness in the name of a witness') regarding a blemished firstborn?
A. It is never valid for any purpose
B. It is valid to permit the firstborn's slaughter, following Rav Ashi's reading of the baraita
C. It is valid only if the witness is a woman
D. It requires four witnesses instead of two
Correct: B — The Gemara concludes the halakha follows Rav Ashi: hearsay testimony is valid wherever a woman's own testimony would be valid, which includes a firstborn's blemish.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
In the story of Rabbi Tzadok's firstborn, what did Rabban Gamliel do after Rabbi Yehoshua was forced to publicly admit his earlier ruling?
A. He apologized immediately and sat Rabbi Yehoshua down
B. He continued lecturing while Rabbi Yehoshua remained standing, until the people made him stop out of outrage
C. He reversed his own position on the matter
D. He expelled Rabbi Yehoshua from the study hall
Correct: B — Rabban Gamliel kept lecturing while Rabbi Yehoshua stood in forced deference, until the assembled people, offended by this treatment, told the disseminator to stop.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
Why is a priest believed when he claims 'an Israelite gave me this firstborn animal with its blemish already on it'?
A. Priests are never suspected of lying about anything
B. Because the claim names a specific Israelite whose account could be checked, and people generally do not lie about matters likely to be revealed
C. Because the mishna requires no verification at all for such claims
D. Because only the Israelite's testimony matters, not the priest's
Correct: B — The principle 'any matter likely to be revealed, people do not lie about' underlies Rav's ruling — since the named Israelite could be questioned, the priest's claim is trusted.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to the mishna, why is everyone — even the owner — trusted to testify about a blemish on an animal tithe offering (ma'aser behema), unlike a firstborn?
A. Because the tithe has no sanctity at all
B. Because the owner had no special incentive to lie, as he could have legitimately caused a blemish in his entire flock before the tithing process began
C. Because tithe animals cannot be blemished
D. Because only priests may own tithe animals
Correct: B — Since 'He shall not distinguish between good and bad' bars pre-selecting the tenth animal, the owner has no special motive to secretly blemish just one animal — he could have blemished the whole flock beforehand if he wished.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
In the new mishna about clear-cut blemishes (a blinded eye, severed foreleg, broken hind leg), what is Rabbi Yosei's position?
A. Three laymen always suffice, even for uncertain blemishes
B. Even a court of twenty-three judges is insufficient — only a true expert may rule on the blemish
C. No blemish may ever be certified by anyone
D. Only priests may certify blemishes
Correct: B — Rabbi Yosei disagrees with the first tanna and holds that even a large court cannot substitute for a genuine expert's ruling, even for seemingly obvious blemishes.
