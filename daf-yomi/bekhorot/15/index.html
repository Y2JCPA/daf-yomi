Bekhorot 15a–15b
כְּמוֹ שֶׁתֹּאכַל אֶת הַצְּבִי וְאֶת הָאַיָּל
The scene: The daf continues yesterday's dispute over whether all consecrated animals require 'standing and valuation' before redemption, reconciling Rav's teaching with two competing traditions. It then derives, from the verse comparing disqualified consecrated animals to a gazelle and a hart, exactly which exemptions apply and which do not — including a surprising twist in how the prohibition on their fat is phrased. The Gemara confronts a genuinely difficult case, offspring conceived before but born after their mother's redemption, and closes by laying out two complete baraitot that track every consequence of blemish preceding or following consecration.

⚖️ Reconciling Rav With the Mishna

Whose view requires 'standing and valuation'?
Objection
Rav taught: 'this is Rabbi Shimon's view' — but since everyone agrees a blemished-before-consecration animal can be redeemed after death, shouldn't he have said 'Rabbi Shimon's view and his disputants'?
Resolution 1
Rav follows Reish Lakish's version: the Rabbis hold Temple-maintenance items needed standing and valuation but altar items did not — so the mishna's burial rule can't be explained by the Rabbis at all, only by Rabbi Shimon.
Challenge
But maybe 'must be buried' reflects a different rule — that one may not redeem sacrifices just to feed them to dogs — not 'standing and valuation' at all!
Resolution 2
Alternatively: Rav follows Rabbi Yoḥanan's version, where the Rabbis do require standing and valuation even for altar items — so the mishna fits the Rabbis fine, and Rav's phrasing stands as written.

🦌 Deriving Exemption From 'Gazelle and Hart'

Tracing exactly how far the comparison extends
1️⃣ 'As you eat the gazelle and the hart, so shall you eat' (Deuteronomy 12:22) teaches disqualified consecrated animals are exempt from firstborn status, like undomesticated game
↓
2️⃣ Does it extend to priestly gifts too? Yes — naming both animals teaches both exemptions, since gifts (unlike firstborn) apply even to females
↓
3️⃣ Does it extend to permitting their fat, like a gazelle's? No — the word 'ach' ('however') limits the comparison strictly to firstborn and gifts
↓
4️⃣ Rava's twist: the Torah's odd phrase 'you shall not eat its blood' (not 'fat') actually teaches the fat prohibition — borrowing blood's language to show the fat carries the same karet penalty as blood, not merely lashes

🐣 Offspring Conceived Before, Born After Redemption

A genuinely difficult case — and a preemptive solution
❓ A fetus conceived while its mother was still consecrated, but born AFTER her redemption — sacred, or not? And what can be done with it?
↓
⚖️ If born BEFORE the mother's redemption: disputed whether it may be fully sacrificed, or only grazed until blemished and then redeemed
↓
Rav Huna
If born AFTER redemption: gather it into a room and let it starve — it can't be sacrificed (deferred sanctity) and can't be redeemed (too weak a sanctity to transfer)
Eretz Yisrael tradition
Better: dedicate the still-unborn fetus to the SAME offering as its mother, just before she is redeemed — preempting the problem entirely
↓
✅ Reason for the preemptive dedication: a rabbinic decree, lest people raise entire flocks of forbidden, unusable offspring

📊 Two Full Baraitot, Side by Side

Every consequence, tracked in parallel

Blemish Preceded Consecration

  • Firstborn & gifts: obligated, before and after redemption
  • Shearing/labor: no lashes, even before redemption
  • Never creates a substitute (temurah)
  • Misuse (me'ila) applies only before redemption
  • Offspring fully non-sacred if born after redemption; redeemable unblemished if born before
  • May be dedicated to any sacrifice desired

Consecration Preceded Blemish

  • Firstborn & gifts: exempt, before and after redemption
  • Shearing/labor: full lashes, even before redemption
  • Always creates a substitute (temurah)
  • Misuse (me'ila) applies only before redemption
  • Offspring fully sacred; may not be redeemed while unblemished
  • May not be dedicated to any sacrifice but the same one as the mother

🎯 Can the Offspring Be Dedicated to Any Sacrifice?

Ravina's question to Rav Sheshet
❓ Per the Eretz Yisrael practice of pre-dedicating a fetus before its mother's redemption — must it match the mother's offering, or can the owner choose freely?
↓
📖 Rav Sheshet: it must match the mother's offering — derived by verbal analogy ('in your gates,' 'in your gates') to the law of a firstborn animal
↓
✅ Just as a firstborn's identity is fixed by birth and cannot be reassigned to a chosen sacrifice, the same fixed identity applies here
↓
📜 A baraita confirms Rav Sheshet's ruling as part of the complete comparison between the two categories of once-blemished consecrated animals
