5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to Ravina, why don't the Rabbis follow the statistical majority that most animals give birth within their first year?
A. Because that majority is action-dependent, not a fixed feature of reality, and such majorities are not followed
B. Because the Rabbis reject the concept of majority entirely
C. Because animals in Eretz Yisrael behave differently
D. Because Rabbi Yishmael personally rejected majorities
Correct: A — Ravina explains that the Rabbis only follow an examinable majority (like most shops selling kosher meat), not a majority that depends on an action such as whether an animal mated — so this statistical claim doesn't override the uncertainty.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to Rava, when is Rabbi Yishmael concerned for the minority possibility of a hidden discharge?
A. Only when it would create a stringency, never a leniency
B. Only when it would create a leniency, never a stringency
C. In every case, without exception
D. Never, under any circumstances
Correct: A — Rava resolves the apparent inconsistency by saying Rabbi Yishmael is concerned for the minority only when that concern leads to a stringent outcome (giving the animal to the priest), not when it would lead to a leniency for the owner.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
In the third explanation of Rabbi Yehoshua's leniency, what does his own opinion hold about 'part of a day' (מִקְצָת הַיּוֹם כְּכוּלוֹ)?
A. Part of a day is never counted as a whole day
B. Part of a day counts as a whole day, allowing pregnancy to begin as early as day thirty after the discharge
C. The concept is irrelevant to animal pregnancy
D. Only human pregnancies use this principle
Correct: B — According to this explanation, Rabbi Yehoshua's own view holds that part of a day counts as an entire day, so the goat could have become pregnant on the thirtieth day after the discharge and still give birth just before the year's end.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to the baraita that reverses the earlier attribution, who holds that milk production alone exempts the next offspring from firstborn status?
A. Rabbi Akiva
B. Rabbi Yehoshua
C. Rabbi Yishmael
D. Rabbi Shimon
Correct: B — The Gemara corrects the initial attribution: a baraita teaches that it is Rabbi Yehoshua who says milk production exempts the offspring, while Rabbi Akiva says it does not.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
What unstated halakha does the Gemara derive from the baraita's insistence that all three daughter-goats, not just one, bear triplets?
A. That goats always give birth to triplets
B. That a goat cannot give birth twice within the same year
C. That triplets are never subject to the animal tithe
D. That Rabbi Shimon disputes the entire baraita
Correct: B — The Gemara notes that the baraita never proposes having the original mother give birth again alongside her granddaughters to reach the tithe quota — implying that a goat does not give birth a second time within a single year.
