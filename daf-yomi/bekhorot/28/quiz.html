QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to the school of Rav, what does the doubled phrase 'it shall be yours' in Numbers 18:18 teach, beyond the connection to the two-day-one-night eating period?
A. That a blemished firstborn is exempt from all halakhot
B. That a blemished firstborn must also be given to the priest, a rule not stated explicitly elsewhere in the Torah
C. That only unblemished firstborns may ever be eaten
D. That the firstborn may only be eaten by the Kohen Gadol
Correct: B — The school of Rav reads the repeated phrase as teaching that even a blemished firstborn must be given to the priest — a halakha that otherwise has no explicit source anywhere in the Torah.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
In the dilemma about extra days beyond the firstborn's first year, what does the baraita about a blemish appearing on day 15 ultimately prove, and what happens to Rabbi Elazar's specific claim?
A. It proves a full fresh 30 days is always granted, and Rabbi Elazar's claim is accepted
B. It proves only that existing days are 'completed' to a total of 30, and Rabbi Elazar's claim that the days run from when the blemish appeared is refuted
C. It proves nothing relevant to the question
D. It proves the owner never gets any extra time at all
Correct: B — The baraita shows that only a completion to a total of 30 days is granted, not a fresh 30-day grant from the blemish's appearance — directly refuting Rabbi Elazar's more generous reading, which the Gemara calls a תְּיוּבְתָּא.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
Why does everyone agree that a cornea blemish examined only after the firstborn's slaughter renders it forbidden, regardless of the Rabbi Meir/Rabbi Yehuda dispute?
A. Because cornea blemishes are never valid grounds for slaughter
B. Because such blemishes can visibly change after death, making it impossible to verify retroactively whether they existed as claimed while the animal was alive
C. Because the Torah explicitly forbids all eye blemishes
D. Because Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yehuda never discuss cornea blemishes
Correct: B — The Gemara explains that cornea blemishes are uniquely unreliable after death since their appearance can change, so no one can be certain after the fact whether the blemish was genuine and permanent while the animal lived.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Rav Huna bar Manoaḥ, why does a non-expert pay only one-quarter the value of a mistakenly-slaughtered small animal, but one-half for a large animal?
A. Because small animals are worth more than large ones
B. Because a rabbinic decree already restricts where small-livestock owners may graze their flocks, sparing the priest some effort and justifying the lower payment
C. Because large animals are never subject to this payment rule
D. Because the difference reflects the animal's age, not its size
Correct: B — Rav Huna bar Manoaḥ explains that the decree restricting small-livestock grazing already spares the priest some burden regarding such animals, which is factored into requiring only a quarter-share payment rather than the half-share required for larger animals.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
What two separate reasons does Rabbi Akiva give for why Rabbi Tarfon need not pay for the cow he mistakenly ruled a tereifa?
A. Both reasons concern only Rabbi Tarfon's wealth and status
B. An error contradicting an explicit Mishna is automatically void, and separately, an expert accepted by the court is exempt even for errors in judgment
C. Rabbi Akiva gives only one reason, not two
D. Both reasons rely solely on Todos the physician's testimony
Correct: B — Rabbi Akiva offers two independent grounds: first, that an error against an explicit mishnaic ruling is automatically void; second, that even an error in legal reasoning is protected by the general exemption granted to an expert accepted by the court.
