QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to Rava, why does the Torah need to write both 'man' (ish) and 'cataract' (dak) regarding disqualifying eye blemishes?
A. The terms are redundant and only one was truly needed
B. 'Man' alone might suggest only conditions causing total blindness; 'cataract' is needed to include conditions that merely impair vision without total blindness
C. Both terms refer to the same exact condition
D. 'Man' refers to age and 'dak' refers to gender
Correct: B — Rava explains each term expands the category: 'man' includes conditions like pale spots that impair sight without removing the eye; 'cataract' further includes merely impaired (not totally absent) vision.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
How did Rav resolve the apparent contradiction between saying Moses was 'ten cubits tall' and the mishna's disqualification of a priest whose body is disproportionate to his limbs?
A. He retracted his statement entirely
B. He clarified that he meant cubits of the Tabernacle's measure, not Moses' own forearm-length — Moses' limbs remained fully proportional to his body
C. He said Moses was exempt from all blemish laws
D. He claimed the mishna doesn't apply to non-priests
Correct: B — Rav explained that 'ten cubits' referred to the Sanctuary's cubit measurement, not a comparison to Moses' own body parts, so Moses was not actually disproportionate.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to the Gemara's discussion of health and urination, why might a Torah scholar urinate in public despite concerns of dignity?
A. Dignity concerns never apply to Torah scholars
B. Holding back urination poses a genuine risk to health, so avoiding that danger takes precedence over modesty concerns, unlike drinking water publicly which is purely a matter of decorum
C. It is required by Jewish law at all times
D. Only priests are permitted to do this
Correct: B — The Gemara establishes that holding back urination causes real physical harm (even citing cases of resulting illness), so avoiding that harm outweighs concerns of public dignity, unlike drinking water in public which carries no such danger.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
What is the key difference between removing an animal's horns with their bone versus removing its hooves with their bone, according to Rav Ḥisda?
A. There is no difference; both cases are treated identically
B. Removing horns with bone disqualifies from the altar but is NOT a full blemish (not redeemable); removing hooves with bone disqualifies AND is a full, redeemable blemish
C. Horns can never be removed under any circumstance
D. Hooves removal is never considered any kind of blemish
Correct: B — Rav Ḥisda distinguishes: horn removal with bone disqualifies the animal from sacrifice but doesn't count as a full blemish enabling redemption, while hoof removal with bone is a genuine, redeemable blemish.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
How does Rabbi Ḥanina ben Antigonus derive that 'mero'aḥ ashekh' refers to a person with unusually dark skin, rather than a testicular condition?
A. He simply rejects the Torah's wording entirely
B. By rearranging the letters of the phrase (מְרוֹחַ אָשֶׁךְ into מַרְאָיו חֲשׁוּכִין), relying on the principle that Sages may add, subtract, and homiletically reinterpret letters
C. By citing a completely unrelated verse
D. He agrees with Rabbi Akiva's reading exactly
Correct: B — Rabbi Ḥanina ben Antigonus uses a letter-rearrangement technique (adding/subtracting letters homiletically) to read the phrase as referring to dark complexion rather than a testicular condition.
