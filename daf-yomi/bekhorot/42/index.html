📖 Overview: Chasing the Tumtum Through Text After Text

Bekhorot 42a–42b
לֹא אָמְרוּ טוּמְטוּם סָפֵק אֶלָּא בְּאָדָם... אֲבָל בְּהֵמָה — מֵטִיל מַיִם בְּמָקוֹם זַכְרוּת זָכָר
The scene: The Gemara searches, baraita after baraita, for proof of whether a tumtum animal is a genuine case of doubt (definitely male or female, just unknown which) or, like a hermaphrodite, a distinct category altogether — and each time it must retreat, editing 'tumtum' out of the source to make it fit. A key resolution comes from Reish Lakish: unlike a human tumtum, an animal tumtum isn't really uncertain at all, since its urination reveals which sex organ is truly present. The daf then extends this same question — whether an unusual physical state implies further hidden abnormalities — into the very different context of a human tumtum's levirate marriage obligations, reconciling two contradictory baraitot through two separate tannaitic disputes.

🗑️ The Recurring Move: 'Omit the Tumtum From Here'

Baraita after baraita seems to prove a tumtum is disqualified only as a genuine doubt — but each proof overreaches
1️⃣ A baraita on valuations (Lev. 27:3–4) excludes 'tumtum and hermaphrodite' from being valued as a certain male or female
↓
2️⃣ Another baraita on peace offerings, another on burnt offerings — each lists 'tumtum and hermaphrodite' together as excluded
↓
3️⃣ Each time, the Gemara must respond the same way: 'Omit the tumtum from here' (סְמִי מִיכָּן טוּמְטוּם) — the source really only speaks of the hermaphrodite, whose exclusion is undisputed
↓
4️⃣ One version even lists 'five things that cannot become consecrated' including tumtum — forcing the Gemara to swap in a different fifth item (an orphaned animal) once tumtum is removed
Why this pattern? Since a tumtum's own status is only DOUBTFUL, no proof-text meant to definitively disqualify a known category can really be about it — every such text must actually mean the hermaphrodite.

🕊️ Bird Offerings: Does 'Male and Female' Language Matter?

Rabbi Eliezer's rule for reading exclusions in the Torah
First Tanna
A tumtum or hermaphrodite bird, if pinched (the bird-offering method), still transmits impurity like an unfit carcass — it's disqualified either way.
Rabbi Eliezer
Not so — such a bird does NOT transmit impurity, because it IS fit for the altar. My rule: wherever the Torah explicitly says 'male' and 'female,' you exclude a tumtum and hermaphrodite. But for BIRD offerings, the Torah never uses those terms — just 'doves and pigeons' — so nothing excludes them there.
Gemara
But if a tumtum is definitely one sex or the other, why would the first tanna disqualify it at all?
Resolution
Once again — omit the tumtum from this baraita; it is discussing the hermaphrodite alone.
The rule that survives: Where 'male/female' language appears, uncertainty (tumtum) or duality (hermaphrodite) is excluded by definition; where it doesn't, no such exclusion applies.

🐄 Animal Tumtum vs. Human Tumtum

Reish Lakish's key distinction resolves the entire debate
CaseStatusWhy
Human tumtumGenuinely uncertainMale and female organs occupy the SAME location in a person — concealed skin creates true doubt
Animal tumtum, urinates from male locationDefinitely maleSex organs are in DIFFERENT locations for animals — the urination reveals the truth
Animal tumtum, urinates from female locationDefinitely female (R' Shimon b. Yehuda) / possibly still uncertain (Rabbis)Rabbis worry the male organ may be internally inverted toward the female location
Rabbi Elazar's ruling: An animal that urinates from the female location is treated as definitely non-sacred (not a firstborn) — following Reish Lakish's principle that animal tumtum status is not truly uncertain.

🔄 Could the Organ Itself Be Hidden and Inverted?

Rav Oshaya pushes back, and a broader principle about change emerges
❓ Rav Oshaya objects: shouldn't we worry the male organ turned inward toward the female location, so urinating from there doesn't prove it's really female?
↓
💡 Abaye: that's only a concern for Rabbi Meir, who worries about minority possibilities — halakha doesn't follow him, so we go with the majority, where this doesn't happen
↓
Abaye bar Avin / R' Ḥananya bar Avin
Even per the Rabbis, this case differs: since the animal ALREADY changed by being a tumtum, perhaps it changed further too — 'since it changed, it changed'
Open dispute
Some Sages accept this extended concern; others reject it entirely
The open question: Does an initial abnormality make further hidden abnormalities more likely to suspect? The Sages are divided on this very principle.

👰 The Human Tumtum's Levirate Marriage Obligations

Two contradictory baraitot, reconciled through two layered disputes
BaraitaRulingExplanation
Baraita 1Tumtum performs ḥalitza; brothers may do ḥalitza OR levirate marriage with his wifeFollows Rabbi Eliezer: even a natural eunuch's wife may undergo levirate marriage
Baraita 2Tumtum's wife performs ḥalitza only, never levirate marriageFollows Rabbi Akiva: a natural eunuch's wife may NOT undergo levirate marriage — and 'since it changed, it changed' extends the eunuch concern to the tumtum
Rabbi Yehuda's view (elsewhere)A tumtum found to be male upon examination does not perform ḥalitza at all — he's like a definite eunuchToo certain a position to be the source of this dispute
Rabbi Yosei b'Rabbi Yehuda's viewA tumtum should avoid ḥalitza, fearing he might turn out female OR a natural eunuchThe better fit for the stricter baraita — his ruling stems from compounded uncertainty, not certainty
Underlying theme: The same logic used for animal tumtum blemishes — does an unusual condition suggest further hidden ones? — resurfaces in the very different context of human marital law.
