Bekhorot 23 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to Ulla, why did the Sages decree that a fully nullified mixture of teruma and non-sacred produce may not touch water?
A. Because water always creates new impurity from nothing
B. Lest someone deliberately add more impure produce, mistakenly believing the majority will nullify it too, when the existing trace could 'revive' the impurity
C. Because teruma may never touch any liquid under any circumstances
D. Because the mixture is always considered fully sacred
Correct: B — Ulla explains this as a rabbinic decree guarding against someone adding a similar impure substance and assuming it too gets nullified, when in fact the surviving trace of the original impurity can 'find its own kind' and become active again.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to Rabbi Yosei ben Rabbi Ḥanina, what is the status of a mixture of red heifer ashes and ordinary ashes where the ordinary ashes are the majority?
A. Impure by both contact and carrying
B. Pure by contact, but still imparts impurity via carrying
C. Fully pure with no impurity whatsoever
D. The mixture must be buried immediately
Correct: B — Rabbi Yosei ben Rabbi Ḥanina rules that although the mixture does not impart impurity through direct contact (since one is assumed to touch the majority), it still imparts impurity through carrying, since the minority of red heifer ash remains present.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to bar Padda, based on Deuteronomy 14:21, what determines when an item with severe impurity (like a stillbirth) stops imparting impurity?
A. When it is no longer fit to be eaten by a dog
B. When it is no longer fit to be eaten by a ger toshav (resident alien permitted such food)
C. Impurity from such an item never ends
D. It stops immediately upon death
Correct: B — Bar Padda derives from the verse 'you may give it to the ger' that only an item fit for a ger toshav retains the halakhic label 'carcass' and its impurity; severe impurity therefore persists until the item is no longer fit even for a ger toshav.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
Why does the Gemara explain that the mass of congealed blood's purity comes specifically from 'nullification,' rather than simply saying it was 'never fit for consumption'?
A. Because it is currently being eaten by people
B. Because it had once been fit for consumption via its mother, had she been slaughtered before it emerged
C. Because the mishna never actually discusses its edibility
D. Because nullification and 'never fit' mean the exact same thing
Correct: B — The Gemara clarifies that the mass was initially fit for consumption as part of its mother's body (had she been slaughtered first), so its current lack of status requires the active mechanism of nullification rather than a claim that it was inherently unfit from the start.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov's ruling, what happens to pure brine purchased from a gentile if any amount of water falls into it?
A. Nothing changes — the brine remains pure
B. The brine becomes impure
C. The water becomes pure instead
D. The mixture must be immediately discarded without further discussion
Correct: B — Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov rules that even a small amount of water falling into otherwise pure brine renders it impure, which the Gemara explains reflects a suspicion that unreliable sellers (amei ha'aretz) had already diluted the brine close to the tipping point.
