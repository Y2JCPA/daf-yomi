📖 Overview: When Does Nullification Truly Erase Impurity?

Bekhorot 23a–23b
מָצָא מִין אֶת מִינוֹ וְנֵיעוֹר
The scene: The daf pursues the question raised at the end of 22b: can impurity that was nullified within a majority ever be reawakened? Ulla's decree about teruma mixed into non-sacred produce, and a running set of objections from Abaye involving red heifer ashes and carcass meat, test whether nullification is a permanent erasure or only a suppression that a new 'match' can revive — a question that circles back directly to the mass of congealed blood from the previous daf. A related dispute between bar Padda and Rabbi Yoḥanan asks how long a decaying carcass keeps transmitting impurity at all. The Gemara then applies these same principles to brine purchased from an unreliable seller, before the daf's new mishna turns to a very different concern: purchasing a nursing animal from a gentile, and whether one must worry its milk actually belongs to another mother's offspring.

♻️ Can Nullified Impurity Ever Revive?

Ulla's decree launches a chain of challenges from Abaye
Ulla
Even a nullified teruma-in-chullin mixture may not touch water — a decree lest someone deliberately add more impure produce, thinking the majority will nullify it too, when in fact the small existing trace 'finds its own kind' and revives.
Abaye
That proves impurity can revive impurity. But does pure water revive impurity, as claimed for the fish brine?
Abaye's Challenge
From red heifer ashes mixed with ordinary ashes: if nullified impurity still 'exists' in some sense, the mixture should at least impart impurity through carrying, even if not by touch!
Rav Dimi
Indeed — Rabbi Yosei b. Rabbi Ḥanina rules exactly that: pure from impurity by contact, but impure via carrying.
Abaye's Challenge
Then apply the same logic to carcass meat nullified within slaughtered meat — it too should impart impurity via carrying!
Final Challenge
But our own mishna states the mass of congealed blood does not impart impurity even via carrying! Rav Dimi had no answer — and fell silent.
Left open: The question of whether nullification is total or reversible remains unresolved at this point in the sugya.

🐕 How Long Does a Carcass Keep Imparting Impurity?

Bar Padda and Rabbi Yoḥanan dispute when decay ends impurity

Bar Padda — Two Standards

  • Severe impurity (e.g. a stillbirth) lasts until the item is no longer fit even for a ger toshav (resident alien permitted such food)
  • Light impurity (an ordinary carcass) lasts only until unfit for a dog
  • Derived from Deuteronomy 14:21: 'you may give it to the ger' — only what's fit for a ger is called 'a carcass' at all

Rabbi Yoḥanan — One Standard

  • Both severe and light impurity last only until the item is no longer fit for a dog
  • The verse instead excludes only meat that decayed from the very start, before ever becoming edible

The Difficulty

  • On Rabbi Yoḥanan's view, since the congealed mass IS still fit for a dog, it should still impart impurity
  • Yet the mishna rules it does not — the Gemara leaves this 'קַשְׁיָא', a genuine difficulty
Shared source: Both opinions read the same verse — 'you shall not eat of any carcass... you may give it to the ger' — but draw different lines from it.

🩸 Why 'Nullified,' Not 'Never Fit'?

Returning to the mass of congealed blood with a sharper question
❓ Rabbi Yoḥanan attributes the mass's purity to nullification in a majority. Why not simply say it was never fit for consumption at all?
↓
🔎 Because it WAS fit for consumption at one point — through its mother
↓
✅ Had the mother been slaughtered before the mass emerged, it would have been edible as ordinary meat, as part of her body
↓
🎓 So its later loss of edibility requires the active mechanism of nullification, not a claim that it was 'always' unfit
Why this matters: The distinction determines whether the mass could ever regain significance — an item 'never fit' has no such potential, but a nullified item might, as the earlier debate over reviving impurity shows.

🧂 Brine and the Suspected Peddlers

Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov: pure brine bought from a gentile, with any water added, becomes impure
StepExplanation
The rulingPure brine into which any amount of water falls becomes impure
Rav Naḥman's inferenceThis shows amei ha'aretz (unreliable sellers) are suspected of secretly mixing roughly half water into the brine they sell
The questionWhy assume specifically 'half'? Even less water mixed in should also revive the impurity!
Answer 1Say instead they're suspected of mixing in water up to just under half
Answer 2Both layers of impurity here (an am ha'aretz's food, and liquids) are only rabbinic; the Sages decreed impurity only for a true majority of water, not for an even half-half split
Practical upshot: A tiny addition of water is enough to revive suspected impurity, precisely because the seller is assumed to have already diluted the brine close to the tipping point.

🐑 A Nursing Animal — and Rav Sheshet's Process of Elimination

New mishna, then a puzzle over what Rav's blanket ruling could possibly mean
1️⃣ New mishna: Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel rules one who buys a nursing animal from a gentile — or sees mixed first-time and repeat mothers nursing in his own flock — need not worry the offspring were swapped
↓
2️⃣ Rav (via Rav Naḥman): 'The halakha follows the anonymous first tanna throughout this whole chapter, except where a dispute is recorded'
↓
3️⃣ Rav Sheshet objects: which mishna could this mean? Not the chapter's opening mishna — Rabbi Yishmael and Rabbi Akiva openly disagree there
↓
4️⃣ Not Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov's ruling either — his opinions are already known to be reliably authoritative ('a kav that is small but clean')
↓
5️⃣ Not Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel's ruling as stated, since an outside baraita records a dissent — nor two later mishnayot, each already resolved or disputed on its own
↓
6️⃣ Conclusion: it must be Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel's ruling after all — Rav teaches that a dissent recorded only in an outside baraita doesn't count as a real 'dispute' for this purpose
Method: Rav Sheshet works through every candidate mishna in the chapter by elimination before the Gemara lands on the answer.
