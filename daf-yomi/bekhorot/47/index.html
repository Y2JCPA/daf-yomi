📖 Overview: Who Counts as a Firstborn?

Bekhorot 47a–47b
הָיוּ לוֹ בָּנִים בְּגַיּוּתוֹ וְנִתְגַּיֵּיר — רַבִּי יוֹחָנָן אוֹמֵר: אֵין לוֹ בְּכוֹר לַנַּחֲלָה
The scene: The daf opens with a dispute between Rabbi Yoḥanan and Reish Lakish: if a man fathered sons as a gentile and then converted, does his first son born after conversion count as a firstborn for the double inheritance portion? The same disagreement resurfaces over whether fathering children before conversion already fulfilled 'be fruitful and multiply.' The Gemara then works through mishnayot about mixed lineage — a convert's or maidservant's child, a Levite's or priest's daughter impregnated by a gentile — asking whose status determines the son's obligations. The daf ends with a new mishna: which offspring is a firstborn for both inheritance and priestly redemption, and the special case of a child born by caesarean section.

🔄 Does a Pre-Conversion Son Block Firstborn Status?

Rabbi Yoḥanan and Reish Lakish on the convert's later-born son
R' Yochanan
He has no firstborn for inheritance — he already had רֵאשִׁית אוֹנוֹ, 'the first of his strength,' before he converted.
Reish Lakish
He does have a firstborn — a convert who just converted is legally like a newborn child. His gentile-born sons don't count at all.
R' Yochanan
Consistently, I say he already fulfilled 'be fruitful and multiply' through those earlier sons — the verse just wants the world inhabited.
Reish Lakish
Consistently, I say he has not fulfilled it — as a new legal person, it is as though he never had children.
Why state both disputes? Each case needed independent teaching: one might have conceded inheritance but not fruitfulness, or vice versa, so the Gemara spells out both.

🕎 Whose Womb, Whose Status?

Tracing the mishna's redemption exemptions to their source
CaseRulingAmora's explanation
Levite's daughter, pregnant by a gentileSon exempt from 5 selaRav Pappa: even those who 'disqualify' the child still follow the mother's tribe
Levite's daughter, pregnant by an IsraeliteDisputed — Mar b. Rav Yosef says exempt'Opening the womb' (פֶּטֶר רֶחֶם) hangs the law on the mother
Priest's daughter, pregnant by a gentileDebatedIf a gentile has relations with her she becomes like a non-priest — so why exempt her son?
Rabba's resolution: A Levite woman retains her sanctity even after captivity or illicit relations and still eats tithe; a priest's daughter who has relations with a gentile loses her ability to eat teruma entirely — so the cases are not parallel.

⚖️ Must a Disqualified Priest's Son Redeem Himself?

Rav Ḥisda and Rabba bar Rav Huna on a ḥalal whose father died young
❓ A priest married a divorcée, had a son (a ḥalal), then died. Must the son redeem himself?
↓
👨 Father died after 30 days: everyone agrees the son need not redeem himself — the father already 'acquired' the redemption as a priest
↓
Rav Ḥisda
Father died within 30 days — son must redeem himself, since his father never acquired the money
Rabba bar Rav Huna
Son need not — he can say 'I stand in the shoes of a man you could never have sued'
↓
🌍 Challenge: a convert's son born pregnant does redeem himself, though his gentile father could never be sued — 'a gentile has no lineage' answers the difficulty
Later ruling: Reish Lakish, cited by Rabbi Shimon Yasinia, sides with Rav Ḥisda: within 30 days the son must redeem himself; after 30 days he need not.

🍼 Mishna: Caesarean Birth and the Son Who Follows

Neither is a firstborn — but Rabbi Shimon splits the difference

The Caesarean Child

  • Not firstborn for inheritance — 'and they bore him children' requires a normal birth
  • Not firstborn for redemption — 'opens the womb' requires opening it

The Son Who Follows

  • Not firstborn for inheritance — not 'the first of his strength'
  • Not firstborn for redemption — a firstborn 'for one matter only' is not a firstborn at all, per the first tanna

Rabbi Shimon's View

  • The caesarean child IS firstborn for inheritance — 'she bears' (Lev. 12:5) includes caesarean birth
  • The son who follows IS firstborn for redemption — a firstborn 'for one matter' still counts
Also in the mishna: A woman miscarrying a water-sac, blood-sac, fish-like or grasshopper-like mass, or one who miscarries by day 40, does not disqualify the next son from full firstborn status.

🔀 Establishing an Uncertain Firstborn's Claims

How the mishna's ruling on inheritance authorization gets explained
1️⃣ A woman who didn't wait three months, remarried, and gave birth — unclear if 9-month son of husband #1 or 7-month son of husband #2
↓
2️⃣ Mishna: firstborn for priestly redemption, but not for inheritance — yet he still takes a share like an ordinary son
↓
3️⃣ Rabbi Yirmeya: the ruling really targets the NEXT son (from husband #2) — he is not the firstborn, since this uncertain son might outrank him
↓
4️⃣ Rabbi Yannai's principle: sons once individually recognized, later intermingled, may write authorization to each other to jointly claim the double portion
Why authorization works here: Because each son was clearly identified before the confusion arose, each can act as the other's agent to claim what one of them certainly deserves.
