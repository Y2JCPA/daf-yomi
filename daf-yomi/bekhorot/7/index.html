📖 Overview: Resolving Old Dilemmas, Opening New Ground

Bekhorot 7a–7b
שֶׁהַיּוֹצֵא מִן הַטָּמֵא — טָמֵא, וְהַיּוֹצֵא מִן הַטָּהוֹר — טָהוֹר
The scene: The daf resolves yesterday's open question — does Rabbi Shimon's strict 'head and majority resembling the mother' standard apply to eating, or only to firstborn status? — before tackling a baraita where Rabbi Eliezer and Rabbi Yehoshua trade seemingly reversed positions on offspring of mixed parentage. From there the Gemara applies the mishna's 'follows the mother' principle to edge cases: a donkey's turbid urine, bee honey, and the membrane surrounding a newborn. A new mishna then asks what happens when one fish swallows another — and how we can tell swallowing from spawning.

🐫 Resolving Rabbi Shimon's Dilemma

Does 'head and majority' govern eating, or only firstborn status?
Attempted proofConclusion
A baraita mentions eating, then says 'if head and majority resemble the mother, obligated in firstborn status'Rejected — the baraita simply shifts topics from eating to firstborn status; it proves nothing about the eating standard
A direct baraita: R' Shimon forbids a camel born of a cow, but if its head and majority resemble the mother, it becomes permitted to eatAccepted — this proves Rabbi Shimon applies the same strict standard to eating as to firstborn status
Conclusion: שְׁמַע מִינַּהּ — Rabbi Shimon requires 'head and majority resembling the mother' in both areas; mere partial resemblance never suffices for him.

⚖️ 'This and That Cause It' — Mixed Parentage

A kosher mother, a non-kosher father: is the odd-looking offspring permitted?
R' Yehoshua
The offspring is permitted only if both parents are kosher of the matching kind — the plural 'seh kesavim, seh izzim' teaches: father a ram, mother a ewe.
R' Eliezer
No — the verse doesn't merely permit what's already obvious. It adds a case: a non-kosher-looking creature born to a kosher mother but sired by a non-kosher father is also permitted.
Gemara
But that's backward! Elsewhere, on a tereifa's offspring, Rabbi Eliezer is the strict one and Rabbi Yehoshua the lenient one!
Resolution
Each follows his general rule except here, where this verse's unusual wording overrides it: R' Eliezer reads an extra 'seh' as leniency; R' Yehoshua reads the plural as stringency.

🐴 Rav Sheshet's Donkey Urine Dilemma

Turbid like milk, or merely water in and water out?
1️⃣ Question: is a donkey's urine forbidden like its milk? It's turbid, unlike horse or camel urine, which resembles plain water
↓
2️⃣ First answer: the mishna says something emerges 'from (מִן) the non-kosher animal,' not merely 'out of' its body — a broader term covering any substance connected to it, including turbid urine
↓
3️⃣ Challenge: but bees are non-kosher, and their honey is permitted! Not everything 'from' a non-kosher creature is forbidden
↓
4️⃣ Resolved: honey is a unique Torah exception (Rabbi Ya'akov's reading of 'these you may eat') — bees merely gather nectar into their bodies, they do not produce it as a true bodily secretion the way milk or urine are produced

👶 The Birth Membrane: A Mere Secretion

עוֹר הַבָּא כְּנֶגֶד פָּנָיו שֶׁל חֲמוֹר מוּתָּר... פִּירְשָׁא בְּעָלְמָא הוּא
Rav Huna's ruling: Rav Huna rules that the membrane emerging opposite a donkey's face at birth is permitted to eat, since it is 'merely a secretion' — not integral to either mother or offspring, and so unaffected by the donkey's non-kosher status. Rav Ḥisda cites apparent support: a baraita ruling the same membrane on a human newborn ritually pure, whether the baby is alive or dead. But the two disagree about exactly which combination the baraita means — both alive, both dead, or one of each — and the exchange ends good-naturedly, with Rav Huna conceding: 'if it is taught, it is taught,' and he cannot dispute an established baraita.

🐟 New Mishna: A Fish Swallows a Fish

Kosher and non-kosher fish found inside one another
CaseRulingReason
Non-kosher fish swallows a kosher fishPermitted to eat the swallowed fishIt is simply a distinct kosher fish discovered inside — not the host's own offspring
Kosher fish swallows a non-kosher fishForbiddenIt is 'not its growth' (אֵינוֹ גִּידּוּלוֹ) — the swallowed fish keeps its own non-kosher status regardless of its host
The catch: Non-kosher fish spawn offspring while kosher fish lay eggs — so if no one saw the swallowing, the fish inside might be the host's own spawn. Rav Sheshet, Rav Pappa, Rav Naḥman, and Rav Ashi each explain, differently, how we can still be confident it was truly swallowed.
