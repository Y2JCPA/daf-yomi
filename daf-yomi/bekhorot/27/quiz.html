QUESTION 1 OF 5
Why may an owner accept payment for the right to direct his teruma to a chosen priest, but not for the priestly gifts (foreleg, jaw, maw)?
A. Because teruma is worth less money than the priestly gifts
B. Because teruma has inherent sanctity that can never be redeemed, so no one mistakes it for mundane food, while the priestly gifts have value-based sanctity a priest might think can be 'bought off'
C. Because priestly gifts may only be given to the Kohen Gadol
D. There is no real distinction between the two cases
Correct: B — The Gemara explains that teruma's inherent, non-redeemable sanctity prevents confusion, whereas the priestly gifts have sanctity tied to their monetary value, creating a risk the priest might treat them as ordinary once payment is involved.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to Shmuel, how does teruma from outside Eretz Yisrael differ from ordinary teruma regarding nullification and consumption during impurity?
A. It cannot be nullified at all, and may never be eaten during impurity
B. It is nullified by a mere majority rather than 1:100, and may be eaten by a priest even during his own days of ritual impurity
C. It follows identical rules to ordinary teruma in every respect
D. It may only be eaten by the Kohen Gadol
Correct: B — Shmuel rules that outside-Israel teruma is nullified in any majority (unlike the strict 100:1 ratio for ordinary teruma) and may be eaten by a priest even while he is ritually impure — both leniencies inapplicable to teruma from Eretz Yisrael.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
In the boat story, what did Rav Amram report that Rav ruled about one impure from corpse-contact eating outside-Israel teruma, and does the Gemara accept this as halakha?
A. Rav ruled it forbidden, and the Gemara agrees
B. Rav ruled that such a person may immerse and eat the teruma, but the Gemara concludes the halakha does not follow this leniency
C. Rav never issued any ruling on this question
D. The Gemara accepts the ruling without any reservation
Correct: B — Rav Amram reported that Rav permitted immersion alone to allow eating outside-Israel teruma even after corpse impurity, but the Gemara explicitly states that the halakha does not follow this leniency.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Rav's derivation from Deuteronomy 15:20 ('שָׁנָה בְשָׁנָה'), how is a firstborn animal's 'year' calculated?
A. From the first day of the Hebrew calendar year
B. From the animal's own birth date, spanning into the following calendar year
C. From the day it develops a blemish
D. From the day it is given to the priest
Correct: B — Rav reads 'year by year' as describing one year entering another, indicating that the firstborn's personal year is counted from its own birth and can span parts of two different calendar years.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to Rav's own derivation (as opposed to the school of Rav's), why is an unblemished firstborn eaten for two days and one night?
A. Because Deuteronomy 15:20 explicitly states this time period
B. Because Numbers 18:18 compares the firstborn's flesh to the breast and thigh of a peace offering, which is eaten for two days and a night
C. Because the firstborn must be eaten before Shabbat begins
D. There is no source for this ruling according to Rav
Correct: B — Rav derives the two-day-one-night eating period from Numbers 18:18, which compares the firstborn's meat to the peace offering's breast and thigh — a comparison that carries over the peace offering's own two-day-one-night eating window.
