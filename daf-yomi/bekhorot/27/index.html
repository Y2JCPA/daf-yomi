📖 Overview: From Teruma's Rules to the Firstborn's Calendar

Bekhorot 27a–27b
אֵיזוֹהִי שָׁנָה שֶׁנִּכְנֶסֶת בַּחֲבֶרְתָּהּ? הֱוֵי אוֹמֵר: שָׁנָה שֶׁל בְּכוֹר
The scene: The daf finishes the previous discussion of paying Temple workers with sacred gifts by drawing a distinction: an owner may accept payment for choosing which priest receives his teruma, but not for the priestly gifts. A cluster of leniencies specific to teruma grown outside Eretz Yisrael follows, illustrated by a vivid travel story involving Rav Naḥman, Rav Amram, and Rami bar Ḥama on a ferry. The Gemara then returns to the mishna's firstborn law, establishing that a firstborn's 'year' is counted from its own birth rather than the calendar year, and offering two independent derivations — one from Rav, one from the school of Rav — for why an unblemished firstborn may be eaten for two days and the night between them, just like the breast and thigh of a peace offering.

🎁 Owner's Discretion: Teruma vs. Priestly Gifts

Why one sacred gift may be 'sold' for the right to choose its recipient, and the other may not
GiftMay the Owner Accept Payment to Direct It?Why
TerumaYesIt has inherent sanctity (קְדוּשַּׁת הַגּוּף) and can never be redeemed — no one will mistake it for ordinary food, even after payment changes hands
Priestly gifts (foreleg, jaw, maw)NoTheir sanctity is value-based (קְדוּשַּׁת דָּמִים) — a priest might think he can 'redeem' them for cash and then treat them like non-sacred food
Underlying logic: The Gemara distinguishes gifts by how easily their sanctity could be mistaken for something purchasable and mundane.

🌍 Special Leniencies for Teruma Outside Eretz Yisrael

Teruma from outside the Land carries a lighter set of restrictions

Nullification & Impurity (Shmuel)

  • Nullified by a mere majority, unlike ordinary teruma's strict 1:100 ratio
  • May be eaten by a priest even during his own days of ritual impurity

Timing & Scope (Shmuel)

  • One may eat the produce first and separate teruma from what remains afterward
  • Forbidden only to a priest whose impurity comes from his own body — not from touching a corpse

Practical Application (Ravina)

  • A menstruating woman may separate ḥalla from dough baked outside Israel
  • It's fed to a pure minor priest, or a token piece is burned and another separated, so the halakha of ḥalla is not forgotten
  • An adult priest may eat the rest even while impure
Contrast: Rava adds that this teruma even avoids the prohibition against paying a priest-helper at the threshing floor with sacred produce.

⛴️ A Boat, a Question, and Two Rejected Leniencies

Rav Naḥman, Rav Amram, and Rami bar Ḥama, traveling together
A Woman
May one impure through corpse-contact immerse and then eat teruma from outside Eretz Yisrael?
R' Naḥman
But do we even have sprinkling of the red heifer's ashes? Without it, how would mere immersion help such a person?
Rami bar Ḥama
Let's wait — shouldn't we be concerned for the opinion of the old man, Rav Amram?
R' Amram (returning)
Rav himself said: one impure from a corpse may immerse and eat outside-Israel teruma.
Gemara's Verdict
The halakha does not follow this leniency — nor a similar one, cited by Mar Zutra in Rav Sheshet's name, for one impure from a creeping animal's carcass.
Lesson: Even generous leniencies for outside-Israel teruma have limits — corpse impurity remains too severe for immersion alone to resolve.

📅 Whose 'Year' Counts for a Firstborn?

Establishing that the firstborn's year runs from its own birth
1️⃣ The mishna's wording implies the firstborn's 'year' is counted from its own birth, not the calendar year
↓
2️⃣ Rav's proof: 'you shall eat it שָׁנָה בְשָׁנָה' (year by year) — read as one year entering another, describing an animal's personal year spanning two calendar years
↓
3️⃣ The school of Rav reads the same phrase differently: 'one day in this year, one day in that' — teaching an unblemished firstborn may be eaten for two days and the night between
↓
4️⃣ Their broader source: all sacrificial animals' ages are counted from birth, derived from 'a lamb in its own year' (Leviticus 12:6) — 'its year,' not the calendar's
Two schools, one conclusion: Rav and his own school reach the same practical rulings about timing through independent readings of related verses.

🍖 Two Days and One Night — Two Separate Derivations

Why an unblemished firstborn may be eaten like a peace offering
AuthoritySource VerseLogic
RavNumbers 18:18: 'their flesh shall be yours, as the breast of waving and as the right thigh'Directly compares the firstborn's meat to the peace offering's breast and thigh, which Torah permits for two days and a night
School of RavDeuteronomy 15:20 ('year by year'), reread as 'one day in this year, one day in that'Derives the same two-day-one-night period from the firstborn's own verse, without relying on the peace-offering comparison
Same outcome, different paths: Both derivations converge on the identical two-day-one-night eating window, but from entirely separate textual routes.
