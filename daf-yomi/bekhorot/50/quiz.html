QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to Rabbi Yoḥanan's final formula, how does one arrive at five redemption sela from worn Hadrianic/Trajanic dinars sold at 25 per unit?
A. Deduct one-sixth only
B. Deduct one dinar first, then one-sixth of the remaining 24
C. Deduct one-third
D. Add one dinar, then deduct one-sixth
Correct: B — After two imprecise attempts, the Gemara concludes: first deduct one dinar (leaving 24), then deduct one-sixth of that remainder, yielding exactly the correct value for five sela.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to Rava, how many dinars did the Torah's original sela weigh?
A. Four dinars
B. Three and one-third dinars
C. Six dinars
D. Two dinars
Correct: B — Rava derives from 'the shekel is twenty gera' (translated as twenty ma'a) and the rule that six ma'a equal one dinar, that the Torah's sela equals three and one-third dinars — before the Sages later added one-sixth to reach four.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
Why does the baraita about redeeming a Jubilee-year field count 48 pundeyon per sela, seemingly contradicting Rava's calculation of 20 ma'a per sela?
A. The baraita is discussing a different verse entirely
B. That baraita reflects coinage after the Sages added one-sixth to the sela's value
C. Pundeyon and ma'a are unrelated units
D. The baraita is simply incorrect
Correct: B — The Gemara explains that the baraita describes the sela's value after the Sages augmented it by one-sixth (four ma'a), whereas Rava's calculation reflects the Torah's original, unaugmented value of twenty ma'a.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
Why did the Sages initially seek to sequester (hide away) all the worn Hadrianic and Trajanic dinars in circulation?
A. Because those coins were counterfeit
B. Because they contained silver and gold that had been plundered from the Jerusalem Temple treasury
C. Because Roman coins were always forbidden
D. Because they lacked a mint mark
Correct: B — Abaye explains the concern was that these particular worn coins contained gold and silver looted from the Temple treasury; a verse in Ezekiel (7:22) about robbers profaning the sacred wealth ultimately permitted their continued use.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to Rav Yehuda citing Rav Asi (in its final, narrowed form), when does an unspecified sum of 'money' in the Torah refer to Tyrian coinage?
A. Every single mention of money anywhere in Tanakh
B. Only when the Torah sets a specific fixed amount, like the five sela of redemption
C. Only in cases involving betrothal
D. Never — it's always provincial coinage
Correct: B — After challenges from oath law, tithe, consecrated property, and betrothal, the rule is refined: only a Torah passage stating a SET, specific sum (like the five sela for redemption, or fifty shekels for a rapist) is understood as Tyrian coinage.
