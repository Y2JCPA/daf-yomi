📖 Overview: The Arithmetic of Redemption Money

Bekhorot 50a–50b
אָמַר רָבָא: סִילְעִים דְּאוֹרָיְיתָא תְּלָתָא וְתִילְתָּא הָוֵי
The scene: Having established that five sela of Tyrian coinage redeem a firstborn, the Gemara now works out the practical exchange rate against dinars in circulation, including worn Roman coins from Hadrian and Trajan. Rava argues the Torah's sela is actually three and a third dinars, not four, based on the twenty gera of a shekel — a claim tested against a baraita about redeeming a consecrated field. The daf then explores why old coinage bearing traces of Jerusalem's gold needed special scriptural permission to use, and closes with Rav Asi's broad rule distinguishing Torah-mandated payments (Tyrian coinage) from rabbinically set fines (provincial coinage), tested against betrothal, tithe, and consecrated property.

🧮 Rabbi Yoḥanan's Formula for Redemption Coins

Working out five sela from worn Hadrianic and Trajanic dinars
1️⃣ Take worn gold dinars of Hadrian and Trajan, sold at 25 dinars per unit
↓
2️⃣ First attempt: deduct one-sixth — but the math comes out short (21 less 1/6)
↓
3️⃣ Second attempt: deduct one-sixth and one more dinar — still imprecise
↓
4️⃣ Final formula: first deduct one dinar (leaving 24), THEN deduct one-sixth of that — yields exactly the five sela needed for redemption
Why so complicated: The Gemara works through several failed formulas before landing on the precise sequence, showing how carefully redemption value had to be calibrated.

⚖️ How Many Dinars Is a Torah Sela?

Rava's claim tested against a baraita on field redemption
Rava
The Torah's sela weighs three and a third dinars — the verse says 'the shekel is twenty gera,' translated as twenty ma'a, and six ma'a make a dinar.
Challenge
But a baraita on redeeming a Jubilee-year field counts 48 pundeyon per sela — that's 24 ma'a, not 20!
Resolution
That baraita describes coinage AFTER the Sages added one-sixth (four ma'a) to every sela — the Torah's original value was still 20 ma'a.
Support
A baraita confirms: the verse 'twenty gera shall be the shekel' permits adding to the value, but never reducing it below twenty.
Real-world echo: Rav Ashi sent 17 dinars for a redemption and asked for a third back; Rav Aḥa replied asking for three MORE dinars — each following a different accounting of the sela's value.

🏛️ Tyrian Coinage vs. Provincial Coinage

Rav Asi's rule, and where it gets refined

Unspecified Torah Money

  • Rav Yehuda cites Rav Asi: 'silver' in the Torah = Tyrian dinars (high value)
  • Applies to firstborn redemption, the ox-slave, rapist, seducer, defamer

Rabbinic Money

  • Money in statements of the Sages = provincial coinage (roughly 1/8 the value)
  • Example: the fine for striking someone is a provincial sela = half a dinar

Refined Version

  • Challenges from betrothal, tithe, and consecrated property force a narrower rule
  • Final version: only a SET, specific Torah sum is Tyrian; general mentions can vary

❓ Testing Rav Asi's Rule Against Other Halakhot

Four objections and how each is resolved
⚖️ Oath of partial admission needs 'two silver [coins]' — is that two Tyrian coins? No, it's derived from a juxtaposition to 'vessels,' not from Rav Asi's rule
↓
🌾 Second tithe money can be copper, not just silver — the doubled word 'money, money' is an amplification allowing any coinage
↓
🕊️ Consecrated property redeemed for a peruta's worth — derived by verbal analogy from tithe, not from Rav Asi's rule
↓
💍 Betrothal follows Beit Hillel's peruta, not Beit Shammai's dinar — so the rule is restated: only a SET Torah sum is Tyrian coinage
