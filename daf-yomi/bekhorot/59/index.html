📖 Overview: Automatic Sanctity and Fractional Counts

Bekhorot 59a–59b
אָמַר רָבָא: עֲשִׂירִי מֵאֵלָיו הוּא קָדוֹשׁ
The scene: Rava rules that the tenth animal becomes sanctified automatically once nine have emerged from the pen, even if the owner never explicitly calls it 'the tenth' or it remains inside. The Gemara works hard to locate Rava's actual source, testing and rejecting several baraitot before settling on a case where the ninth was mistakenly called 'tenth' and the true tenth died while still in the pen. Rava's companion principle — that 'a count fit to reach ten' exempts the counted animals even if the count is ultimately never completed — undergoes the same rigorous sourcing process. The daf closes with detailed baraitot working through complex scenarios: fourteen or nineteen lambs emerging through two different pen openings, and why one may never pre-select ten animals for tithing without actually running them through the counting process.

🔟 Does the Tenth Need to Be Named?

Rava's claim and the Gemara's four attempts to source it
Rava
The tenth animal is sanctified of its own accord — even if it stays in the pen and is never explicitly called 'tenth.'
Attempt 1
From the baraita: 'the tenth shall be sacred... in any case' — even unnamed. Rejected: maybe it means he called it 'sacred' just not by the specific word 'tenth.'
Attempt 2 & 3
From cases where the owner mistakenly called the ninth 'tenth' and stayed silent, or the ninth 'tenth' and the true tenth then died. Both rejected as distinguishable — perhaps selection or death changes things.
Final source
A baraita: ninth called 'tenth,' and the true tenth simply REMAINS in the pen — the ninth is eaten only with a blemish, and the TENTH IS TITHE. This works even without ever leaving or being named.

Whose opinion is really being cited?

📊 'A Count Fit to Reach Ten' Exempts

Rava's second principle, and its eventual textual source
❓ Where does Rava's rule come from — that animals counted as part of a group that COULD have reached ten are exempt, even if the count never finishes?
↓
🚫 Attempt: from the mishna where a counted animal jumps back into the pen, exempting all — but maybe that's because the tenth ALSO came out and was tithed on their behalf
↓
🚫 That's ruled out by the mishna's own wording distinguishing 'counted' animals from 'already tithed' animals
↓
✅ Final source: the verse says an animal must be one that WILL 'pass' under the rod — not one that has already effectively passed, i.e., already part of a fit-to-reach-ten count

🚪 Fourteen Lambs, Two Exits

Rava's detailed scenarios for the counting procedure

Scenario: 6 + 4, then 4 remain

  • If the last 4 exit through the 6's gate: take one as tithe; the other 4 (from the other gate) combine with a future gathering
  • If not: the first 6 are exempt (fit-to-ten); the other groups of 4 each combine with future gatherings

Scenario: 4 + 6, then 4 remain

  • If the last 4 exit through the 6's gate: take one as tithe; the first 4 are exempt
  • If not: the first 4 and the 6 are all exempt (fit-to-ten); the last 4 combine forward
Why so detailed: Rava's point: an UNCERTAIN fit-to-ten count (since the remaining animals COULD exit either gate) still exempts — not just a definite one, which he had already stated once.

🐑 No Pre-Selecting Before the Count

Why all animals must pass through the pen together
1️⃣ Rava: with fifteen lambs, one may NOT separate out ten in advance, bring only those into the pen, and take one as tithe
↓
2️⃣ Reason (implicit): the count must reflect the animal's true position among ALL the candidates, not an artificially pre-selected subset
↓
3️⃣ Proper method: bring all fifteen into the pen, let ten emerge naturally, and take the tenth as tithe
↓
4️⃣ The remaining five combine with animals born later, for tithing at the next gathering time
