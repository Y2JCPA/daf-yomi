QUESTION 1 OF 5
What does Rav Pappa clarify about the mishna's blemish of 'feet wide like a goose's'?
A. It applies only if the toes are completely unsplit, like a true goose foot
B. It applies once the feet are flat, even if the toes remain somewhat split
C. It never applies to human feet
D. It applies only to the left foot
Correct: B — Rav Pappa extends the blemish to any flat-footed condition, even if the toes retain some separation, rather than requiring an exact match to a goose's completely unsplit foot.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
Why does Rabbi Yoḥanan call the halakha about an extra digit's purity status 'like words of prophecy'?
A. Because it was revealed through prophetic vision
B. Because it defies logical consistency — an extra digit imparts impurity by touch/carrying but not in a tent, when logically it should be either fully a limb (all three types of impurity) or not a limb at all (none)
C. Because it concerns future events
D. Because he disagreed with all the Sages on this point
Correct: B — Rabbi Yoḥanan points out the apparent contradiction: if it counts as a limb it should impart tent-impurity too, and if it doesn't count as a limb it shouldn't impart any impurity — yet the halakha does neither consistently, defying ordinary reasoning.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to the story about Rabbi Yishmael's students, why did they find 252 bones instead of the standard 248 when examining a corpse?
A. They miscounted due to poor lighting
B. Shmuel explained they had examined a woman's corpse, which has four additional parts (two 'hinges' and two 'doors') derived from verses about childbirth and the womb
C. The corpse had extra fingers
D. They counted teeth incorrectly
Correct: B — Shmuel explained the discrepancy by noting that a woman's body has four additional structural elements beyond the standard 248 bones, based on verses comparing childbirth to hinges and doors.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
How does Rav Pappa resolve the apparent contradiction between the mishna disqualifying a 'kipe'aḥ' (very tall priest) and the Torah's praise of tall stature (Amos 2:9)?
A. He says the mishna is simply incorrect
B. The disqualifying kipe'aḥ refers to one who is tall AND gaunt/unhealthy-looking, not simply tall with a healthy build, which the Torah does praise
C. Height is never a disqualifying factor at all
D. Only priests under a certain height are disqualified
Correct: B — Rav Pappa distinguishes between healthy tallness (praised) and the mishna's kipe'aḥ, which specifically describes someone unnaturally long, thin, and unattractive.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to the mishna's final comparison, which category of flaw disqualifies an ANIMAL from sacrifice but does NOT disqualify a priest from Temple service?
A. Extreme skin tone
B. Being a dwarf
C. Being a tereifa, born by caesarean section, or involved in bestiality
D. Being deaf-mute
Correct: C — The mishna lists tereifa status, caesarean birth, involvement in a transgression, and killing a person as flaws disqualifying only an animal — while extreme skin tone, dwarfism, and deaf-muteness disqualify only a person, not an animal.
