📖 Overview: Limbs, Bones, and Complexions

Bekhorot 45a–45b
עָשׂוּ דִּבְרֵיהֶם כְּדִבְרֵי נְבִיאוּת, מָה נַפְשָׁךְ? אִי אֵבֶר הוּא — בְּאֹהֶל נָמֵי תְּטַמֵּא
The scene: The mishna continues cataloguing blemishes that disqualify a priest: bowed legs, protruding heels, webbed or extra fingers and toes. A tangent into the purity laws of an extra digit leads Rabbi Yoḥanan to call the resulting halakha inexplicable, 'like words of prophecy.' This opens into a broader digression on the 248 bones of the human body, a startling story about the corpse of an executed woman, and a rabbinic dispute over what physical features are uniquely female. The daf then returns to disqualifying features — extreme skin tones, height, and Reish Lakish's eugenic marriage advice — before closing with the mishna's careful distinction between flaws that disqualify only a person and flaws that disqualify only an animal.

🖐️ The Extra Digit's Baffling Purity Status

Rabbi Yoḥanan: this halakha has no logical explanation at all
1️⃣ An extra digit WITH a bone but WITHOUT a nail, from a corpse: imparts impurity through touch and carrying, but — oddly — NOT through being in the same tent
↓
2️⃣ Rabbi Yoḥanan objects: this makes no sense! If it's a true 'limb,' it should impart tent-impurity too; if it's NOT a limb, it shouldn't impart impurity even by touch!
↓
3️⃣ Two attempted resolutions: Rav Aḥa's view treats it like a mere 'barley-sized bone fragment' (which imparts touch-impurity only); Rav Pappa's view says it's a rabbinic decree, made to avoid confusion with a TRUE extra limb (one counted in the row)
↓
4️⃣ Practical purpose: the Sages deliberately made this impurity milder than Torah law would require for a real limb, specifically so people would know NOT to burn contaminated teruma or consecrated items over it
Rabbi Yoḥanan's verdict: 'The Sages made their words like words of prophecy' — meaning this ruling simply defies ordinary logical categories.

🦴 248 Bones, and What Makes a Woman Different

A gruesome case tests the Sages' anatomical count
Story
Rabbi Yishmael's students boiled the flesh off an executed woman's corpse to count her bones — and found 252, not the standard 248!
Shmuel
You must have examined a WOMAN — Scripture adds her four extra parts: two 'hinges' and two 'doors.'
R' Elazar / R' Yehoshua / R' Akiva
Each derives one such part from a verse: 'hinges' (1 Samuel 4:19, childbirth pangs), 'doors' (Job 3:10, the womb), and — per R' Akiva — a 'key' too (Genesis 30:22)
Challenge
But the students found only 4 extra, not 5 (no 'key')! Perhaps the tiny key-bone simply dissolved while boiling.
Rav / Abaye
None of these extra parts impart tent-impurity anyway, since Numbers 19:14 requires something 'equal for all people' — and these unique 'hinges' are just flesh, not bone, in either sex.
Why it matters: This tangent, springing from the extra-digit purity puzzle, roots the entire count of 248 human bones in real (if grim) forensic testing.

🎨 Skin, Height, and Reish Lakish's Marriage Advice

New mishna: extreme physical traits that disqualify a priest but not an animal

Skin Tone Extremes

  • Kushi: extremely dark skin
  • Giḥor: extremely red skin
  • Lavkan: extremely pale/white skin

Kipe'aḥ (Height)

  • Rav Zevid: extremely tall — but doesn't the Torah praise tall stature (Amos 2:9)?
  • Rav Pappa's resolution: only disqualifies if tall AND gaunt/unhealthy-looking, not simply tall and robust

Reish Lakish's Advice

  • Two tall people shouldn't marry — risk of an excessively tall child
  • Same logic for two short, two pale, or two dark-skinned people — avoid amplifying extremes in offspring
Note on the drunk priest: The mishna's 'drunk' refers specifically to intoxication from something OTHER than wine — wine-drunkenness actually profanes the service itself (Rabbi Yehuda even extends this to milk or honey).
