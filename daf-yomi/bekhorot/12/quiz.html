5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to ben Bag Bag, from where is the mishna's list of animals unfit to redeem a firstborn donkey derived?
A. From a direct verse listing each animal by name
B. From a verbal analogy between the word 'lamb' used for redemption and the same word used for the Paschal offering
C. From an oral tradition with no textual source
D. From the laws of animal tithes
Correct: B — Ben Bag Bag derives the exclusion list via a verbal analogy: since 'lamb' (seh) in the Paschal offering excludes these categories of animals, the same word used for redeeming a firstborn donkey excludes them too.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
What is the dispute between Mar Zutra and Rav Ashi regarding redeeming a firstborn donkey with a ben pekua (a lamb removed alive from its slaughtered mother)?
A. Both permit it without dispute
B. Mar Zutra forbids it, treating it as already-slaughtered meat; Rav Ashi permits it since it currently lives and runs
C. Both forbid it entirely
D. The dispute concerns only its monetary value
Correct: B — Mar Zutra treats the ben pekua as equivalent to slaughtered meat and forbids using it for redemption, while Rav Ashi holds that since it is alive and moving, it qualifies as a genuine lamb.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to Rav Naḥman, what does the requirement that a priest redeem a firstborn donkey in the owner's presence actually teach?
A. That priests cannot be trusted with any monetary transactions
B. That priests in general are suspected of using a firstborn donkey without ever redeeming it, since the lamb remains theirs either way
C. That only some specific priests are untrustworthy
D. That the owner must always be a Levite
Correct: B — Rav Naḥman teaches this suspicion applies broadly to every priest, not just a known offender, since a priest benefits from keeping the donkey unredeemed just as much as from redeeming it.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
How does Rava resolve Abaye's challenge that Rabbi Eliezer's comparison of a firstborn donkey to a firstborn son should also permit deriving benefit from an unredeemed donkey?
A. He concedes benefit is permitted after all
B. He explains the comparison applies only to financial responsibility for a lost lamb, not to the permissibility of deriving benefit from the donkey
C. He rejects Rabbi Eliezer's opinion entirely
D. He says a firstborn son also requires burial if he dies
Correct: B — Rava limits the son-donkey comparison strictly to the question of who bears financial loss for the redemption lamb, keeping it separate from the unrelated question of deriving benefit from the donkey itself.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
How does Rav Naḥman reconcile the baraita stating redemption is 'immediate' with the one stating it doesn't apply before thirty days?
A. He says the two baraitot simply contradict each other with no resolution
B. 'Immediate' means redeeming right away is valid after the fact, even though the ideal practice is to wait until thirty days
C. He rules that only the thirty-day baraita is authoritative
D. He says immediate redemption applies only to firstborn sons
Correct: B — Rav Naḥman explains that redeeming immediately is effective if done, but the preferred, ideal practice is to wait until the donkey is thirty days old — paralleling the redemption of a firstborn son.
