📖 Overview: What Counts as a Valid 'Lamb'

Bekhorot 12a–12b
נֶאֱמַר כָּאן ״שֶׂה״, וְנֶאֱמַר לְהַלָּן ״שֶׂה״
The scene: A new mishna lists animals unfit to redeem a firstborn donkey — a calf, an undomesticated animal, a slaughtered animal, a tereifa, a hybrid, and a koy — deriving the whole list from a verbal analogy to the Paschal lamb. The Gemara then tests the boundary with four unresolved edge cases, before addressing a structural safeguard: a priest may not simply take an unredeemed donkey on his own promise to redeem it later. A second mishna raises what happens if the designated lamb dies before reaching the priest, and the daf closes by fixing exactly when redemption must take place.

🚫 Which Animals May Not Redeem a Firstborn Donkey?

The mishna's list, and Rabbi Eliezer's exceptions
Excluded from redemptionRabbi Eliezer's view
A calf or an undomesticated animal (חַיָּה)Excluded — no dispute
A slaughtered animal or a tereifaExcluded — no dispute
A kilayim (sheep-goat hybrid)Permitted — 'it is still a seh (lamb)'
A koy (uncertain domesticated/undomesticated status)Forbidden — 'because it is a doubt'
Source (ben Bag Bag): The word 'seh' (lamb) used here for redemption is linked, by verbal analogy, to the same word used for the Paschal offering (Exodus 12:3) — whatever is excluded there is excluded here.

🤔 Four Borderline Cases: Good Enough to Redeem With?

Testing the edges of the mishna's categories
❓ A ben pekua — a lamb removed alive from its ritually slaughtered mother's womb. A slaughtered animal, or a true living lamb?
↓
Mar Zutra
Forbidden — legally like 'meat in a basket,' as if already slaughtered
Rav Ashi
Permitted — it currently runs and lives, so it is called 'a lamb'
↓
🐐 A 'nidmeh' — born of two sheep parents but looking like a goat. Does the ban on a true hybrid extend to this merely goat-looking lamb too? Left open
↓
⛔ Disqualified consecrated animals: Rav Mari links their verse ('like the gazelle and the hart') to the same phrase excluding wild game from redemption
↓
🌾 An animal bought with Sabbatical-year produce: certainly forbidden for a definite firstborn; genuinely uncertain for an uncertain one

🤝 Why Must the Priest Redeem in the Owner's Presence?

A baraita's safeguard, and what it reveals
Baraita
If a priest tells an Israelite, 'give me your firstborn donkey and I'll redeem it myself' — the Israelite may not hand it over unless the priest redeems it right there, in front of him.
Rav Naḥman
This teaches something real: priests in general are suspected of using a firstborn donkey for labor without ever redeeming it!
Objection
Isn't that obvious from the baraita's own words?
Clarified
You might think this suspicion applies only to a known offender. Rav Naḥman teaches it applies to every priest — the lamb stays his either way, whether or not he ever redeems the donkey.

💀 If the Designated Lamb Dies First

Rabbi Eliezer and the Rabbis, and Abaye's sharp challenge
1️⃣ Rabbi Eliezer: the owner bears responsibility and must supply another lamb — like the five sela for a firstborn son, still owed even if the money is lost
↓
2️⃣ The Rabbis: no responsibility — like money set aside for second-tithe produce, desanctified the instant it's designated
↓
3️⃣ Abaye challenges Rabbi Eliezer: if compared to a firstborn son, benefit from an unredeemed donkey should be permitted too — yet you rule it must be buried if it dies, implying benefit is forbidden!
↓
4️⃣ Rava resolves: the comparison to a firstborn son applies only to financial responsibility for the lamb — not to whether one may derive benefit from the donkey. Two separate questions

⏰ When Must a Firstborn Donkey Be Redeemed?

Reconciling two seemingly contradictory baraitot
ClaimResolution
One baraita: redemption is immediate ('לְאַלְתַּר')Means: if redeemed right away, it is valid — not that one must redeem instantly
Another baraita: no redemption before 30 days, though one may extend indefinitely afterSets the ideal, latest-not-earliest timing — paralleling a firstborn son's redemption
Rav Naḥman's reconciliation: Redeeming immediately is effective after the fact, even though the ideal practice is to wait thirty days — just as redeeming a firstborn son within his first thirty days is likewise effective, though not the ideal timing.
