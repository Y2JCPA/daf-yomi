📖 Overview: A New Perek — The Tithing of Animals

Bekhorot 53a–53b
מַעְשַׂר בְּהֵמָה נוֹהֵג בָּאָרֶץ וּבְחוּצָה לָאָרֶץ, בִּפְנֵי הַבַּיִת וְשֶׁלֹּא בִּפְנֵי הַבַּיִת
The scene: A new mishna opens the second half of Bekhorot: the mitzva of animal tithe (ma'aser behema) applies both inside and outside Eretz Yisrael, with or without a standing Temple, and to non-sacred animals but never to those already consecrated. It applies to cattle and flock animals, but the two categories are never tithed together, while sheep and goats — different species prohibited from crossbreeding — are tithed together as one 'flock.' The Gemara immediately probes each clause: how does this square with Rabbi Akiva's view limiting animal tithe sacrifice to Eretz Yisrael, and why, in practice, has the custom of tithing animals lapsed entirely in the post-Temple era?

🐂🐏 Squaring the Mishna With Rabbi Akiva

A baraita seems to limit animal tithe to Eretz Yisrael entirely
Baraita
Rabbi Akiva: the verse groups animal tithe with grain tithe (Deut. 12:6) — wherever you bring up grain tithe, you bring up animal tithe; nowhere else.
Challenge
That directly contradicts the mishna's claim that animal tithe applies outside Eretz Yisrael too!
Resolution
Not a contradiction — the baraita discusses bringing the tithed animal to Jerusalem to be SACRIFICED; the mishna discusses whether the animal becomes CONSECRATED at all.
Result
Outside Israel, the animal tithe is still holy, but only eaten by the owner once it develops a blemish — it can never be sacrificed there.

🚫 Why Animal Tithe Has Lapsed in Practice

Two competing explanations for the modern non-observance
❓ If animal tithe applies even without a Temple, why does no one separate it today?
↓
👶 Rav Huna: a decree due to concern for an 'orphan' — a baby animal whose mother died before birth, which is actually EXEMPT but might get mistakenly counted
↓
❓ But that concern existed even when the Temple stood — and could always be solved by public announcement!
↓
⚠️ Rabba's answer: the real reason is fear of mishap (tekala) — sanctified animals with nowhere to be sacrificed risk being used improperly, worked, sheared, or eaten unslaughtered
Compare consecration generally: A baraita rules that today one should not consecrate, valuate, or dedicate items at all — and if one does, an animal must be locked away to die rather than risk misuse.

🔥 Firstborn vs. Tithe: Why the Difference?

If mishap is the concern, why still consecrate firstborn animals today?

Firstborn Animal

  • Consecration is automatic — it happens the moment it leaves the womb
  • Not within human control to prevent
  • Solution: sell the ears to a gentile in advance, or inflict a blemish before birth (R' Yehuda)

Animal Tithe

  • Consecration depends on a deliberate act of counting
  • One CAN avoid it by inflicting blemishes in advance — but no one knows which animal will emerge tenth
  • Blemishing the WHOLE herd risks leaving no fit animals once the Temple is rebuilt
Final reasoning: For firstborns, ordinary (non-firstborn) animals remain available for future offerings; for animal tithe, blemishing an entire flock to avoid the decree would leave too few unblemished animals for sale or sacrifice.

🌾 One Kind Can't Cover Another

A principle borrowed from teruma, later applied to tithe itself
1️⃣ Mishna (Terumot): one may not separate teruma from one type of produce to exempt a different type — such teruma isn't valid teruma
↓
2️⃣ Source: 'Give the best of THIS, and the best of THAT' (Numbers 18:12) — oil and wine, each separately
↓
3️⃣ This is the seed of the same principle developed further on the next daf: distinct species cannot be tithed for one another
Looking ahead: This teruma-based principle becomes the springboard for explaining exactly why cattle and flock, though both 'animals,' must be tithed as two separate categories.
