📖 Overview: From Feeding Tests to Hidden Blemishes

Bekhorot 39a–39b
חוּטִין הַחִיצוֹנוֹת שֶׁנִּפְגְּמוּ, וְשֶׁנִּגְמְמוּ, הַפְּנִימִיִּם שֶׁנֶּעֶקְרוּ
The scene: The daf opens by piling up unresolved procedural questions about the fig-bulk fodder test for 'constant tears' — before or after eating, tied or free, in the field or the city — each left as a standing dilemma. A practical question follows: if the animal is only confirmed blemished after the three-month test, does that blemish apply retroactively? New mishnayot then add blemishes of the nose, lip, and teeth (external gums count, but the hidden inner teeth do not), before the Gemara opens a much larger question: does a defect in an internal organ, invisible from outside, count as a disqualifying 'lack' at all? The daf closes with the mishna's list of tail and genital blemishes, including a three-way dispute over damaged testicles.

The Gemara piles up procedural questions about the moist/dry fodder test — and answers none of them
1️⃣ Fed only at the first meal, or at every meal? Before eating (clearly effective, like medicine) or after eating?
↓
2️⃣ Before drinking (clearly effective, like barley) or after drinking?
↓
3️⃣ While tied up, or untied (clearly effective, since the animal is at ease)? Alone, or with a companion animal?

⏪ Does the Blemish Apply Retroactively?

אֲכַל וְלָא אִיתַּסִּי — לְמַפְרֵעַ הָוֵי מוּמָא, אוֹ מִיכָּן וּלְהַבָּא הָוֵי מוּמָא
Pinḥas's question to Shmuel: If the animal ate the fodder as required and was not healed, is the blemish considered to have existed retroactively from the start, or only from the moment it is confirmed? The practical difference: if the animal was redeemed during the three-month test and someone used the redemption money, did he misuse consecrated property? Shmuel had no answer, remarking wryly, quoting Isaiah 33:23, ״פִּסְחִים בָּזְזוּ בַז״ — 'the lame take the prey,' since even his unlearned brother could ask a question he could not answer.

🫘 Does a Missing Internal Organ Count as a 'Lack'?

Two baraitot on kidneys seem to contradict — how many tannaim are really disputing, and about what?
Baraita 1
"The two kidneys" (Leviticus 3:4) excludes an animal with only one kidney, or with three, from the altar.
Baraita 2
But the singular "he shall take it away" comes to INCLUDE an animal with one kidney as fit!
Rav Ḥiyya bar Yosef
No dispute at all — both agree an internal lack disqualifies. One baraita means it was BORN with two and lost one (disqualified); the other means it was born with only one from the start (naturally whole, so fit).
Rabbi Yoḥanan
No dispute either — but the real variable is TIMING: lacking a kidney before slaughter disqualifies; lacking one only after the blood is collected does not, since the verse's requirements apply only up through the blood's collection.
Rava's addendum: Since an offering can shift from 'in its first year' to 'in its second year' between slaughter and blood-collection, 'hours disqualify' sacrificial animals — timing is measured precisely.

🐑 New Mishna: Tail and Genital Blemishes

The mishna's list, plus a three-way dispute over damaged testicles (Leviticus 22:24)

The Mishna's List

  • Damaged genital pouch (but NOT if entirely removed, and NOT the organ itself — R' Elazar)
  • Tail damaged from the tailbone (not from a joint, which heals)
  • Exposed tailbone, or a full fingerbreadth of flesh missing between vertebrae

R' Yehuda: All in the Testicles

  • Bruised, crushed, torn, or cut testicles are all blemishes — they visibly shrink or hang loosely

R' Elazar b. Yaakov / R' Yosei

  • R' Elazar b. Yaakov: all four terms apply only to the penis, not the testicles
  • R' Yosei: bruised/crushed testicles ARE a blemish (they're simply gone); torn/cut testicles are NOT (they're still physically present)
Underlying question: Rabbi Yosei ben HaMeshullam even reports a case where a wolf tore off an animal's genital pouch and it grew back — showing regeneration can undercut a blemish claim.
