Bekhorot 39 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
What is the Gemara's ultimate conclusion about the many procedural questions raised concerning the fig-bulk fodder test (before/after eating, tied/untied, city/field)?
A. All are resolved leniently
B. All are resolved strictly
C. They are left unresolved (teiku)
D. Only the city/field question is resolved
Correct: C — Each of these dilemmas is explicitly left standing with no resolution, marked teiku.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
What is the practical difference Pinḥas raises regarding whether a confirmed blemish (after the 3-month tear test) applies retroactively?
A. Whether the animal may be eaten at all
B. Whether redemption money used before the blemish was confirmed constitutes misuse of consecrated property (me'ila)
C. Whether the animal must be examined by three witnesses
D. Whether the priest may eat the meat
Correct: B — If the blemish is retroactive, using the redemption money before confirmation would be considered misuse of still-consecrated property; if not, no misuse occurred.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to the mishna's ruling (as explained by Rabbi Ḥanina ben Antigonus), why don't the internal 'double teeth' count as a disqualifying blemish for outside slaughter?
A. Because teeth are never considered blemishes
B. Because their location is hidden/internal, and a blemish generally must be exposed to permit slaughtering the firstborn outside the Temple
C. Because only the nose and lip can be blemished
D. Because double teeth cannot be damaged
Correct: B — The recurring principle in this sugya is that a disqualifying blemish for outside slaughter must be visible/exposed; the double teeth are hidden and therefore excluded.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
How does Rabbi Yoḥanan resolve the apparent contradiction between two baraitot about an animal missing a kidney?
A. He says the baraitot cannot be reconciled
B. The dispute is about timing: lacking a kidney before slaughter disqualifies the offering, but lacking one only after the blood is already collected does not
C. He rules that a missing kidney is never a disqualification
D. He says both baraitot are talking about the same case
Correct: B — Rabbi Yoḥanan explains that everyone agrees an internal lack disqualifies, but the difference is timing: the requirement of wholeness applies only up through the collection of the blood, not afterward.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
In the three-way dispute over damaged testicles (bruised, crushed, torn, or cut), what does Rabbi Yosei hold?
A. All four terms apply only to the penis
B. All four terms apply only to the testicles equally
C. Bruised/crushed testicles are a blemish since they are effectively gone, but torn/cut testicles are not, since they remain physically present
D. None of the four terms ever create a blemish
Correct: C — Rabbi Yosei distinguishes based on whether the testicle still physically exists: bruised or crushed means it is essentially destroyed (a blemish), while torn or cut means it is still present, just detached (not a blemish by his reasoning).
