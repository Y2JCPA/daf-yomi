📖 Overview: Resemblance, Generations, and Precise Measurement

Bekhorot 17a–17b
רָחֵל שֶׁלֹּא בִּיכְּרָה וְיָלְדָה שְׁנֵי זְכָרִים, וְיָצְאוּ שְׁנֵי רָאשֵׁיהֶן כְּאַחַת
The scene: The daf closes out the sugya on animals accepted from a gentile as a guaranteed investment (צֹאן בַּרְזֶל), debating exactly how many generations of offspring remain exempt from firstborn sanctity. It then returns to the mishna's ewe that gave birth to a goat-like creature, working through four different accounts of what Rabbi Meir and the Rabbis actually disputed — first-shearing wool, paternity concerns, or which ancestor's appearance controls firstborn status. A cluster of 'everyone agrees' rulings follows about wool, dye, leprosy marks, and libations from an animal or plant that has 'changed' from its natural kind. A new mishna then poses a ewe giving birth to twin males whose heads emerge simultaneously, launching a Gemara-wide investigation into whether two events — in Heaven's hands or in human hands — can ever coincide with true precision.

🔟 How Many Generations Stay Exempt?

Rav Huna and Rav Yehuda argue over the guaranteed-flock (צאן ברזל) mishna
Gemara
Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel said offspring are exempt even until ten generations. But Rav Huna holds the first tanna never spoke of generations at all — so what does 'ten generations' mean?
Rav Huna
That phrase refers to the mishna's second case, where the Jew established the offspring in place of the mothers. There, the first tanna does descend to generations.
Gemara
Come and hear an objection to Rav Yehuda: one who accepts tzon barzel from a gentile — the direct offspring are exempt, but offspring of offspring are not!
Rav Yehuda
I could say the mishna's phrase 'the offspring are exempt' refers to the offspring of the offspring, not the original animals' direct young.
Gemara
Others report the mishna as 'they and their offspring are exempt' — that refutes Rav Huna!
Rav Huna
I could say 'they and their offspring' means the original animals and their direct young are exempt, but the offspring of that generation are obligated.
Method: Each sage rereads the disputed wording of the mishna rather than concede — a recurring pattern of resolving apparent refutations.

🐐 What Was Rabbi Meir Really Ruling On?

Rav Oshaya's baraita: a ewe born to a goat, or a goat born to a ewe — Rabbi Meir obligates, the Rabbis exempt

Not About Wool

  • First guess: Rabbi Meir requires firstborn status only if offspring resembles its mother (Numbers 18:17) — so his ruling must concern first-shearing of wool instead
  • Objection: tough-fleeced lambs are exempt from first shearing (Job 31:20) — a goat-like fleece is tough
  • Rabba's fix: really about concern for the father's seed for slaughtering it and its offspring together

Firstborn — Mother or Grandmother?

  • Case: a ewe born to a ewe born to a goat (resembles mother, not grandmother)
  • Rabbi Meir: follow the mother's appearance — firstborn applies
  • Rabbis: follow the mother's mother's appearance — this is נִדְמֶה, exempt
  • Alt. case: ewe born to goat born to ewe — did its 'ovine nature return to its place'?

Rav Ashi — Partial Resemblance

  • Case: offspring has only some of its mother's characteristics
  • Rabbi Meir (= our mishna): firstborn status applies even with partial resemblance
  • Rabbis = Rabbi Shimon: requires head and majority of the body to resemble the mother
Rabbi Yoḥanan's caveat: Even Rabbi Meir concedes the New Moon goat-offering must be the 'son of a female goat' since Creation — derived from the extra word 'one' (Numbers 28:15), or from Leviticus 22:27 excluding both diverse kinds and נִדְמֶה.

🧵 An Animal 'That Has Not Changed'

A chain of sages apply the same verbal analogy (from Deuteronomy 22:11's 'linen') across unrelated halakhot
SageCaseRuling
Rabbi Aḥa bar YaakovWearing wool of a changed animal with linenNo flogging for kilayim — 'linen unchanged' means the wool, too, must be unchanged
Rav PappaTzitzit dyed with such woolUnfit for the sky-blue (תְּכֵלֶת) thread
Rav Naḥman bar YitzḥakA garment woven from such woolDoes not become impure with leprous marks (Leviticus 13:47)
Rav AshiA grapevine trellised over a fig treeIts wine is unfit for libations (Leviticus 23:37, 'offering and libations')
Ravina (challenge) / Rav Ashi (answer)Flax grown over a shrubStill valid — only the vine's scent changed; the flax's scent did not
Pattern: 'Changed' is measured by a real, functional alteration (like altered scent), not merely by unusual growing conditions.

👶 Twins Whose Heads Emerge Together

New mishna: a ewe's first delivery is two males, both heads out at once
❓ Who gets the firstborn — the owner or the priest?
↓
Rabbi Yosei HaGelili
Both go to the priest — 'the males shall be to the Lord' (Exodus 13:12) is plural
The Rabbis
Two events cannot coincide precisely — one truly preceded the other, so one lamb to the owner, one to the priest
↓
Rabbi Tarfon
The priest chooses the better of the two lambs
Rabbi Akiva
Value is assessed between them; the second grazes until blemished, then the owner may eat it
↓
⚖️ Gifts of the priesthood (foreleg, jaw, maw) apply to the owner's lamb too — except Rabbi Yosei exempts it
If one twin dies: Rabbi Tarfon: the survivor is divided; Rabbi Akiva: it stays with the owner, since the burden of proof is on the claimant. A male and a female together: the priest gets nothing at all.

⚖️ Can Two Things Ever Coincide Precisely?

The Gemara tests אֶפְשָׁר לְצַמְצֵם against cases across the Temple and beyond
1️⃣ School of Rabbi Yannai: Rabbi Yosei HaGelili holds even events in Heaven's hands (twin births) can coincide exactly — so human measurement certainly can. The Rabbis deny this for Heaven's hands — but what about human hands?
↓
2️⃣ Proof attempt: the red line marking the altar's exact midpoint (Middot) — rejected, since the line is deliberately widened for safety
↓
3️⃣ Proof attempt: the Torah gives exact measurements for Temple vessels and the altar — rejected, since 'do it as best you can' is all God requires
↓
4️⃣ Rav Ketina: an impure oven split into two equal pieces leaves both impure, since exact division is impossible — Rav Kahana rejects this: earthenware has irregular indentations
↓
5️⃣ Rabbi Eliezer's eglah arufah ruling: if a corpse is found exactly between two towns, both bring heifers — suggesting human measurement can be precise. The Gemara begins to reject this proof too, continuing onto 18a
Open question: By the end of 17b, no proof for or against human precision (אפשר לצמצם בידי אדם) has survived unchallenged.
