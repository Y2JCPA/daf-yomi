Bekhorot 17 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to Rav Huna, to which case of the tzon barzel mishna does Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel's phrase 'even until ten generations' refer?
A. The case of the direct offspring only
B. The case where the Jew established the offspring in place of the mothers
C. A case not discussed in the mishna at all
D. The case of a gentile's firstborn animal
Correct: B — Rav Huna holds the first tanna does not normally speak of generations, so Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel's remark must apply to the mishna's second case, where offspring were substituted for the mothers and generational descent is relevant.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
In the baraita about a ewe that gave birth to a goat-like creature, what does Rabbi Meir's ruling concern according to Rav Ashi's interpretation?
A. Whether the wool may be used for tzitzit
B. An offspring that has only some, not all, of its mother's characteristics
C. Whether the priest may eat the offspring
D. The New Moon goat offering
Correct: B — Rav Ashi explains that Rabbi Meir's mishna-based position obligates firstborn status even when the offspring has only partial resemblance to its mother, while the Rabbis (Rabbi Shimon) require the head and majority of the body to resemble the mother.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
What do Rabbi Aḥa bar Yaakov, Rav Pappa, and Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak all agree about wool from an animal that resembles a different species?
A. It may be eaten without concern
B. It is fit for the Temple altar
C. It cannot be used for kilayim liability, tekhelet, or become impure with tzaraat marks — because it counts as 'changed'
D. It automatically confers firstborn status
Correct: C — All three derive from the verbal analogy to unchanged linen that only wool from an animal 'that has not changed' (i.e., resembles its own kind) can trigger these halakhot; a nidmeh animal's wool is excluded from each.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
In the new mishna, when a ewe's first two offspring emerge with both heads out at once, what does Rabbi Yosei HaGelili rule?
A. Both males belong to the owner
B. One belongs to the owner and one to the priest
C. Both males are given to the priest, based on the plural 'the males shall be to the Lord'
D. The owner must wait until one is blemished
Correct: C — Rabbi Yosei HaGelili reads Exodus 13:12's plural 'the males' as requiring both simultaneously-born males to go to the priest, since he holds two events can coincide precisely even in Heaven's hands.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
What is the Gemara's rejection of Rav Ketina's proof from the impure oven split into two equal pieces (both remaining impure)?
A. The oven was never truly impure
B. Earthenware has irregular indentations, making an exactly even split especially difficult
C. The case has nothing to do with precise measurement
D. Ovens are not covered by the laws of tumah
Correct: B — Rav Kahana answers that earthenware vessels are unusual because their break points are uneven due to indentations, so this case cannot prove anything about whether precise division is generally possible.
