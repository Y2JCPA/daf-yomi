QUESTION 1 OF 5
Why does the Gemara need to explain that the Torah's 'garav' differs from the mishna's 'garav'?
A. Because the Torah never mentions garav
B. The Torah's garav is dry and incurable (a true blemish), while the mishna's garav is moist and curable (not a permitting blemish) — resolving an apparent contradiction
C. Because garav only applies to hermaphrodites
D. There is no difference; the terms are identical
Correct: B — The Gemara distinguishes dry, incurable garav (the Torah's blemish) from moist, curable garav (the mishna's non-blemish), resolving the apparent contradiction between the sources.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to the baraita, why does the Torah use the word 'min' (from) three separate times regarding a sacrificial animal's fitness?
A. It is a stylistic repetition with no legal meaning
B. To exclude, respectively, an old animal, a sick animal, and a foul-smelling animal — each needing its own exclusion since none can be logically derived from the others
C. To exclude only male animals
D. To include additional species of animals
Correct: B — Each exclusion is independently necessary: an old animal is naturally aged (not sick), a sick animal could recover (unlike old age), and a foul-smelling animal is neither weak nor incurable, so none could be inferred from the others.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to Abaye, why doesn't the hermaphrodite's female sex organ count as the Torah's blemish of 'cracked' (charutz)?
A. Because charutz applies only to the eyes
B. Because 'cracked' is grouped with 'broken' in the verse, and just as 'broken' requires a bone, so does 'cracked' — and the female organ isn't located near bone
C. Because hermaphrodites cannot have any blemishes
D. Because the Torah never mentions charutz
Correct: B — Abaye reads 'cracked' (חָרוּץ) as parallel to 'broken' (שָׁבוּר) in the same verse, requiring the blemish to be at a bone — which excludes the fleshy female organ.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to the Rabbis' view in the mishna (as opposed to Rabbi Shimon), what is the halakhic status of a hermaphrodite (androginos) firstborn?
A. It is definitely a firstborn with a permanent blemish
B. It has no firstborn status at all; it is treated as a regular, non-sacred animal that may be sheared and worked
C. It must always be sacrificed on the altar
D. Its status depends on which parent it resembles
Correct: B — The Rabbis hold that a hermaphrodite is a distinct category altogether, lacking firstborn sanctity, and may therefore be treated like any ordinary animal.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to Rav Ḥisda, what is the key difference between a tumtum and an androginos regarding their status as a possible firstborn?
A. There is no difference between them
B. The dispute in the mishna applies only to an androginos (a distinct category per the Rabbis); a tumtum is universally agreed to be a genuine doubt between male and female, and is consecrated due to that doubt
C. A tumtum is always female and an androginos is always male
D. Both are always treated as definitely male
Correct: B — Rav Ḥisda distinguishes: the Rabbis' dispute with Rabbi Shimon concerns only the androginos; everyone agrees a tumtum is genuinely uncertain (definitely one sex or the other, just unknown which), and so it is held consecrated due to the doubt.
