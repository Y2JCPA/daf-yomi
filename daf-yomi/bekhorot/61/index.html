📖 Overview: Closing Perek Ma'aser Behema — and the Tractate

Bekhorot 61a
הֲדַרַן עֲלָךְ מַעְשַׂר בְּהֵמָה, וּסְלִיקָא לַהּ מַסֶּכֶת בְּכוֹרוֹת
The scene: The final daf resolves the three-way tannaitic dispute from the previous daf over what happens to two animals that emerged together as the tenth and were both called 'tenth': one view has them graze until blemished, one has them sacrificed, and one has them put to death. The Gemara assigns each ruling to a different tanna's broader position, then explains that the 'they must die' ruling actually addresses animal tithe in the present era, when consecrated animals cannot be sacrificed and pose a risk of misuse. The daf's final topic turns practical: if one sends an agent to tithe his flock and the agent makes a naming error, whose mistake is it — comparing this case to an agent sent to separate teruma. With this, Bekhorot's final perek, and the entire tractate, concludes.

🌱 Three Fates for the Intermingled Pair

Reconciling three baraitot that seem to contradict each other

They Graze

  • Follows the Rabbis' view
  • One may not bring sacrifices into a situation of likely disqualification
  • Both would need eating within the STRICTER of tithe's and peace-offering's time limits — risking disqualification

They Are Sacrificed

  • Follows Rabbi Shimon's view
  • He permits bringing sacrifices even into a situation of likely disqualification

They Must Die

  • Follows Rabbi Yehuda: a mistaken tithe designation has the status of a SUBSTITUTE animal
  • And a substitute for animal tithe itself must be left to die

💀 But Does Rabbi Yehuda Really Hold Substitutes Die?

A challenge from the earlier mishna and its resolution
Challenge
The mishna quotes Rabbi Meir describing R' Yehuda's own view: 'if it were a substitute, it would NOT be sacrificed' — implying R' Yehuda actually holds it IS sacrificed, not put to death!
Further proof
A Sifra baraita (unattributed = Rabbi Yehuda) explicitly derives that the eleventh animal, mistakenly called tenth, is included in 'if his offering be a peace offering' — meaning it's sacrificed.
Resolution
Rabbi Shimon bar Abba, before Rabbi Yoḥanan: the 'they must die' baraita is NOT about ordinary times — it's specifically about animal tithe TODAY, when there's no Temple to sacrifice in.
Why not just one?
The baraita specifies TWO animals (not one) because with only one animal there's no great loss in simply waiting for a blemish — but with two, the greater loss might tempt people to keep them, so the ruling stresses: even so, they must die.

💀 Why Nowadays Specifically Requires Death

Connecting back to the broader theme of consecration without a Temple
🏛️ Without a Temple, animals with sacrificial-level sanctity cannot be brought as offerings
↓
⚠️ Leaving them to graze until blemished risks misuse — shearing, labor, or premature eating, echoing the 'mishap' concern from daf 53
↓
☠️ For animals with the elevated, sacrifice-eligible sanctity of the mistakenly-designated eleventh, the Sages require they be left to die immediately

🌾 Comparing to the Teruma Agent — and Closing the Tractate

Why tithing errors differ from teruma-separation errors
1️⃣ Challenge: an agent sent to separate teruma acts on his own judgment of the owner's generosity — even guessing wrong, his teruma is valid
↓
2️⃣ Answer: with teruma, there's a RANGE of acceptable amounts (generous to stingy) — the owner implicitly authorized any reasonable guess
↓
3️⃣ With animal tithe, a naming error isn't a matter of differing legitimate judgment — it's simply a MISTAKE, which the owner never authorized
↓
4️⃣ With this final exchange, the Gemara concludes Perek Ma'aser Behema — and the entire Tractate Bekhorot
הדרן עלך: The daf ends with the traditional formula marking both the close of this perek and the completion of the whole tractate.
