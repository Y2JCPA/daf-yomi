📖 Overview: Setting the Calendar and the Counting Procedure

Bekhorot 58a–58b
בְּאֵי זֶה צַד מְעַשְּׂרִין? כּוֹנְסָן לַדִּיר, וְעוֹשֶׂה לָהֶם פֶּתַח קָטָן
The scene: The Gemara explains why animal tithe has three yearly gathering times rather than one: so that plenty of untithed, sellable animals remain available for pilgrims ascending to Jerusalem before each festival. It works out the precise meaning of 'adjacent' (peras) as fifteen days, and traces the different dates given by Rabbi Akiva, ben Azzai, and Rabbi Elazar and Rabbi Shimon to their underlying dispute over when the new year for animal tithe begins — first of Nisan, Elul, or Tishrei. A famous aside has ben Azzai admit that only tradition, not logic, can settle disputes reaching back to the prophets Haggai, Zechariah, and Malachi. The daf then turns to a new mishna detailing exactly how tithing is performed: animals are herded through a narrow gate one by one, counted to ten, and the tenth is marked with red paint and declared consecrated.

🐑 Why Three Gathering Times, Not One

כְּדֵי שֶׁתְּהֵא בְּהֵמָה מְצוּיָה לְעוֹלֵי רְגָלִים
Rabbi Tanḥum's explanation: The three gathering times exist so that animals remain readily available for those making pilgrimage to Jerusalem for each festival. Even though the mishna permits selling or slaughtering animals freely before the gathering date arrives, people generally prefer to perform the mitzva with their property first — tithing — and only then sell or eat the remainder. The gathering time is called a 'goren' (threshing floor) because, just as gathered produce becomes obligated in tithe, animals reaching this point become obligated too.

🆕 When Does the Animal-Tithe Year Begin?

A dispute that reshapes every gathering date
R' Elazar/Shimon
New year is 1 Tishrei — so our third gathering falls on 29 Elul (not 1 Tishrei itself, since that's Rosh Hashanah, a Festival when tithing/painting is forbidden).
R' Meir
New year is 1 Elul instead.
Ben Azzai
Since the two views disagree, animals born in Elul are tithed entirely BY THEMSELVES — never combined with either the preceding or following year's animals.
R' Yochanan
Ben Azzai couldn't resolve whose reasoning was stronger because this entire dispute rests on a chain of tradition from the prophets Haggai, Zechariah, and Malachi — not on logic.

🧄 Ben Azzai's Famous Humility

כׇּל חַכְמֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל דּוֹמִין עָלַי כִּקְלִיפַּת הַשּׁוּם חוּץ מִן הַקֵּרֵחַ הַזֶּה
A baraita's report: Ben Azzai said: 'All the Sages of Israel appear to me as unimpressive as garlic peel, except for this bald one' — referring to Rabbi Akiva. Yet even ben Azzai, with all his confidence, could not determine whose reasoning about the new year for animal tithe was more compelling, because the entire matter rested on received tradition rather than logical argument.

🎨 The Mishna's Tithing Procedure

From narrow gate to red paint
1️⃣ Gather the animals into a pen with a narrow opening so only one can exit at a time
↓
2️⃣ Count aloud as each emerges: one, two, three... up to nine
↓
3️⃣ The tenth to emerge is painted with red dye (sikra) and declared: 'This is tithe'
↓
4️⃣ Even without paint or a counting-rod, or if animals were counted while lying down — the tenth is still sanctified after the fact, though if one simply grabbed ten from a hundred without counting, that is NOT valid tithe
Rabbi Yosei b'Rabbi Yehuda's exception: He alone holds that even grabbing one animal from ten without any counting still counts as valid tithe — deriving this from teruma's ability to be separated 'by estimate and thought.'
