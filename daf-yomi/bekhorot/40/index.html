📖 Overview: Cataloguing Limbs, Jaws, and a Contested Testicle

Bekhorot 40a–40b
בַּעַל חָמֵשׁ רַגְלַיִם, אוֹ שֶׁאֵין לוֹ אֶלָּא שָׁלֹשׁ, וְשֶׁרַגְלָיו קְלוּטוֹת כְּשֶׁל חֲמוֹר, וְהַשָּׁחוּל וְהַכָּסוּל
The scene: After a brief note on the Temple's two measuring cubits (used to prevent misuse of consecrated funds), the daf turns to a mishna disputing how to determine whether a firstborn has one testicle or two — a disagreement vivid enough to spark a real halakhic clash between Rabbi Akiva and Rabbi Yohanan ben Nuri over an actual case. Further mishnayot catalogue blemishes of the legs and hooves (extra or missing legs, donkey-like hooves, uneven thighs), then record the blemishes counted by the expert Ila, including some the Sages initially hadn't heard of but ultimately accepted. The daf closes with disputes over doubled ears and how many tail joints a kid or lamb needs to avoid being blemished.

📏 Two Cubits, One Safeguard

אוּמָּנִין נוֹטְלִין בַּקְּטַנָּה, וּמַחְזִירִין בַּגְּדוֹלָה, שֶׁלֹּא יָבוֹאוּ לִידֵי מְעִילָה
Why the Temple kept two measuring cubits: One cubit in the chamber of Shushan HaBirah was a half-fingerbreadth longer than Moses' cubit, the other a full fingerbreadth longer. Artisans were paid using the smaller measure but had to return equivalent work measured by the larger one — a built-in margin ensuring they never inadvertently took more from the Temple treasury than they earned, avoiding the sin of misusing consecrated property (מְעִילָה). This same 'fingerbreadth' unit, one-quarter of a handbreadth, is used elsewhere to measure the flesh gap between a firstborn's tail vertebrae.

🥚 How Do You Know If It Has One Testicle or Two?

A dispute vivid enough to become a real, contested case
Mishna
A firstborn with no testicles, or only one, may be slaughtered — but how do you determine the count without cutting the animal open?
R' Yishmael
Simple: two scrotal sacs means two testicles; one sac means only one testicle.
R' Akiva
Not so fast — seat the animal on its rump and press the sac. If a testicle is there, it will eventually emerge.
Real case
In Piran of Beit Menaḥem: pressed, nothing emerged — animal slaughtered — the testicle was found stuck to the loins all along! R' Akiva permitted the meat; R' Yoḥanan ben Nuri forbade it.
R' Akiva
'Until when will you consume the property of the Jewish people?!' (by needlessly forbidding valid meat)
R' Yoḥanan b. Nuri
'Until when will you feed the Jewish people sacrificial meat outside the Temple?!' (since the testicle was there all along — no blemish existed)
The stakes: A single ambiguous pressing test led two great Sages to accuse each other of causing either needless loss or forbidden consumption.

🐷 Ila's Three Additions

The Sages initially hesitated, then accepted all three

Round Eye

  • An eye round like a human's (not an animal's usual shape)
  • Gemara connects it to a parallel dispute in Nidda over identifying a miscarried fetus

Pig-Like Mouth

  • Upper lip overlapping the lower, pig-fashion
  • Rav Pappa: counts even without a pointed snout, as long as it overlaps

Damaged Tongue

  • Most of the 'speaking segment' of the tongue removed (Rabbi Yehuda's definition)
  • Distinguished from removing most of the tongue generally
Related ruling: A lower jaw protruding beyond the upper is also a blemish for an animal specifically when the jaw contains bone — unlike a person, where any lip overlap counts, since 'a man of the seed of Aaron' requires an ordinary human appearance.
