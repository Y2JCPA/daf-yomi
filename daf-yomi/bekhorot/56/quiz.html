Bekhorot 56 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
From which halakha does the Gemara derive that a purchased or gifted animal is exempt from animal tithe?
A. The laws of the paschal offering
B. The laws of redeeming a firstborn son
C. The laws of teruma
D. There is no textual source; it is a rabbinic enactment
Correct: B — The Gemara derives the exemption from the juxtaposition in Exodus 22:28–29 comparing animal tithe ('so you shall do with your oxen and sheep') to the redemption of a firstborn son, who likewise must be one's own biological child, not purchased or adopted.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to Rava's explanation of Rabbi Yoḥanan, why do ten fetuses purchased while still in the womb nevertheless enter the pen to be tithed once born?
A. Because fetuses are never subject to any exemption
B. Because the exclusionary verse ('so you shall do') applies only 'at the time of doing' — when the animal reaches an age fit for offerings — not while it was an unformed fetus
C. Because Rabbi Yoḥanan disagreed with the underlying exemption entirely
D. Because purchased fetuses are considered gifts, not purchases
Correct: B — Rava explains that the exclusion for purchased animals applies specifically 'at the time of doing,' i.e., once the animal could be brought as an offering — a fetus in the womb was not yet at that stage when purchased, so the later-born animal remains eligible for tithe.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to Abaye, why does an animal given as payment to a JEWISH prostitute NOT get disqualified from being sacrificed, while an animal given to a gentile prostitute IS disqualified?
A. Jewish prostitutes are permitted by Torah law
B. The disqualification derives from a verbal analogy to forbidden relations where betrothal cannot take effect — which applies to a gentile prostitute, not a Jewish one
C. Gentile animals are always more valuable
D. There is no real difference between the two cases
Correct: B — Abaye derives the disqualification from a verbal analogy to forbidden sexual relations, where betrothal never takes effect — a category matching a gentile prostitute but not a Jewish one, upon whom betrothal could take effect.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to the new mishna, when are brothers or partners obligated in the half-shekel premium (kalbon) but EXEMPT from animal tithe?
A. When they have not divided their father's estate at all
B. When they divided both the money and the animals from their father's estate, and then reestablished a partnership
C. Only if one brother is a priest
D. Never — these two obligations always go together
Correct: B — The mishna teaches that once brothers fully divide both the animals and the money and then form a new partnership, they are treated as ordinary partners (obligated in the kalbon) rather than co-heirs of a single estate (which would obligate animal tithe).
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to Rabbi Yoḥanan (the most lenient view in the dispute), does dividing an estate by giving each brother an equal mix of both large and small goats count as establishing separate, individual ownership?
A. Yes, always
B. No — even that kind of division does not establish that each share was his 'from the outset'
C. Only if done before a court
D. Only for firstborn sons
Correct: B — Rabbi Yoḥanan holds the most lenient position: even dividing the herd proportionally (equal types to each brother) does not establish that each brother's share was designated for him from the start, so reuniting the estate keeps them exempt from animal tithe as partners.
