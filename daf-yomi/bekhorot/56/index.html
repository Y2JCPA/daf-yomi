📖 Overview: Ownership, Timing, and Family Partnership

Bekhorot 56a–56b
לָקַח עֲשָׂרָה עוּבָּרִין בִּמְעֵי אִמָּן — כּוּלָּן נִכְנָסִין לַדִּיר לְהִתְעַשֵּׂר
The scene: The Gemara grounds yesterday's exemption for purchased or gifted animals in a verbal comparison to the redemption of a firstborn son, then immediately tests the analogy's limits: if one purchases ten pregnant animals while the fetuses are still in the womb, do those fetuses — once born into the buyer's ownership — still count for animal tithe? Rabbi Yoḥanan says yes, and Rabbi Elazar even reports dreaming of him to confirm the explanation. The daf also examines when an animal given as payment to a prostitute disqualifies it from tithe, distinguishing Jewish from gentile prostitutes. It closes with a new mishna: brothers or partners who haven't divided their father's estate are obligated in animal tithe but exempt from the half-shekel premium (kalbon), and vice versa once divided — generating a four-way analysis and a dispute over what counts as a genuine division.

👶 Deriving the Exemption from a Firstborn Son

How 'so you shall do with your oxen and your sheep' teaches animal tithe
📜 Verse: 'The firstborn of your sons you shall give to Me. So you shall do with your oxen, and your sheep...' (Exodus 22:28–29)
↓
🔗 Just as a son cannot be a firstborn if purchased or adopted, an animal owned only by purchase or gift is excluded from tithe too
↓
❓ But why apply this to animal TITHE rather than a sin offering or peace offering or the pilgrimage burnt offering?
↓
✅ Because like a son's redemption, animal tithe has no fixed calendar date, doesn't atone for sin, and isn't brought as a vow or gift — matching all three features uniquely

🐑 Ten Fetuses Purchased Before Birth

Testing whether the exemption applies from conception or from birth
Question
If the son analogy is exact, a purchased animal should NEVER be tithed — so why does Rabbi Yoḥanan say fetuses purchased in the womb DO enter the pen once born?
Rava
The verse says 'SO YOU SHALL DO' — the exclusion applies only from the time of 'doing,' i.e., once the animal is old enough to be offered. A fetus wasn't yet excludable at that point.
Objection
But a baraita says the exemption applies even to a young animal not yet old enough to sacrifice — so why not to a fetus too?
Rabbi Elazar
That baraita isn't reliable; and even if it were, it follows Rabbi Shimon ben Yehuda's minority view that treats an unripe animal like a firstborn — sanctified early, sacrificed later.
The dream: Rabbi Elazar reports seeing Rabbi Yoḥanan in a dream as confirmation that his explanation ('at the time of doing') correctly represents the master's teaching.

👨‍👩‍👦 Brothers, Partners, and Two Different Obligations

The new mishna's four possible combinations

Obligated in Kalbon Only

  • Divided the MONEY but not the animals
  • Still partners in livestock, but each holds his own half-shekel

Obligated in Both / Neither

  • Divided animals but not money: exempt from both
  • Divided nothing at all: obligated in animal tithe only (still one estate)

Obligated in Animal Tithe Only

  • Divided BOTH money and animals, then reunited as ordinary partners
  • Now treated like true strangers-in-partnership, not co-heirs
Rabbi Yirmeya's structure: The rule flows from a single principle: partners owe the premium but not animal tithe; individual owners owe animal tithe but not the premium — everything follows from whether they still legally share the SAME estate.

🐐 What Counts as a Real Division?

Rav Anan, Rav Naḥman, Rabbi Elazar, and Rabbi Yoḥanan disagree
1️⃣ Rav Anan: only division by SWAPPING types (kids for goats) counts as a real division — dividing each type proportionally leaves the estate legally unified
↓
2️⃣ Rav Naḥman disagrees: even swapping types doesn't count as a real division — reuniting still counts as pure partnership, exempt from tithe
↓
3️⃣ Rabbi Elazar's version: the same debate, but framed around unequal-value swaps (nine large goats for ten kids)
↓
4️⃣ Rabbi Yoḥanan takes the most lenient position: even an unequal-value swap doesn't establish a true division
