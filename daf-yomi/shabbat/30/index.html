📖 Overview: David's Death, Kohelet's Contradictions, and Hillel's Patience

Shabbat 30a–30b
לְעוֹלָם יְהֵא אָדָם עַנְוְותָן כְּהִלֵּל וְאַל יְהֵא קַפְּדָן כְּשַׁמַּאי
The scene: Having established in the mishna that a lamp may be extinguished for a critically ill person, the Gemara asks why the mishna phrases this case as 'exempt' rather than 'permitted,' and works out that it is describing a case of mortal danger while stylistically mirroring the liable clause. This opens an extended aggadic detour: Rabbi Tanḥum is asked whether one may extinguish a lamp for a sick person, and answers with a sweeping homily reconciling apparent contradictions in Solomon's own words about life and death, tracing King David's forgiveness for his sin, the Temple dedication, and the story of David's death while studying Torah on Shabbat. The Gemara then explains why the Sages nearly suppressed the books of Ecclesiastes and Proverbs due to internal contradictions, and closes the daf with the famous teaching that one should be patient like Hillel, not impatient like Shammai — introducing test cases about to be told.

🛌 Reading the Mishna's 'Exempt' Carefully

Two levels of illness, two different rules
❓ The mishna says one who extinguishes a lamp for a sick person is 'exempt' — but if the illness is life-threatening, shouldn't it say 'permitted'?
↓
✅ Answer: the mishna IS about mortal danger, and it truly is permitted ab initio — 'exempt' is used only for stylistic match with the liable clause that follows
↓
⚖️ Contrast: Rabbi Oshaya's baraita (not life-threatening, so he may not extinguish ab initio, but if he did, he is exempt) follows Rabbi Shimon's view — a different, non-dangerous case

📖 Rabbi Tanḥum Resolves Solomon's Contradictions

Why Kohelet seems to praise death over life
Question
May one extinguish a lamp before a sick person on Shabbat?
R' Tanḥum
Solomon, where is your wisdom? Your father David said 'the dead praise not the Lord' — yet you said 'I praised the dead more than the living,' and also 'a living dog is better than a dead lion'!
Resolution 1
David meant: engage in Torah before death, since the dead are idle from mitzvot. Solomon's praise of 'the dead' refers to the righteous forefathers, whose merit outlasts any living king's decree.
Resolution 2
'A living dog is better than a dead lion' teaches that life itself is preferable — as shown by Solomon's own dilemma when David died and lay exposed.
Conclusion
A lamp is called נֵר, and a person's soul is also called נֵר — better to extinguish an ordinary lamp than to endanger the lamp of a human soul.

👑 David: Sin, Forgiveness, and Death

Three linked episodes
EpisodeKey detail
David's request for a signGod forgives his sin but says the forgiveness will be made known only in Solomon's lifetime, not David's
The Temple gatesThey would not open for the Ark until Solomon said 'remember the good deeds of David Your servant' — then fire descended and all Israel knew David was forgiven
David's deathHe studied Torah every Shabbat to keep the Angel of Death at bay; the Angel shook the garden trees, David went to look, the staircase broke, and in that pause of study he died
Solomon's dilemma: David's body lay in the sun with hungry dogs nearby; the study hall advised placing bread or an infant on the body so it could be moved indirectly, since the dead may not be handled directly on Shabbat.

📜 Why Kohelet and Proverbs Nearly Vanished

The Sages' method for resolving apparent contradictions
1️⃣ The Sages sought to suppress (ginzu) the Book of Ecclesiastes because its statements seem to contradict each other
↓
2️⃣ Kept because its beginning and end are Torah-true: 'what profit has man under the sun' (none — but before the sun, i.e. Torah, there is profit) and 'fear God and keep His mitzvot, for this is the whole man'
↓
3️⃣ 'Vexation is better than laughter' vs. 'I said of laughter, praiseworthy': resolved as God's anger at the righteous now vs. His joy with them in the World-to-Come
↓
4️⃣ The Book of Proverbs faced the same challenge — 'answer not a fool' vs. 'answer a fool': resolved as Torah matters (answer) vs. mundane matters (don't)
Key teaching: The Divine Presence rests only amid the joy of a mitzva — not sadness, laziness, frivolity, or idle talk (Elisha needed a minstrel to restore his prophecy).

😌 Be Patient Like Hillel, Not Impatient Like Shammai

The Sages' baraita opens a new set of stories
לְעוֹלָם יְהֵא אָדָם עַנְוְותָן כְּהִלֵּל וְאַל יְהֵא קַפְּדָן כְּשַׁמַּאי
The teaching: The Sages taught: A person should always be patient like Hillel and not impatient like Shammai. The Gemara introduces this principle with 'there was an incident involving two people' — setting up the famous stories (continued on the next daf) of would-be converts who tested Hillel's and Shammai's tempers with outrageous demands.
