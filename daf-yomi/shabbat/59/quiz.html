Shabbat 59 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
In the Gemara's resolved version, why does Rabbi Yoḥanan say a clapperless bell keeps its ritual impurity?
A. Because it is still fit to give a child water to drink
B. Because it is still fit to be struck against earthenware, producing a similar sound
C. Because metal never loses impurity
D. Because a craftsman could repair it
Correct: B — After the Gemara reverses the attribution, Rabbi Yoḥanan is the one who requires a same-type reuse, and striking the bell on earthenware still produces sound — matching its original purpose.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to Rabbi Yoḥanan, how does a metal animal-shoe get reused by a PERSON in a way that keeps it susceptible to impurity?
A. It is melted down into jewelry
B. It is used to smear oil on the body
C. It is strapped to one's own foot while fleeing battle, to run over thorns
D. It is used as a drinking cup
Correct: C — Rabbi Yoḥanan's answer preserves the same type of use as the original (footwear), which is why the Gemara uses this source to prove he requires a same-type reuse.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
What is an 'ir shel zahav' (city of gold), as the Gemara describes it?
A. A miniature siege engine
B. A gold tiara engraved with a depiction of Jerusalem, like the one Rabbi Akiva made for his wife
C. A type of ancient coin
D. A woven belt
Correct: B — Rabba bar bar Ḥana said in Rabbi Yoḥanan's name that it is 'Jerusalem of Gold,' the ornament Rabbi Akiva made for his wife.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
Why do the Rabbis forbid going out with a city-of-gold ornament ab initio, even though they consider it an ornament and not a burden?
A. Because it is too heavy to wear comfortably
B. Because they fear she might remove it to show a friend and come to carry it in the public domain
C. Because gold ornaments cannot become ritually impure
D. Because only priests' wives may wear it
Correct: B — The Rabbis agree it is an ornament (so one who goes out with it is exempt from a sin-offering), but still forbid it ab initio out of concern she will take it off to show someone and end up carrying it.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
Why does the mishna treat a ring with an engraved seal as a burden for a woman, unlike a plain ring?
A. A sealed ring is a man's tool for sealing documents, so it is not a woman's ornament
B. Sealed rings are always more valuable and thus forbidden
C. Sealed rings cannot become ritually impure
D. Sealed rings are permitted only for brides
Correct: A — Following Rabbi Neḥemya's principle that a ring's status follows its seal, a ring bearing a seal used for sealing documents is treated as a man's implement — a burden, not an ornament, for a woman.
