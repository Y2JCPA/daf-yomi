📖 Overview: Setting Processes in Motion Before Shabbat

Shabbat 18a–18b
וְשָׁוִין אֵלּוּ וְאֵלּוּ, שֶׁטּוֹעֲנִין קוֹרַת בֵּית הַבַּד וְעִגּוּלֵי הַגַּת
The scene: The mishna records Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel's report that his family gave white clothes to a gentile launderer no fewer than three days before Shabbat, and that Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel agree that one may load the olive press and the wine press before Shabbat so the oil and wine keep flowing in on Shabbat itself. The Gemara first traces the underlying dispute about when kneading becomes 'kneading,' then surveys a Tosefta's list of processes people may start before Shabbat and let run on their own — irrigation, incense, sulfur, salve — contrasted with the water mill, which may not be started because of the noise it makes, or because Jewish-owned utensils must themselves 'rest.' That principle of shevitat kelim, traced to Beit Shammai, is then tested against case after case: sulfur under silverware, tubs, lamps, pots on the fire, animal traps. The daf closes by turning to how much advance notice a gentile needs before Shabbat to sell him something, lend him something, or send him on an errand.

👔 The Mishna: Two Customs of Advance Preparation

Loading presses and laundering clothes before Shabbat begins
CaseRulingDetail
White clothes to a gentile laundererRabban Shimon ben Gamliel's family practiceGiven no fewer than three days before Shabbat, since white clothes are hardest to launder
Loading the olive-press beamBeit Shammai and Beit Hillel agree: permittedSet up before Shabbat so oil keeps being squeezed out through Shabbat
Loading the circular wine pressBeit Shammai and Beit Hillel agree: permittedSame principle — the grapes keep yielding wine on their own
Why these two agree: Later the Gemara explains: Beit Shammai only decreed against actions that would make one liable to a sin-offering if done on Shabbat itself. Squeezing oil or wine this way carries no such liability, so there was no reason to forbid starting it before Shabbat.

🍞 When Does Kneading Begin?

Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi vs. Rabbi Yosei on flour, ashes, and ink
Baraita
One person adds the flour, another adds the water into the vessel. Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi: the second person is liable for kneading, even though he never actually kneaded it.
R' Yosei
Not liable until he actually kneads the dough. Merely wetting it isn't enough.
Abaye
Maybe Rabbi Yosei only says that about flour, which can be kneaded — but ink can't be kneaded, so maybe wetting alone counts as the full act there?
Gemara
No — a parallel baraita about ashes (which also can't be kneaded) has Rabbi Yosei ben Rabbi Yehuda saying the same thing: not liable until actually kneaded.
Gemara
But were the two baraitot taught together? If not, maybe 'ashes' there really meant kneadable soil.
Answer
Since they weren't taught side by side, that reading can't be proven — but Rav Yosef holds this baraita about ink follows Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi: adding water alone is enough to be liable.
Bottom line: The mishna's rule that adding water to dry ink powder already counts as 'soaking it' follows Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi's position on kneading.

💧 Set It and Let It Run — Except the Mill

A Tosefta's list of Friday actions that continue automatically through Shabbat
📜 Tosefta: before Shabbat one may open a canal to fill a garden all day, place incense under clothes, sulfur under vessels, salve on an eye, a bandage on a wound — all continue 'on their own'
↓
🚫 EXCEPTION: wheat may not be placed in a water mill unless it will finish grinding while it is still day
↓
Rabba
Forbidden because the mill makes noise — it looks like Shabbat is being desecrated
Rav Yosef
Forbidden because of shevitat kelim — a Jew's utensils must also 'rest' on Shabbat (Exodus 23:13)
↓
✅ Rav Yosef's reason is adopted: the obligation for utensils to rest, not mere noise
Why this matters: Once 'resting of utensils' is the accepted reason, the Gemara must explain why incense, sulfur, and animal traps — which also keep 'working' through Shabbat — are nevertheless permitted.

⚖️ Testing Beit Shammai's Rule Case by Case

If utensils must rest by Torah law (Beit Shammai), why are some active utensils allowed?

No Action Performed

  • Incense/sulfur: permitted because placed on the ground, not held in a vessel obligated to rest
  • Flax bundles drying in an oven: the oven 'sits idle,' the drying happens on its own
  • Fish/bird/animal traps: permitted only when they are passive hooks and nets — a trap that actively works is forbidden

Declared Ownerless (Hefker)

  • Fermenting tub, Shabbat lamp, cooking pot, roasting spit — all continue their process into Shabbat
  • Beit Shammai permits these only if the owner declares them hefker while it is still day
  • Once ownerless, they are no longer a Jew's utensils obligated to rest
Underlying dispute: Rav Oshaya cited Rav Asi: shevitat kelim by Torah law is Beit Shammai's position, not Beit Hillel's. For Beit Hillel, even a utensil that performs a genuine action may be left running.

🕊️ How Much Notice a Gentile Needs Before Shabbat

Selling, lending, or handing something to a gentile so he doesn't work for you on Shabbat
1️⃣ Beit Shammai: give it to him only if there's time for him to reach his own house before dark
↓
2️⃣ Beit Hillel: sufficient if there's time to reach a house adjacent to the town wall — a shorter distance
↓
3️⃣ Rabbi Akiva: sufficient if there's time merely to exit the Jew's own doorway — what the gentile does afterward doesn't matter
↓
4️⃣ Rabbi Yosei bar Yehuda: Rabbi Akiva isn't a fourth opinion — he is simply explaining what Beit Hillel really meant
Same clock, different mitzva: A parallel dispute applies to selling leavened bread (chametz) to a gentile before Passover: Beit Shammai require certainty it will be used up before Passover; Beit Hillel permit selling as long as the Jew himself could still eat it.
