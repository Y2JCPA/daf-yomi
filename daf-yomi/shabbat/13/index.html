📖 Overview: Fences, a Hard Dilemma, and a New Mishna

Shabbat 13a–13b
וְאֶל אִשָּׁה בְּנִדַּת טוּמְאָתָהּ לֹא תִקְרָב
The scene: The daf extends the mishna's concern about a zav eating with a zava into a further rabbinic fence, and then takes up a difficult dilemma: may a menstruating woman (niddah) share a bed with her husband if both remain fully clothed? After weighing several proofs, the Gemara settles the question from a verse in Ezekiel and tells a sobering story about a scholar who died young for treating the law too lightly. The daf then opens the new mishna describing the eighteen decrees issued in the attic of Ḥananya ben Ḥizkiya ben Garon, including the story of Megillat Ta'anit and the start of the list of decrees disqualifying teruma.

🚫 A Fence Around a Fence — Whom May a Zav Eat With?

Extending the mishna's ban beyond the zav and zava themselves
Mishna
A zav may not eat with a zava, lest familiarity lead to sin — 'come and see how far purity spread in Israel,' since even two who are both impure may not share a meal.
Extension
A scrupulous zav (parush) may also not eat with a zav who is an am ha'aretz, lest the am ha'aretz get him used to spending time together.
Abaye
The concern is that the am ha'aretz will feed him untithed produce.
Rava
Most amei ha'aretz do tithe their produce — rather, the concern is that he'll grow accustomed to the am ha'aretz and, even after his own purity returns, be fed impure food.

🛌 May a Niddah and Her Husband Share a Bed, Both Clothed?

Rav Yosef's dilemma, tested against two mishnayot, then settled
❓ Rav Yosef's dilemma: may a menstruating woman sleep beside her husband, she in her clothes and he in his?
↓
🧀 First attempt: the fowl-with-cheese mishna — rejected, since one table means one mind, unlike two people who each keep their own awareness
↓
🍖 Second attempt: two guests eating meat and cheese at one table — also rejected, since there is no visible reminder there, while sleeping fully dressed is itself an unusual, noticeable change
↓
Ezekiel 18:6
Juxtaposes 'a woman in her impurity' with 'his neighbor's wife' — just as clothed contact with a forbidden married woman is prohibited, so is clothed contact with a niddah wife
Rabbi Pedat (dissent)
The Torah forbade only intimacy that constitutes actual forbidden relations (Leviticus 18:6) — mere affectionate contact is not included

💔 'Blessed Is the One Who Killed Him' — Eliyahu's Story

A cautionary tale about honoring the law of niddah
וְאֶל אִשָּׁה בְּנִדַּת טוּמְאָתָהּ לֹא תִקְרָב
The story: A devoted student, who studied and served Torah scholars extensively, died at half his years. His grieving widow told Eliyahu that during her days of impurity her husband never touched her, not even with his little finger — but during her 'white days,' after the bleeding stopped and before her immersion, they ate, drank, and slept together with bodily contact, with no further intent. Eliyahu answered: 'Blessed is the Omnipresent who killed him, for he showed no honor to the Torah' — since 'you shall not approach a woman in her menstrual impurity' (Leviticus 18:19) forbids even affectionate contact, not only relations. Rav Dimi later clarified this meant sleeping in one bed without actual touching; in the West they said a sinar (belt) separated them — yet since even that remained prohibited, he was held responsible.

📜 Megillat Ta'anit and the Attic of Ḥananya ben Ḥizkiya

The mishna's introduction to the eighteen decrees
QuestionAnswer
Who wrote Megillat Ta'anit?Ḥananya ben Ḥizkiya and his faction, who cherished the memory of Israel's troubles and salvations
Why not record every trouble?Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel: they are too numerous to write; also, 'a madman does not feel his own wound,' and 'dead flesh does not feel the scalpel' — only pain still felt gets recorded
Why is Ḥananya 'remembered for good'?He saved the book of Ezekiel from suppression — its contents seemed to contradict Torah law — sitting isolated with 300 jugs of oil until he resolved every contradiction
What happened that day in his attic?Sages gathered to visit him; when counted, Beit Shammai's students outnumbered Beit Hillel's, and eighteen decrees were issued following Beit Shammai's opinion

📋 The Eighteen Decrees Begin: What Disqualifies Teruma

Items that render teruma unfit without transmitting further impurity
1️⃣ One who ate food with first- or second-degree ritual impurity disqualifies teruma he touches — but (per Rabbi Yehoshua) does not transmit further impurity onward
↓
2️⃣ One who drinks impure liquids, or whose head and majority of his body entered drawn water right after immersing
↓
3️⃣ A pure person on whom three log of drawn water fell; a Torah scroll; unwashed hands; one who immersed but still awaits nightfall
↓
4️⃣ Foods and vessels that became impure through contact with certain liquids — the list the Gemara now works through case by case
