📖 Overview: Habits, Lice, and Bedside Manners

Shabbat 12a–12b
שַׁבָּת הִיא מִלִּזְעוֹק וּרְפוּאָה קְרוֹבָה לָבֹא
The scene: The daf resolves the contradiction about a zav's pouch, then works through several of the mishna's Shabbat-eve precautions in detail. Unlike the tailor's needle, tefillin may be worn out at dusk because a person is obligated to keep feeling them. The Gemara analyzes killing lice, delousing clothing in public, and reading by the light of a lamp — including how far the Sages' concern about adjusting the wick extends. It closes with the halakhot and etiquette of comforting mourners and visiting the sick on Shabbat, a leniency the Sages permitted only with difficulty.

🕯️ Reading by Candlelight — How Far Does the Decree Reach?

Rabba, the bonfire case, and an exception for an important person
1️⃣ Rabba: the ban applies even if the lamp is two stories high, or ten houses stacked one atop another — height gives no leniency, since it is a flat decree
↓
2️⃣ One person may not read alone; but two together, engaged in the same matter, may — since they remind each other. Engaged in two different matters, even two may not
↓
3️⃣ Around a bonfire (Rav Huna), even ten people seated apart may not read, since no one notices another adjusting the coals
↓
4️⃣ Rava: an important person may read, since he would never personally touch a dirty lamp — yet Rabbi Yishmael ben Elisha, who treated himself as a commoner for Torah, nearly adjusted the wick anyway, and (per Rabbi Natan) recorded the act in his notebook to bring a sin-offering when the Temple is rebuilt

😷 Weekday Kindnesses on Shabbat

Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel dispute matchmaking, teaching, mourning, and sick-visits

Beit Shammai forbids

  • Arranging a match to betroth children
  • Committing a child to learn Torah or a trade
  • Comforting mourners
  • Visiting the sick
  • — these are weekday matters, out of place on Shabbat

Beit Hillel permits

  • All of the above — since each act includes an element of mitzva

Still, a caution (R' Ḥanina)

  • 'Only with great difficulty did they permit comforting mourners and visiting the sick on Shabbat' — both visitor and mourner feel pain in the act

🗣️ What to Say at a Sick Person's Bedside

Several formulas, all built around 'Shabbat is here, from crying out'
WhoFormula or Practice
Baraita (standard)'It is Shabbat, when one may not cry out, and healing is soon to come'
Rabbi MeirAdds: 'Shabbat's merit is capable of engendering compassion'
Rabbi Yehuda'May the Omnipresent have compassion on you and on all the sick of Israel'
Rabbi Yosei'...on you, among the sick of Israel' — including him within the community
Shevna of JerusalemSays shalom on entering; on leaving, the full formula plus 'His compassion is abundant, rest in peace'
Where to sit: One should sit neither on the bed nor on a chair, but wrap himself and sit before the patient, since the Divine Presence rests above the sick person's head (Psalms 41:4, 'God will support him on the bed of illness').

🪱 Killing a Louse on Shabbat

From Rabbi Eliezer's severity to Rav Huna's practical ruling
❓ Rabbi Eliezer: one who kills a louse on Shabbat is as though he killed a camel
↓
⚖️ Beit Shammai forbid killing a louse on Shabbat; Beit Hillel permit it
↓
Tosefta
May squeeze the louse and throw it away, but not kill it outright
Abba Shaul
May take and throw it, but not squeeze it — in his view killing a louse is Torah-forbidden
↓
✅ Rav Huna: the halakha is squeeze and throw — the dignified way, even on weekdays. (Rabba and Rav Sheshet simply killed lice; Rava threw them into water; Rav Naḥman told his daughters, 'kill them, and let me hear the sound of the combs')

🧣 Tefillin at Dusk — Unlike the Tailor's Needle

A kal vaḥomer from the High Priest's frontplate
Baraita
A person may go out with tefillin on Shabbat eve at nightfall, ab initio — unlike the tailor's needle or scribe's quill.
Why?
Rabba bar Rav Huna: a person must constantly touch his tefillin — a kal vaḥomer from the High Priest's frontplate (tzitz), which has only one mention of God's name yet the Torah says must never leave his awareness (Exodus 28:38).
Result
Tefillin carry many more mentions of God's name, so one certainly remains aware of them and is unlikely to carry them out absentmindedly.
Ḥananya
adds a related practice: feel your clothing at dusk on Shabbat eve to check you haven't forgotten something in your pockets — Rav Yosef called this 'a significant halakha for Shabbat.'
