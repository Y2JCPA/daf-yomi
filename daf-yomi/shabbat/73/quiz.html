Shabbat 73 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
Why does Rava exempt someone who intended to throw an object two cubits in the public domain, but it landed four cubits away?
A. Because throwing objects is never forbidden on Shabbat
B. Because he had no intent to execute a throw covering the forbidden four-cubit distance
C. Because four cubits is not actually a forbidden distance
D. Because he was in a private domain the whole time
Correct: B — Rava holds that liability requires intent to perform the specific forbidden act; since the person only intended a permitted two-cubit throw, he is exempt even though the result was a forbidden four-cubit throw.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to Rabbi Yoḥanan, what does the mishna's tally of 'forty-less-one' primary labors come to teach?
A. That there are exactly 39 verses in the Torah about Shabbat
B. That if one performs all of these labors in a single lapse of awareness, he is liable to a separate sin-offering for each and every one
C. That only 39 labors can ever be performed in one day
D. That the number has no halakhic significance
Correct: B — Rabbi Yoḥanan explains that the specific count teaches that unwitting violation of all the primary categories of labor during a single lapse in awareness generates liability for each labor separately, not one collective liability.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
How many primary categories of labor (avot melachot) does the mishna list as forbidden on Shabbat?
A. Thirty-six
B. Thirty-nine
C. Forty
D. Forty-two
Correct: B — The mishna states 'avot melachot arba'im chaser achat' — forty-less-one, that is, thirty-nine.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Rav Kahana, why is one who prunes a tree specifically because he needs the wood liable to bring TWO sin-offerings?
A. Once for reaping (severing the branch) and once for planting (since pruning stimulates growth)
B. Once for building and once for plowing
C. Once for kindling and once for extinguishing
D. Two sin-offerings are never actually required in Jewish law
Correct: A — Rav Kahana holds that pruning for the wood combines cutting something off (reaping) with encouraging the tree's future growth (planting), making the person liable for both primary categories.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
Why does even Rabbi Yehuda agree that one who digs a hole only because he needs its dirt (not to make a hole) is exempt?
A. Because digging is never forbidden on Shabbat
B. Because Rabbi Yehuda's rule that one is liable for labor 'not needed for its own sake' applies only to a constructive result, and marring the ground this way is destructive
C. Because he only used his hands, not a tool
D. Because the amount of dirt removed was too small to count
Correct: B — Rabbi Abba explains that Rabbi Yehuda's stringency about labor performed for an incidental purpose is limited to cases where the physical result is constructive; here the resulting hole is a destructive mark on the ground, so even Rabbi Yehuda exempts it.
