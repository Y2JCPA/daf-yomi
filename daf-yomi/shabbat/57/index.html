📖 Overview: A New Chapter on Women's Shabbat Ornaments

Shabbat 57a–57b
לֹא תֵּצֵא אִשָּׁה לֹא בְּחוּטֵי צֶמֶר וְלֹא בְּחוּטֵי פִשְׁתָּן וְלֹא בִּרְצוּעוֹת שֶׁבְּרֹאשָׁהּ
The scene: A new mishna opens a new topic: which ornaments a woman may or may not wear into the public domain on Shabbat, and why an unwitting violation carries no sin-offering. The Gemara is immediately struck by the mishna's odd mention of ritual immersion and explains the connection: hair-ties that would interpose during a mitzva immersion are restricted on Shabbat precisely because a woman might loosen and carry them. The daf then works through woven chains, the meaning of totefet, sarvitin, and kavul, and a careful, multi-layered tracing of whether hair strings, unlike wool strings, ever interpose at all.

💍 The Mishna's List: What a Woman May Not Wear Out

Items she may not go out with into the public domain on Shabbat
CategoryForbidden items
Hair-tiesWool strings, flax strings, or straps braided into the hair
Head ornamentsTotefet, unsewn sarvitin, kavul (in the public domain)
JewelryCity-of-gold ornament, katla, nose rings, a ring with no seal, an unperforated needle
Crucial closing line: If she unwittingly went out wearing any of these, she is exempt from a sin-offering — showing this is a rabbinic decree, not a Torah-level prohibition.

🛁 Why Does This Mishna Mention Immersion?

A Shabbat law explained through a ritual-purity concern
❓ The Gemara asks: immersion has nothing to do with Shabbat — why does the mishna add 'she may not immerse in them until she loosens them'?
↓
💡 Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak (citing Rabba bar Avuh): the mishna is explaining its own reason — a 'what is the reason' structure, not an unrelated addition
↓
🔗 On weekdays, tight wool/flax ties block the water (chatzitza) unless loosened first
↓
✅ Therefore on Shabbat: she may not go out wearing them, lest a mitzva immersion arise, she loosen them to immerse validly, and then carry them four cubits in the public domain
Elegant structure: The 'immersion' clause is not a digression — it IS the reason for the Shabbat prohibition.

⛓️ Woven Chains and the Rule of 'She Doesn't Strangle Herself'

Two extensions of the same underlying logic
Rav Kahana
What about hollow woven chains braided into the hair — do they interpose?
Rav
Woven items were never included in the decree — water reaches through the weave regardless.
Rav Yosef (on necklaces)
Why does Rav Huna limit the interposition concern to hair, and not straps around the neck?
Resolution
Because a woman does not strangle herself — she never ties neck-straps tight enough to block the water.
Abaye's challenge
But girls may not go out with straps (ḥavakin) around their necks — why forbid it if it never interposes?
Ravina
That refers to a katla, a broad decorative strap — a woman does tighten that one, since it pleases her to look fuller-figured.
Underlying principle: The concern is always chatzitza — will the tie realistically be pulled tight enough to block the immersion water?

🎀 Naming the Head Ornaments

Four terms, four positions on the head

Totefet & Sarvitin

  • Totefet: a band running from ear to ear across the forehead
  • Sarvitin: strands hanging down to the cheeks, fastened to the hairnet
  • Poor women: made of colored materials; wealthy women: silver or gold

Kavul

  • Understood as a wool cap, worn beneath the hairnet
  • Permitted in a courtyard; Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar permits it even in public
  • Rule: anything beneath the hairnet may be worn out; anything above it may not

Istema

  • A small hat or ribbon gathering stray hairs
  • Not subject to diverse-kinds (kilayim)
  • Cannot contract leprosy impurity — but may not be worn into the public domain
Guiding logic: A woman will not uncover her hair in public even to show off an ornament — so whatever stays hidden beneath the net is generally permitted.

📏 Tracing the Halakha on Hair Strings

Does the law follow Rabbi Yehuda, that hair strings never interpose?
1️⃣ Rav Yosef, citing Rav Yehuda citing Shmuel: the halakha follows Rabbi Yehuda specifically regarding hair strands (not wool strings)
↓
2️⃣ Abaye objects: saying 'halakha follows Rabbi Yehuda' implies the Rabbis dispute him — but no view says hair strands interpose!
↓
3️⃣ Possible answer: Rabbi Yehuda simply said 'just as you agree hair doesn't interpose, agree wool doesn't either' — not proof of real disagreement
↓
4️⃣ Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak confirms from the mishna's precise wording — 'a woman may go out with hair strings, her own or another's' — that this must follow the Rabbis, who never disputed hair strands at all
Conclusion: All opinions agree hair strands never interpose; the genuine dispute concerns wool strings alone.
