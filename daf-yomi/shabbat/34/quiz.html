Shabbat 34 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
What convinced Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai that parts of the Tiberias marketplace were definitely pure?
A. A prophetic dream
B. An Elder recalled that Rabbi Yochanan ben Zakkai once grew teruma lupines there
C. The ground was uniformly hard everywhere
D. A count of the city's graves
Correct: B — An Elder told him that ben Zakkai, a priest, had planted and harvested teruma lupines there — proof the spot was presumed pure.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to the mishna, what are the three things a person must say at home on Friday at dusk?
A. Have you prayed? Have you eaten? Have you rested?
B. Have you tithed? Have you made an eiruv? Light the lamp
C. Have you bathed? Have you dressed? Light the candles
D. Have you locked the door? Fed the animals? Swept the house?
Correct: B — The mishna lists: ask whether tithes were separated, whether the eiruv was placed, and then instruct that the lamp be lit.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
During 'doubtful dusk' (safek chashecha), which of the following is still permitted according to the mishna?
A. Lighting the Shabbat lamp
B. Immersing ritually impure vessels
C. Tithing produce known to be definitely untithed
D. Insulating hot water for Shabbat
Correct: D — The mishna permits tithing demai, placing an eiruv, and insulating hot water during doubtful dusk, but not lighting the lamp, immersing vessels, or tithing definitely-untithed produce.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
How does Rav resolve the apparent contradiction between the mishna's opening (implying no action at doubtful dusk) and its later permission to place an eiruv then?
A. The mishna is simply mistaken
B. The opening refers to Torah-level boundary eiruvin, which need certainty; the later clause refers to rabbinic courtyard eiruvin
C. Both clauses refer to the same type of eiruv
D. The contradiction is never resolved
Correct: B — Rav distinguishes eiruvei techumin (Torah law, requiring certainty) from eiruvei chatzerot (rabbinic, tolerant of the doubt of twilight).
QUESTION 5 OF 5
In Rava's case of two eiruvin placed for two different people — one before dusk, one during twilight — what is the ruling?
A. Neither one acquires a valid eiruv
B. Only the one placed before dusk is valid
C. Only the one placed during twilight is valid
D. Both acquire their eiruv, because uncertainty in rabbinic law is decided leniently
Correct: D — Since twilight's status as day or night is unresolved, and this is a rabbinic-level question, the doubt is resolved leniently for both people.
