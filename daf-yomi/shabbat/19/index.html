📖 Overview: Timing, Muktzeh, and What May Finish on Its Own

Shabbat 19a–19b
אֵין צוֹלִין בָּשָׂר בָּצָל וּבֵיצָה אֶלָּא כְּדֵי שֶׁיִּצּוֹלוּ מִבְּעוֹד יוֹם
The scene: The daf opens with a run of practical rulings about interacting with gentiles before Shabbat: how long before Pesach one may still sell kutach containing chametz, that food left for a dog or a gentile in a courtyard need not be chased if they carry it off, and how much lead time is required before renting utensils, sending letters, setting sail, or laying siege to a city. It returns to Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel's laundering custom and Beit Shammai's decrees, then takes up a new question: may one crush garlic, grapes, or honeycombs before Shabbat and let the liquid finish seeping out on Shabbat itself? That leads into the dispute between Rav and Shmuel over muktzeh — items set aside for a non-food purpose. The daf ends with a fresh mishna setting the standard for how much roasting or baking must already be done before Shabbat begins.

Food is different: One may put food before a dog or a gentile in a courtyard; if the dog or the gentile picks it up and carries it into the public domain, no one need attend to it, since the Shabbat prohibition never applied to either of them.

🫒 May Crushed Grapes and Honey Finish Seeping Out on Shabbat?

Two mishnayot, two tannaim, and a question about what 'kneading' liquid out really means
R' Akiva
Garlic, unripe grapes, and stalks of wheat crushed while it was still day — one may not finish extracting their liquid once it gets dark.
R' Yishmael
One may continue tending to them and finish after dark — the liquid comes out 'on its own' under the weight.
Gemara
Is our mishna's rule — that whatever comes about on its own is fine — following Rabbi Yishmael?
R' Elazar
No — it follows a different mishna: honeycombs crushed before Shabbat, whose honey seeps out on Shabbat, are permitted, per Rabbi Elazar.
Gemara
But honey is food both before and after — no real 'squeezing.' Garlic and grapes go from food to liquid — that's true melacha!
Gemara
A later baraita reveals Rabbi Elazar (with Rabbi Shimon) permits even olives and grapes — so the honeycomb case does extend that far after all.
Resolution: Rava bar Chanina (citing Rabbi Yochanan) distinguishes: everyone agrees it's forbidden if the item was never ground at all; the dispute is only for items already ground but not yet fully pounded.

🐐 Why the Baraita Repeats Itself for the Gentile

One might think the dog's food and the gentile's food are governed differently
📜 One may place food before a dog in the courtyard; if it carries the food out, no one need attend to it
↓
❓ The baraita then repeats the identical rule for a gentile — why state it twice?
↓
💡 One might have thought: the dog's food is the courtyard-owner's responsibility (he owns the dog), while the gentile's food is not his responsibility at all — so perhaps he may NOT give it to him
↓
✅ The baraita teaches: even so, it is permitted — the same leniency applies to the gentile as to the dog
Point of the repetition: Without the second clause, one might wrongly assume a stricter rule applies whenever the person eating the food is a stranger rather than the homeowner's own animal.

🐐 Muktzeh: Set Aside for a Different Purpose

Rav and Shmuel disagree across several parallel cases

Rav: Forbidden

  • Oil press mats/oil, ship-cargo mats — designated for a specific use, so muktzeh applies
  • A goat raised for milk, a ewe for wool, a hen for eggs, oxen for plowing, dates for commerce — may not be eaten or slaughtered
  • Follows the position that muktzeh is a real, meaningful category

Shmuel: Permitted

  • All the same items may be used freely, since Shmuel holds muktzeh mostly does not apply
  • A student who ruled leniently like Shmuel's view (following Rabbi Shimon) in Rav's own town was excommunicated — not because he was wrong, but for public disrespect
  • Follows Rabbi Shimon's position in the parallel tannaitic dispute with Rabbi Yehuda
Not just theory: The Gemara also reports a dispute between two students about the best way to rescue possessions from a fire on Shabbat — one vessel at a time, repeatedly, or several vessels at once — paralleling a dispute between Rabba bar Zavda and Rav Huna.

🍞 New Mishna: How Much Must Be Cooked Before Shabbat?

Roasting, baking, and two Temple exceptions to the rule
1️⃣ Meat, an onion, or an egg may only be put up to roast if there is enough time for them to roast while it is still day
↓
2️⃣ Dough or a cake may only be placed in the oven if there is enough time for its surface to form a crust before dark
↓
3️⃣ Rabbi Eliezer is more lenient: enough time for just the bottom crust to harden
↓
4️⃣ EXCEPTION: the Paschal lamb may be lowered into the oven right at nightfall, and the fire of the Temple's Chamber of the Hearth may be kindled right at nightfall too
Why the exceptions work: Both exceptions rely on the same idea: the people involved — the members of a Paschal group, the priests tending the hearth — are meticulous about mitzvot and will not come to stoke the coals on Shabbat itself.
