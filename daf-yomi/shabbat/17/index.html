📖 Overview: Finishing the Eighteen Decrees

Shabbat 17a–17b
וְאוֹתוֹ הַיּוֹם הָיָה קָשֶׁה לְיִשְׂרָאֵל כַּיּוֹם שֶׁנַּעֲשָׂה בּוֹ הָעֵגֶל
The scene: The daf opens with Rabbi Akiva's careful correction of the ox-goad impurity ruling, then completes the tally of the eighteen decrees: the tense story of how the dispute over harvesting grapes for the press led Beit Shammai's students to physically outvote Beit Hillel's, several more decrees protecting teruma and separating Jews from gentile culture, and finally a new mishna opening a fresh topic — whether one may begin, on Friday, a process that will only finish on its own once Shabbat has started.

🐫 Rabbi Akiva's Correction: When Does an Object Transmit Corpse-Impurity?

Untangling a 'garbled halakha,' per Rabbi Tarfon's protest
❓ Rabbi Tarfon: the common version of the ox-goad ruling is garbled — 'I will bury my sons if this is not so!'
↓
🔧 Rabbi Akiva: I will correct it so that the Sages' words all hold together
↓
To the carrier
Impure only if the object is at least the width of an ox-goad
To the object itself
Becomes impure at any size at all, acting as its own 'tent' over the corpse
To others it touches
Transmits impurity onward only if it has an opening a handbreadth wide
↓
📏 Rabbi Yannai adds: this 'ox-goad' is narrow in width but a handbreadth in circumference — the Sages decreed on its circumference precisely because its width alone would not have qualified

⚔️ A Sword in the Study Hall

Grapes, olives, and the day Beit Shammai outvoted Beit Hillel
וְאוֹתוֹ הַיּוֹם הָיָה קָשֶׁה לְיִשְׂרָאֵל כַּיּוֹם שֶׁנַּעֲשָׂה בּוֹ הָעֵגֶל
The confrontation: Hillel and Shammai disputed whether grapes gathered for the press become susceptible to impurity through their own seeping juice. Hillel challenged: 'If so, why do we gather grapes in purity but not olives?' Shammai retorted: 'If you provoke me, I will decree impurity on the olive harvest too!' The dispute grew so heated that they stuck a sword in the study hall doorway, declaring that whoever wished to enter could, but no one could leave. That day Hillel sat bowed before Shammai like one of the students — a day the Gemara calls 'as difficult for Israel as the day the Golden Calf was made.' Even so, the people did not accept the decree from Hillel and Shammai themselves; only when their own students later reissued it was it accepted.

🍇 Why Does Gathering Grapes Cause Susceptibility to Impurity?

Three attempts to explain the decree
SageExplanation
Rabbi Ze'iri (first version)Lest one gather the grapes in impure baskets, whose impurity would confer deliberate-liquid status on the seeping juice — though this only works for one view of how impure vessels affect liquids
Rabbi Ze'iri (revised)Lest one gather into sealed, pitched baskets, where the juice pools instead of spilling out — making its presence there effectively wanted
RavaJuice is squeezed out willfully when separating clusters stuck together — just as a person sometimes squeezes a cluster on purpose, to test whether the grapes are ripe enough to harvest

📋 More of the Eighteen — Growths, a Dark Road, and Gentile Goods

Completing the list of decrees from Ḥananya's attic
1️⃣ Produce grown from planted teruma is itself teruma — lest a priest keep impure teruma to plant later, forget it is impure, and come to eat from what grows
↓
2️⃣ One caught by nightfall on the road, Shabbat eve, with a purse must hand it to a gentile companion rather than carry it in Torah-permitted increments of under four cubits at a time
↓
3️⃣ A single chained decree covers gentile bread and oil (because of their wine), their wine (because it leads to marrying their daughters), and their daughters (because of idolatry)
↓
4️⃣ The chain's final link: even a gentile infant is treated as one who transmits impurity like a full zav — to keep Jewish children from growing overly familiar with gentile children

🕰️ New Mishna: Starting on Friday, Finishing on Shabbat

May a process begun before Shabbat simply continue on its own?
Beit Shammai
One may only begin soaking dye or ink, drying flax in the oven, or dyeing wool on Shabbat eve if enough daylight remains for the process to be substantially finished before Shabbat starts.
Beit Hillel
Permit starting these processes even without enough daylight left — they may simply continue on their own once Shabbat has begun.
Same principle
Beit Shammai: traps for animals, birds, or fish must be set only with enough daylight to catch something before Shabbat; selling to a gentile or giving him hides or laundry likewise needs enough time for him to finish or reach a nearby place.
Same principle
Beit Hillel permits all of these regardless of how much daylight remains.
