📖 Overview: Set-Aside Objects and What Frees Them

Shabbat 50a–50b
לֹא שָׁנוּ אֶלָּא שֶׁלֹּא יִחֲדָן לְהַטְמָנָה, אֲבָל יִחֲדָן לְהַטְמָנָה — מְטַלְטְלִין אוֹתָן
The scene: The daf continues the theme of muktzeh — objects set aside from Shabbat use — and asks what act, thought, or prior use is enough to free such an object for Shabbat handling. Rava rules that wool fleece designated for insulating food may be moved, but Ravina limits this to fleece not taken from a merchant's shelf. Rav, Shmuel, and Rav Asi disagree about cut palm branches: must one tie them, merely intend to use them, or simply sit on them once before Shabbat? The same question resurfaces with a basket of earth, with cleaning agents like sand and natron, and finally with a pot buried in a basket of wool fleece.

🐑 Wool Fleece: Designated for Insulation or Still Set Aside?

The mishna's cover falls off the fleece — does that prove fleece may never be moved?
Mishna
He lifts the cover, and the fleece falls by itself — implying that even fleece used to insulate food may not be moved on Shabbat.
Rava
Not so. This applies only where one did not designate the fleece for insulating. If he designated it for insulating food, one may move it — it is no longer set aside.
Ravina
Rava's original statement stands, but the mishna is discussing fleece taken from a merchant's shelves (הֶפְתֵּק) — that fleece is still meant to be returned for sale, so it stays set aside even while insulating food.
Baraita
Confirmed: shelf-fleece may not be moved; but if a homeowner prepared fleece for his own use, he may move it.
Principle: Ownership and intent for personal use — not just present function — determine whether an object is muktzeh.

🌴 Sitting on Cut Palm Branches — Three Opinions

Branches cut for firewood, then reconsidered for sitting: what makes them permitted?
AmoraWhat is requiredUnderlying logic
RavTie the branches together before ShabbatAgrees with the first tanna: set-aside items need an action to change their status
ShmuelMerely intend on Shabbat eve to sit on themAgrees with Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel: thought alone suffices
Rav AsiBriefly sit on them on Shabbat eve — no tying, no intent neededFollows the baraita: any use before Shabbat, however brief, permits use on Shabbat
Support for Rav Asi: The Gemara finds his view in a Tosefta about wound-dressings used briefly before Shabbat, and in the mishna about straw with a pillow spread on it before Shabbat.

🏺 A Basket of Earth: Is an Action Required?

Rav Yehuda permits using earth brought in on Shabbat eve — but what is the rule behind it?
❓ Rav Yehuda: one may bring in a basket of earth on Shabbat eve and use it all Shabbat, if he designated a corner for it (Mar Zutra)
↓
🤔 The Sages before Rav Pappa: this must follow Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel — the Rabbis require an action, not mere designation!
↓
Object CAN be acted on
e.g. palm branches — tying is possible, so the Rabbis require it
Object CANNOT be acted on
earth cannot be 'tied' — so even the Rabbis agree thought alone suffices
↓
✅ Rav Pappa: even according to the Rabbis, this ruling on earth stands — no contradiction
Result: The requirement of an 'action' only applies where an action is physically possible to perform.

🧼 Sand and Natron: Two Baraitot, One Dispute

May silver utensils be cleaned with sand and natron on Shabbat?

Rabbi Yehuda — Prohibits

  • An unintentional act is forbidden
  • Sand/natron unintentionally smooths and polishes the silver
  • Two sub-opinions: whether sand actually scrapes at all

Rabbi Shimon — Permits

  • An unintentional act is permitted
  • Cleaning is the intent; smoothing is a side-effect
  • Yet even he forbids washing hair with them (may cause hair to fall out)

Practical Face-Washing

  • Rav Yehuda: powdered frankincense — permitted
  • Rav Yosef: jasmine residue — permitted
  • Rava: ground pepper; Rav Sheshet: berada (aloe/myrtle/violet mix) — none cause hair loss
Resolved: Two tanna'im both follow Rabbi Yehuda but disagree only about whether sand and natron actually scrape the silver.

🍯 Removing a Pot from a Basket of Fleece

The mishna's dispute: may one lift the pot straight up, or must the basket be tilted?
1️⃣ Rabbi Elazar ben Azarya: tilt the basket on its side so the fleece falls away, then take the pot — lifting straight up risks disturbing the set-aside fleece
↓
2️⃣ The Rabbis: one may simply lift the pot out and later replace it, without tilting
↓
3️⃣ Rav (via Rabbi Abba): everyone agrees that once the cavity in the fleece has actually collapsed, returning the pot is forbidden
↓
4️⃣ Resolution: the real dispute is only whether to be concerned in advance that lifting the pot might collapse the cavity
Takeaway: Both opinions agree on the underlying halakha; they disagree only on how much preventive concern to apply.
