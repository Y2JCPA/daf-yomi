Shabbat 50 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to Ravina, why may wool fleece taken from a merchant's shelves (heftek) not be moved even when used to insulate food?
A. It is ritually impure
B. It is still designated to be returned to the shelf for sale
C. It belongs to a non-Jew
D. It was never touched before Shabbat
Correct: B — Ravina explains the mishna is about shelf-fleece, which remains set aside for resale regardless of its temporary use for insulation.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
What does Rav Asi require in order to permit sitting on cut palm branches on Shabbat?
A. Tying the branches together
B. Formally declaring an intention to use them
C. Briefly sitting on them before Shabbat, even without tying or intending
D. Nothing at all — they are always permitted
Correct: C — Rav Asi holds that any use of the item before Shabbat, however brief, is enough to permit its use on Shabbat, as shown by the baraita about wound bandages used briefly beforehand.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
Why does Rav Pappa say that even the Rabbis (who normally require an action) agree that thinking about using the basket of earth is sufficient?
A. Because earth is not really muktzeh
B. Because the Rabbis only require an action for objects on which an action is physically possible to perform
C. Because Rav Yehuda overruled the Rabbis
D. Because earth was never set aside in the first place
Correct: B — Rav Pappa limits the Rabbis' requirement of an action to cases where a preparatory action can actually be performed; since nothing can be 'done' to loose earth, thought alone suffices.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Rabbi Yehuda, why is cleaning silver utensils with sand and natron prohibited on Shabbat?
A. Because sand is muktzeh
B. Because it unintentionally smooths and polishes the silver, and Rabbi Yehuda forbids unintentional acts
C. Because natron is used only for washing hair
D. Because it is a weekday-only activity
Correct: B — Rabbi Yehuda holds that an unintentional act (davar she'eino mitkavein) is prohibited, so the incidental smoothing caused by sand/natron makes the cleaning forbidden.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
What is the actual point of dispute between Rabbi Elazar ben Azarya and the Rabbis regarding the pot buried in a basket of fleece?
A. Whether the pot may be eaten from at all
B. Whether one must be concerned in advance that removing the pot straight up will collapse the fleece's cavity
C. Whether fleece may ever insulate food
D. Whether the basket itself is muktzeh
Correct: B — Everyone agrees that once the cavity has actually collapsed, returning the pot is forbidden; the dispute is only whether to be preemptively concerned that lifting the pot will cause the collapse.
