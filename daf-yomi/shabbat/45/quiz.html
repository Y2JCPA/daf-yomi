QUESTION 1 OF 5
Why does Rav permit placing a lamp atop a palm tree before Shabbat but not before a Festival?
A. Palm trees are forbidden on Festivals
B. Since the lamp is muktzeh on Shabbat no one will climb the tree, but since it's movable on a Festival someone might climb up and misuse the tree itself
C. Lamps cannot burn on Festivals
D. There is no real distinction
Correct: B — The distinction only makes sense if Rav holds like Rabbi Yehuda that the lamp is muktzeh on Shabbat, preventing anyone from climbing to retrieve it; on a Festival where it's movable, climbing the tree becomes a concern.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to Rabbi Yochanan's original answer to Reish Lakish, what is the one true case of set-aside (muktzeh) for Rabbi Shimon?
A. Any food left overnight
B. Oil in a lamp while it is actually burning
C. Any object touched on Shabbat
D. Wool that gets wet
Correct: B — Rabbi Yochanan answered that the only case of muktzeh Rabbi Shimon recognizes is oil in a burning lamp, since it was set aside for its mitzva (kindling) and thereby for its prohibition (extinguishing).
QUESTION 3 OF 5
How does the Gemara reconcile the sukkah-decorations baraita (forbidding use of hung fruit) with Rabbi Yochanan's statement limiting muktzeh to 'oil in the lamp'?
A. It rejects the sukkah baraita entirely
B. It refines the statement: muktzeh applies to anything set aside for a mitzva alone, similar in kind to the burning-lamp case, not literally identical to it
C. It says sukkah decorations were never really forbidden
D. It concludes Rabbi Shimon has no opinion on sukkot
Correct: B — The Gemara restates Rabbi Yochanan's principle to mean muktzeh applies to anything 'similar to' oil in the lamp — set aside for its mitzva alone is sufficient, resolving the apparent contradiction.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Shmuel (in the name of Rav Yehuda), what is the sole case of muktzeh for Rabbi Shimon regarding food left to dry?
A. Any fruit left outside
B. Dried figs and raisins alone
C. Only vegetables
D. Nothing — Rabbi Shimon has no muktzeh at all
Correct: B — Rav Yehuda said in the name of Shmuel that dried figs and raisins are the only case of muktzeh recognized by Rabbi Shimon, later confirmed to also exclude unripe dates.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
How does Rav Yosef reconcile the two conflicting reports about Rabbi Yochanan's final halakhic ruling on set-aside?
A. He says both reports are equally authoritative and unresolved
B. He explains that the earlier report ('they said the halakha follows Rabbi Shimon') meant others said it in Rabbi Yochanan's name, while Rabbi Yochanan himself actually held like Rabbi Yehuda
C. He dismisses both reports as unreliable
D. He says Rabbi Yochanan changed his mind daily
Correct: B — Rav Yosef reads the phrase 'they said' as distancing Rabbi Yochanan from that ruling — implying it was reported by others, while Rabbi Yochanan's own position (per Rav Yitzchak's later report) followed Rabbi Yehuda.
