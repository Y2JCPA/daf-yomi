📖 Overview: How Far Does Set-Aside Extend for Rabbi Shimon?

Shabbat 45a–45b
אֵין מוּקְצֶה לְרַבִּי שִׁמְעוֹן אֶלָּא שֶׁמֶן שֶׁבַּנֵּר בְּשָׁעָה שֶׁהוּא דּוֹלֵק, הוֹאִיל וְהוּקְצָה לְמִצְוָתוֹ הוּקְצָה לְאִיסּוּרוֹ
The scene: The daf opens by showing that Rav's own rulings — a lamp placed atop a palm tree before Shabbat but not before a Festival, and the leniency to move a Hanukkah lamp in danger — align him with Rabbi Yehuda's view that set-aside items may not be moved, except under exigent circumstances. Reish Lakish then asks Rabbi Yochanan how far Rabbi Shimon's leniency on set-aside really extends, testing it against sown wheat and incubating eggs. The Gemara pushes this question through sukkah decorations, drying figs and raisins, and unripe dates, before turning to a dispute over wild versus domesticated animals and finally working through several conflicting reports of exactly whose halakha — Rabbi Yehuda's or Rabbi Shimon's — was ultimately accepted.

🌴 A Lamp on a Palm Tree: Shabbat vs. Festival

Rav's distinction only makes sense if he follows Rabbi Yehuda
📜 Rav: one may place a lamp atop a palm tree before Shabbat to burn there, but not before a Festival
↓
❓ If Rav held like Rabbi Shimon (no muktzeh at all), what difference would there be between Shabbat and a Festival?
↓
✅ Since the lamp is set-aside on Shabbat, no one will climb the tree to move it; but since it's movable on a Festival, one might climb up and improperly use the tree itself
Objection and answer: Rav permitted moving a Hanukkah lamp away from Persian fire-priests in danger, seemingly against muktzeh — but Rav Kahana and Rav Ashi confirm this was only because 'Rabbi Shimon is worthy to rely on in exigent circumstances.'

🌾 How Far Does Rabbi Shimon's Leniency Go?

Reish Lakish tests the boundary of muktzeh with two hard cases
Reish Lakish
Wheat sown in the ground, or eggs placed under a hen to incubate — since one actively rejected them by his own hand, does even Rabbi Shimon agree they're set-aside? Or is there no difference from ordinary cases?
R. Yochanan
There is only one true case of set-aside for Rabbi Shimon: oil in a lamp while it is burning.
R. Yochanan
Since it was set aside for its mitzva — kindling the Shabbat lamp — it is thereby also set aside for its prohibition, the concern of extinguishing it.
Immediate challenge: But a baraita forbids using sukkah decorations (nuts, fruit, wreaths) until the festival ends — proof that 'set aside for a mitzva alone' does apply more broadly for Rabbi Shimon.

🎪 Expanding the Cases: Sukkah, Figs, and Dates

Rabbi Yochanan's statement is refined rather than overturned

Sukkah Decorations

  • Nuts, fruit, and wreaths hung for beauty: forbidden to use until the festival ends
  • Wood from the sukkah itself: also forbidden (even Rabbi Yehuda agrees here)
  • Unless the owner stipulated otherwise in advance

Dried Figs & Raisins

  • Shmuel: the only case of set-aside for Rabbi Shimon
  • One who leaves food on the roof to dry needs prior designation to eat it on Shabbat

Unripe Dates

  • Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi to his son: still only figs and raisins are muktzeh for Rabbi Shimon
  • Unripe dates left to ripen are not included
Refined rule: Rabbi Yochanan's principle is restated: set-aside for Rabbi Shimon applies only in a case 'similar to oil in the lamp' — set aside for its mitzva alone is enough, without any separate Torah prohibition.

🐐 Wild Animals vs. Domesticated Animals

A mishna dispute tests whether Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi holds any muktzeh at all
CategoryDefinitionMay be watered/slaughtered on Yom Tov?
Domesticated (bayyatot)Graze beyond the town limits by day, return to sleep within themYes
Wild (midbariyot)Go out at Passover, return only with the rainy seasonNo — set aside, unattended
Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi's viewBoth categories above count as domesticatedYes for both
Three resolutions: Perhaps these animals resemble figs/raisins (an exception even Rabbi Shimon grants); or Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi spoke according to Rabbi Shimon without holding it himself; or he spoke within the Rabbis' own framework to win a partial concession.

🏺 Settling the Halakha: Conflicting Reports

Several amoraim report Rabbi Yochanan's final ruling differently
1️⃣ Rabba bar bar Chana, in the name of Rabbi Yochanan: 'They said the halakha follows Rabbi Shimon' — but Rabbi Yochanan himself once ruled strictly about a hen's roost
↓
2️⃣ Resolved: that roost held a dead chick (or an egg laid on Shabbat) — even Rabbi Shimon agrees such items are set aside
↓
3️⃣ Rav Yitzchak son of Rabbi Yosei later reported: Rabbi Yochanan actually ruled like Rabbi Yehuda; Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi ruled like Rabbi Shimon
↓
4️⃣ Rav Yosef reconciles this: the earlier report ('they said...') meant others said it in Rabbi Yochanan's name — he himself never held that way
Supporting evidence: Rabbi Asi, a student of Rabbi Yochanan, would not move a candelabrum that fell on his cloak — consistent with Rabbi Yochanan following Rabbi Yehuda's stricter view.
