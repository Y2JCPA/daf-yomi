📖 Overview: Liturgy for an Unwritten Holiday

Shabbat 24a–24b
מַהוּ לְהַזְכִּיר שֶׁל חֲנוּכָּה בְּבִרְכַּת הַמָּזוֹן
The scene: The Gemara first confirms Rav Chisda's ruling: everything disqualified for the Shabbat lamp becomes permitted again for a Festival lamp, except 'burnt oil,' since consecrated items may never be burned on a Festival. That prohibition against burning consecrated items on Yom Tov opens a string of dilemmas about how Chanukah — a rabbinic institution with no Torah source — should be woven into the liturgy: is it mentioned in Grace after Meals, is Rosh Chodesh treated differently there, and is Chanukah mentioned in the additional Musaf prayer at all? A concluding halakha, drawn from Yom Kippur's closing prayer, settles how a day's own character governs which prayers mention it. The daf then turns to a fresh mishna listing which oils may be used for the Shabbat lamp at all.

🍽️ Should Chanukah Be Mentioned in Grace After Meals?

Rabbinic origin versus the value of publicizing the miracle
❓ Dilemma: since Chanukah is only rabbinic, do we skip mentioning it in Birkat HaMazon — or mention it anyway for pirsumei nisa?
↓
⚖️ Rav Huna's ruling: one does NOT mention it — but if he wishes to anyway, he adds it in the blessing of Hoda'ah (thanksgiving)
↓
📖 Same question raised for Rosh Chodesh (a Torah-level day): Rav says mention it; Rabbi Chanina says don't
↓
✅ Rav Zerika: follow Rav — supported by Rabbi Oshaya's baraita distinguishing days with a musaf offering (mention required) from days without one (not required)
The dividing line: Days with an additional Temple offering — Rosh Chodesh, the intermediate days of a Festival — get mention in Birkat HaMazon; Monday/Thursday fast days and ma'amadot do not, and even skipping the day's mention in the Amida there doesn't require repeating the prayer.

🙏 Chanukah in the Musaf Prayer: A Real Dispute

Does a day without its own additional prayer still get mentioned in someone else's?
Rav Huna & R' Yehuda
One does not mention Chanukah in Musaf — Chanukah has no additional-offering prayer of its own, so it has no place there.
Rav Nachman & R' Yochanan
One does mention it — the day itself carries the obligation, regardless of which prayer is being recited.
Abaye
Compare to Rosh Chodesh on Shabbat: the haftarah reader need not mention Rosh Chodesh, since without Shabbat there'd be no haftarah reading at all that day.
Gemara
Not comparable — there, the haftarah has no other connection to Rosh Chodesh; here, Chanukah IS mentioned in the evening, morning, and afternoon prayers already.
Better parallel
Rav (via Rav Achadvoi): a Festival's Mincha haftarah reader on Shabbat need not mention the Festival — since without Shabbat, there's no haftarah at Mincha on Yom Tov either.
Final halakha (next daf's conclusion): The Gemara ultimately follows Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi's principle from Yom Kippur: a day obligated in its own set of prayers gets mentioned in all of them, once that day's own character truly requires it.

🛢️ New Mishna: How Fussy Must the Oil Be?

Three positions on lighting the Shabbat lamp

Rabbi Yishmael

  • Forbids tar (itran) specifically
  • Reason: bad smell — 'in deference to Shabbat' so no one is driven from the room

The Sages

  • Permit essentially all oils that burn properly
  • Explicitly: sesame, nut, radish, fish, gourd oil, even tar and naphtha

Rabbi Tarfon

  • Most restrictive: olive oil only
  • Reflects the view (echoed by Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi, next daf) that olive oil is simply the most select

📜 Four Sources for 'No Burning Consecrated Items on Yom Tov'

Each Sage roots the same halakha in a different verse
1️⃣ Chizkiya: the doubled phrase 'until morning' in the Paschal offering law (Exodus 12:10) grants a second morning — the Festival's morning is excluded from burning
↓
2️⃣ Abaye: 'the burnt-offering of Shabbat, on its own Shabbat' (Numbers 28:10) — implicitly excludes a weekday offering being brought on Shabbat or Yom Tov
↓
3️⃣ Rava: 'it alone shall be done for you' (Exodus 12:16) — 'it' permits food prep, but not preparatory labors like burning that aren't themselves eating-related
↓
4️⃣ Rav Ashi: the term שַׁבָּתוֹן itself frames Yom Tov as carrying its own positive command to rest, which a mere positive act cannot override
