📖 Overview: Hillel's Patience, Shammai's Impatience, and Fear of Heaven

Shabbat 31a–31b
עַל שָׁלֹשׁ עֲבֵירוֹת נָשִׁים מֵתוֹת בִּשְׁעַת לֵידָתָן: עַל שֶׁאֵינָן זְהִירוֹת בַּנִּדָּה, בַּחַלָּה, וּבְהַדְלָקַת הַנֵּר
The scene: Continuing from the previous daf's teaching to be patient like Hillel and not impatient like Shammai, the Gemara tells four vivid stories: a man who bets 400 zuz that he can anger Hillel with mocking ethnic questions, and three gentiles who come to convert on outrageous conditions — accepting only the Written Torah, learning the whole Torah 'on one foot,' and becoming High Priest. Shammai rebuffs each; Hillel converts all three and teaches them Torah's substance through patience. The Gemara then explores fear of Heaven as the foundation beneath Torah study, with Reish Lakish mapping the six orders of Mishna onto a verse in Isaiah, and Rava describing the questions a soul faces after death. The daf's aggadic run on lighting the Shabbat lamp then closes with a new mishna: women die in childbirth for negligence in three mitzvot — niddah, separating ḥalla, and kindling the Shabbat lamp — and the Gemara explains why negligence in each corresponds to danger specifically in childbirth.

👑 Learning Royal Protocol From the Torah

The convert reasons an a fortiori from David himself
וְהַזָּר הַקָּרֵב יוּמָת
The teaching: Hillel told the would-be High Priest that one must first learn 'the protocols of royalty' — meaning, study Torah. When the convert reached 'the stranger who draws near shall be put to death' (Numbers 1:51) and asked about whom it spoke, Hillel answered: 'Even King David.' The convert reasoned: if this restriction applies even to Israel, God's beloved children, all the more so to a convert who arrived with nothing but his staff and bag. He returned to Hillel and thanked him for bringing him under the wings of the Divine Presence, retracting his demand to be made High Priest.

📚 Fear of Heaven: The Foundation Beneath Torah

Two teachings on why fear of Heaven must come first

Reish Lakish — Isaiah 33:6

  • Maps six words of the verse to the Six Orders of Mishna (Zeraim, Moed, Nashim, Nezikin, Kodashim, Teharot)
  • Concludes: even after mastering all six, 'the fear of the Lord is his treasure' — the true prize

Rabba bar Rav Huna — the Treasurer

  • One who has Torah but not fear of Heaven is like a treasurer given the inner keys but not the outer
  • He cannot even enter to use what he holds
  • Rabbi Yannai: 'Woe to one who builds a gate but has no courtyard'
Rava's postmortem questions: Did you deal faithfully in business? Did you fix times for Torah? Did you engage in procreation? Did you hope for salvation? Did you debate wisely? — yet all depend on whether fear of the Lord was one's treasure.
