Shabbat 56 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to the Gemara's defense, what was the actual failing of the sons of Samuel?
A. They worshipped idols
B. They did not travel to judge Israel in their own towns as their father did, staying home instead to enrich their attendants
C. They stole from the Temple treasury
D. They refused to serve as judges at all
Correct: B — The verse 'in his ways they did not walk' is explained as their failure to emulate Samuel's practice of traveling to judge Israel in its various towns, rather than an accusation of outright corruption.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
How does Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi read the phrase 'to do evil' (rather than 'and he did evil') regarding David?
A. As proof David actually sinned twice
B. As indicating David only sought to do evil but did not carry it out, unlike every other Torah figure described with 'and he did'
C. As a scribal error
D. As referring to a different king entirely
Correct: B — Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi notes that every other instance of evil in the Torah is written with 'and he did,' but here it says 'to do,' suggesting David intended evil without actually performing it.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
What contradiction does Abaye the Elder point out in Rav's statements about David?
A. Whether David was a king or a prophet
B. Rav says no sin is found in David's life except regarding Uriah, yet Rav also says David accepted a slanderous report about Mephibosheth
C. Whether David fought the Philistines or the Amalekites
D. Whether David wrote the Psalms
Correct: B — Abaye notes the tension between Rav's statement that David's only sin was the Uriah affair, and Rav's separate statement that David accepted lashon hara — the Gemara leaves this difficulty (kashya) unresolved.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to the accepted explanation (citing Rabbi Yosei), why is Solomon blamed for building the high places of idolatry, similar to how Josiah is praised for destroying them?
A. Because he personally worshipped every idol
B. The verse juxtaposes them: Josiah is credited for what happened under his reign even though earlier kings did much of the work, and Solomon is blamed for what happened under his watch, since he failed to prevent it
C. Because Solomon lived longer than Josiah
D. Because the verses are unrelated
Correct: B — The Gemara explains that just as Josiah is credited with destroying idols largely removed by his predecessors, Solomon is blamed for the high places built under his reign because he failed to protest, even if he did not personally worship there.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
How does the Gemara reinterpret the verse that 'there was no king like Josiah who returned to the Lord,' given the claim that Josiah never actually sinned?
A. It refers to Josiah's mother's repentance, not his own
B. It refers to his repaying, from his own money, every judgment he issued between the ages of 8 and 18, in case his youthful rulings had erred — not repentance from actual sin
C. It is simply rejected as inauthentic
D. It refers to Josiah's decision to rebuild the Temple
Correct: B — The Gemara explains that Josiah personally reimbursed parties from cases he judged as a child (ages 8-18) out of concern he may have judged incorrectly, and this act of returning money is what the verse calls 'returning to the Lord,' not repentance for sin.
