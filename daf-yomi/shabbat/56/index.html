📖 Overview: Defending David, Solomon, and Josiah

Shabbat 56a–56b
כׇּל הָאוֹמֵר דָּוִד חָטָא אֵינוֹ אֶלָּא טוֹעֶה
The scene: Continuing the sequence begun with Reuben, Rabbi Shmuel bar Naḥmani in the name of Rabbi Yonatan defends the reputations of the sons of Samuel, King David, King Solomon, and King Josiah, reinterpreting biblical verses that seem to describe their sins. The Gemara does not let these defenses stand unchallenged: it raises real textual difficulties, cites contrary views (including from Rav himself), and works through the political and moral complexity of David's conduct toward Uriah and Mephibosheth, and Solomon's tolerance of his wives' idolatry — closing the chapter with a debate over whether Josiah was a penitent at all.

⚖️ Did the Sons of Samuel Really Sin?

The verse says they sought unjust gain — but what did that actually mean?
TannaHow they 'sought unjust gain'
Rabbi MeirThey vocally demanded their tithe portions from other Levites
Rabbi YehudaThey imposed on homeowners to buy their merchandise
Rabbi AkivaThey took an extra basket of tithes by force, beyond their due
Rabbi YoseiThey took only what was owed them — but improperly seized it by force
The core defense: 'In his ways they did not walk' — unlike their father Samuel, who traveled to judge Israel in their own towns, they stayed home to enrich their own attendants. That failure to emulate, not outright corruption, is what the verse condemns.

👑 'Anyone Who Says David Sinned Is Mistaken'

Rabbi Shmuel bar Naḥmani's defense meets the plain reading of Nathan's rebuke
Defense
'David succeeded in all his ways, and the Lord was with him' — could sin have touched him while the Divine Presence remained?
Challenge
But Nathan rebuked him: 'Why have you despised the Lord's word, to do evil?'
Answer
He sought to do evil but did not actually do it. Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi notes: elsewhere the Torah says 'and he did' evil; here it says only 'to do' evil.
On Uriah's death
'You killed Uriah with the sword' means: you should have judged him before the Sanhedrin for treason and did not — for Uriah called Joab 'my lord' in the king's presence.
On taking Bathsheba
'You took his wife' means you had rights to her — soldiers going to David's wars wrote a conditional bill of divorce beforehand.
Bottom line: David's intentions were faulted, but according to this reading his actions were halakhically defensible.

🗣️ A Contradiction: Did David Accept Slander?

Rav's own statements seem to clash
❓ Rav: examining David's whole life, no sin is found except the matter of Uriah
↓
⚡ But Rav also said: David accepted a slanderous report (about Mephibosheth, from Ziba) — a contradiction left unresolved ('kashya')
↓
📖 Story: Ziba slandered Mephibosheth twice; David believed him even after catching him in a lie the first time
↓
Rav's view
David simply accepted the slander uncritically
Shmuel's view
David saw genuine corroborating evidence — Mephibosheth's disheveled appearance and bitter words
↓
💥 Consequence: when David divided the estate between them, a Divine Voice foretold that Rehoboam and Jeroboam would divide the kingdom
Rav Yehuda cites Rav: Had David not accepted that report, the kingdom would not have split, Israel would not have worshipped idols, and we would not have been exiled.

🏛️ Solomon: Innocent Intent, or Failure to Protest?

Two proposed readings of his relationship to idolatry

Sought but never built

  • 'His wives turned his heart' — only an attempt, he did not follow
  • 'He built an altar for Chemosh' — only sought to build
  • Rejected: the same verb describes Joshua actually building an altar, so it must mean actual construction here too

Accepted explanation (Rabbi Yosei)

  • Solomon did build the high places — later destroyed by Josiah
  • But he is blamed the way Josiah is praised: for what happened under his watch, even though earlier righteous kings share responsibility too
  • 'He did evil' — because he should have protested his wives' idol worship and did not
Rav Yehuda, citing Shmuel: It would have been better for that righteous man to have served idolatry as a menial servant than for Scripture to write of him, 'he did evil in the sight of the Lord.'

🕊️ Josiah: Righteous From the Start, or a Penitent?

Two very different readings of the same praise
1️⃣ Rabbi Shmuel bar Naḥmani: 'anyone who says Josiah sinned is mistaken' — the verse calls him fully righteous from the start
↓
2️⃣ Difficulty: 'like him there was no king who returned to the Lord' — implying repentance from actual sin
↓
3️⃣ Resolution: every judgment he issued between ages 8 and 18 he personally repaid from his own funds, in case his youthful rulings had erred — not repentance for sin
↓
4️⃣ Rav disagrees entirely: 'there is no greater penitent than Josiah' — and names contemporary penitents in his own generation as parallels
Chapter's close: The chapter 'Bameh Behema' ends here, on this open question of how far genuine repentance, and genuine sin, extend even among the righteous.

⚖️ Sons of Samuel: did not sin outright, but failed to emulate their father's devoted judging
↓

↓

🏛️ Solomon is blamed for failing to protest his wives' idolatry, not for worshipping it himself
