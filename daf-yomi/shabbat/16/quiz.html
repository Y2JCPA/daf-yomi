Shabbat 16 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
Why does a broken-and-recast glass vessel NOT reassume its former impurity, unlike a metal vessel?
A. Because glass cannot actually be melted and recast
B. Because glass's basic impurity is only rabbinic, and 'previous impurity' is itself a rabbinic concept — the Sages did not stack one rabbinic decree on another
C. Because glass vessels are never impure at all
D. Because only priests use glass vessels
Correct: B — Since both glass's underlying impurity and the concept of 'previous impurity' are rabbinic, the Sages did not compound one rabbinic decree with another for glass.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
Why did Shel Tziyyon the queen break and recast her impure vessels before her son's wedding feast, and what did the Sages rule?
A. She wanted more elaborate vessels; the Sages praised her
B. She hoped to escape their ritual impurity; the Sages ruled the new vessels reverted to their previous impurity anyway
C. She was required to do so by law; the Sages agreed the impurity was gone
D. She acted to honor Shimon ben Shataḥ; the Sages exempted her
Correct: B — Shel Tziyyon melted and recast her impure vessels hoping to purify them, but the Sages ruled that recast metal vessels reassume their previous impurity.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
What is Rava's distinct reason for decreeing 'previous impurity' on recast metal vessels, as opposed to Abaye's reason?
A. That the metal itself is inherently dangerous
B. That people might conclude same-day immersion alone was sufficient to purify the vessel, undermining the requirement to wait until nightfall
C. That metal vessels are too expensive to purify properly
D. That recasting always leaves a vessel physically flawed
Correct: B — Rava worried that if a recast vessel appeared purified simply through immersion that same day, people would wrongly conclude same-day immersion suffices generally.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Beit Hillel, does a vessel merely forgotten (not deliberately placed) under a drainpipe disqualify a mikveh that fills partly from its overflow?
A. Yes, in all cases
B. No — only a deliberately placed vessel disqualifies the mikveh
C. Only if the vessel is made of stone
D. Only on Shabbat
Correct: B — Beit Hillel distinguishes intention from forgetfulness: only a vessel deliberately placed to catch rainwater disqualifies the mikveh, unlike Beit Shammai who forbids both cases.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
What decree did Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak cite to complete Rabbi Yosei's shorter count of the eighteen decrees?
A. That glass vessels are impure
B. That the daughters of the Samaritans (Kutim) are considered menstruants from the cradle
C. That metal vessels reassume previous impurity
D. That a mikveh needs forty se'a of water
Correct: B — Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak explained that the decree treating Samaritan daughters as menstruants from birth was also issued that day, completing the tally to eighteen even according to Rabbi Yosei's count.
