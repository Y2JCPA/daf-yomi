📖 Overview: Recast Vessels and Rainwater

Shabbat 16a–16b
מַעֲשֶׂה בְּשֶׁל צִיּוֹן הַמַּלְכָּה שֶׁעָשְׂתָה מִשְׁתֶּה לִבְנָהּ
The scene: The daf continues untangling the halakhot of glass vessels, asking why a broken-and-recast glass vessel does not reassume its former impurity the way a metal vessel does. That leads into the story behind Shimon ben Shataḥ's decree on metal vessels — his own sister tried to dodge impurity by melting down her vessels — and into Abaye and Rava's two different explanations for that decree. The daf then turns to a mishna about vessels left under a drainpipe to catch rainwater, and closes by opening two further items on the list of eighteen decrees.

🔮 Glass Vessels: A Puzzle of 'Previous Impurity'

Why glass and metal behave differently once recast
❓ If glass, like metal, can be melted and recast, shouldn't a remade glass vessel reassume its former impurity, the way metal does?
↓
📜 Mishna on metal: broken = pure; remade into a new vessel = reverts to its old impurity
↓
📜 Mishna on glass: broken = pure; remade into a new vessel = impure only from that point forward, never retroactively
↓
✅ Resolved: glass's own impurity is only rabbinic, and 'previous impurity' is itself a rabbinic concept — the Sages did not stack one rabbinic decree atop another
A further question: Shouldn't flat glass vessels at least become impure like flat metal ones (which are impure by Torah law)? No — the Sages deliberately spared them, so people would recognize glass's impurity as rabbinic and never burn teruma or consecrated food merely for touching it.

👑 Shel Tziyyon the Queen and the Melted Vessels

The incident behind Shimon ben Shataḥ's decree on metal
מַעֲשֶׂה בְּשֶׁל צִיּוֹן הַמַּלְכָּה שֶׁעָשְׂתָה מִשְׁתֶּה לִבְנָהּ
The story: Shimon ben Shataḥ's sister, Shel Tziyyon the queen, made a wedding feast for her son. All her vessels became ritually impure, so she broke them, gave the pieces to a smith, and had him weld them into new vessels — hoping to escape the impurity entirely. The Sages ruled that the new vessels reassumed their previous impurity regardless. The reason: if people could simply break and recast an impure metal vessel to dodge the elaborate purification process for corpse-impurity — sprinkling the water of the red heifer on the third and seventh days — that entire mitzva would fall into disuse.

🤔 Abaye vs. Rava: Why Fear a Recast Metal Vessel?

Two rationales for the decree, tested by one case
Abaye
The concern is that one may not perforate the vessel with a hole large enough to truly nullify it before recasting.
Rava
The concern is that people will conclude same-day immersion alone was sufficient to purify it, undermining the requirement to wait until nightfall.
Test case
If the vessel was broken completely, Abaye's concern no longer applies — the hole was certainly large enough.
Still applies
But Rava's concern remains even then, since onlookers might still conclude that same-day immersion alone had purified it.

☔ Vessels Under the Drainpipe — Deliberate or Forgotten?

Do they disqualify a ritual bath that fills from their overflow?
ViewRuling
Beit ShammaiA vessel catching rainwater under a drainpipe disqualifies a mikveh if it overflows into it — whether placed there deliberately or simply forgotten
Beit HillelOnly a deliberately placed vessel disqualifies the mikveh; a forgotten one does not
Rabbi MeirThey were counted in Ḥananya's attic and Beit Shammai's view prevailed — though Beit Shammai concedes a vessel forgotten in the courtyard itself (not under the pipe) stays pure
Rabbi YoseiThe dispute was never actually resolved — it still stands
A finer point (school of Rav): All agree the water is unfit if the vessel was set out just as clouds were massing to rain, and fit if set out while clouds were scattering. They dispute only the case where clouds massed, scattered, and massed again before rain finally fell.

📋 Two More of the Eighteen Decrees

Completing Rabbi Yosei's shorter count, and opening a new topic
1️⃣ Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak: to complete Rabbi Yosei's tally, the daughters of the Samaritans (Kutim) were decreed to have the status of menstruants from the very cradle — issued on that same day
↓
2️⃣ Another decree concerns any movable object the width of an ox-goad — it transmits corpse-impurity like a 'tent' over the dead
↓
3️⃣ Rabbi Tarfon strongly objects: 'I will bury my sons if this is not a garbled halakha!' — insisting the ruling as commonly understood is wrong
↓
4️⃣ As Rabbi Tarfon tells it, the true case was narrower: a farmer's ox-goad, resting on his shoulder with one end passing over a grave, itself becomes impure — but does not transmit impurity onward to anything else
