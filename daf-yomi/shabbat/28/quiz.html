QUESTION 1 OF 5
What does the gezera shava between 'tent' in the corpse-impurity verse and 'tent' in the Tabernacle verse teach?
A. That any tent material can transmit impurity
B. That only a tent made of linen, like the Tabernacle's, is considered a tent for corpse-impurity purposes
C. That the Tabernacle itself could become impure
D. That tents made of beams are impure
Correct: B — The Gemara derives that just as the Tabernacle's tent covering was linen, a tent over a corpse must be linen to transmit impurity — though the amplifying repetition of 'tent, tent' extends this beyond the exact Tabernacle specifications, within limits.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
How does Rava mi-Barnish ultimately prove that a non-kosher animal's hide becomes impure as a tent over a corpse?
A. By direct verse about non-kosher hides
B. By an a fortiori inference from goats' hair, which is not susceptible to tzaraat impurity yet does become impure as a corpse-tent
C. By citing a Mishnah explicitly
D. By comparing it to sheretz impurity, which the Gemara accepts without objection
Correct: B — After derivations from tzaraat and sheretz impurity were both refuted, Rava mi-Barnish succeeded with a fortiori reasoning from goats' hair, which is less susceptible than non-kosher hide yet still becomes a corpse-tent.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to the Gemara's conclusion, for what specific part of tefillin does Rav Yosef's rule (only kosher-animal hide for 'heavenly service') actually apply?
A. The parchment scrolls inside
B. The boxes (batim)
C. The straps (retzuot), specifically that they must come from a kosher animal (their required blackness was already a separate Sinai tradition)
D. The tying hair and sewing sinews
Correct: C — After the parchment, boxes, and binding materials were shown to already be governed by explicit verses or Sinai traditions, the Gemara concludes Rav Yosef's rule applies to the straps, adding the requirement that they be from a kosher animal beyond their already-known required blackness.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Rabbi Meir (via Rabbi Ela and Reish Lakish), what happened to the taḥash after the Tabernacle was built?
A. It was sacrificed as an offering
B. It became a domesticated farm animal
C. It was hidden away and no longer exists, having been a unique one-horned creature that appeared to Moses only for that purpose
D. It was replaced by ram skins permanently
Correct: C — Rabbi Meir describes the taḥash as a unique creature with one horn, of uncertain species, that appeared to Moses just for the Tabernacle's construction and was then suppressed, never to be found again.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
In the new mishna about a folded, unsinged garment-wick, what is the core dispute between Rabbi Eliezer and Rabbi Akiva regarding lighting it on Shabbat?
A. Whether wicks may ever be lit on Shabbat
B. Whether folding alone removes the cloth's vessel-status — R' Eliezer says no (so once lit it becomes a forbidden 'broken vessel'), R' Akiva says yes (so it is like plain wood)
C. Whether the wick must be exactly three by three handbreadths
D. Whether only linen wicks may be used
Correct: B — Per Rav Oshaya/Rav Adda bar Ahava, the case involves a Festival falling on Friday: R' Eliezer holds folding doesn't cancel vessel-status, so partial burning leaves a forbidden broken vessel; R' Akiva holds folding does cancel it, so lighting it is like lighting plain wood.
