📖 Overview: The Taḥash, Tefillin Hides, and a Folded Wick

Shabbat 28a–28b
פְּתִילַת הַבֶּגֶד שֶׁקִּיפְּלָהּ וְלֹא הִבְהֲבָהּ, רַבִּי אֱלִיעֶזֶר אוֹמֵר טְמֵאָה הִיא, וְאֵין מַדְלִיקִין בָּהּ. רַבִּי עֲקִיבָא אוֹמֵר טְהוֹרָה הִיא, וּמַדְלִיקִין בָּהּ
The scene: The Gemara derives, via a gezera shava linking 'tent' in the corpse-impurity verses to 'tent' in the Tabernacle, that only linen fully qualifies as an impurity-susceptible tent — yet other coverings, like the Tabernacle's ram-skin and taḥash-skin roofs, are drawn back in through a separate verse. This launches a long dilemma: was the mysterious taḥash animal that provided the Tabernacle's covering kosher or not, and can a non-kosher animal's hide become impure as a tent over a corpse at all? After tracing several failed derivations, Rava mi-Barnish proves it by a fortiori from goats' hair. The Gemara also pins down which materials tefillin boxes, straps, and stitching require, then Rabbi Meir describes the one-horned taḥash itself. The daf ends with a new mishna: a garment-wick folded but not yet singed — is it still susceptible to impurity, and may it be lit on Shabbat? Rabbi Eliezer and Rabbi Akiva disagree on both counts.

⛺ Deriving 'Tent' From the Tabernacle

A gezera shava, then a needed correction
📜 Numbers 19:14 ('a man dies in a tent') and Exodus 40:19 ('he spread the tent over the Tabernacle') both say 'tent'
↓
🧵 Just as the Tabernacle's tent was linen, only a linen tent transmits corpse-impurity
↓
❓ Should the analogy also require the Tabernacle's specific twisted, six-ply threads? No — the repeated word 'tent, tent' broadens the rule beyond that detail, but not without limit
↓
✅ Conclusion: the amplification is bounded — it establishes linen generally, not every construction detail
Follow-up: A parallel question about the Tabernacle's beams (were they part of the 'Tabernacle'?) sets up Rabbi Elazar's dilemma about the animal-hide coverings.

🐐 Can a Non-Kosher Hide Become a Corpse-Tent?

Multiple failed derivations before a successful one
R' Elazar
Dilemma: does the hide of a non-kosher animal become impure as a tent over a corpse?
Rava
Derive it from tzaraat's 'or in a hide' (Lev. 13:48), which includes non-kosher hide.
Objection
Refuted — tzaraat is more stringent, since even unwoven warp and woof threads become impure from it.
Alt.
Then derive it from sheretz impurity, which also includes non-kosher hide (Lev. 11:32).
Objection
Also refuted — sheretz is stringent in a different way, transmitting impurity even at a lentil-bulk.
Rava mi-Barnish
Better: an a fortiori from goats' hair! It is not susceptible to tzaraat, yet does become a corpse-tent. So non-kosher hide, which is susceptible to tzaraat, certainly becomes a corpse-tent.
Conclusion: The kal vachomer from goats' hair succeeds where the direct comparisons to tzaraat and sheretz both failed.

🦄 Rabbi Meir's Taḥash: A Vanished Creature

One horn, uncertain kind, gone after the Mishkan
תַּחַשׁ שֶׁהָיָה בִּימֵי מֹשֶׁה בְּרִיָּה בִּפְנֵי עַצְמָהּ הָיָה, וְלֹא הִכְרִיעוּ בּוֹ חֲכָמִים אִם מִין חַיָּה הוּא אִם מִין בְּהֵמָה הוּא
The tradition: Rabbi Ela, citing Reish Lakish, reports that Rabbi Meir described the taḥash of Moses's time as a creature unto itself — the Sages could not determine whether it was a wild or domesticated species. It had a single horn on its forehead, appeared to Moses just in time for the Tabernacle's construction, and was then hidden away, never to be seen again. Its single horn suggests it was kosher (compare Adam's one-horned ox, Psalms 69:32), but the comparison to the one-horned keresh (a wild species) leaves the question of wild-versus-domesticated unresolved.

🕯️ New Mishna: A Folded but Unsinged Wick

Rabbi Eliezer and Rabbi Akiva disagree on two separate questions
1️⃣ Mishna: a cloth wick folded into shape but not yet singed — Rabbi Eliezer: impure, may not be lit; Rabbi Akiva: pure, may be lit
↓
2️⃣ On impurity: R' Eliezer holds folding alone doesn't cancel the cloth's garment-status; R' Akiva holds it does
↓
3️⃣ On lighting (Rav Oshaya/Rav Adda bar Ahava): the case is a cloth exactly three-by-three fingerbreadths, lit on a Festival that falls on Friday
↓
4️⃣ Both need Rabbi Yehuda's rule (only whole vessels, not broken ones, may fuel a Festival fire) and Ulla's rule (most of the wick must be lit)
↓
5️⃣ R' Eliezer: once part burns, what's left is a 'broken vessel' — forbidden. R' Akiva: folding already removed vessel-status, so it's just plain wood
