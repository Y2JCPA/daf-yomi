QUESTION 1 OF 5
How does Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak resolve the contradiction between the impurity-mishna and the Shabbat-mishna regarding a sealed ring?
A. He says the two mishnayot describe different rings entirely
B. He says ritual impurity and Shabbat use different criteria: impurity asks if it's 'an implement with which work is done' (true either way), Shabbat asks only if it's a burden (true only when sealed)
C. He says the dispute was never actually resolved
D. He says sealed rings cannot become impure
Correct: B — Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak argues the two areas of halakha rest on entirely different verses and principles, so there is no real contradiction to begin with.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to Rava, why may a woman go out on Shabbat with an unperforated needle, even though parting hair is forbidden that day?
A. Because it is disguised as a garter
B. Because it has a gold plate on its other end, which she rests against her forehead as an ornament on Shabbat instead of using it to part her hair
C. Because unperforated needles cannot become impure
D. Because the mishna never actually forbids it
Correct: B — Rava explains that on weekdays the needle parts her hair, but on Shabbat she wears it tucked into her head covering with its gold plate against her forehead, serving purely as an ornament.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to the mishna, what is the liability of a man who goes out on Shabbat wearing a spiked sandal or an unauthenticated amulet?
A. He is liable to bring a sin-offering
B. He is not liable to bring a sin-offering, even though it is forbidden ab initio
C. He is completely exempt, since there was never a prohibition
D. He is liable to receive lashes
Correct: B — The mishna closes its list with 'and if he went out, he is not liable to bring a sin-offering' — these are rabbinic-level prohibitions, not Torah-level carrying violations.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Shmuel, what triggered the incident that led the Sages to ban the spiked sandal?
A. A refugee's sandal turned backward, making his footprints look like someone leaving a hideout cave, which caused a panicked stampede
B. A sandal accidentally started a fire
C. A spiked sandal was stolen from the Temple
D. Soldiers used spiked sandals to break into a synagogue
Correct: A — Shmuel relates that Jews hiding from religious persecution in a cave panicked when one man's reversed sandal print looked like someone had left, leading them to trample one another.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
What distinguishes nails placed in a sandal 'for strength' from nails placed 'for beauty'?
A. Beauty-nails are always gold, strength-nails are always iron
B. Strength-nails, inserted to reinforce the sole, are forbidden; a small decorative number of nails, arranged for appearance, is permitted
C. There is no halakhic difference between them
D. Strength-nails are only used on children's sandals
Correct: B — Rav Yehuda taught in Shmuel's name that nails for reinforcing the sandal's structure are forbidden, while a modest number placed for decoration (the sages debate the exact count) is permitted.
