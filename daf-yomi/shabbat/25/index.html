📖 Overview: Benefit, Deference, and the Meaning of Prosperity

Shabbat 25a–25b
אֵיזֶהוּ עָשִׁיר? כׇּל שֶׁיֵּשׁ לוֹ נַחַת רוּחַ בְּעׇשְׁרוֹ
The scene: The daf resolves the previous discussion: burning impure teruma is prohibited only on a Festival, since it may be burned — and its warmth benefited from — on an ordinary weekday. Two independent verses ground this permission, and a kal vachomer argument tests whether teruma or fully consecrated items are the stricter category, weighing their competing lists of stringencies. The Gemara then returns to the new mishna's ban on lighting with tar, explaining Rabbi Yishmael's concern as rooted in Shabbat's status as an obligation one must not walk away from. That leads into vivid material on preparing for Shabbat with dignity: Rabbi Yehuda bar Ila'i's weekly bathing ritual, homiletic readings on what exile takes away, and a closing baraita cataloguing four tannaim's very different definitions of wealth.

🔥 Deriving Permission to Benefit From Burning Impure Teruma

וְאָמְרָה תּוֹרָה: בִּשְׁעַת בִּיעוּרָהּ תֵּיהָנֵי מִמֶּנָּה
Rav teaches: just as it is a mitzva to burn impure consecrated items, so too impure teruma — and the Torah says one may benefit from it while it is being destroyed. The source: 'And I, behold, I have given you the charge of My terumot' (Numbers 18:8) — the plural implies two terumot, pure and impure, and both are 'given to you,' meaning you may burn the impure one under your own cooking pot. Rabbi Abbahu offers an alternate source, from the tithe-confession: 'I have not eaten from it in mourning, nor destroyed it while impure' — implying that impure teruma, unlike ma'aser, MAY be destroyed for benefit.

⚖️ Teruma or Consecrated Items — Which Is Stricter?

A kal vachomer argument tested against two competing mnemonics
Question
Why does 'you may not destroy it while impure' exclude only teruma from the no-benefit rule, and not fully consecrated items — which are surely stricter?
For קדש (PNKA"S)
Consecrated items carry: piggul, notar, liability for me'ilah, karet, and are forbidden to an onen — five stringencies teruma lacks.
For תרומה (MPCHZ)
But teruma carries its own: death by Heaven's hand, an added fifth for unwitting misuse, no possibility of redemption, and it's forbidden to non-priests.
Resolution
Consecrated items are still stricter overall — their stringencies are simply more numerous — so it is consecrated items that remain fully forbidden, and teruma that is excluded from the ban.
Alternative
Or more simply: consecrated items carry the unique punishment of karet, which settles the comparison without counting.
One more proof: Rav Nachman bar Yitzchak reads Deuteronomy 18:4 — 'give him' pure teruma fit to eat, not teruma fit only 'for his fire' — implying that impure teruma is indeed meant to be burned for benefit.

🌸 Why Rabbi Yishmael Bans Tar as a Lamp Oil

A chain of reasoning rooted in the obligation to sit by the Shabbat light
🤔 Rava: tar's bad smell might cause a person to abandon the light and leave the room
↓
❓ Abaye objects: so let him leave — what's the problem?
↓
💡 Rava: because kindling the Shabbat lamp is a chovah — an obligation — and one must eat specifically by that light
↓
📜 Rav (cited by Rav Nachman bar Rav Zavda): kindling the Shabbat lamp is chovah; washing hands and feet in hot water Friday evening is only optional (reshut)
↓
✅ The Gemara's own addendum: 'I say — it is a mitzva,' pointing to Rabbi Yehuda bar Ila'i's practice as the model

🛀 Rabbi Yehuda bar Ila'i's Friday Preparation

Dignity for Shabbat, and a lesson to his students about tzitzit
1️⃣ Every Friday, a basin full of hot water was brought to him; he washed his face, hands, and feet
↓
2️⃣ He wrapped himself and sat in fine linen sheets with fringes — 'and he resembled an angel of the Lord of Hosts'
↓
3️⃣ His students, lacking such fringed linens themselves, tried to hide the corners of their own garments from him out of embarrassment
↓
4️⃣ He reassured them with the halakha: Beit Shammai exempt a linen sheet from tzitzit (shatnez concerns), Beit Hillel obligate it, and halakha follows Beit Hillel — though the Sages later restricted it, fearing the garment would be worn at night without fringes' purpose applying
The larger theme: A homiletic reading of Lamentations 3:17 lists what exile removes: the lighting of Shabbat lamps (Rabbi Abbahu), the bathhouse (Rabbi Yirmeya), hot-water washing (Rabbi Yochanan), a fine bed and bedding (Rabbi Yitzchak Nappacha), and a made bed with an adorned wife fit for a Torah scholar (Rabbi Abba).

💎 Who Is Wealthy? Four Tannaim, Four Definitions

A baraita with the mnemonic מטק״ס — Meir, Tarfon, Akiva, Yosei
SageDefinition of Wealth
Rabbi MeirAnyone who finds satisfaction (nachat ruach) in what he has
Rabbi TarfonAnyone who owns a hundred vineyards, a hundred fields, and a hundred slaves working them
Rabbi AkivaAnyone whose wife's deeds are becoming
Rabbi YoseiAnyone who has a bathroom near his table
Closing note on this daf: Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar adds one more restriction: one may not light with tzori (balsam sap) — its fragrance is so appealing that Rabba fears a person might absentmindedly take some from the burning lamp, diminishing the light.
