📖 Overview: Barrels, Pillars, and a Pit-Side Eiruv

Shabbat 8a–8b
תַּשְׁמִישׁ עַל יְדֵי הַדְּחָק לָא שְׁמֵיהּ תַּשְׁמִישׁ
The scene: Daf 8 works through a series of concrete objects and spaces to sharpen exactly what makes something count as the public domain. It opens with a round reed barrel thrown into the street, testing whether its width or its loose, protruding reed-ends determine its status, then turns to Ulla's ruling that a nine-handbreadth pillar counts as public domain because people rest their burdens on it — and asks whether the same applies to a pit of equal depth. A close reading of the mishna about a shallow swamp yields the key distinction between mere passage under duress, which still counts, and use under duress, which does not — a principle then tested against a baraita that validates an eiruv placed in a pit. The daf closes by working out, through elimination, exactly what kind of threshold the Tosefta's 'exempt domain' must be.

🪣 A Round Barrel Thrown Into the Public Domain

Ten handbreadths tall — does its width make it an independent domain?
❓ One throws a round reed barrel, ten handbreadths tall, into the public domain. Is he liable for carrying an object, or did he throw in an independent private domain that never truly 'entered' the public domain?
↓
Diameter under 6 handbreadths
Abaye: LIABLE — too narrow to be its own private domain, so the law treats it as a mere object
Diameter 6 handbreadths or more
Abaye: EXEMPT — wide enough to count as an independent private domain in its own right
↓
🌾 Rava disagrees entirely: EXEMPT regardless of width — the loose reed-ends of the weave inevitably poke up above ten handbreadths, so no part of the barrel ever fully entered the public domain's zone
↓
🔄 Turned mouth-down instead: Rav Ashi holds one is liable even at seven-and-a-half handbreadths — lavud (treating a 3-handbreadth gap as sealed) applies only to real partitions, not the rim of a barrel
Underlying question: Every version of this case asks the same thing: has the thrown object truly come to rest within the public domain's own airspace, or has some part of it escaped that zone entirely?

📏 Nine Handbreadths and the Question of Pits

Ulla's graded scale of how a protrusion — or a depression — relates to the public domain
Height or depthStatusReasoning
Under 3 handbreadthsPart of the public domain itselfThe multitudes step directly on it — lavud treats it as touching the ground
3 to 9 handbreadthsNeither public domain nor an independent domainToo high to be trodden on, too low for people to rest a burden there
9 handbreadths or more (a pillar)Ulla: counts as public domainPeople do rest their burdens (מְכַתְּפִין) on a protrusion that tall
A pit of the same depth (a hole, not a pillar)Disputed: Rav Yosef says the same rule applies; Rava says it does notRava: using a pit is inconvenient — 'תַּשְׁמִישׁ עַל יְדֵי הַדְּחָק לָא שְׁמֵיהּ תַּשְׁמִישׁ,' use under duress is not called use
The core dispute: Rav Yosef treats a hole exactly like a pillar of equal depth; Rava insists that stepping down into a pit is fundamentally less convenient than resting a load atop a raised pillar.

🕳️ Passage Under Duress vs. Use Under Duress

A close reading of the swamp mishna settles Rava's distinction
1️⃣ Mishna: where the public domain passes through a shallow swamp (רְקָק מַיִם, under ten handbreadths deep), throwing an object four cubits into it creates liability, exactly as in ordinary public domain
↓
2️⃣ The mishna repeats this ruling for BOTH the dry season and the rainy season — necessary, since each season gives its own reason to doubt the swamp still counts
↓
3️⃣ It also repeats 'the public passes through it' twice, which otherwise seems redundant
↓
4️⃣ Conclusion: PASSAGE through an area, even inconvenient passage, still counts as passage and establishes public-domain status — but mere USE (standing, placing an object) under duress does NOT count as use
Applies the distinction: This is exactly the line Rava draws between a nine-handbreadth pillar (use is convenient) and a pit of the same depth (use is under duress).

🍽️ An Eiruv Placed in a Pit

Testing 'use under duress' against a baraita that validates the eiruv
Baraita
One who intends to establish his Shabbat residence in the public domain, and places his eiruv food in a pit less than ten handbreadths deep — his eiruv IS valid.
Gemara
But that pit is only a karmelit! If use under duress isn't real use, how could placing food there ever validly establish an eiruv?
Rava, first answer
Sometimes both he and his eiruv are together in the karmelit — the baraita loosely calls his spot 'public domain' only to mean 'not a private domain.'
Rava, second answer
Or: he stands in true public domain, his eiruv in the karmelit — following Rebbi's own view that rabbinic shevut decrees were never applied during the fleeting moment of twilight, when the eiruv need be usable only an instant.
Not a dodge: Rava insists these answers are not mere excuses — the swamp mishna's own redundancy independently proves the passage/use distinction is real.

🚪 Process of Elimination: What Kind of Threshold?

The Tosefta permits taking and giving on a threshold — but which threshold?

Not a Public-Domain Threshold

  • If it were public domain, taking from the homeowner would mean carrying out of the private domain — forbidden outright

Not a Private-Domain Threshold

  • If it were private domain, taking from the poor person would mean carrying in from the public domain — forbidden outright

Not Even a Karmelit Threshold

  • Even without Torah liability, a karmelit is rabbinically prohibited
  • The Tosefta permits this threshold ab initio — something a karmelit could never allow
By elimination: The threshold must be a true exempt domain, under four by four handbreadths — where, per Rav Dimi citing Rabbi Yochanan, people from both the public and private domains may rest burdens there, as long as they never exchange objects between the two sides.
