📖 Overview: Protest, Retribution, and Reinterpreting Scripture

Shabbat 55a–55b
אֵין מִיתָה בְּלֹא חֵטְא, וְאֵין יִסּוּרִין בְּלֹא עָוֹן
The scene: Continuing the theme that one who could protest wrongdoing and did not is held responsible, the Gemara tells of Rabbi Zeira urging Rabbi Simon to rebuke the Exilarch's household even if they will not listen, and cites the dramatic vision of Ezekiel 9, where God marks the righteous for protection and the wicked for destruction — then retracts that protection once He is reminded the righteous, too, failed to protest. This launches a survey of when the Patriarchs' merit ceased to protect Israel, then a debate over whether death and suffering always signal personal sin, tested against Moses, Aaron, and four others said to have died only because of Adam's sin. The daf closes with the sages defending the reputations of biblical figures, beginning with Reuben.

📜 Marking the Righteous and the Wicked

Ezekiel's vision, and the one good decree God ever retracted
🕊️ God tells Gabriel: mark the righteous with an ink Tav (protection), the wicked with a blood Tav (for destroying angels)
↓
❓ Midat HaDin (Divine Justice) asks: what is the difference between these and those?
↓
🗣️ God: these are fully righteous, those fully wicked. Midat HaDin: but the righteous could have protested the wicked's sins, and did not!
↓
God's defense
I know their protest would not have been accepted
Midat HaDin's reply
It was known to You — but was it known to them?
↓
💔 God retracts: even the righteous who failed to protest are struck, starting from those closest to the Sanctuary — the fully devoted
Rabbi Aḥa bar Ḥanina: Never did a good decree leave God's mouth and get reversed for the worse — except this one.

⏳ When Did the Patriarchs' Merit Cease?

Four sages locate the turning point at four different biblical moments
SageEraProof text
RavDays of Hosea'I will uncover her lewdness... none shall deliver her from My hand' (Hosea 2:12)
ShmuelDays of ḤazaelGod was gracious to Israel 'because of His covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob' (II Kings 13:23) — the last such mention
Rabbi Yehoshua ben LeviDays of Elijah'Let it be known this day' — implying not afterward (I Kings 18:36)
Rabbi YoḥananDays of Hezekiah'From now and forever the zeal of the Lord... performs this' (Isaiah 9:6) — zeal replaces ancestral merit
Shared premise: All four agree the merit did eventually run out; they disagree only about the historical marker.

⚖️ Is There Death Without Sin?

Rav Ami's principle meets a sharp challenge
Rav Ami
There is no death without sin, and no suffering without iniquity — proven from Ezekiel 18:20 and Psalms 89:33.
Angels
Then why did Moses and Aaron, who observed the entire Torah, still die? God answered only: 'One event befalls the righteous and the wicked' (Ecclesiastes 9:2) — implying death is not always tied to sin!
Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar
Even Moses and Aaron died for their own sin — 'Because you did not believe in Me' (Numbers 20:12). Had they believed, their time would not yet have come.
Gemara's conclusion
The baraita listing four who died sinless can only follow Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar — meaning death without sin does exist elsewhere. This is a conclusive refutation of Rav Ami.
Result: Rav Ami's sweeping principle is rejected — some people do die, or suffer, without any sin of their own.

🤔 Did Reuben Sin With Bilhah?

Rabbi Shmuel bar Naḥmani's exoneration, and the tannaitic dispute it rests on

Innocent

  • 'The sons of Jacob were twelve' — all equally righteous even after the incident
  • He merely rearranged his father's bed, protesting the honor of his mother Leah
  • Aḥerim: he rearranged two beds — his father's and the Divine Presence's
  • Pachaz read backwards: 'you trembled, you recoiled, sin fled from you'

Guilty

  • Rabbi Eliezer: Pachaz = 'you were impulsive, liable, contemptuous'
  • Rabbi Yehoshua: Pachaz = 'you trampled the law, sinned, were promiscuous'
  • The plain sense of 'he lay with Bilhah his father's concubine' supports this reading
Why it matters: Rabban Gamliel objects: could Reuben's own tribe stand at Mount Eival and curse 'cursed is he who lies with his father's wife' if their ancestor had truly done so?
