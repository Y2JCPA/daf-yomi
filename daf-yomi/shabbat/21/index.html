📖 Overview: From Disqualified Fuels to the Origin of Chanukah

Shabbat 21a–21b
בְּכ״ה בְּכִסְלֵיו יוֹמֵי דַחֲנוּכָּה תְּמָנְיָא אִינּוּן
The scene: Continuing the mishna's list of disqualified oils and wicks, the Gemara clarifies that all of them remain fine for warmth or a bonfire — only their use as a wick for a lamp is restricted. It identifies kik oil, explains Rabba's two underlying reasons (poor wicks flicker; poor oils aren't drawn upward), and works through whether one may mix a good oil into a bad one to render it usable. That leads to a Temple-oil standard, and then — triggered by whether Shabbat-disqualified materials may fuel a Chanukah lamp — the daf turns fully to the halakhot of Chanukah: how many lights the mehadrin min hamehadrin light each night, where the lamp is placed, and the Gemara's own telling of the miracle of the single flask of oil.

🛢️ Disqualified as a Wick, Fine for a Bonfire

כׇּל אֵלּוּ שֶׁאָמְרוּ אֵין מַדְלִיקִין בָּהֶן בְּשַׁבָּת, אֲבָל עוֹשִׂין מֵהֶן מְדוּרָה
A Tosefta clarifies the scope of the mishna's ban: all the materials disqualified for the Shabbat lamp's wick may still be piled into a bonfire — for warmth or for light, on the ground or on a stove. The Sages only prohibited making a wick from them. Along the way, the Gemara identifies שֶׁמֶן קִיק (kik oil): Shmuel heard from seafarers it comes from a coastal bird; Reish Lakish says it is oil from the plant of Jonah, which Rabba bar bar Chana describes seeing — like a ricinus tree, grown for shade over shop entrances, its seeds pressed for oil.

🥜 Can a Bad Oil Be 'Fixed' by Mixing In a Good One?

Abaye presses Rabba on whether a small addition of kosher oil helps
Abaye
May one add any amount of a permitted oil into a disqualified oil, and then light the mixture?
Rabba
One may not light it. Why? Because — one may not light it. (No further reason is given.)
Abaye
But a Tosefta says: if you wrap a permitted wick around a nut and light it — Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel says that was his father's very custom!
Rabba
That case is about floating the wick on the oil using the nut, not combining materials to burn together.
Objection
But Rav Beruna cited Rav: melted forbidden fat or dissolved fish innards may have any amount of good oil added, and then be lit!
Resolution
Those are drawn by the wick even in their natural state; ordinary disqualified oils are not. And the Sages won't add a decree on top of a decree.
Net result: Ordinary disqualified wicks and oils can't be salvaged by dilution — but fat or fish oil that already burns reasonably well may be boosted with any amount of good oil.

🕎 Using Shabbat-Disqualified Materials for the Chanukah Lamp

Three amoraic positions, turning on what happens if the light goes out
OpinionIf extinguished, must one relight?Ruling on disqualified wicks/oils
Rav HunaYes — must relight (kavta zakuk lah)Forbidden on Chanukah too, weekday or Shabbat — must burn reliably from the start
Rav ChisdaNo — but light may still be usedPermitted on weekdays; forbidden on Shabbat, lest one adjust the wick
Rav (via Rabbi Zeira)No relighting required, and its light may not be usedPermitted even on Shabbat — no concern about adjusting, since using the light is forbidden anyway
How halakha settled: Abaye at first didn't accept this ruling in Rabbi Yirmeya's name, but accepted the identical ruling once it came in Rabbi Yochanan's name — later admitting he wished he had learned it from the start, since knowledge from one's youth is retained best.

8️⃣ Beit Shammai vs. Beit Hillel on the Nightly Count

The mehadrin min hamehadrin — the most scrupulous practice

Beit Shammai: Count Down

  • Light eight on night one, decreasing to one
  • Reason A: corresponds to the incoming days of Chanukah still ahead
  • Reason B: corresponds to the bulls offered on Sukkot, which decrease day by day

Beit Hillel: Count Up

  • Light one on night one, increasing to eight
  • Reason A: corresponds to the outgoing days already completed
  • Reason B: 'one elevates in sanctity, never lowers' — the accepted halakha
Real people, real practice: Rabbi Yochanan reports two Elders in Sidon who each followed a different house, each explaining his own practice with one of the two reasons above.

✨ Why We Light: The Chanukah Story

Placement rules and the miracle itself, from Megillat Taanit
🏛️ Mitzva: place the lamp at the entrance outside; upstairs, at the window facing the public domain; in danger, on the table
↓
🕯️ Rava: one needs a separate light to use for its light — unless there's a bonfire, or unless one is an 'important person' who still needs a separate light
↓
⚔️ When the Greeks entered the Sanctuary, they defiled all the oil in it
↓
🏺 When the Hasmoneans defeated them, they searched and found only one flask of pure oil — sealed by the High Priest — enough for one day
↓
🎉 A miracle occurred and it burned eight days; the next year the Sages fixed these days as a holiday of Hallel and thanksgiving
Same daf, closing note: A mishna on damages is then cited to introduce the next sugya: liability when a spark, or a camel's flax passing a storekeeper's lamp, causes a fire.
