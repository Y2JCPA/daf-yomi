📖 Overview: From 'Or a Garment' to Flax Alone

Shabbat 27a–27b
כׇּל הַיּוֹצֵא מִן הָעֵץ אֵין מַדְלִיקִין בּוֹ, אֶלָּא פִּשְׁתָּן
The scene: The Gemara continues tracing the sources for which fabrics can contract ritual impurity, now focusing on the extra word 'or' in 'or a garment' (Leviticus 11:32) and what it comes to include — small wool/linen swatches, or entirely different fabrics like camel's hair and silk. Abaye, Rava, Rav Pappa, and Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak each propose a different use for the derivation, including whether it teaches the laws of kilayim (forbidden mixtures) or tzitzit, and why a blind person's garment is obligated in tzitzit while a night garment is not. The daf then reaches a new mishna: of everything that grows from a tree, only flax may be used for the Shabbat lamp, and only flax can contract tent-impurity over a corpse — a rule the Gemara supports from the story of Rahab hiding Joshua's spies under flax stalks on her roof.

🧵 What Does 'Or a Garment' Add?

Abaye and Rava read the extra word differently
Baraita
'Garment' alone teaches only a whole garment. From where do we include a three-by-three swatch of other fabrics? The verse says: אוֹ בֶגֶד, 'or a garment' (Leviticus 11:32).
Abaye
But I hold other fabrics never become impure at all! I use 'or a garment' instead to include a wool/linen swatch of three by three fingerbreadths for sheretz impurity.
Rava
No extra verse is even needed for sheretz — the Torah already revealed this rule for tzaraat, and the same applies to sheretz.
Abaye
That comparison fails: tzaraat is more stringent, since even warp and woof threads alone can become impure from it.
Result: The two amoraim keep sparring over exactly what work the word 'or' is doing — and for whom.

🐫 What Fabrics Does 'Or a Garment' Include?

Four amoraim, four different answers
AmoraWhat 'or a garment' teaches
Tanna d'bei R' Yishmael (per Abaye)Includes camel's hair, rabbit's wool, goat hair, silk (shirayin, kalakh, serikin) as impurity-susceptible fabrics
RavaDistinguishes three-by-three fingerbreadths (not impure) from three-by-three handbreadths (impure) in other garments
Rav PappaSuggested it teaches that kilayim (wool-linen mixture) applies even to merely placing a garment on oneself — rejected as mistaken
Rav Naḥman bar YitzḥakIt teaches that the mitzva of tzitzit applies only to wool and linen garments
Rav Pappa's view rejected: An a fortiori argument shows that if wearing a mixture (full-body benefit) is limited to wool and linen, merely placing it on oneself must be even more limited — so his statement was called 'a mistake.'

👁️ Whose Garment Needs Tzitzit?

Deriving the blind person's obligation, and nightwear's exemption
❓ Numbers 15:39 — tzitzit is 'that you may look upon it.' Does this exclude a night garment, or a blind person's garment?
↓
📜 Deuteronomy 22:12 says tzitzit go on 'your covering, with which you cover yourself' — this includes a blind person's garment, since he too covers himself
↓
Included
A blind person's garment — it is visible to others, even if not to him
Excluded
A night garment — not visible to anyone, so 'that you may look upon it' excludes it
Why not other fabrics? Since the verse is already discussing a wool/linen garment, its amplification naturally extends only to another wool/linen garment — not to unrelated fabrics.

🌳 New Mishna: Only Flax Comes From a Tree

Two rules share one exception
כׇּל הַיּוֹצֵא מִן הָעֵץ אֵין מַדְלִיקִין בּוֹ, אֶלָּא פִּשְׁתָּן. וְכׇל הַיּוֹצֵא מִן הָעֵץ אֵינוֹ מִטַּמֵּא טוּמְאַת אֹהָלִים, אֶלָּא פִּשְׁתָּן
The mishna: Of everything that grows from a tree, only flax may be used to light the Shabbat lamp — other tree products burn poorly. And of everything that grows from a tree, only flax can become impure through tent-impurity over a corpse: if a house or tent made of flax covers a corpse, the tent itself becomes impure, unlike tents made of other tree-derived materials.

📜 Why Is Flax Called a 'Tree'?

Two derivations from the Gemara
1️⃣ Question: Flax does not look like a tree at all — where do we learn it counts as one?
↓
2️⃣ Mar Zutra's proof: Joshua 2:6 — Rahab hid the spies 'under the פִּשְׁתֵּי הָעֵץ', the trees of flax, on her roof
↓
3️⃣ Second question: why does only flax (among tree products) contract tent-impurity over a corpse?
↓
4️⃣ Rabbi Elazar's answer: a verbal analogy (gezera shava) links the word 'tent' used regarding impurity to the word 'tent' used elsewhere about flax
Takeaway: Both special rules for flax — as lamp fuel and as tent-impurity conductor — rest on flax's unique scriptural classification as a 'tree' product.
