📖 Overview: Mapping Out the Domains of Shabbat

Shabbat 6a–6b
אַרְבַּע רְשׁוּיוֹת לַשַּׁבָּת: רְשׁוּת הַיָּחִיד, וּרְשׁוּת הָרַבִּים, וְכַרְמְלִית, וּמְקוֹם פְּטוּר
The scene: Daf 6 opens by resolving the previous daf's unfinished question: can one be liable for carrying an object out through a colonnade — an exempt domain — the way one is liable for carrying through the ordinary midpoint of a public domain? After testing several precedents and refining Rabbi Eliezer ben Ya'akov's ruling about the 'sides' of the public domain, the Gemara settles the matter and clarifies that ben Azzai's exemption applies only to carrying by hand, not to throwing. The Gemara then cites the Tosefta's foundational map of the four domains of Shabbat law — private domain, public domain, karmelit, and exempt domain — working through their definitions, the special status of thresholds, and why Rabbi Yehuda's proposal to fence off part of a public street with posts and beams is firmly rejected.

🏘️ Four Domains of Shabbat

The Tosefta's foundational map of where carrying is, and isn't, prohibited by Torah law
DomainExamplesCarrying rule
Public domain (רְשׁוּת הָרַבִּים)A main street, a large plaza, alleyways open on both endsTorah-level liability for transfer to/from a private domain: sin-offering if unwitting, karet or stoning if intentional
Private domain (רְשׁוּת הַיָּחִיד)A ditch 10 handbreadths deep and 4 wide; a fence 10 high and 4 wideSame Torah-level liability for improper transfer to/from the public domain
KarmelitA sea, a valley, a colonnade — frequented by the public, but with no proper partitionsCarrying beyond 4 cubits, or to/from RH or RY, is only rabbinically prohibited
Exempt domain (מְקוֹם פָּטוּר)An area under 4×4 handbreadths, such as many thresholdsNo prohibition at all — except one may not relay an object between RH and RY through it
Note: Shared courtyards and alleyways not open at both ends carry the same restriction as a karmelit, unless the residents join them with an eiruv into a single legal domain.

🔍 Chasing a Precedent Through the Colonnade

Can carrying an object through an exempt domain still trigger liability?
1️⃣ First try: like carrying across the public domain generally — an in-between point is exempt only because the labor isn't finished. Rejected: that midpoint is still a potential place of LIABILITY, while the colonnade is an absolute exempt zone
↓
2️⃣ Second try: like carrying exactly four cubits — a midpoint is exempt only for THIS person, but a place of liability for the world at large. Rejected: the colonnade is exempt for everyone, with no exceptions
↓
3️⃣ Third try: like carrying via the narrow 'sides' of the public domain, where the multitudes don't gather — a lesser status within the same physical domain
↓
4️⃣ Rav Acha's refinement: this comparison to Rabbi Eliezer ben Ya'akov's 'sides of the public domain' works only where there are no boundary stakes (חִיפּוּפֵי) — precisely then it parallels the colonnade
Result: One who carries from a store through the colonnade to the plaza is liable — the sides-of-the-public-domain precedent, properly qualified, holds.

🎯 Ben Azzai's Concession on Throwing

Walking through an exempt domain is different from an unbroken throw
❓ Does ben Azzai's exemption for carrying an object BY HAND through the colonnade also apply to one who THROWS it across?
↓
Carrying by walking
EXEMPT, according to ben Azzai — each step is treated as if he came to a stop, so he 'rests' inside the colonnade itself
Throwing or extending the hand
LIABLE, even according to ben Azzai — the object's flight, or the outstretched arm, is uninterrupted; there is no stopping point along the way
↓
✅ Rabbi Yochanan: ben Azzai concedes on throwing; a baraita confirms the identical distinction for מוֹצִיא, מַכְנִיס, מוֹשִׁיט, and זוֹרֵק
The dividing line: Walking involves repeated, momentary 'placements'; throwing and reaching do not — that is what separates ben Azzai's exemption from his liability.

🚧 Posts and Beams: A Private Domain Carved Out of the Street?

Why the Tosefta insists on calling the real private domain 'full-fledged'
R' Yehuda
One who has two houses on opposite sides of a public street may set posts (לֶחִי) on each side, or a beam (קוֹרָה) spanning across — and then carry freely in the space between, as though it were a private domain.
Rabbis
One may not fashion an eiruv of the public domain in that way! Only genuine partitions create a private domain.
Gemara
Then why does the Tosefta stress 'זוֹ הִיא רְשׁוּת הַיָּחִיד גְּמוּרָה' — this is a FULL-FLEDGED private domain? What is the extra word 'full-fledged' excluding?
Gemara
Precisely Rabbi Yehuda's posts-and-beam construction — lest you think the Rabbis merely forbid carrying there, but would still treat it as a private domain for the more severe prohibition of throwing an object into it. 'Full-fledged' teaches: no, the Rabbis reject it as a private domain entirely.
Parallel exclusion: The identical emphasis for the public domain excludes Rabbi Yehuda's separate ruling that a path passing through pilgrimage wells must be diverted — the Tosefta insists only its own listed examples count as a full public domain.
