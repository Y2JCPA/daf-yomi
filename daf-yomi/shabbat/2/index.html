📖 Overview: Opening Tractate Shabbat With Carrying

Shabbat 2a–2b
יְצִיאוֹת הַשַּׁבָּת, שְׁתַּיִם שֶׁהֵן אַרְבַּע בִּפְנִים, וּשְׁתַּיִם שֶׁהֵן אַרְבַּע בַּחוּץ
The scene: Tractate Shabbat opens not with a general statement of the Shabbat prohibitions but with the specific, technical melakha of carrying between the private and public domains. The mishna pictures a poor person standing outside and a homeowner standing inside, and works through eight scenarios of handing objects across the boundary, sorting out who is liable and who is exempt. The Gemara immediately compares this mishna's phrasing to nearly identical language used elsewhere in the Mishna — about oaths, about impurity, about leprosy signs — to ask why our mishna adds the extra detail of 'inside' and 'outside.' It resolves this with Rav Pappa's distinction between liability and exemption, then investigates whether the term for 'carrying out' also covers carrying in, and closes with a numbers puzzle about how many distinct actions the mishna is really describing.

🚪 The Mishna's Eight Cases

A poor person outside, a homeowner inside — who completes the act decides who is liable
ScenarioWhat HappenedRuling
Poor person extends his hand in and places an object in the homeowner's hand, or takes one from it and pulls outPoor person alone performs the complete act of carryingPoor person liable; homeowner exempt
Homeowner extends his hand out and places an object in the poor person's hand, or takes one from it and pulls inHomeowner alone performs the complete act of carryingHomeowner liable; poor person exempt
Poor person extends his hand in; homeowner takes from it, or places into it and the poor person pulls outThe act is split between two peopleBoth exempt
Homeowner extends his hand out; poor person takes from it, or places into it and the homeowner pulls inThe act is split between two peopleBoth exempt
Key point: The Torah prohibition of carrying requires one person to perform the whole act — lifting from one domain and placing in the other. Whenever the act is divided between two participants, neither has completed a full melakha, so both are exempt by Torah law.

🔍 Why Spell Out 'Inside and Outside' Here?

Shevuot's parallel mishna just says 'two that are four' and stops
Gemara
We learned in tractate Shevuot: oaths that render one liable to a sin-offering are also 'two that comprise four.' Why does our mishna add 'inside' and 'outside,' when the mishna in Shevuot teaches 'two that are four' and nothing more?
First answer
Here, in Shabbat, the primary discussion of these halakhot, the tanna teaches both the primary categories of labor (אָבוֹת) and their subcategories (תּוֹלָדוֹת). There, in Shevuot, only the primary categories are taught.
Gemara
But the primary categories are just the two acts of carrying out! Where does Shevuot's count of four come from?
Rav Pappa
Rather: here the tanna teaches cases of liability and cases of exemption. There, in Shevuot, only cases of liability are taught, and nothing else.
Parallel structure: The Gemara measures this against two other 'two that are four' teachings — awareness of impurity before and after eating sacred food or entering the Temple, and the two-plus-two shades of leprosy signs, baheret and se'et and their lighter derivatives.

🔄 Does 'Carrying Out' Include Carrying In?

The mishna in Shevuot only says יְצִיאוֹת — 'acts of carrying out'
1️⃣ Objection: Shevuot's mishna speaks of יְצִיאוֹת, 'carrying out.' Yet its four cases must include carrying IN too, or there is no way to reach four
↓
2️⃣ Rav Ashi: The tanna in Shevuot also calls carrying in by the name 'carrying out' — proven from a mishna that calls one who transfers an object 'from domain to domain' liable, which plainly includes carrying into the private domain
↓
3️⃣ Reason: any act of lifting an object from its place and moving it to another domain is called הוֹצָאָה, 'carrying out' — the term is general, not literal
↓
4️⃣ Ravina: our own mishna proves it precisely — it opens with 'acts of carrying out' (יְצִיאוֹת), yet its very first case is the poor person handing an object into the homeowner's hand
Alternative: Rava offers a different fix entirely: read the word not as רְשׁוּיוֹת יְצִיאוֹת ('acts of carrying out') but as רְשׁוּיוֹת ('domains') — 'the domains of Shabbat are two that comprise four' — removing the difficulty altogether.

⚖️ 'Two That Are Four' — Three Parallel Teachings

The Gemara reads our mishna against two other mishnayot using the identical formula

Shabbat: Carrying

  • Two basic acts (carrying out from private to public, or public to private)
  • ×2 for inside/outside = four cases each, eight total
  • Includes BOTH liability and exemption cases

Shevuot: Oaths

  • Swearing to do / not to do an act in the future (from the Torah)
  • Swearing that one did / did not do it in the past (added by amplification)
  • Teaches only liability cases

Tumah & Nega'im

  • Awareness of impurity: forgetting before, or forgetting after, eating sacred food or entering the Temple
  • Leprosy signs: baheret and se'et, each with a lighter derivative shade
  • Both cited as models for 'two that are four'
Method: The Gemara assumes a shared formula must mean a shared structure — until it finds, case by case, where the parallel breaks down.

🔢 Rav Mattana's Count: Eight, Twelve, or Sixteen?

A closing puzzle about how many actions the mishna truly describes
❓ Rav Mattana to Abaye: the mishna's second half lists four cases where 'both are exempt' — but each involves TWO people acting together. Isn't that eight actions, for a total of twelve, not eight?
↓
🤨 Abaye: by that reasoning, even the mishna's FIRST half — its four straightforward liability cases — involves both a giver and a receiver. That would make sixteen actions!
↓
💬 Rav Mattana: 'That is not difficult — granted...' — the daf breaks off mid-answer, to be resumed on 3a
