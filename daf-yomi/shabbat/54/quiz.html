Shabbat 54 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
What is the halakhic conclusion (per Rabbi Yoḥanan, as transmitted by Ravin) regarding goats going out with bound udders?
A. It is always prohibited
B. It is permitted in all cases, following the first, unnamed tanna
C. It depends on the goat's owner
D. It is permitted only on weekdays
Correct: B — The Gemara concludes that when Ravin came from Eretz Yisrael, he reported that Rabbi Yoḥanan ruled in accordance with the first tanna, who permits binding a goat's udder in all circumstances.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to Rav Yehuda's explanation, what does 'akud' mean in the new mishna about camels?
A. Tying two forelegs together only
B. Binding a foreleg to a hind leg, similar to the binding of Isaac
C. Placing a saddle without a strap
D. Leading several camels in a chain
Correct: B — Rav Yehuda explains that akud refers to binding a foreleg and a hind leg together, likened to the akeidah, the binding of Isaac.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
Why does Rav Ashi say it is forbidden to tie several camels together and pull the lead one?
A. Because it constitutes a forbidden mixture (kilayim)
B. Because it makes the owner appear as though heading to market to sell or deliver merchandise
C. Because camels cannot be tied to each other under any circumstances
D. Because it causes the camels pain
Correct: B — Rav Ashi explains the concern is one of appearance (mechzei): tying camels in a train and pulling them makes the owner look like he is going to market, which is inappropriate on Shabbat.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
What was ultimately revealed about the ownership of the cow that went out with a strap between its horns, associated with Rabbi Elazar ben Azarya?
A. It genuinely belonged to Rabbi Elazar ben Azarya
B. It belonged to his neighbor, and was called by his name only because he failed to protest her practice
C. It was a wild, ownerless cow
D. It belonged to the Sages collectively
Correct: B — A baraita reveals the cow was not his but his neighbor's; because he did not protest her conduct, it was attributed to him — to his discredit.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to Rav Huna's accepted explanation, what does 'ḥanunot' refer to regarding ewes?
A. Oil-soaked wool placed on the forehead against cold
B. Two oil pouches placed at birthing time
C. A wood chip from the ḥanun tree placed in the nose to induce sneezing and expel head-worms
D. A jeweled ornament for wealthy owners' flocks
Correct: C — After two more indulgent suggestions were rejected as too luxurious for a sheep, Rav Huna's explanation — a chip from the ḥanun tree inducing a sneeze that expels worms — was accepted, fitting the term's meaning of showing mercy.
