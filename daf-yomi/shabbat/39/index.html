📖 Overview: The Line Between the Sun's Heat and the Fire's Heat

Shabbat 39a–39b
בְּחַמָּה — דְּכוּלֵּי עָלְמָא לָא פְּלִיגִי דִּשְׁרֵי. בְּתוֹלְדוֹת הָאוּר — כּוּלֵּי עָלְמָא לָא פְּלִיגִי דַּאֲסִיר
The scene: Continuing from the mishna's rules about soaking and rinsing salted fish, the Gemara turns to a subtler question: is heat that comes from the sun treated like heat from fire? Rav Nachman lays out the three-way distinction — direct sunlight, derivatives of fire, and derivatives of the sun — and the Gemara tests it against an egg rolled in warm sand, a hot rooftop, and the famous incident of the Tiberias hot-water pipe, whose true source of heat turns out to be a matter of dispute. The daf closes with the related question of how one may wash after Shabbat with water heated the day before.

🥚 Two Reasons Not to Roll an Egg in Sand

Rabba and Rav Yosef disagree on why — tested by a hot rooftop
Rabba
The Sages forbade insulating an egg in sun-warmed sand as a decree — lest one come to insulate it in hot ashes instead, which is truly forbidden.
Rav Yosef
No — the real reason is that insulating displaces the dirt, which resembles digging.
Test case
Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel permits rolling an egg on a hot roof but forbids it on hot lime. On Rabba's reasoning, a roof is nothing like ash — no decree needed.
Same result
And on Rav Yosef's reasoning too: a typical roof has no loose dirt to displace, so no prohibition applies there either.
Where they truly differ: Only with loose earth — which involves no 'digging' — does the practical difference emerge: Rav Yosef would permit it, Rabba would still forbid it.

😈 Why the Tiberias Hot Springs Don't Prove Rabbi Yosei Wrong

The Rabbis' challenge, and Rabbi Yosei's answer
הַהוּא תּוֹלְדוֹת אוּר הוּא, דְּחָלְפִי אַפִּיתְחָא דְגֵיהִנָּם
The exchange: The Rabbis challenged Rabbi Yosei: the people of Tiberias ran cold water through hot springs — a derivative of the sun, since no fire heated it directly — and yet the Sages forbade using that water. Doesn't that show even sun-derivatives are prohibited? Rabbi Yosei answered that the comparison fails: Tiberias's hot springs are not sun-derivatives at all. They are, in fact, derivatives of fire, because the water passes over the mouth of Gehenna itself and is heated by that subterranean fire — an entirely different category from ordinary sun-warmed water.

🛁 May One Rinse the Whole Body With Water Heated Before Shabbat?

Three tannaim, and the halakha that emerges

Rabbi Meir

  • Forbids rinsing the whole body
  • Applies to both hot and cold water
  • No exception for water heated before Shabbat

Rabbi Shimon

  • Permits rinsing the whole body
  • Since the water was heated before Shabbat began
  • The mishna's unattributed ruling follows this view

Rabbi Yehuda

  • Hot water: forbidden
  • Cold water: permitted
  • Rabbi Yochanan rules the halakha follows this view
