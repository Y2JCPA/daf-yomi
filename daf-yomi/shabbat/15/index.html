📖 Overview: Tracing the Decrees to Their Origins

Shabbat 15a–15b
שְׁלֹמֹה גְּזַר לְקׇדָשִׁים
The scene: The daf opens by distinguishing Solomon's original decree, which covered only consecrated items, from the later decree extending impurity concerns to teruma as well. It then catalogs the only three matters Hillel and Shammai personally disputed — the measure for ḥalla, how much drawn water disqualifies a ritual bath, and a difficult question about a niddah's uncertain flow — before tracing several other decrees, including the impurity of foreign land and of glass vessels, back through layered historical stages to their true origins.

🍇 Two Near-Disputes, Explained Away

Why the tally still stays at exactly three
Question
Only three disputes? What about laying hands (semikha) on festival offerings — Hillel permits it, Shammai forbids it?
Answer
That dispute is far older, going back to Sages who preceded them; Rav Huna meant only disputes original to Hillel and Shammai themselves.
Question
What about one who harvests grapes for the press — Shammai says the seeping juice renders them susceptible to impurity, Hillel says it does not?
Answer
Except for that one case — there, Hillel ultimately fell silent and did not respond to Shammai, effectively conceding the point.

🌍 The Staged Decree on the Land of the Nations

Reconciling early Sages with a late timeline
❓ Yosei ben Yo'ezer and Yosei ben Yoḥanan lived generations before the '80 years before the destruction' — so how could they be the ones who decreed on foreign land?
↓
1️⃣ Yosei ben Yo'ezer and Yosei ben Yoḥanan: teruma touching a clod of foreign earth is held 'in abeyance'; nothing said yet about its airspace
↓
2️⃣ The Sages of the last 80 years: extend 'in abeyance' to the airspace of foreign lands too
↓
3️⃣ The Sages of Usha (after the destruction): upgrade contact with the clod itself to 'burn' — its airspace remains merely in abeyance
Six related uncertainties: A related mishna lists six cases of uncertain impurity — beit haperas, foreign earth, an am ha'aretz's clothing, a found vessel, spittle, and urine — where definite contact still requires teruma to be burned, even though the impurity itself is only in doubt.

🔮 Glass Vessels — Earthenware or Metal?

Rabbi Yoḥanan's ruling, tested against a puzzling mishna

Like earthenware

  • Rabbi Yoḥanan (citing Reish Lakish): since glass begins from sand, the Sages equated it with earthenware
  • Problem: earthenware vessels are never purified by immersion — yet a mishna treats glass vessels as immersible

The special exception

  • A perforated glass vessel patched by dripping in molten lead
  • Rabbi Meir: 'everything follows the reinforcing material' — since lead (a metal) can be purified by immersion, so can this vessel

The Rabbis disagree

  • The Rabbis deem such a lead-patched glass vessel ritually pure regardless, rejecting Rabbi Meir's 'follows the facilitator' principle here
