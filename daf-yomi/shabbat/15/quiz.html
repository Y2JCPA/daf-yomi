Shabbat 15 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
What compromise did the Rabbis reach on the minimum dough obligated in ḥalla, between Shammai's one kav and Hillel's two kav?
A. Exactly one kav
B. Exactly two kav
C. One and a half kav
D. Three kav
Correct: C — The Rabbis ruled neither like Shammai nor like Hillel, but at one and a half kav (later recalibrated as measures changed).
QUESTION 2 OF 5
How much drawn water was ultimately established to disqualify a ritual bath, according to the testimony of two weavers in the name of Shemaya and Avtalyon?
A. Nine kav
B. A full hin (twelve log)
C. Three log
D. One se'ah
Correct: C — The Sages upheld the weavers' testimony that three log of drawn water disqualifies a ritual bath, overriding both Hillel's and Shammai's larger measures.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
Why doesn't the Gemara count the dispute over laying hands (semikha) on festival offerings as one of Hillel and Shammai's own three disputes?
A. Because they never actually discussed the topic
B. Because it is an ancient dispute that predates them
C. Because Rabbi Meir later resolved it
D. Because it only concerns Temple-era practice
Correct: B — The Gemara explains that this dispute over semikha goes back to Sages who came before Hillel and Shammai, so it is not counted among their own original three disagreements.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Rabbi Yishmael ben Yosei's report of his father's teaching, what marked forty years before the Temple's destruction?
A. Rome first invaded the land
B. The decree of impurity on the land of the nations and glass vessels
C. The Sanhedrin left the Chamber of Hewn Stone, meaning it no longer judged capital cases
D. The eighteen decrees were formally ratified
Correct: C — Forty years before the destruction, the Sanhedrin relocated from the Chamber of Hewn Stone to the stores on the Temple Mount, understood to mean it ceased judging capital cases.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
How does the Gemara resolve the tension between equating glass with earthenware (which cannot be purified by immersion) and a mishna suggesting glass vessels can be purified by immersion?
A. It rejects the mishna as unreliable
B. It explains the mishna refers to a perforated glass vessel patched with lead, which Rabbi Meir treats as following the metal that reinforces it
C. It says all glass vessels are always impure regardless of immersion
D. It claims glass vessels were never actually equated with earthenware
Correct: B — The Gemara explains the mishna's case involves glass sealed with dripped lead; Rabbi Meir holds that such a vessel follows the status of its reinforcing material — lead, a metal that can be purified by immersion.
