Shabbat 26 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to the Gemara, why is tar forbidden for lighting the Shabbat lamp?
A. Only because it smells bad
B. Because it is volatile and, in addition, due to a decree lest one siphon from it
C. Because it is too expensive
D. Because it is derived from an impure animal
Correct: B — The Gemara answers that two reasons were stated together: tar is volatile and potentially dangerous, and there is also a rabbinic decree lest one take sap from it.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
Why may a priest not use ritually impure tevel for light, even during the week?
A. Because tevel is always forbidden to touch
B. Because the verse 'My terumot' (plural) teaches that both pure and impure teruma may be used only from the moment it is actually separated, and tevel's teruma has not been separated
C. Because only oil, not produce, may fuel a lamp
D. Because tevel is reserved exclusively for the Temple
Correct: B — Numbers 18:8 speaks of two terumot; just as pure teruma may be used only once separated, impure teruma (still part of the tevel) may not be used before it is separated.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
How does Rabbi Yoḥanan ben Nuri respond to a stringent list of permitted lamp oils?
A. He agrees and adds more restrictions
B. He notes that Babylonians, Medians, Alexandrians, and Cappadocians each have only their local oil, so nothing is forbidden except what the Sages explicitly named
C. He rules that only olive oil may ever be used
D. He says lighting is entirely optional
Correct: B — Rabbi Yoḥanan ben Nuri points out that different regions have access only to their local oils (sesame, nut, radish, naphtha), so the prohibition applies only to substances the Sages specifically named.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Rav Yosef, who were the 'vinedressers' left by Nebuzaradan in Jeremiah 52:16?
A. Farmers who grew grapes near Jerusalem
B. Balsam collectors working from Ein Gedi to Ramata
C. Priests who tended the Temple vineyard
D. Fishermen along the coast
Correct: B — Rav Yosef taught that the 'vinedressers' were the poor who collected balsam sap in the region from Ein Gedi to Ramata.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
What is the practical difference between Abaye's and Rava's understanding of Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar and the school of Rabbi Yishmael?
A. Whether wool can ever become impure
B. Whether a three-by-three handbreadth swatch of a fabric other than wool or linen can contract tzaraat impurity
C. Whether linen is a valid sukkah roofing material
D. Whether tzori may be used for lighting
Correct: B — Rava holds the two opinions disagree specifically about three-by-three handbreadth swatches of other (non-wool, non-linen) garments becoming impure; Abaye held they were saying the same thing.
