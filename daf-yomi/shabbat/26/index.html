📖 Overview: From Lamp Oil to Sukkah Roofing

Shabbat 26a–26b
אֵין מַדְלִיקִין בְּטֶבֶל טָמֵא בַּחוֹל, וְאֵין צָרִיךְ לוֹמַר בְּשַׁבָּת
The scene: The Gemara continues the second chapter's list of forbidden Shabbat-lamp fuels, first pinning down why tar is banned and then asking why impure untithed produce (tevel) may not be used for light even on a weekday. A cautionary tale about a mother-in-law's cruel trick with balsam oil leads into a discussion of who actually gathered balsam and the sky-blue ḥilazon dye. The daf then works through a Tosefta of five tannaim disputing exactly which oils are fit for the Shabbat lamp, with Rabbi Yoḥanan ben Nuri objecting that people in Babylonia, Media, Alexandria, and Cappadocia have no choice but local oils. The sugya closes by pivoting to a related material question: whether cloth made from tree products (other than linen) and swatches of three-by-three fingerbreadths can become ritually impure with tzaraat.

🔥 Why No Tar? One Reason or Two?

The Gemara pins down the Mishna's rationale
Gemara
Let the Master simply say tar is banned because it is volatile עָף and might catch fire!
Answer
He stated one reason and another: it is volatile, and there is a decree lest one siphon off tar to use for something else.
Story
A cruel mother-in-law told her daughter-in-law: go anoint yourself with balsam oil, then go light the lamp. The oil caught fire and she was burned.
Why the story here: It illustrates the real danger of volatile substances near open flame, motivating the Sages' extra caution with tar and similar oils.

🕯️ Impure Tevel — Forbidden Even on a Weekday

A verse about two kinds of teruma
וַאֲנִי הִנֵּה נָתַתִּי לְךָ אֶת מִשְׁמֶרֶת תְּרוּמוֹתָי
The derivation: Naphtha is banned because it is volatile — but why ban ritually impure tevel, untithed produce that has not yet had teruma separated from it? The Gemara answers from Numbers 18:8, 'I have given you the charge of My terumot' — the plural teaches two terumot, pure and impure. Just as pure teruma may be used only from the moment it is actually separated, so too impure teruma may be used only from that moment. Since tevel's teruma has not yet been designated, a priest may not yet benefit from any part of it, including for light.

🛢️ The Tosefta's Five Opinions on Shabbat Oil

Each tanna draws the line differently
TannaRuling
Rabbi Shimon ben ElazarMay not light with tzori (balsam sap) — it is not from the tree itself
Rabbi YishmaelMay not light with anything that comes from a tree at all
Rabbi Yishmael ben BerokaMay light only with what comes from a fruit
Rabbi TarfonMay light only with olive oil
Rabbi Yoḥanan ben NuriObjects: Babylonians have only sesame oil, Medians nut oil, Alexandrians radish oil, Cappadocians only naphtha — so nothing is forbidden except what the Sages explicitly named
Practical result: Fish oil and tar are permitted; Rabbi Shimon Shezuri adds gourd oil and naphtha; Sumakhos limits animal-derived oils to fish oil alone.

🌿 Balsam Collectors and Ḥilazon Trappers

Jeremiah's 'poorest of the land' identified
📜 Jeremiah 52:16 — Nebuzaradan left 'the poorest of the land' as vinedressers and husbandmen
↓
🍇 'Vinedressers' (Rav Yosef): balsam collectors from Ein Gedi to Ramata
↓
🐚 'Husbandmen': trappers of the ḥilazon snail, source of sky-blue dye, from Tyre's Ladder to Ḥaifa
The point: Both trades yielded only a meager living for the poor, which is why they were left to work them.

📜 Which Fabrics Can Contract Tzaraat?

Tracing the verses from 'garment' to 'three by three'
1️⃣ Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar: only wool and linen fabric counts as a 'garment' for tzaraat impurity — other tree-based cloth does not
↓
2️⃣ The school of Rabbi Yishmael reaches the same rule from Leviticus 13:47, which specifies wool or linen garments for the leprosy plague
↓
3️⃣ Abaye: the two sources agree entirely. Rava disagrees — they differ over three-by-three handbreadth swatches of other fabrics
↓
4️⃣ A baraita derives from 'the garment' (וְהַבֶּגֶד) that swatches of three-by-three fingerbreadths in wool/linen can also become impure, since warp and woof threads alone already can
↓
5️⃣ Two exclusions in the verses ('a garment of wool or linen') teach that fabrics other than wool and linen cannot contract tzaraat impurity at all
Bridge to next daf: This impurity discussion, triggered by tree-derived tzori and sukkah roofing, sets up the Gemara's next steps in defining 'garment.'
