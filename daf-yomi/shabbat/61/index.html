📖 Overview: Sandals, Shoe-Laces, Tefillin, and Amulets

Shabbat 61a–61b
וְלֹא בְּקָמֵיעַ בִּזְמַן שֶׁאֵינוֹ מִן הַמּוּמְחֶה
The scene: The daf opens by finishing the mishna's single-sandal case — when a foot wound justifies wearing only one sandal, which foot gets it? — and widens into the order in which one puts on shoes, ties them, washes, and applies oil, weighing Rabbi Yoḥanan's tefillin-based custom against a conflicting baraita. It then returns to the mishna's list, explaining through Rav Safra why tefillin are forbidden on Shabbat and why going out with them still carries no sin-offering liability, regardless of the dispute over whether Shabbat is even a day for tefillin. The rest of the daf works out exactly what makes an amulet 'from an expert' — testing combinations of writers and amulets — and closes by asking whether amulets carry any sanctity, checking that question against saving them from a fire, burying them when discarded, and entering a bathroom while wearing one.

🦶 An Odd Sandal — Which Foot?

With a wound, the mishna permits a single sandal — but on which foot?
Gemara
The mishna implies: with a wound on the foot, one MAY go out wearing a single sandal. On which foot does he wear it?
Rav Huna
On the wounded foot. A sandal exists to prevent pain — wearing just one shows everyone it's for the wound, not for hiding a carried sandal.
Ḥiyya bar Rav
On the healthy foot! A sandal exists for comfort, and the visible wound on the other foot itself explains why it has none.
Gemara
Rabbi Yoḥanan told his attendant, 'Give me my sandal.' Handed the RIGHT one, he objected: 'You've made this foot into a wounded one!' Doesn't that prove he follows Rav Huna?
Rejection
Not necessarily — he may have meant the opposite: 'you've made my bare LEFT foot look wounded,' which fits Ḥiyya bar Rav instead.
Left unresolved: The exchange with Rabbi Yoḥanan's attendant cannot decide between the two views.

👟 Right Foot First? Reconciling Two Customs

Rabbi Yoḥanan's tefillin-parallel meets a conflicting baraita
1️⃣ Rabbi Yoḥanan: 'like tefillin, so shoes' — tefillin go on the LEFT arm, so a shoe goes on the LEFT foot first
↓
2️⃣ Objection, from a baraita: 'One puts on the right shoe, and afterward the left'
↓
3️⃣ Rav Yosef: since both are taught, either practice is valid — 'one who did this way, did properly; one who did that way, did properly'
↓
4️⃣ Abaye pushes back: maybe Rabbi Yoḥanan simply never heard this baraita — a real ruling is still needed
↓
5️⃣ Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak: 'one who fears Heaven fulfills both' — like Mar, son of Rabbana, who puts on the right shoe untied, then the left shoe tied, then goes back and ties the right
Also taught: Removing shoes: left off first, then right, so the right stays on longest. Washing and oiling: right first, then left — but oiling the whole body starts at the head, 'king over all the limbs.'

📿 Tefillin on Shabbat — Forbidden and Exempt, Either Way

Rav Safra's two independent rulings
דִּילְמָא אָתֵי לְאֵיתוּיֵי בִּרְשׁוּת הָרַבִּים
Rav Safra: The mishna forbids going out with tefillin. Do not say this is only because some hold Shabbat is not a proper day for donning tefillin at all — even for those who hold it IS a proper day, one may not go out wearing them, lest he come to carry them by hand through the public domain. And on the mishna's closing line, that one who does go out incurs no sin-offering: do not say that is only true for those who hold Shabbat IS a day for tefillin (so no violation occurs at all) — even for those who hold it is NOT, he is still exempt, דֶּרֶךְ מַלְבּוּשׁ עֲבִידָא, because wearing tefillin is done in the manner of a garment, not blatant carrying.

🧿 What Proves an Amulet 'From an Expert'

Rav Pappa separates two questions: is the WRITER an expert, and is the AMULET itself effective?
CaseAmulet proven effective?Writer proven an expert?
3 amulets, 3 people, each healed 3 times (9 total cures)YesYes
3 amulets, 3 people, each healed onceNoYes
1 amulet, healed 3 different peopleYesNo
3 amulets for 1 person, all healed himUncertainUncertain — left as a teiku
Baseline (Tosefta): An amulet counts as 'from an expert' once it has healed one person three times, whether written or made of herbal roots, and whether the patient was dangerously ill or not.

🔥 Do Amulets Carry Sanctity?

The Gemara tests the question against three practical halakhot
❓ Do amulets bearing Torah verses and Divine names carry sanctity?
↓
Rescue from a Shabbat fire?
NO — a baraita: blessings and amulets, even with Torah passages, are not saved; they burn in place
Require burial (geniza) if discarded?
YES — by analogy: God's name on a vessel's handle or a bed-leg must be cut off and buried
↓
🚻 Third test: may one enter a bathroom wearing one? The mishna implies yes — 'not with an amulet, unless from an expert' — so an expert's amulet may be worn everywhere, bathroom included
↓
✅ Since its power to heal doesn't depend on where it's worn, one may even carry it in his HAND on Shabbat, not only around the neck
