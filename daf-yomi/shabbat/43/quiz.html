Shabbat 43 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
How does Rabba answer Abaye's objection that a vessel may catch a ceiling leak, even though this seems to be an uncommon case of preservation?
A. The mishna is discussing Shabbat eve only
B. It refers to new houses, which leak frequently — making it a common case
C. Leaks are never protected against
D. Water damage is not a concern on Shabbat
Correct: B — Rabba explains the mishna refers to new houses, which commonly leak, so catching the leak fits his rule that vessels may be moved only for common mishaps.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to Rav Yosef, why may one not place a vessel beneath a hen to catch its egg?
A. It is disrespectful to the hen
B. Doing so negates the vessel's readiness for use, since the egg can never become usable that Shabbat
C. Eggs laid on Shabbat may be eaten immediately
D. The vessel becomes ritually impure
Correct: B — Rav Yosef explains that receiving the set-aside egg renders the vessel itself unusable and unmovable — tantamount to breaking it — unlike tevel, which could still become usable if tithed.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
Why may a mat be spread over a beehive to protect it from sun or rain?
A. Bees are never muktzeh
B. Because there is honey inside that one is permitted to use, or specific honeycombs designated before Shabbat
C. Beehives may always be moved freely
D. Only in the summer, and never for another reason
Correct: B — The Gemara explains the mat is being spread for the sake of the honey (or, in the rainy season, two specifically pre-designated honeycombs), not for the bees themselves.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Rabbi Yitzchak, under what condition may one move a vessel to a location where a forbidden object like an egg happens to be?
A. Never, under any circumstances
B. Only if he independently needs the vessel's location for another reason
C. Only on Friday
D. Only if the egg is covered first
Correct: B — Rabbi Yitzchak's objections are all resolved by explaining that one needed to move the vessel to that spot for an unrelated reason (tzarich limkomo), after which he may set it anywhere, including over the egg.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
What is the core dispute between Rav and Shmuel regarding moving a corpse from bed to bed when no loaf or infant is available to place on top of it?
A. Whether a corpse may ever be moved at all
B. Whether moving an object in an atypical, indirect manner (tiltul min hatzad) counts as an act of 'moving'
C. Whether beds may be carried on Shabbat
D. Whether the corpse must first be covered
Correct: B — Rav holds that moving something in an atypical way still counts as moving (so it's forbidden without a loaf/infant), while Shmuel holds it does not count as moving (so it's permitted).
