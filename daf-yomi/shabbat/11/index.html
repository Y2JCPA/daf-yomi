📖 Overview: Sayings of Rav, and the Mishna of Precautions

Shabbat 11a–11b
לְעוֹלָם אַל יְשַׁנֶּה אָדָם בְּנוֹ בֵּין הַבָּנִים
The scene: The daf continues a chain of teachings that Rava bar Meḥasseya transmits in the name of Rav Ḥama bar Gurya in the name of Rav — practical and moral wisdom about synagogues, rulers, illness, and dreams. It then turns to a new mishna: a set of rabbinic decrees meant to keep people from carrying on Shabbat out of habit, such as a tailor going out with his needle near nightfall. The Gemara analyzes how far these 'fences' extend — to a karmelit, to an object merely stuck in clothing — and works through a contradiction in the sources about a zav's pouch.

💭 Rav's Maxims — What to Prefer, What to Endure

Transmitted by Rava bar Meḥasseya in the name of Rav Ḥama bar Gurya, in the name of Rav
MaximContent
Whom to live under (best to worst)Under Ishmael rather than a stranger (Rome); under a stranger rather than a Ḥabar (Persian fire priest); under a Ḥabar rather than a Torah scholar; under a Torah scholar rather than an orphan or widow
What to endure (any X but not Y)Any illness but not intestinal disease; any pain but not heart pain; any ache but not headache; any evil but not an evil wife
A city's rooflineAny city whose houses' roofs are higher than the synagogue is ultimately destroyed (Ezra 9:9) — Rav Ashi credited building Mata Meḥasseya's synagogue tall, though it was later destroyed for a different sin
The reach of government: Rav: 'Even if all seas were ink, reeds were quills, and the heavens were parchment, it would not suffice to write the full scope of governmental affairs' (alluded to in Proverbs 25:3).

😴 A Fast to Undo a Bad Dream

Rav, Rav Ḥisda, and Rav Yosef refine the remedy
1️⃣ Rav: A fast is as effective against a bad dream as fire is to chaff
↓
2️⃣ Rav Ḥisda adds: only if held on that very day of the dream
↓
3️⃣ Rav Yosef adds further: one may even hold this fast on Shabbat itself
↓
4️⃣ Case in point: Rav Yehoshua, son of Rav Idi, refused a fine third-born calf at Rav Ashi's table, explaining that a dream-fast — unlike an ordinary fast — cannot simply be postponed to another day

📚 Interrupting Torah Study for Shema and Prayer

Rabbi Yoḥanan qualifies a baraita's ruling
Baraita
Torah scholars engaged in study stop for Shema, but do not stop for prayer.
R' Yoḥanan
That was only taught for the likes of Rabbi Shimon ben Yoḥai and his colleagues, whose Torah study is their sole vocation.
By contrast
For the likes of us, who also engage in other pursuits, we stop our study for both Shema and prayer.
Exception
The Elders of Hagronya, quoting Rabbi Elazar ben Tzadok: 'When we were engaged in intercalating the year in Yavne, we stopped for neither Shema nor prayer' — that work was too urgent to interrupt.

🧵 The Mishna's Fences Against Carrying Out of Habit

Decrees issued so a person will not forget and violate Shabbat
❓ Mishna: near nightfall on Shabbat eve, a tailor may not go out with his needle, nor a scribe with his quill
↓
💡 Reason: lest he forget it is Shabbat and carry it into the public domain
↓
Same mishna
One may not shake lice from clothing, nor read by candlelight, lest one adjust the wick
Parallel decree
A zav may not eat with the zava, lest familiarity lead to sin
↓
⚖️ Gemara: does this same 'fence upon a fence' extend to drinking across a karmelit? Abaye: yes, it is treated the same as private/public domain. Rava: no — one does not build a rabbinic fence to protect another rabbinic decree

🪡 The Needle — Held in Hand, or Stuck in Cloth?

The manner of carrying determines liability, echoed in the zav's pouch

Held in the hand

  • The typical way of carrying — a full-fledged act by Torah law
  • All agree the mishna's ban near dusk applies here

Stuck in clothing

  • Not the typical manner — only a rabbinic shevut
  • Rabbi Meir: exempt by Torah law, even for a craftsman
  • Rabbi Yehuda: a craftsman who carries in his craft's own typical manner is liable

The zav's pouch (parallel case)

  • Two baraitot conflict — one exempts, one obligates a sin-offering
  • Resolved by Rav Yosef as Rabbi Meir vs. Rabbi Yehuda, or by Rav Hamnuna as a zav with two sightings (needs the pouch) vs. three (does not)
