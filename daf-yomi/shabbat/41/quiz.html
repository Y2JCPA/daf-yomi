Shabbat 41 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to the Gemara, when is it permitted to cover one's genitals with one's hands while bathing in a river?
A. Never, under any circumstances
B. Only while descending into the water
C. Only while emerging from the water, facing people on the bank
D. Only if one is a priest
Correct: C — While descending (facing no one), covering oneself looks like denying Avraham's covenant and is forbidden; while emerging and facing onlookers, it is permitted for modesty.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
Why was Rabbi Abbahu permitted to float in a particular pool despite the general prohibition on floating in water on Shabbat?
A. He was a Torah scholar and exempt from the rule
B. The pool had embankments, making it resemble an enclosed vessel rather than an open body of water
C. It was not actually Shabbat
D. Floating was always permitted for everyone
Correct: B — A baraita forbids floating in an open pool lacking embankments; Rabbi Abbahu's pool had them, distinguishing it from a river or ocean.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to the mishna, what is the key difference between a mulyar and an antikhi once both are swept of coals?
A. There is no difference; both are permitted
B. One may drink from a swept mulyar, but not from a swept antikhi, since its copper continues to heat the water
C. The mulyar is always forbidden and the antikhi always permitted
D. Both are forbidden under all circumstances
Correct: B — A swept mulyar (water inside, coals outside) is permitted, while an antikhi remains forbidden because its copper construction keeps conducting heat to the water.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
Regarding an urn emptied of its hot water, what does Rav Adda bar Mattana say determines whether adding cold water is permitted?
A. The time of day
B. The quantity of water added — a small amount becomes hot, a large amount only loses its chill
C. Whether the water is drawn from a well or a river
D. Whether a priest performs the action
Correct: B — A small quantity added would heat up fully in the still-hot urn (forbidden); a large quantity merely loses its chill without truly heating (permitted).
QUESTION 5 OF 5
What is the dispute between Rav and Shmuel regarding adding water to an emptied urn to the point of 'tempering' the metal?
A. Both permit it without any limit
B. Rav permits only up to the measure needed to warm the water; Shmuel permits even filling to the tempering measure
C. Both forbid it entirely
D. The dispute concerns only wooden vessels
Correct: B — Rav restricts the leniency to the amount needed merely to warm the water, while Shmuel permits even a quantity sufficient to temper (harden) the urn.
