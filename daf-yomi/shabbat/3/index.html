📖 Overview: Exemptions, a Laden Body, and a Wandering Hand

Shabbat 3a–3b
כֹּל פְּטוּרֵי דְשַׁבָּת פָּטוּר אֲבָל אָסוּר, בַּר מֵהָנֵי תְּלָת דְּפָטוּר וּמוּתָּר
The scene: Daf 3 opens by using Shmuel's rule — that nearly every exempt ruling on Shabbat is nonetheless rabbinically prohibited — to sharpen the previous daf's open question about counting the mishna's cases. The Gemara then defends the mishna's ruling that two people who together complete one prohibited labor are both exempt, rooting it in the verse's word 'בַּעֲשֹׂתָהּ.' From there it moves to Rav's dilemma about a person laden with food across his body rather than in his hand, and Abaye's proof that an outstretched hand belongs to no fixed domain. The daf closes with an extended, multi-layered attempt to reconcile two baraitot that disagree about whether one may bring a fruit-filled hand back after extending it into the public domain.

🦌 Exempt But Prohibited — Except for Three

Shmuel's rule, and the narrow exceptions to it

The General Rule (Shmuel)

  • Almost every exempt ruling in the halakhot of Shabbat is 'exempt but prohibited'
  • Torah law imposes no liability, but the Sages forbade the act anyway
  • This applies whenever a defined action was actually performed

Three True Exceptions

  • 🦌 Trapping a deer — sitting at the entrance of a house it already entered on its own
  • 🐍 Trapping a poisonous snake — permitted because of the danger it poses
  • 🩹 Draining an abscess — lancing a boil to relieve the pus
Why these three: Even Shmuel's cases are 'exempt but prohibited' when a defined action was performed; these three were singled out precisely because they ARE defined actions that the Sages nonetheless permitted outright.

🔢 Solving Rav Mattana's Count

Why the mishna's total stays at eight, not twelve
1️⃣ Rav Mattana's challenge stands: the mishna's second half has four 'both exempt' cases, each really performed by two separate people
↓
2️⃣ The Gemara answers: only cases where the person performed an act of lifting (עֲקִירָה) are counted, because lifting can lead toward sin-offering liability if the act continues
↓
3️⃣ Under no circumstances can one who merely places an object come to violate a more serious prohibition — placing alone is a dead end, halakhically
↓
4️⃣ So the mishna's tally counts liftings, not participants — which is why the total remains eight cases, not twelve
Principle: The mishna's bookkeeping tracks potential paths to greater liability, not the raw number of people whose hands were involved.

📜 'When He Does It' — And a Body Laden With Food

Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi grounds the mishna's exemption, then Rav tests its limits
Gemara
The mishna's second half rules 'both exempt' — but a severe Torah prohibition WAS carried out between the two of them! How can both go free?
Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi
Leviticus 4:27 says one who sins is liable בַּעֲשֹׂתָהּ — 'when he does it.' One who performs the whole transgression is liable; one who performs only part of it is not.
Rav
If another person loaded me with food and drink across my back, and I walked out with it — is moving my whole body like lifting the object with my hand? Am I liable?
Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi
Liable — and it is not comparable to the case of a hand merely holding an object. A body comes to rest in a fixed place; a hand does not.
The distinction: גּוּפוֹ נָיַיח, יָדוֹ לָא נָיַיח — 'his body is at rest, his hand is not.' A laden body counts as an act of lifting in a way a mere hand does not.

✋ Whose Domain Is a Hand?

Abaye proves an outstretched hand belongs to neither domain
❓ Abaye: is a person's hand, once extended into a different domain, considered part of that domain — or of the one he stands in?
↓
Not like the public domain
The poor person's hand carrying an object INTO the private domain: if the homeowner takes it, the homeowner is not liable. Were the hand 'public domain,' this would be a full transfer.
Not like the private domain
The homeowner's hand carrying an object OUT to the public domain: if the poor person takes it, the poor person is not liable, for the identical reason.
↓
✅ Conclusion: an outstretched hand is legally neither the public domain nor the private domain — it belongs to neither
What's next: This sets up Abaye's own dilemma — while hanging between domains, does the hand instead take on the status of a karmelit?

🍎 A Hand Full of Fruit — Five Attempted Resolutions

Two baraitot disagree: may one bring a fruit-filled hand back after extending it outside?
Proposed distinctionPermitted to bring hand backProhibited to bring hand back
HeightHand extended above ten handbreadths (outside the public domain's airspace)Hand extended below ten handbreadths (within the public domain)
TimingHand extended while it was still day, before Shabbat beganHand extended after dark, once Shabbat had already begun
IntentHand extended unwittinglyHand extended intentionally
Penalizing the unwittingSages did not penalize an unwitting act on account of intentional onesSages DID penalize it, lest people come to act intentionally
DestinationReturning it to the SAME courtyard he stood inReturning it to a DIFFERENT courtyard
Rava's version: Same courtyard — since his goal of moving the fruit elsewhere was never achieved, forcing him to bring it back is itself a kind of penalty. Different courtyard — his goal WAS achieved, so the Sages prohibit completing the transfer.
