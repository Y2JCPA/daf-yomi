📖 Overview: Measuring the Edge of Shabbat

Shabbat 35a–35b
כּוֹכָב אֶחָד — יוֹם, שְׁנַיִם — בֵּין הַשְּׁמָשׁוֹת, שְׁלֹשָׁה — לַיְלָה
The scene: The Gemara continues its precise search for the boundary between day and night. It first pins down the exact fraction of a mil that twilight lasts, then follows Abaye and Rava outside at sunset to work out which horizon the mishna's 'eastern face' actually means. Rabbi Chanina and Rabbi Chiyya offer physical landmarks on Mount Carmel for measuring twilight and for finding Miriam's well. The Gemara then reconciles Rabbi Yehuda's and Rabbi Yosei's twilight for priestly immersion and teruma-eating, gives a starlight test for nightfall, and closes with the six shofar blasts that walked the town from the fields, to the shops, to the lit Shabbat lamp.

📏 Two Disputes That Run in Opposite Directions

Rabba and Rav Yosef disagree on twilight's length — and, oddly, on vessel size the other way

Twilight (mil)

  • Rabba: three-quarters of a mil
  • Rav Yosef: two-thirds of a mil
  • Difference: half a danka (1/12 of a mil) — Rav Yosef's twilight is shorter

Wicker Vessel (kor)

  • Rabba: permitted to move up to 2 kor; 3 kor prohibited
  • Rav Yosef: permitted up to 3 kor; 4 kor prohibited
  • Here Rav Yosef's permitted size is larger
Abaye's proof: Abaye asked Rabba this in practice, and even a 2-kor vessel was forbidden — matching the mishna that a vessel holding 40 se'a of liquid (= 2 kor dry) is beyond the size of a 'vessel' for impurity, and so beyond the size one may move on Shabbat. From this Abaye derived that dry goods exceed liquid capacity by one-third.

🧭 Which Way Is the 'Eastern Face' of the Sky?

Abaye catches Rava looking the wrong direction
Abaye
I saw Rava gazing westward at dusk. But the baraita defines twilight as lasting 'as long as the eastern face of the sky is reddened' — why look west?
Rava
Do you think it means the eastern face itself? No — it means the face of the sky that causes the east to redden, which is the west.
Alt. version
Some say it was the reverse: Rava saw Abaye looking east and asked him the same question.
Answer
Same resolution either way — and your mnemonic is a window: light from the west lands on the opposite wall, so that is what one actually watches.
Point: 'Eastern face' in the baraita describes the effect (a red glow visible in the east), not the direction one must look.

📯 The Six Shofar Blasts of Friday Afternoon

Each blast moves the town one step closer to Shabbat
1️⃣ First blast: field workers stop hoeing, plowing, and all field labor; nearer workers wait for the farther ones so all enter town together
↓
2️⃣ Second blast: shop shutters come down and stores lock up; hot water and pots are still on the stove at this point
↓
3️⃣ Third blast: pots are removed, hot water is insulated, and the lamp is lit — a pause follows, long enough to fry a small fish or stick bread to an oven wall
↓
4️⃣ Tekia–terua–tekia sounds, and Shabbat is formally accepted
Two views on the third blast: Rabbi Natan says it signals lighting the lamp; Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi says it signals removing tefillin, since they are not worn on Shabbat.

🎺 May a Shofar or Trumpet Be Moved on Shabbat?

The sexton's synagogue shofar meets the laws of set-aside (muktze)
❓ A baraita permits moving a shofar but forbids moving trumpets on Shabbat — why the difference?
↓
💡 Rav Yosef: it depends on ownership — an individual's shofar has a Shabbat use (pouring water for a child through its bent mouth); a community's shofar does not
↓
🤔 Abaye objects: couldn't a communal shofar also give water to a poor child? And another baraita permits moving trumpets too, just like a shofar!
↓
✅ Resolution: three baraitot track three tannaim — Rabbi Yehuda (only useful items may be moved), Rabbi Shimon (no set-aside restriction applies at all), Rabbi Nechemya (an object's primary function still bars it, even for another use)
