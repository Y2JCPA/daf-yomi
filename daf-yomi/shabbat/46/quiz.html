Shabbat 46 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
Why is it forbidden to move a candelabrum made of actual removable joints on Shabbat?
A. It is too heavy
B. It might fall apart while being moved, and reassembling it constitutes prohibited construction
C. It is disrespectful to the household
D. Candelabra are always forbidden
Correct: B — Abaye explains the concern is that the candelabrum could come apart into its component pieces during moving, and reassembling it would be a Torah-level act of building.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
Why does the Gemara forbid moving even a large candelabrum that merely has decorative grooves (not real joints)?
A. Large candelabra are always heavier
B. By rabbinic decree, since it could be mistaken for a genuinely jointed candelabrum
C. It has no practical use
D. It belongs to a different category entirely
Correct: B — Because large candelabra are commonly made of real joints, a rabbinic decree forbids even a large grooved one, out of concern people would mistake it for the jointed type.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
Why did Rabbi Abbahu move a lamp at Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi's home but not at Rabbi Yochanan's home?
A. He held different opinions in each place
B. He personally held like the lenient Rabbi Shimon throughout, but deferred to Rabbi Yochanan's stricter ruling out of respect when in his place
C. Rabbi Yochanan's house had no lamps
D. It was a scribal error
Correct: B — The Gemara explains Rabbi Abbahu consistently held like Rabbi Shimon (lenient), but out of honor for Rabbi Yochanan he did not act against his host's own ruling in his locale.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Rav Avya, why may a naphtha lamp be moved even though it is unpleasant to be near, while ordinary pebbles suitable for covering a vessel may not?
A. Naphtha lamps are lighter than pebbles
B. A lamp already carries the legal status of a vessel (torat keli), so a secondary use suffices to keep it movable; raw pebbles never had that status
C. Pebbles are always forbidden regardless of use
D. There is no real difference between them
Correct: B — Rav Avya distinguishes: an item that is already a recognized vessel remains movable if it has any secondary use, but raw material like pebbles needs to be specifically designated before Shabbat to become movable.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
How does Rabba resolve the apparent contradiction between Rabbi Shimon permitting leftover lamp oil but treating an unexpectedly blemished firstborn animal as set aside?
A. He says Rabbi Shimon contradicts himself
B. A person predictably anticipates exactly when a lamp will go out, but does not anticipate an unpredictable, uncertain blemish appearing on an animal
C. Blemished animals are never usable on Shabbat regardless
D. Oil and animals follow completely unrelated rules with no comparison possible
Correct: B — Rabba explains the difference is predictability: one sits and expects the lamp's oil to run out, but a blemish appearing, being permanent, and being examined by a Sage are all uncertain, so it was never anticipated.
