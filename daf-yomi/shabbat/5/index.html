📖 Overview: Covered Domains, a Hand's Worth, and a Drop of Rain

Shabbat 5a–5b
יָדוֹ שֶׁל אָדָם חֲשׁוּבָה לוֹ כְּאַרְבָּעָה עַל אַרְבָּעָה
The scene: Daf 5 continues hunting for the source behind the mishna's liability for a hand too small to meet the usual four-by-four-handbreadth requirement. After testing and rejecting an explanation based on covered domains, the Gemara turns to a baraita of Acherim about someone who catches a thrown object, then works through several increasingly technical proposals — a basket, a lowered hand, a short person — before Rava and, independently, Rabbi Yochanan cut through the tangle with a simple principle: a person's hand is significant enough, on its own, to count as four by four handbreadths. The daf then applies related reasoning to gathering rainwater, a floating nut, and oil on wine, before turning to the separate question of when a person carrying an object all day is considered to have made a halakhic placement at all.

🏠 Covered Domains — A Rule That Doesn't Travel

Rebbi's liability for a covered private domain cannot be stretched to a covered public one
Domain typeRulingReason
Covered private domainLiable, even without a defined landing spotבֵּיתָא כְּמַאן דְּמַלְיָא דָּמְיָא — the house is considered as though filled; anything passing through it is as if it landed on solid ground
Uncovered private domainNot liableWithout a roof, there is no basis to treat the airspace as 'filled'
Covered public domainNot liable (a separate ruling of Rav)Carrying four cubits there is exempt, because a covered public domain is unlike the desert encampment on which the melakha of carrying is modeled
Why this fails: Since even Rebbi's own reasoning cannot extend from a covered private domain to a covered public one, a covered roof cannot be the true explanation for our mishna's liability.

🎯 Acherim: It Depends on the Catcher, Not Just the Thrower

Rabbi Zeira finds a different tannaitic source for the mishna
❓ If a covered domain isn't the answer, whose opinion explains our mishna's liability for a mere hand?
↓
💡 Rabbi Zeira: it is the opinion of אֲחֵרִים (Acherim), from a baraita about one who catches a thrown object
↓
Stood still and caught it
The thrower is LIABLE — his own lifting, combined with the catcher's stillness, completes the act
Ran toward it and caught it
The thrower is EXEMPT — the catcher's own movement, not the thrower's act, brought the object to rest
↓
✅ Since Acherim impose liability even though the receiving hand has no 4×4 surface, they too require no defined area for placing
Not airtight: The Gemara immediately questions the proof: perhaps only placing needs no 4×4 for Acherim, while lifting still does; or perhaps the catcher extended the folds of his cloak, which does provide 4×4.

🖐 A Person's Hand Is Worth Four by Four

A tangle of forced readings, cut through by one simple principle
1️⃣ Rabbi Abba first tries: the mishna really means a basket (טְרַסְקָל) in hand, not the bare hand — but the mishna's own words say 'his hand,' not 'a basket'
↓
2️⃣ Rabbi Abbahu tries instead: the poor person lowered his hand below three handbreadths off the ground, where the principle of lavud makes it 'as good as touching the ground' — forcing the mishna's word 'stands' to mean 'bends,' 'in a pit,' or 'a very short person'
↓
3️⃣ Rava objects: would the tanna really go to all this trouble rather than simply state the halakha?
↓
4️⃣ Rava's principle — echoed independently by Rabbi Yochanan: יָדוֹ שֶׁל אָדָם חֲשׁוּבָה לוֹ כְּאַרְבָּעָה עַל אַרְבָּעָה — a person's hand is significant enough, on its own, to count as four by four handbreadths
Extended further: Rabbi Yochanan adds: even if the object lands in ANOTHER person's hand without his intending to catch it, the hand's significance still applies — it does not depend on the receiver having deemed his own hand significant.

💧 Water on Water, and a Floating Nut

Applying the same logic of 'has it come to rest?' to gathering rainwater

Gathered off a Sloped Wall

  • Works for a rolling SCROLL — a book naturally settles against an incline
  • Fails for WATER — water never truly rests on a slope; it keeps flowing

Gathered off a Water-Filled Hole

  • Succeeds: water already sitting in a hole has come to rest
  • Rava's principle: מַיִם עַל גַּבֵּי מַיִם הַיְינוּ הַנָּחָתָן — water on top of water IS its own placement

A Floating Nut

  • A nut floating on water is NOT considered placed — it drifts
  • Rava's dilemma: a nut inside a floating VESSEL — follow the nut (at rest) or the vessel (still moving)? Unresolved — teiku
Same test elsewhere: The Gemara notes a parallel dispute over oil floating on wine — whether the two are considered connected — which likewise turns on this question of what counts as truly 'at rest.'

🚶 Carrying All Day Without Stopping

When does a person laden with objects make a halakhic 'placement'?
R' Yochanan
One laden with food and drink who enters and exits a domain all day long is not liable by Torah law — not until he stands still.
Abaye
And that is specifically if he stopped in order to rest. If he merely paused to adjust the burden on his shoulder, that does not count as a placement.
Gemara
Proof: the same distinction applies to carrying four cubits in the public domain — stopping to rest exempts him mid-way, but stopping to shift his load does not.
Baraita
One who carries an object from a store to a plaza via a colonnade is liable — yet ben Azzai exempts him, holding that walking itself counts as coming to rest at every single step.
Left open: The Rabbis, unlike ben Azzai, hold that walking is not like standing — so how can passing through the colonnade, an exempt domain, ever produce liability? The daf ends without an answer; it is taken up on daf 6.
