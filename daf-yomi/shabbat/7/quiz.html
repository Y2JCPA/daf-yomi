Shabbat 7 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
What is a karpef, and why is it still a true private domain by Torah law despite the Sages restricting it?
A. A small basket; it is exempt because it is under 4×4 handbreadths
B. A large fenced enclosure not built for residence; it has genuine partitions, so Torah law recognizes it as a private domain even though the Sages treat it like a karmelit for lacking residents
C. A section of the Temple; it is always fully permitted
D. A type of public well; it is always a public domain
Correct: B — A karpef has real partitions, which is all Torah law requires for private-domain status; the Sages layered on a karmelit-like restriction only because no one actually resides there.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
Why is the space between the pillars alongside the public domain treated as a karmelit rather than a full public domain?
A. Because it is roofed
B. Because the multitudes walk there, but not in a direct, unbroken path
C. Because it belongs to the Temple
D. Because no one is permitted to enter it
Correct: B — Even though people do pass through that space, their use of it is not direct and convenient the way walking down the open street is, so it remains a karmelit.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to the discussion of an upright brick standing in the public domain, when is an object that lands ON TOP of it exempt from liability?
A. Only if the brick belongs to a homeowner
B. When the brick is at least three handbreadths high, since the multitudes don't step on top of it
C. Never — landing on top is always liable
D. Only on Shabbat afternoon
Correct: B — Once a brick is three handbreadths high, the multitudes no longer tread on its top surface, so it is treated as a domain separate from the public domain around it.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
What does Shmuel mean by telling Rav Yehuda not to get involved in Shabbat questions 'above ten handbreadths'?
A. That the Torah does not apply above ten handbreadths
B. That karmelit status does not exist above a height of ten handbreadths
C. That priests may not enter spaces above ten handbreadths
D. That objects thrown above ten handbreadths are never counted
Correct: B — The Gemara concludes Shmuel meant that a karmelit 'extends up to ten' handbreadths and no further — above that height, the area is no longer governed by karmelit rules at all.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to Abaye, why does digging a four-by-four handbreadth pit in the floor of a low-ceilinged house, completing its depth to ten handbreadths, permit carrying throughout the entire house?
A. Because digging always creates an exempt domain
B. Because the dug-out spot becomes a private domain, and the rest of the house is treated as its 'holes,' which share the private domain's status
C. Because digging removes the house's roof status
D. Because the house then becomes a karmelit
Correct: B — Abaye reasons that the pit itself qualifies as a private domain, and since holes of a private domain are universally agreed to carry the same status as the private domain, the whole house becomes fit for carrying.
