Shabbat 38 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
How do Rabba and Rav Yosef read the ruling 'it is no different' regarding one who forgot a pot on the stove?
A. Forgetting is always forbidden, just like intentional cooking
B. Since forgetting involves no active deed, one may eat the food even if the pot was left intentionally
C. The ruling applies only to eggs
D. Forgetting is punished more severely than intentional cooking
Correct: B — Rabba and Rav Yosef distinguish actively cooking (judged by intent) from merely forgetting a pot (no act performed, so permitted even if left on purpose).
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to Rabbi Yehuda's view in the baraita, why is fully-heated hot water permitted to eat, while fully-cooked food is not?
A. Water is never considered 'cooked'
B. Water shrivels and deteriorates with more heat, so there is no temptation to stoke the coals; food like cabbage or beans improves, creating that temptation
C. Food is always more expensive than water
D. There is no such distinction in Rabbi Yehuda's view
Correct: B — Rabbi Yehuda's rule hinges on shrivel-and-improve versus shrivel-and-deteriorate: food that would benefit from more heat is forbidden due to the risk of stoking coals.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
Why did Rabbi Yosei forbid the shriveled eggs left on the stove at Tzippori but not the hot water?
A. Eggs are always forbidden on Shabbat
B. Shriveled eggs actually improve with more cooking, creating concern that someone would stoke the coals
C. The water had already cooled down
D. Eggs cannot be reheated according to Torah law
Correct: B — The Gemara explains that shriveled eggs shrivel-and-improve (as shown by Rav Chama bar Chanina's story of enjoying such eggs as a guest), unlike hot water.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
What does Rav Oshaya's story about carrying a kettle to Rabbi Chiyya the Great demonstrate?
A. That returning a pot is forbidden under all circumstances
B. That Beit Hillel's 'even return' leniency applies even to returning a pot during Shabbat itself, not only beforehand
C. That kettles may never be moved on Shabbat
D. That only priests may pour hot water on Shabbat
Correct: B — Rav Oshaya cites this incident to support Rav Sheshet's view that the mishna's permission to return a pot applies even when done on Shabbat itself.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to the new mishna, what is the status of a kupach lit with pomace or wood?
A. It is treated like a stove and food may be placed on top
B. It is treated like an oven, so even leaning a pot against it is forbidden
C. It has no restrictions at all
D. It is only used for baking bread, never for pots
Correct: B — A kupach lit with pomace or wood takes on the stricter status of an oven, where even leaning a pot against it is prohibited.
