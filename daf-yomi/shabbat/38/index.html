📖 Overview: Forgotten Pots, Returned Pots, and a New Kind of Fire

Shabbat 38a–38b
הַמְבַשֵּׁל בְּשַׁבָּת, בְּשׁוֹגֵג — יֹאכַל, בְּמֵזִיד — לֹא יֹאכַל
The scene: The daf opens with the question left hanging at the end of the previous one: if a pot was forgotten on the stove and cooked through on Shabbat, may its food be eaten? The Gemara works through two readings of a terse ruling, a later penalty enacted against people who falsely claimed to have 'forgotten,' and a story about Rabbi Yosei's differing rulings on hot water versus shriveled eggs. It then asks whether Beit Hillel's permission to 'return' a pot applies even during Shabbat itself, before a new mishna introduces two more heat sources — the oven (tanur) and the intermediate kupach — each with its own rules.

🍲 The Forgotten Pot: Two Ways to Read 'It Is No Different'

A sage's terse ruling sparks a fundamental debate
❓ One who cooks on Shabbat: unwitting may eat, intentional may not — 'and it is no different.' No different from what?
↓
Rabba & Rav Yosef (lenient)
Only actively cooking is judged by intent. One who merely forgot a pot performed no act at all — even if he left it intentionally, he may still eat
Rav Nachman bar Yitzchak (strict)
A cook has no reason to deceive, so unwitting cooking is always fine. But a 'forgetter' might be lying — even genuine forgetting becomes forbidden
↓
🤔 A baraita is cited that fits neither view cleanly — the Gemara leaves the difficulty standing: קַשְׁיָא

🥚 Rabbi Yosei's Visit to Tzippori

Hot water permitted, shriveled eggs forbidden — and why
בֵּיצִים מְצוּמָּקוֹת שֶׁנִּשְׁתַּהוּ עַל גַּבֵּי כִּירָה, וְאָסַר לָהֶן
The incident: When Rabbi Yosei visited Tzippori, he found hot water left on the stove and did not forbid it, but he found eggs shriveled from overcooking left on the stove — and he forbade them. The Gemara initially wonders if he was ruling out eating them that Shabbat, but concludes he was only warning against doing so the following Shabbat. The reason for the distinction: shriveled eggs actually improve the longer they cook, as Rav Chama bar Chanina recalled eating many delicious shriveled eggs, the size of crab apples, as a guest of Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi — so leaving eggs risks someone stoking the coals for a better result.

🔄 Does 'Even Return' Include Returning on Shabbat Itself?

Beit Hillel's leniency tested against real practice
Rav Sheshet
When the mishna says Beit Hillel 'even permit returning' a pot, that includes returning it during Shabbat itself — not only while it is still Friday.
Rav Oshaya
Once we stood before Rabbi Chiyya the Great on Shabbat, carried a kettle of hot water up a floor, poured him a cup, and returned it to its place — and he said nothing to us.
R. Tadai
But this is only permitted while the pot is still in one's hand; once set down on the ground, returning it is forbidden.
R. Chiyya/R. Yochanan
Not so — even a pot already placed on the ground may still be returned to the stove.
Left unresolved: Later amora'im further debate whether original intent to return makes the difference, and dilemmas about hanging a pot on a stick or transferring between urns are left as teiku, unresolved.

🔥 New Mishna: Stove, Oven, and the In-Between Kupach

Three heat sources, three different levels of restriction

Stove (Keirah)

  • Discussed in the prior mishna
  • Placing food on top is permitted once swept/ashed
  • Moderate heat

Kupach (small stove)

  • Lit with straw/rakings: treated like a stove — leaning permitted, placing on top forbidden
  • Lit with pomace/wood: treated like an oven — even leaning is forbidden
  • Rav Ashi: heat greater than a stove's, less than an oven's

Oven (Tanur)

  • Nothing may be placed inside or on top, even when lit with quick-burning straw
  • Even leaning against it is forbidden
  • The most intense heat of the three
Sizing rule: Rabbi Yosei bar Chanina: a kupach holds space for one pot; a stove holds space for two — which is also why, for ritual purity, a broken stove split lengthwise stays pure but split widthwise remains two functioning (impure) stoves, while a broken kupach is always pure either way.
