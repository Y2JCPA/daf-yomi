QUESTION 1 OF 5
Why is a Chanukah lamp placed above twenty cubits invalid?
A. Because oil burns poorly at that height
B. Because, like an invalid sukka roof or alleyway beam, people do not ordinarily raise their eyes above twenty cubits
C. Because Roman law forbade it
D. Because the mishna requires the lamp to be at eye level exactly
Correct: B — The Gemara compares the twenty-cubit limit to the identical standard for a sukka's roofing and an alleyway's symbolic beam — objects above that height are not usually noticed.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to the halakha reached on this daf, on which side of the doorway should the Chanukah lamp be placed?
A. On the right, opposite the mezuza
B. On the left, so the lamp and the mezuza flank the person entering
C. Directly above the doorway
D. Wherever is most convenient
Correct: B — The accepted halakha follows Rav Shmuel of Difti: place the lamp on the left, with the mezuza on the right, so that a person entering is surrounded by mitzvot.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
How does Rav Yosef defend the ruling that one may not count money by the light of the Chanukah lamp, against Shmuel's objection that the lamp has no sanctity?
A. By citing an explicit verse forbidding it
B. By comparing it to the law of covering the blood of a slaughtered animal, which also lacks formal sanctity but may not be treated with contempt
C. By arguing the Chanukah lamp is fully consecrated like Temple vessels
D. He concedes Shmuel is correct
Correct: B — Rav Yosef argues from the blood-covering law — an act that, though the blood has no sanctity, must not be done disrespectfully — establishing the principle that mitzva objects should not be treated with contempt even without formal sanctity.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Abaye's summary, in how many matters did Rabba rule like Shmuel rather than like Rav, and what are they?
A. Two: lighting lamp-to-lamp and tzitzit only
B. Three: lighting lamp-to-lamp, moving tzitzit between garments, and the law of dragging (gerira) per Rabbi Shimon
C. Four, including a ruling about sukka decorations
D. None — Rabba always followed Rav
Correct: B — Abaye reports that in all other matters Rabba followed Rav, except these three: lighting from lamp to lamp, untying tzitzit from one garment to another, and following Rabbi Shimon's lenient ruling on dragging.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
What problem does the Temple menorah's western lamp raise for the dispute over whether lighting or placing accomplishes the Chanukah mitzva?
A. It proves placing always accomplishes the mitzva with no difficulty
B. Since the Temple lamps were fixed in place, priests seemingly had to light lamp-to-lamp, which is difficult for the view that this 'weakens' the mitzva
C. It has no bearing on the discussion at all
D. It proves that only priests may perform this mitzva
Correct: B — Since the menorah's lamps couldn't be removed to light one from another with direct contact, priests needed a wood chip or long wick to light lamp-to-lamp — creating a difficulty for the opinion that lighting one Chanukah lamp from another 'weakens' the mitzva, a difficulty the Gemara leaves unresolved (kashya).
