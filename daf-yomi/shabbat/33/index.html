📖 Overview: National Sins and the Cave of Rabbi Shimon bar Yoḥai

Shabbat 33a–33b
כׇּל הֵיכָא דַּהֲוָה מָחֵי רַבִּי אֶלְעָזָר, הֲוָה מַסֵּי רַבִּי שִׁמְעוֹן
The scene: The daf continues its catalogue of sin-and-punishment pairings — perverted justice, false oaths and desecrating Shabbat, bloodshed, forbidden relations and idolatry, neglecting the Sabbatical year, and vulgar speech — each traced to a specific verse of rebuke, and each showing that societal sins bring national catastrophe: sword, wild beasts, exile, destruction of the Temple. The Gemara then catalogues bodily signs of sin (hidrokan, jaundice, poverty, askara) before turning to its most famous narrative: Rabbi Yehuda praised Rome's public works, Rabbi Yosei stayed silent, and Rabbi Shimon bar Yoḥai denounced them as self-serving — a comment that reached the authorities and cost Rabbi Shimon a death sentence. He and his son Rabbi Elazar fled to a cave, sustained for twelve years by a miraculous carob tree and spring while immersed in Torah study. Emerging too harshly critical of ordinary working people, they were sent back for another year, and finally reconciled to the world through an old man's simple devotion to Shabbat with two myrtle branches.

🩹 Reading the Body for Signs of Sin

The Sages diagnose spiritual causes for physical ailments
Baraita
Four signs: hidrokan (edema) for sin, jaundice for baseless hatred, poverty for arrogance, and askara (throat disease) for slander.
Objection
But women, gentiles, and children also get askara — none are obligated in Torah study, so how can dereliction in Torah be its cause?
Reply
Each still causes idleness from Torah: wives distract husbands, gentiles distract Israel, children distract their fathers.
Final challenge
But schoolchildren, who do study, also get askara!
R' Guryon
When there are no righteous adults in the generation, schoolchildren are 'seized' for the generation's sins instead — proven from Song of Songs 1:8's image of lambs held as collateral for negligent shepherds.

🕳️ Twelve Years in the Cave

Rabbi Shimon bar Yoḥai and his son Rabbi Elazar in hiding
1️⃣ They hide first in the study hall, fed daily by Rabbi Shimon's wife, then flee to a cave when the danger grows — fearing she might be tortured into revealing them
↓
2️⃣ A miracle provides a carob tree and spring; they bury themselves in sand to spare their clothing and study Torah all day, dressing only to pray
↓
3️⃣ After twelve years, Elijah announces the emperor's death and the decree's end — but emerging, they see ordinary people plowing and burn what they look upon in disdain for 'abandoning eternal life'
↓
4️⃣ A Divine Voice sends them back for twelve more months: 'Did you come out to destroy My world? Return to your cave!'
↓
5️⃣ Emerging again, an old man running with two myrtle branches for Shabbat — one for 'Remember,' one for 'Observe' — restores their peace of mind about ordinary people's devotion

🌳 Gratitude After the Miracle

Rabbi Shimon repairs what troubled the town, following Jacob's example
וַיָּבֹא יַעֲקֹב שָׁלֵם... וַיִּחַן אֶת פְּנֵי הָעִיר
The model: After the cave, Rabbi Shimon's son-in-law Rabbi Pineḥas ben Ya'ir found him physically marked by years of hardship, yet Rabbi Shimon insisted the ordeal had sharpened his Torah — where once Rabbi Pineḥas answered his questions with twelve resolutions, now Rabbi Shimon answered his with twenty-four. Reasoning from Jacob, who came 'whole' to Shechem and then 'graced the city' with civic improvements, Rabbi Shimon resolved that since a miracle had been done for him, he too should repair something for the community's benefit — asking whether there was a problem needing his attention.
