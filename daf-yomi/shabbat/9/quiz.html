Shabbat 9 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to Rava's distinction, when does carrying an object through an exempt domain like a threshold escape Torah liability?
A. Whenever the object is food
B. Only when the object actually comes to rest in the exempt domain, splitting the transfer into two separate acts
C. Never — it is always liable
D. Only on Friday afternoon
Correct: B — If the object merely passes through the exempt domain's airspace without landing, the transfer remains one continuous act and he is liable; only an actual landing there breaks it into two non-liable halves.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to Rav Yehuda citing Rav, what kind of threshold is Acherim's ruling (open door = 'inside') describing?
A. The threshold of the Temple
B. The threshold of an alleyway, half covered toward the inside and half uncovered
C. Any threshold whatsoever, without qualification
D. A threshold in the public domain only
Correct: B — Rav Yehuda explains that when the door is open, the covering's edge acts as a partition sealing the alleyway threshold as 'inside'; when locked, that sealing effect disappears.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
Why does Rabbi Meir forbid resting a burden atop a ten-handbreadth, four-wide pillar standing INSIDE a private domain, even though the pillar is itself a private domain by measurement?
A. Because pillars are always forbidden on Shabbat
B. As a rabbinic decree, modeled on a mound of the same size in the public domain, where the same act would be a genuine Torah violation
C. Because the pillar lacks a roof
D. Because only priests may use pillars
Correct: B — The Sages forbade it lest people confuse this permitted case with the genuinely forbidden case of lifting an object onto a similarly sized mound in the public domain, which constitutes real carrying from public to private domain.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to the mishna on 9b, which of these activities may one NOT begin close to the time of mincha, until he has prayed?
A. Only eating a meal
B. Sitting before the barber, entering the bathhouse or tannery, eating, and sitting in judgment
C. Only sitting in judgment
D. Only entering the bathhouse
Correct: B — The mishna lists all five: the barber, the bathhouse, the tannery, eating, and judgment — none may be begun close to mincha, though if already begun, one need not stop.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to the mishna, what is the rule about interrupting one of these activities to pray once it has already begun?
A. One must always stop for both Shema and the Amida
B. One stops to recite Shema, but does not stop to recite the Amida prayer
C. One never stops for any prayer once an activity has begun
D. One stops only for the Amida, not for Shema
Correct: B — The mishna states the principle explicitly: people interrupt these activities for Shema, but not for the Amida prayer.
