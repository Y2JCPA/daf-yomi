Temurah 32 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
What did Rav Huna rule about an animal consecrated for the altar that its owner then pledged as a dedication to the priests?
A. The dedication takes effect and he must pay its value
B. He has done nothing — the pledge is ineffective
C. The animal must be buried
D. The priests may choose whether to accept it
Correct: B — Rav Huna read 'every dedicated item is most holy' (Lev. 27:28) to mean that only what is already most holy can be dedicated. The Gemara ultimately declares this a conclusive refutation.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
How does Ulla reinterpret the phrase 'every dedicated item is most holy'?
A. As barring the sale of dedicated property
B. As teaching that one is liable for me'ilah on a dedication to the priests
C. As requiring the priests to be from the serving watch
D. As permitting redemption of blemished animals
Correct: B — Since Rav Huna's reading is refuted, Ulla assigns the phrase a different job. The Gemara defends the need for it by noting that even 'most holy' items required separate verses to bring them under me'ilah.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to Ulla, what by Torah law prevents a burnt offering consecrated for Temple maintenance from being sacrificed?
A. It must first be redeemed by appraisal
B. Nothing at all — it may be sacrificed immediately
C. Only 'the delay of the treasurers', since the treasurer is now the owner who must stand over it
D. It may never be sacrificed and must be buried
Correct: C — By Torah law a burnt offering cannot be consecrated by value. The rule requiring redemption before slaughter is rabbinic, as shown by the baraita's own concession that if he slaughtered it first, what he did is done.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
On Rabbi Yoḥanan's account of the Rabbis, what happens to an animal consecrated for the altar that died before being redeemed?
A. It is redeemed like any other
B. It is buried without redemption, since it was included in standing and valuation
C. It is given to the priests
D. It is redeemed only if it was blemished from the outset
Correct: B — Rabbi Yoḥanan holds the Rabbis included both altar and maintenance animals in standing and valuation, and what cannot be stood and valued cannot be redeemed. Reish Lakish limits that to maintenance animals.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
Why does the baraita reject reading Leviticus 27:11–12 as referring to a non-kosher animal?
A. Non-kosher animals can never be consecrated
B. Because Leviticus 27:27 already states separately that a non-kosher animal is redeemed according to valuation
C. Because the priest may not appraise a non-kosher animal
D. Because the verse mentions 'an offering unto the Lord'
Correct: B — The later verse already covers non-kosher animals, so the earlier one would be redundant on that reading. It is therefore taken to address blemished consecrated animals.
