QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to the Gemara's conclusion, whose opinion does Rabbi Hoshaya's ruling (two sin offerings as a guarantee — the unused one grazes) actually represent?
A. The Rabbis, since it's obviously redundant for them
B. Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi (Rebbi), whose stringent 'dies' rule applies only to true replacement-for-loss, not mere guarantee-insurance
C. Rabbi Shimon, who treats all cases unconditionally
D. No named authority — it stands alone
Correct: B — After testing against the Rabbis (redundant) and Rabbi Shimon (contradicted, since he applies 'dies' unconditionally), the Gemara concludes Rabbi Hoshaya represents Rebbi's own distinction between genuine loss-replacement and mere guarantee.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
What logical tool does Rav Pappa use to correct Rav Shimi bar Ziri's baraita about timing (lost at designation vs. lost at atonement)?
A. A verbal analogy (gezeirah shava)
B. An a fortiori inference (kal vachomer) — the lenient position in a harder case must also apply in an easier case
C. A tradition from Sinai
D. A simple textual emendation with no logical basis
Correct: B — Rav Pappa uses a kal vachomer: since the Rabbis are lenient (grazes) even in the more severe 'lost at designation' case, they cannot be strict (dies) in the less severe 'lost at atonement' case — forcing a correction to unanimous agreement there.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to Beit Shammai, why is it forbidden to flay a firstborn animal 'from the feet,' even when it is blemished?
A. Because it's always forbidden to slaughter any animal on any day
B. Because a blemished firstborn retains real sanctity (an Israelite may not even share it with a priest), so labor-like handling remains forbidden
C. Because firstborns can never be blemished
D. Because only priests may touch firstborn animals
Correct: B — Beit Shammai hold that a firstborn's sanctity persists even after it becomes blemished, evidenced by their ruling that an Israelite cannot share ownership of it with a priest — making labor-like flaying still prohibited.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to the new mishna, what happens if a designated fetus is born as TWO males (with 'if male, a burnt offering' having been declared)?
A. Both are sacrificed as burnt offerings
B. One is sacrificed as a burnt offering; the other is sold to someone else who needs a burnt offering, and its sale proceeds are non-sacred
C. Both are left to die
D. Neither is sacrificed
Correct: B — Since only the original condition ('if male') can apply to one specific offering, one male is sacrificed as a burnt offering while the second is sold to another owner in need of one, with the money becoming non-sacred.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to Rav Yehuda, when is it permitted to inflict a blemish on a firstborn fetus before it is born?
A. Only when the Temple stands
B. Only nowadays, when the Temple does not stand and firstborns cannot be sacrificed regardless
C. Never, under any circumstances
D. Only if the mother is also blemished
Correct: B — Rav Yehuda limits his permissive ruling to the present era without a Temple, where a firstborn can only ever be eaten once blemished anyway — distinguishing it from Temple times, when the mishna's designation-based artifice (not blemishing) is required instead.
