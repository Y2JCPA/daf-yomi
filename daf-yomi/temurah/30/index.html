📖 Overview: What May Not Come Onto the Altar

Temurah 30a–30b
לֹא תָבִיא אֶתְנַן זוֹנָה וּמְחִיר כֶּלֶב בֵּית ה׳ אֱלֹהֶיךָ לְכׇל נֶדֶר — כִּי תוֹעֲבַת ה׳ אֱלֹהֶיךָ גַּם שְׁנֵיהֶם
The scene: Deuteronomy 23:19 bars two things from the Temple: etnan zonah, an animal given to a prostitute as her fee, and mechir kelev, an animal given in exchange for a dog. The daf works out the precise boundaries of both. It asks who counts as a prostitute, whether 'price' means money or barter, how the ban spreads when a dog is bundled into a division of property, and — the chapter's sharpest question — whether grinding the wheat into flour launders it. Along the way it settles what happens to the offspring of a disqualified animal and even to its dung.

👰 Is a Widow Paid by a High Priest an 'Etnan'?

Why the baraita chose that case rather than the obvious one
Gemara
The baraita follows Rabbi Elazar, who holds that an unmarried man who has relations with an unmarried woman not for marriage has made her a prostitute — so her fee is barred.
Gemara
But if so, why cite the widow paid by a High Priest? Let it simply teach the unmarried case, which is the paradigm!
Answer
Precisely because you might have reasoned the opposite way: the unmarried case is only a rabbinic-level impropriety, so that is where the ban belongs; but where the union is forbidden by Torah law, perhaps the fee is too remote to be called an etnan.
Answer
So the baraita teaches the harder case: even a Torah-forbidden union — a High Priest with a widow — produces an etnan that may not come onto the altar.
Abaye
Abaye, by contrast, holds like the Rabbis, who disagree with Rabbi Elazar about the unmarried couple entirely.
Principle: The prohibition tracks the payment, not the severity of the sin — a forbidden relationship does not exempt the fee.

🐑 Nineteen Lambs and One Dog

The mishna's case of two partners dividing their common property
🤝 Partners hold 19 lambs and 1 dog in common, then divide
↓
Partner A
Takes 10 lambs — received in exchange for the other's 9 lambs and the dog
Partner B
Takes 9 lambs + the dog — nothing was exchanged for a dog
↓
❓ Why are all ten barred? Just set one lamb aside against the dog and the other nine are clean!
↓
💡 The case is where the dog is worth more than any single lamb. No one lamb can absorb it, so the dog's value is spread across all ten — and every one of them is tainted.
Note: 'Price' (מְחִיר) here means an item bartered, not money — proven from Psalms 44:13, 'You sell Your people without wealth, and have not set their prices high.'

🔢 'Both of Them' — Two Are Barred, Not Four

The verse names exactly two categories, and the count is doing real work
CaseStatusReason
A lamb given to a prostitute as her fee🚫 BarredEtnan zonah — stated explicitly
A lamb given in exchange for a dog🚫 BarredMechir kelev — stated explicitly
A lamb paid for relations with one's dog✅ PermittedThere is no 'prostitution' with an animal
A lamb paid as the price of a prostitute, to buy her as a maidservant✅ PermittedThat is a purchase price, not a fee for services
The offspring of a barred animal✅ Permitted'Them' — but not their offspring
The derivation: Had the verse meant only two loosely, it could have written 'the payment of a prostitute or a dog'. Writing payment of one and price of the other shows they are two distinct mechanisms — and 'both of them' caps the list at two.

🌾 Does Physical Change Launder the Payment?

He gave her wheat and she milled it; olives and she pressed them; grapes and she made wine

Beit Hillel — Permitted

  • 'Them' — but not their offspring
  • 'Them' — but not their changed form
  • The milling makes a new object, outside the ban
  • Difficulty: they have no good reading of the word 'even' (גַּם)

Beit Shammai — Prohibited

  • They too derive 'them, but not their offspring'
  • But the extra word 'even' (גַּם) includes the changed form
  • Flour, oil and wine stay barred

Two Other Readings of the Verse

  • Rabbi Elazar: 'into the House of the Lord' excludes the red heifer, slaughtered outside on the Mount of Olives
  • The Rabbis: it includes beaten gold plates for the Temple
  • Rabbi Yosei bar Yehuda: not even for the space behind the Ark Cover
Rav Yosef's source: Guryon of Aspork taught the baraita as an explicit Beit Shammai / Beit Hillel dispute, which resolves the two contradictory baraitot.

🍼 How Far Does the Taint Spread?

Offspring, dung, and the fetus in the womb
1️⃣ Rava: an animal that was the object of bestiality while pregnant — its offspring is barred too, as though mother and young were both used
↓
2️⃣ Same for a pregnant cow that gored and killed — it is as if both gored
↓
3️⃣ But offspring of a set-aside or idol-worshipped animal are permitted: only the mother was set aside or worshipped (a second version reverses this — the extra girth benefits the owner)
↓
4️⃣ Rav Aḥadevoi bar Ami in Rav's name: betrothal with the dung of a stoned ox works; with the dung of idolatrous calves it does not
↓
5️⃣ Why? Extra girth flatters an idol's calf, so its dung is part of the offering ('anything you produce from it'); a stoned ox gains its owner nothing, and only 'its flesh' is forbidden (Ex. 21:28)
The mishna's dispute: Rabbi Eliezer bars the offspring of a tereifa; Rabbi Ḥanina ben Antigonus disqualifies even a kosher animal that suckled from one. Rav Naḥman and Rava then argue over exactly which case Rabbi Eliezer and the Rabbis are fighting about — conceived-then-used, or used-then-conceived.

📋 Temurah 30 — Summary Map

📜 'You shall not bring the payment of a prostitute or the price of a dog… for both of them are an abomination'
↓
🔢 'Both of them' = two categories, not four → payment for relations with a dog, and the price paid for a prostitute herself, are permitted
↓
🐑 Dog worth more than any one lamb → its value spreads over all ten, barring them all
🌾 Wheat milled into flour → Beit Hillel permit ('them, not their changed form'), Beit Shammai bar it ('even')
↓
🍼 Offspring generally permitted — except where mother and fetus are treated as one, as in bestiality or goring during pregnancy
↓
🎓 Key Principle: a consecrated animal cannot become an etnan — 'for any vow' excludes it, since one cannot prohibit what is not his
