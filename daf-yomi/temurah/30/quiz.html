Temurah 30 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to the mishna, which of these IS permitted to be sacrificed on the altar?
A. A lamb given to a prostitute as her fee
B. A lamb given in exchange for a dog
C. A lamb given as the price to purchase a prostitute as a maidservant
D. Flour a prostitute milled from wheat she was paid, according to Beit Shammai
Correct: C — The verse's 'both of them' limits the ban to two categories. Payment for relations with a dog, and the purchase price of a prostitute herself, fall outside it and are permitted.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
Why are all ten lambs barred when partners split nineteen lambs and a dog?
A. Because a dog can never be owned in partnership
B. Because the dog is worth more than any single lamb, so its value is spread across all ten
C. Because the Rabbis penalized the partner who took the larger share
D. Because the lambs were consecrated before the division
Correct: B — If one lamb could be set aside against the dog, the other nine would be clean. The Gemara answers that the case is where the dog outvalues each individual lamb, so no single lamb absorbs it.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
What is the dispute between Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel on this daf?
A. Whether a widow paid by a High Priest creates an etnan
B. Whether the offspring of a tereifa may be sacrificed
C. Whether payment that has physically changed form — wheat milled into flour — remains barred
D. Whether 'price' in the verse means money or barter
Correct: C — Beit Hillel read 'them' to exclude the changed form and permit the flour, oil or wine; Beit Shammai read the extra word 'even' as including it. Rav Yosef cites Guryon of Aspork for this attribution.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
Why may one betroth a woman with the dung of an ox sentenced to stoning, but not with the dung of calves used for idol worship?
A. The stoned ox's dung is worth less than a peruta
B. Extra girth flatters an idolatrous calf so its dung is part of the offering, while a stoned ox's girth benefits its owner not at all
C. Idolatrous calves are never actually stoned
D. Dung is always permitted; the case concerns the animals themselves
Correct: B — The Gemara gives both a logical reason (the owner gains from a fatter idol, not from a fatter condemned ox) and a scriptural one ('anything you produce from it' for idolatry, versus 'its flesh shall not be eaten' for the stoned ox).
QUESTION 5 OF 5
How can a consecrated animal ever become an etnan, given that one cannot prohibit what is not his?
A. It cannot, under any circumstance
B. Rav Hoshaya: where he registers the prostitute for his Paschal offering, following Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi that a Paschal offering counts as personal property for registration
C. Where the animal was consecrated after being given to her
D. Where the owner later redeems the animal
Correct: B — Rav Hoshaya explains the case as registering her for a share in his Paschal offering. Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi holds one may 'sustain himself from the lamb', so the money received for registering others is non-sacred and the transaction is his to make.
