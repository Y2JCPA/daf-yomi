QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to the new mishna, what is the key difference between an individual's sin offering and a communal sin offering when the owner already achieved atonement via a replacement?
A. Both are left to die
B. The individual's offering is left to die; the communal offering is not (Rabbi Yehuda dissents)
C. Neither is ever left to die
D. Only the communal offering is left to die
Correct: B — The mishna rules that an individual's sin offering in this situation is left to die, while a communal sin offering is not — though Rabbi Yehuda holds even a communal offering should die.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
How does the Gemara resolve the apparent contradiction between the mishna (Rabbi Yehuda: dies) and a baraita (seemingly Rabbi Yehuda: graze) about the recovered sin offering?
A. It concludes one of the sources must be a forgery
B. It distinguishes the timing of when the original animal was found (before sacrifice of the replacement vs. still lost at the time of atonement), or attributes the difference to two versions of Rabbi Yehuda's view
C. It says Rabbi Yehuda never actually existed
D. It simply ignores the contradiction
Correct: B — The Gemara offers two resolutions: distinguishing whether the original animal was found before or after the replacement's sacrifice, or attributing the difference to Rabbi Yehuda's view aligning with either Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi or the Rabbis in a related dispute.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to Rav Pappa, why doesn't the 'owner died' rule apply to communal sin offerings, even though individual contributors inevitably die before the offering is sacrificed?
A. Because communal offerings are never actually sacrificed
B. Because a community itself does not 'die,' unlike an individual owner
C. Because the Temple treasury refunds all contributions
D. Because only priests contribute to communal offerings
Correct: B — Rav Pappa's principle, proven from the ongoing use of treasury-chamber funds for Festival and New Moon goat offerings despite the deaths of some contributors, is that a community as a collective entity cannot 'die.'
QUESTION 4 OF 5
How does Rabbi Yoḥanan explain the sin offerings described in Ezra 8:35, brought generations after the sin of idolatry under King Zedekiah, given that idolatry was committed intentionally?
A. He says the verse is not really describing sin offerings
B. He explains it was a הוֹרָאַת שָׁעָה — a provisional emergency ruling permitting sin offerings even for an intentional sin
C. He says only unintentional idolaters were included
D. He denies the offerings were ever brought
Correct: B — Rabbi Yoḥanan resolves the difficulty (sin offerings normally atone only for unwitting sin) by explaining this was a one-time provisional ruling (horaat sha'ah) suited to the exceptional circumstances of the return from exile.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
In the story of the pious man and the goat, what turned out to be his only transgression?
A. Idol worship
B. Keeping a goat in his house in violation of the Sages' ban on raising small livestock in inhabited Eretz Yisrael
C. Failing to pray
D. Theft
Correct: B — Despite his friends' initial alarm at seeing the goat (fearing it signaled something sinister), a thorough examination — confirmed by the man himself on his deathbed — found his only sin was keeping the goat, violating the rabbinic prohibition against raising small livestock in settled areas of Eretz Yisrael.
