QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi, why does he disagree with Rabbi Shimon specifically regarding a female designated for a PASCHAL offering (but not a guilt offering)?
A. He disagrees with Rabbi Shimon on both cases equally
B. A leftover Paschal offering becomes an individual peace offering (open to females), giving the mother real sanctity; a leftover guilt offering funds only communal gifts, which can't generate temurah anyway
C. Paschal offerings are always male
D. He agrees fully with Rabbi Shimon on everything
Correct: B — Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi distinguishes the two cases based on what the 'leftover' offering becomes: a peace offering (individual, female-eligible) for Pesach, versus communal gifts (never eligible for temurah) for a guilt offering.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
Why doesn't a king's female goat sin offering count as having 'sin offering' status when an individual designates a male goat instead of his own required female?
A. Because kings never sin
B. Because a king and an ordinary individual are considered 'two distinct bodies' — one's offering type cannot transfer status to the other's mismatched case
C. Because goats can never be sin offerings
D. Because the Gemara rejects the entire premise
Correct: B — The Gemara explains that offering-status must belong to a case that could genuinely apply to the SAME person's situation; a king's male goat and an individual's female goat belong to different categories of people and cannot cross-validate each other.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to Rabbi Yehoshua's opinion on consecrating all one's possessions, what happens to males that are fit for sacrifice?
A. They are sold for Temple maintenance only
B. They are sacrificed as burnt offerings themselves
C. They are always left to graze
D. They are given to the poor
Correct: B — Rabbi Yehoshua holds that since burnt-offering intent is assumed by default, fit males are sacrificed directly as burnt offerings, while females (which can't be burnt offerings) are sold to fund burnt offerings.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to the new mishna, what is Rabbi Elazar's position on the money from selling a guilt offering's substitute-line (once blemished)?
A. The money is simply forfeited
B. The money funds an INDIVIDUAL burnt offering, with the owner's semikha, libations, and (if a priest) hide rights
C. The money funds a communal gift offering, like the Rabbis say
D. The animals are never sold at all
Correct: B — Rabbi Elazar holds the proceeds fund an individual burnt offering — distinct from the Rabbis' view of a communal gift offering, which lacks semikha, personal libations, and individual hide/service rights.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
What two objections does Rava raise against the first version of Rav Naḥman's statement (that before atonement, everyone agrees the offspring can be sacrificed directly)?
A. There are no objections; Rava agrees fully
B. One cannot achieve atonement via an item from a transgression, and a supporting baraita shows only the FIRST offspring (not this 'second-generation' one) is ever sacrificed
C. Rava says the mishna doesn't exist
D. Rava says only priests can raise this question
Correct: B — Rava objects that the substitute's offspring stems from a forbidden act (disqualifying it from achieving atonement) and that Rav Ḥananya's baraita limiting sacrifice to only the first offspring contradicts sacrificing this twice-removed offspring.
