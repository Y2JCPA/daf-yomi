📖 Overview: Individual Offerings, Communal Offerings, and a Digression on Writing

Temurah 14a–14b
קׇרְבְּנוֹת הַיָּחִיד עוֹשִׂין תְּמוּרָה, וְאֵין קׇרְבְּנוֹת הַצִּיבּוּר עוֹשִׂין תְּמוּרָה
The scene: A new mishna catalogs the structural differences between offerings brought by an individual and those brought by the community: individual offerings generate temurah, may be male or female, and must be replaced if missed; communal offerings do neither, but override Shabbat and impurity if their time is fixed (Rabbi Meir refines this last point). The Gemara tests each general statement against counter-examples — birds, offspring, substitutes, and various offering-types restricted to one sex — resolving each by reading the mishna's claims narrowly. A discussion of when 'missed' communal offerings and their libations may still be brought triggers a lengthy digression on whether halakha may ever be written down, before the daf returns to carefully parsing Numbers 29:39, phrase by phrase, to identify which offerings each clause is teaching about — including a story about Avshalom's nazirite vow.

⚖️ New Mishna: Individual vs. Communal Offerings

Each type has powers the other lacks
FeatureIndividual offeringsCommunal offerings
Generate temurah?YesNo
Male or female?Both permittedMales only
Must be replaced if missed?Yes — owner is liable to bring it laterNo — the day passes and it's gone (but libations already begun must still be completed)
Override Shabbat/impurity?No (unless time-fixed, per R' Meir)Yes
Rabbi Meir's refinement: The real distinction isn't individual vs. communal at all — it's whether the offering's TIME is fixed. The High Priest's daily griddle-cakes and the Yom Kippur bull are individual offerings, yet they too override Shabbat and impurity.

🐦 Does EVERY Individual Offering Make Temurah?

The Gemara tests the mishna's opening claim against three exceptions
❓ Birds are individual offerings, yet they never make temurah!
↓
💬 Answer: the mishna means only animal offerings, not birds
↓
❓ An offspring is an individual offering, yet (per the Rabbis) it doesn't make temurah!
↓
💬 Answer: this mishna follows Rabbi Yehuda, who holds offspring DOES make temurah
↓
❓ A substitute itself is an individual offering, yet it can't generate a further substitute!
↓
✅ Final answer: the mishna refers only to the PRIMARY offering — now even the Rabbis' view works too
Method: Each challenge narrows the mishna's scope rather than overturning it — a pattern seen throughout the tractate.

♀️♂️ Does EVERY Individual Offering Allow Both Sexes?

Counter-examples and how each is reconciled
Offering typeApparent problemResolution
Burnt offering (olah)Only male animals allowed — never femaleThere IS a female option: the bird burnt offering, which has no male/unblemished requirement
Sin offering (chatat)Only female animals allowed — never maleThere IS a male option: the king's goat sin offering (שְׂעִיר נָשִׂיא)
Guilt offering (asham)Only male allowed, and no communal parallel existsThe mishna's rule applies only to offerings shared by both individuals AND the community — guilt offering has no communal form
Alternative resolution—The mishna means only SOME individual offerings (e.g., peace offerings) allow either sex — not a universal rule
Recurring lesson: Broad mishnaic statements in this tractate consistently turn out to describe the typical case, not an exceptionless rule.

✍️ Can Oral Torah Be Written Down?

A digression sparked by a debate over sending halakha in a letter
עֵת לַעֲשׂוֹת לַה׳ הֵפֵרוּ תּוֹרָתֶךָ
The story: Rav Dimi wanted to send Rav Yosef a ruling about libations in a letter, but the Gemara objects: Rabbi Yoḥanan taught that 'those who write down halakhot are like those who burn the Torah' — the Oral Law must stay oral, just as the Written Law must stay written (derived from Exodus 34:27, and from the school of Rabbi Yishmael's reading of 'these words'). Yet the Gemara notes that Rabbi Yoḥanan and Reish Lakish themselves read from a written scroll of aggada on Shabbat, justified by the verse 'It is time to act for the Lord — they have violated Your Torah' (Psalms 119:126): sometimes one rule must yield so the Torah as a whole is not forgotten. New material, in particular, may need to be written down precisely because memory alone cannot preserve it.

📜 Parsing Numbers 29:39, Phrase by Phrase

Rav Dimi (citing Rabbi Yoḥanan) matches each clause to a distinct category of offering
1️⃣ 'These you shall offer... in your appointed seasons' — the OBLIGATORY Festival offerings (appearance, Festival, additional)
↓
2️⃣ 'Beside your vows and voluntary offerings' — teaches these may be sacrificed on the intermediate days (chol hamoed)
↓
3️⃣ 'And your burnt offerings' (not already covered) — must mean the new mother's and the leper's burnt offerings
↓
4️⃣ 'And your meal offerings' (not already covered) — must mean the sota's meal offering of jealousy
↓
5️⃣ 'And your libations and peace offerings' — libations follow peace offerings: day only; 'peace offerings' includes the NAZIRITE's peace offering (Abaye argues it should instead mean the Paschal-overflow peace offering)
Resolved via Avshalom: Abaye's challenge about whether a nazirite offering counts as 'vowed/voluntary' is settled by identifying Avshalom's Hebron trip (II Samuel 15:7-8) as fulfilling a genuine nazirite vow — private altars were then still permitted, so he could sacrifice anywhere he chose.
