Temurah 23 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to Rav Huna's framing, what determines the Rebbi/Rabbis dispute?
A. Which animal was born first
B. Whether the owner consulted the court before sacrificing one of the two available animals, versus just taking one and sacrificing it without asking
C. The color of the animals
D. Whether the animals are male or female
Correct: B — Rav Huna holds that everyone agrees an animal taken and sacrificed without consulting the court causes the other to die; the dispute is only about what the court should advise when consulted.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to Rabbi Abba's framing, what determines the Rebbi/Rabbis dispute?
A. Whether the animal was female
B. Whether atonement was achieved specifically through the LOST animal (Rebbi says the replacement then also dies) or through the non-lost one (agreed by all: the lost one dies)
C. The value of the animals
D. Whether the owner was a priest
Correct: B — Rabbi Abba holds everyone agrees atonement via the non-lost animal causes the lost one to die; the dispute is only about achieving atonement via the ORIGINALLY lost animal, and whether the replacement is then treated as 'the lost one' too.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
How does the Gemara resolve the challenge that the mishna's own wording seems to refute BOTH Rav Huna's and Rabbi Abba's framings?
A. It concludes both framings are wrong and rejects them entirely
B. It explains the mishna teaches only the absolute, unconditional case (sacrifice causes death) and simply omits the more nuanced disputed scenarios
C. It says the mishna text is corrupted
D. It says only Rav Huna survives the challenge
Correct: B — The Gemara's resolution is that a mishna need not spell out every disputed nuance — here it teaches only the clear-cut case where the replacement WAS sacrificed, leaving the more contested scenarios for other sources to address.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Rabbi Ami, what happens to unused money that was designated merely 'as a guarantee' (not to replace something already lost)?
A. It must be cast into the Dead Sea
B. It is allocated for communal gift offerings — even according to Rabbi Shimon's generally stricter view
C. It can never be used for any purpose
D. It automatically becomes the property of the priests
Correct: B — Rabbi Ami teaches that even Rabbi Shimon, who is generally stringent about atonement disqualifying the other resource, concedes that mere guarantee-money (not replacing a genuine loss) funds communal gift offerings when unused.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
What Temple detail does the Gemara cite to prove that Rabbi Shimon does hold surplus sin/guilt-offering funds can become communal gift offerings (נְדָבָה)?
A. The design of the Temple menorah
B. The thirteen collection horns in the Temple, six of which were designated for communal gift offerings — supported by Yehoyada the priest's teaching on 'it is a guilt offering'
C. The layout of the Holy of Holies
D. The schedule of priestly watches
Correct: B — The Gemara cites the mishna about the Temple's thirteen shofar-shaped collection horns, six of which funded communal gift offerings from surplus sin/guilt-offering money, defended by Yehoyada's own teaching that such surplus should fund burnt offerings.
