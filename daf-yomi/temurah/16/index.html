📖 Overview: What Was Lost, What Was Restored, and a New Mishna

Temurah 16a–16b
אַרְבָּעָה נִתְּנוּ לָהֶן, וְהֶעֱמִידוּם עַל חָמֵשׁ
The scene: The daf continues its meditation on forgotten Torah: three thousand halakhot, and later seventeen hundred a fortiori inferences and verbal analogies, were lost during the mourning for Moses — the latter restored only through Otniel ben Kenaz's brilliant reasoning, celebrated in the story of his marriage to Caleb's daughter Achsah. The Gemara then returns to halakha, working out exactly how Rabbi Shimon's five 'sin offerings left to die' were transmitted at Sinai — a dispute between Reish Lakish (four given directly, a fifth added by analogy) and Rabbi Natan (only one given directly, four added by analogy) — and why three of the five could never apply to a communal offering to begin with. The daf closes by opening a new mishna: the comparative stringencies between consecrated animals and their substitutes, in both directions.

😢 Grief, Forgetting, and a Refusal to Ask Heaven

Joshua, then Samuel, decline heavenly guidance to recover lost halakhot
1️⃣ 3,000 halakhot were forgotten during the mourning period for Moses. The people ask Joshua to seek heavenly guidance to recover them
↓
2️⃣ Joshua refuses: 'לֹא בַשָּׁמַיִם הִיא' — 'It is not in Heaven' (Deut. 30:12) — halakha is no longer determined by prophecy
↓
3️⃣ Later, before his own death, Moses offers to resolve Joshua's doubts — but Joshua, having 'never left the tent,' says he has none, and immediately loses 300 halakhot and gains 700 new uncertainties
↓
4️⃣ God tells Joshua the halakhot cannot be restored — instead, distract the people with war (the invasion of Canaan) so they forget their anger at him
Later loss: A further 1,700 a fortiori inferences (kal vachomer), verbal analogies (gezeirot shavot), and scribal fine points were also forgotten during Moses' mourning.

💡 Otniel ben Kenaz Recovers What Was Lost

Sharp reasoning succeeds where mourning could not
וַיִּלְכְּדָהּ עׇתְנִיאֵל בֶּן קְנַז... וַיִּתֶּן לוֹ אֶת עַכְסָה בִתּוֹ לְאִשָּׁה
The story: Rabbi Abbahu teaches that Otniel ben Kenaz restored the 1,700 forgotten teachings through sheer intellectual sharpness (פִּלְפּוּלוֹ), reading the conquest of 'Kiryat Sefer' — 'the city of the book' — as a metaphor for reclaiming lost Torah. Caleb rewarded him with his daughter Achsah's hand. The Gemara then unpacks Achsah's own request for 'upper and lower springs' (Joshua 15:19): she asks her father not for wealth but recognizes her husband, devoted purely to Torah, cannot provide it — so Caleb gives fertile fields instead. Otniel/Jabez's prayer (I Chronicles 4:10) is read by Rabbi Natan as a plea for Torah, students, retained learning, worthy friends, and freedom from the evil inclination — and by Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi as a more literal prayer for health, children, and livelihood.

🔢 How Many 'Dying' Sin Offerings Came From Sinai?

Reish Lakish and Rabbi Natan disagree on the transmission
Reish Lakish
FOUR sin-offering cases were given directly at Sinai as 'left to die.' A fifth was also meant to be included, but since the Sages forgot WHICH one, they applied 'dies' to all five, for safety.
Rabbi Natan
No — only ONE case was given directly as 'dies.' The other four were meant to merely GRAZE, but since it was forgotten which was which, all five were treated as dying.
Shared logic
Either way: since the directly-given cases include ones that structurally CANNOT apply to a community (offspring, temurah, owner-death), the transmission must have been about individual offerings only.
Conclusion
This is how Rabbi Shimon justifies applying the rule to the two ambiguous cases (atonement-by-replacement, year passed) as individual-only too — 'learn the unspecified from the specified.'
Why it matters: Both opinions reach the same halakhic outcome by different routes — but disagree sharply on how much was originally clear versus reconstructed.

📋 Why Three of Rabbi Shimon's Five Can't Be Communal

Structural impossibilities vs. genuinely ambiguous cases
CaseWhy it can't (or might) apply to a community
Offspring of a sin offeringCommunities never bring FEMALE sin offerings — so there's no communal offspring case at all
Substitute (temurah) of a sin offeringA community cannot perform temurah in the first place
Owner died'A community does not die' (established earlier, daf 15)
Atonement achieved via a replacementNo structural block — genuinely ambiguous, resolved by analogy to the other three
The offering's first year passedAlso ambiguous — resolved the same way
The logical move: Since three cases are unambiguously individual-only, and all five were transmitted together as one halakhic unit, the remaining two must follow the same pattern.

⚖️ New Mishna: Kodashim vs. Temurah — Mutual Stringencies

Each side is stricter than the other in different ways

Consecrated Animals Stricter

  • Consecrated animals generate a substitute; a substitute cannot generate another
  • Community/partners CAN consecrate animals, but CANNOT perform temurah on them
  • One can consecrate fetuses and limbs directly, but cannot substitute for them

Temurah Stricter

  • A non-sacred animal with a PERMANENT blemish, substituted for an unblemished offering, receives full inherent sanctity
  • Ordinary consecration cannot confer that level of sanctity on a permanently blemished animal
  • (Continues onto the next daf with further stringencies)
The pattern for the chapter ahead: Temurah and ordinary consecration aren't simply 'stricter' or 'more lenient' than each other across the board — each has its own unique severities the other lacks.
