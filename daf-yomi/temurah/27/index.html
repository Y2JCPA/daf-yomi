📖 Overview: Reading Intent Into Ambiguous Words and Actions

Temurah 27a–27b
מִי אָמַר לְאַתְפּוֹסֵי וְלָקֵי, אוֹ דִלְמָא כֹּל הֵיכָא דְּאִיכָּא הֶיתֵּרָא לָא שָׁבֵיק אִינִישׁ הֶיתֵּרָא וְעָבֵיד אִיסּוּרָא
The scene: Building on yesterday's discovery that 'תַּחַת' can mean either substitution or mere redemption, Rabbi Ashi supplies a physical test: where the owner's hand rests when he speaks determines which he meant. Abaye and Rav Ashi then push this ambiguity through an escalating series of dilemmas involving mixed groups of blemished and unblemished, sacred and non-sacred animals — testing whether people default to the permitted reading (redemption) or the forbidden one (illegal substitution), with the answer shifting once a pattern of three prior violations is established. A separate debate between Rabbi Yoḥanan and Reish Lakish over whether making up a value gap when desacralizing a blemished animal is Torah law or rabbinic law spirals through several reversed attributions before resolving. Ulla then rules on how appraisals by two versus three assessors affect later reassessment. The daf — and Chapter Five — closes with a mishna cataloging exactly which substitution declarations are valid and which are empty.

🖐️ Two Meanings of 'תַּחַת,' Settled by the Hand

Rava and Rabbi Ashi refine when the same word means association vs. desacralization
ScenarioMeaning of 'תַּחַת'Reasoning
Unblemished altar-offering, standard caseAssociation (substitution)'the leprous spot remains in its place' (Lev. 13:23) — the original sanctity stays, and a new one joins it
Bedek habayit (Temple upkeep) itemDesacralizationNo temurah applies to bedek habayit at all — 'in place of brass I will bring gold' (Isaiah 60:17) fits replacement instead
Blemished altar-offering (Rava's case)DesacralizationEven where temurah COULD technically apply, this phrasing can still mean simple redemption
Hand resting on the CONSECRATED animal (Rabbi Ashi)DesacralizationTouching the sacred animal signals intent to release ITS sanctity
Hand resting on the NON-SACRED animal (Rabbi Ashi)Association (substitution)Touching the non-sacred animal signals intent to consecrate IT
The takeaway: Context — and even physical gesture — can resolve what would otherwise be genuinely ambiguous language.

🐐 Escalating Dilemmas: Which Animal Goes With Which?

Abaye and Rav Ashi test the principle 'one doesn't abandon the permitted path for a forbidden one'
1️⃣ Two blemished sacred animals, two unblemished non-sacred animals: 'these are in place of those' — does he mean illegal SUBSTITUTION (2 lashes) or legal DESACRALIZATION (no lashes)? Both readings are grammatically possible
↓
2️⃣ Mixed sets (one blemished, one unblemished on each side): does he pair 'unblemished-with-unblemished' (permitted) and 'blemished-with-blemished' (forbidden separately), or cross-pair in the worse way?
↓
3️⃣ Larger sets (3-for-3, then 4-for-4): once TWO unblemished pairings are clearly intentional substitution, does that establish a 'pattern of transgression' that makes the THIRD (blemished) pairing ALSO presumed intentional?
↓
4️⃣ Rav Ashi's culminating case: with THREE confirmed unblemished substitutions, is the person now 'established' as a transgressor, so the fourth (blemished) pairing follows suit — or does the preference for a permitted reading persist even then?
Resolution: Every one of these dilemmas is left as תֵּיקוּ — the law never firmly resolves how far the presumption of innocence extends once a pattern starts to emerge.

💰 Must the Value Gap Be Made Up by Torah Law?

Rabbi Yoḥanan and Reish Lakish, reversed and re-reversed
Rabbi Yoḥanan
Desacralization works by TORAH law even with a huge value gap; making up the DIFFERENCE is only a RABBINIC requirement (to protect the Temple treasury).
Reish Lakish
Even making up the difference is required by TORAH law.
Challenge
But a known mishna lists consecrated property among things EXEMPT from the laws of exploitation (ona'ah) — suggesting no Torah-level value requirement at all!
Resolution attempts
Several proposed fixes follow: distinguishing 'exploitation' (a small gap) from 'nullified sale' (a large gap); reversing which sage said what; and finally, Rav Ḥisda's reading — 'exempt from exploitation' actually means MORE strict, not less, since even a tiny gap requires repayment for consecrated property.
Where it lands: The final version keeps Rabbi Yoḥanan and Reish Lakish as originally stated, defending the 'exempt from exploitation' mishna via Rav Ḥisda's stricter reading rather than reversing anyone's position.

⚖️ Ulla: Does a Later, Higher Appraisal Ever Matter?

The number of assessors changes everything

Assessed by TWO people

  • Not the halakhically required number
  • If three OTHER people later assess it higher, the owner MUST pay the difference

Assessed by THREE people (proper)

  • The halakhically correct process was followed
  • Even if a hundred people LATER assess it higher, the owner owes NOTHING further
  • Explained: this whole requirement is only RABBINIC, and the Sages were lenient once the proper process was completed
Countered but resolved: Objections (from testimony rules, and 'the Temple always has the advantage') are answered by confirming this entire appraisal requirement is rabbinic, not Torah law — matching Rabbi Yoḥanan's position from the prior debate.

📜 New Mishna: Precise Words for a Valid Substitution

Chapter Five closes with the exact phrasing that does — and doesn't — work
StatementEffect
'This is in place of A burnt offering' (no specific animal named)Says NOTHING — too vague to specify a substitute for
'This is in place of THIS sin offering' / 'THIS burnt offering'Valid — specific enough
'This is in place of a sin/burnt offering I have in the house' (and he really has one)Valid — specific enough via reference
Non-kosher or blemished animal declared 'THIS IS a burnt offering'Says NOTHING — such an animal can't BE an offering itself
Non-kosher or blemished animal declared 'THIS IS FOR a burnt offering'Valid — sold, proceeds buy a real burnt offering
Rav's clarification: This mishna follows neither Rabbi Meir (who'd read even the vague statement charitably as referring to a specific animal) nor Rabbi Shimon (who allows selling a mismatched female without waiting for a blemish) — it reflects the more literal, majority position.
