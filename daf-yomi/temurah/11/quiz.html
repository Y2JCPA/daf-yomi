Temurah 11 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to Rabbi Zeira's final answer, which limbs does even Rabbi Yehuda concede spread sanctity to the entire animal?
A. Any limb whatsoever
B. Only limbs whose removal would render the animal a tereifa (vital organs)
C. Only the legs
D. No limbs — Rabbi Yehuda never concedes
Correct: B — Rabbi Zeira explains that Rabbi Yehuda's rule that limb-consecration doesn't spread applies only to non-vital limbs; for a limb whose removal would make the animal a tereifa, even Rabbi Yehuda agrees the sanctity extends throughout.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to the discussion of Rabbi Eliezer's list (kilayim, tereifa, tumtum, androginos, etc.), how does Shmuel explain 'neither sacred nor sanctify'?
A. These animals can never be eaten
B. They cannot become a substitute, nor can a substitute be made for them
C. They are automatically non-sacred forever
D. They can only be sacrificed as guilt offerings
Correct: B — Shmuel clarifies that Rabbi Eliezer's statement is specifically about temurah: these animals cannot serve as, or receive, a valid substitute — not that they can never have any sanctity at all.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
After the extended debate, to what specific case does the bar Padda/Rabbi Yoḥanan dispute ultimately narrow?
A. Whether any animal at all can be consecrated
B. An unblemished fetus in the womb of a blemished mother
C. Whether priests can consecrate animals
D. Whether temurah applies to birds
Correct: B — Abaye clarifies that everyone agrees an unblemished fetus in an unblemished mother is consecrated; the true dispute is only about an unblemished fetus inside a blemished mother, where bar Padda ties the fetus's status to its mother's and Rabbi Yoḥanan treats it as a separate body.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Rabbi Yosei and Rabbi Shimon, what verse teaches that consecrating one limb sanctifies the entire animal?
A. "He shall not exchange it"
B. "All that any man gives of it to the Lord SHALL BE SACRED" (Lev. 27:9) — the extra phrase includes the whole animal
C. "Both it and its substitute shall be holy"
D. There is no verse; it's pure logic
Correct: B — Rabbi Yosei and Rabbi Shimon read the seemingly redundant phrase 'shall be sacred' in Leviticus 27:9 as expanding the consecration to the entire animal, not merely the limb explicitly given.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to Rava's explanation to Abaye, why is it prohibited (by rabbinic law) to use for labor an animal whose fetus alone was consecrated, while shearing it remains permitted?
A. Both are equally forbidden by Torah law
B. Labor weakens the animal and reduces the fetus's value, which the Sages decreed against; shearing doesn't weaken it, so it was never restricted
C. Shearing is always forbidden for any consecrated item
D. There is no difference between the two activities
Correct: B — Rava explains the Sages specifically prohibited activities like labor that actively diminish value through weakening, while non-weakening activities like shearing were left permitted.
