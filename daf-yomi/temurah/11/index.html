📖 Overview: The Limits of Partial Consecration

Temurah 11a–11b
רַגְלָהּ שֶׁל זוֹ עוֹלָה — כּוּלָּהּ עוֹלָה
The scene: The daf finishes the bar Padda–Rabbi Yoḥanan dispute over fetal sanctity, narrowing their true disagreement to an unblemished fetus inside a blemished mother. A new thread opens with Rabbi Eliezer's list of animals — hybrids, a tereifa, one born by caesarean, a tumtum, a hermaphrodite — that are 'neither sacred nor sanctify,' sharpened by Shmuel to mean specifically that they cannot serve as or receive a substitute. The mishna's other dispute, between Rabbi Yosei and the Rabbis over whether consecrating a single limb sanctifies the whole animal, is traced to competing readings of Leviticus 27:9. The daf closes with a cluster of Rava's dilemmas — about birds, and about a limb consecrated purely for its value — and a practical discussion with Abaye about shearing wool or working an animal only partially consecrated.

Even a die-hard defense of bar Padda has its limits
❓ R' Yirmeya: if this mishna follows Rabbi Yehuda, doesn't Rabbi Yehuda deny that consecrating a leg sanctifies the whole animal? Then how can consecrated limbs ever spread sanctity?
↓
💬 R' Zeira: we're speaking here of a limb whose removal would make the animal a tereifa (fatally injured) — even Rabbi Yehuda concedes THIS extends to the whole animal

Narrowed dispute: Ultimately Abaye pins the bar Padda/R' Yoḥanan dispute down to one precise case: an unblemished fetus inside a blemished mother — is the fetus a separate 'body' that can still receive inherent sanctity?

🦵 Does Consecrating One Leg Sanctify the Whole Animal?

Rabbi Yosei and Rabbi Shimon vs. Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yehuda
R' Yosei/Shimon
'All that any man gives of it to the Lord shall be sacred' (Lev. 27:9) — the extra phrase 'shall be sacred' includes the ENTIRE animal, not just the limb given.
R' Meir/Yehuda
No — 'anything OF IT that one gives to the Lord, it shall be sacred' — only the part actually given is sacred, 'of it,' not all of it.
Practical outcome
For R' Meir/Yehuda: the animal is sold to fund burnt offerings, with all proceeds non-sacred except the value attributable to the consecrated limb.
Rava clarifies
That sale works only to a buyer who vowed to bring a burnt offering using only the animal's vital limbs — since the consecrated limb need not be among them, the sale isn't invalidated.
Underlying agreement: Even Rabbi Yehuda concedes that consecrating a limb whose removal would kill the animal (or make it a tereifa/neveila) DOES spread sanctity to the whole body.

❓ Rava's Chain of Unresolved Dilemmas

Each new case tests how far 'one limb sanctifies all' can be pushed
1️⃣ A bird fit for sacrifice: does Rabbi Yosei's rule (verse says 'behema,' an animal) apply, or does 'korban' cover it too? — Teiku
↓
2️⃣ One consecrates a limb purely for its value (not as an offering): does inherent sanctity (kedushat haguf) spread to the whole animal too, layering two expansions at once? — Teiku
↓
3️⃣ Attempted resolution: Rava elsewhere says consecrating a male animal 'for its value' still gives it inherent sanctity automatically — so shouldn't a single limb work the same way?
↓
4️⃣ Rejected: consecrating the WHOLE animal for value is different from consecrating just ONE limb for value — the dilemma stands
Pattern: Rava repeatedly probes the outer edge of 'partial consecration spreading further' — and repeatedly the Gemara leaves the answer open.

🐑 How Far Does Partial Consecration Restrict the Animal?

Abaye questions Rava about shearing wool and using the animal for labor

Shearing (Gizah)

  • One limb consecrated (R' Yehuda: sanctity doesn't spread)
  • Rava: compare to a jointly-owned firstborn, which may be sheared
  • Rejected — here sanctity DID take hold on that limb, unlike a gentile-owned share
  • Conclusion left tentative

Labor (Avoda)

  • Consecrating only the skin — may the animal still work?
  • Compared to a consecrated fetus: mother may be sheared but not worked (labor weakens/reduces value)
  • Abaye: is that prohibition Torah law or rabbinic?
  • Rava: only rabbinic — shearing (non-weakening) was never restricted, but labor (weakening) was
Underlying logic: The Sages restrict activities that would actively diminish the value of the consecrated part, even by rabbinic decree, while permitting those that don't.
