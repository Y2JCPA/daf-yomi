📖 Overview: Closing Chapter One — the Boundaries of Temurah

Temurah 13a–13b
יָחִיד עוֹשֶׂה תְּמוּרָה, וְאֵין הַצִּבּוּר וְהַשּׁוּתָּפִין עוֹשִׂין תְּמוּרָה
The scene: The Gemara supplies verse sources for the mishna's rules that a substitute cannot itself generate a further substitute, and that an animal's offspring cannot become one either (Rabbi Yehuda dissents on the latter). A new mishna then draws the outer boundaries of temurah altogether: birds and meal offerings cannot generate substitutes (the verse says only 'animal'), nor can animals owned by the community or by partners, nor items consecrated for Temple maintenance (bedek habayit) — only an individual's own altar-offering can. The Gemara traces each exclusion to its verse, debates whether bedek habayit even counts as a 'korban,' and explores the special double substitution unique to the animal tithe (temurat shmo vs. temurat gufo). The daf — and the chapter — closes with a question about whether a voluntary burnt offering counts as an 'obligatory' offering for purposes of temurah.

🕊️ New Mishna: Who and What CAN'T Make a Substitute

Only an individual's own altar-offering generates temurah

Cannot Make Temurah

  • Birds (doves/pigeons) — verse says only 'בְּהֵמָה,' an animal
  • Meal offerings — same reason
  • Community-owned animals — 'לֹא יַחֲלִיפֶנּוּ' implies a single, individual owner
  • Partnership-owned animals — same reason
  • Bedek habayit (Temple maintenance) items

CAN Make Temurah

  • An individual's own animal altar-offering
  • Rabbi Shimon's proof: the animal tithe (מַעֲשֵׂר) models the pattern — individual, altar-bound, obligatory, not in partnership
Rabbi Shimon's method: Since the animal tithe is singled out for its own substitution verse, its four traits (individual/altar/obligatory/non-partnership) define exactly which offerings the general rule covers.

❓ Is Bedek HaBayit Called a 'Korban'?

Two baraitot seem to contradict each other
Baraita 1
'קׇרְבָּן' in the temurah verse refers only to what is CALLED a korban — excluding bedek habayit, which is not called a korban.
Objection
But another baraita treats bedek habayit AS a korban — deriving from 'entrance of the Tent of Meeting' that only items fit for the altar are excluded from the offering-outside-Temple prohibition, implying bedek habayit otherwise would count!
Rabbi Ḥanina resolves
No contradiction: the first baraita follows the Rabbis (bedek habayit is NOT called korban); the second follows Rabbi Shimon (it IS called korban, though excluded from temurah via the ma'aser juxtaposition instead).
Further precision
Even for the Rabbis, bedek habayit IS called 'the Lord's offering' (קׇרְבַּן ה'), just never 'an offering TO the Lord' (קׇרְבָּן לַה') — the phrase reserved for altar offerings.
Takeaway: Whether bedek habayit is excluded from temurah by the word 'korban' or by analogy to the animal tithe depends entirely on which tanna you follow.

🔀 The Animal Tithe's Double Substitution

Temurat Shmo (substitute of its name) vs. Temurat Gufo (substitute of its body)
FeatureTemurat Shmo (miscounted animal)Temurat Gufo (ordinary substitute)
Sacrificed on the altar?Yes — as a peace offeringNo — never sacrificed
Redeemed if blemished?Yes, proceeds to Temple treasuryNo — 'it shall not be redeemed'
Takes effect on unfit (blemished) animals?No — only on a fit animalYes — even on an unfit one
Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi's point: Since 'temurat shmo' (a counting error making the 10th and 11th animals both sacred) is unique to the tithe, the Torah had to separately confirm that ordinary temurah (temurat gufo) applies to the tithe too.

💰 Does a Voluntary Burnt Offering Count as 'Obligatory'?

Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak challenges Rava on Rabbi Shimon's requirement
1️⃣ Rabbi Shimon requires temurah-eligible offerings to be 'דָּבָר שֶׁבָּא בְּחוֹבָה' — something obligatory. Does that exclude a voluntary burnt offering?
↓
2️⃣ Rava: no — once a person accepts a voluntary offering upon himself, it becomes his personal obligation, and DOES generate temurah
↓
3️⃣ What Rabbi Shimon's rule really excludes: a burnt offering purchased from LEFTOVER funds (e.g., money remaining after buying a sin/guilt offering)
↓
4️⃣ This connects back to Rabbi Avin's earlier unresolved dilemma (9b–10a): does repeat substitution work on an animal now reclassified via leftover funds? Depends on whose position (R' Eliezer vs. R' Shimon) applies — left as an open question (תִּיבְּעֵי)
Chapter ends here: הֲדַרַן עֲלָךְ הַכֹּל מְמִירִין — 'We will return to you, [chapter] Everyone Substitutes.' This marks the close of the tractate's first chapter.
