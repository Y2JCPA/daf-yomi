Temurah 3 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
How does the Gemara leave Rami bar Ḥama's dilemma about a gentile-consecrated, Jewish-atoned offering?
A. It rules that the consecrator always controls substitution
B. It rules that the one achieving atonement always controls substitution
C. It is left unresolved as teiku
D. It rules the animal may never be sacrificed
Correct: C — The Gemara explicitly concludes 'תֵּיקוּ' — the question of whether continuous ownership by someone capable of substitution is required remains unresolved.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
By what level of law is a gentile's consecrated animal exempt from the prohibition of misuse (me'ilah)?
A. It is not exempt at all
B. By rabbinic law only
C. By Torah law, via a 'sin'-'sin' verbal analogy to teruma
D. Only if the gentile converts
Correct: C — The Gemara states that deriving benefit ab initio is forbidden only rabbinically, while liability for misuse after the fact is waived by Torah law, derived by verbal analogy from teruma's verse naming 'the children of Israel.'
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to Rabbi Yosei, for which consecrated items of a gentile IS one liable for misuse?
A. None — he agrees fully with Rabbi Shimon
B. Only altar offerings (kodshei mizbe'ach)
C. Only items consecrated for Temple maintenance (kodshei bedek habayit)
D. Only libations
Correct: C — Rabbi Yosei distinguishes: the 'sin'-'sin' exemption applies only to items with inherent bodily sanctity like teruma (altar offerings); items whose sanctity is monetary (bedek habayit) remain subject to misuse liability.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Rav (via Rav Yehuda), what is the general rule about lashes for violating a Torah prohibition?
A. Lashes always apply regardless of action
B. Lashes apply only if the transgression involved a physical action; without an action, one is exempt
C. Lashes never apply to prohibitions
D. Lashes apply only to positive commandments
Correct: B — Rav's rule is that a prohibition transgressed through action incurs lashes, while one transgressed without any action does not — subject to specific named exceptions.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
Which three transgressions did Rabbi Yosei HaGelili list as exceptions where lashes apply even without a physical action?
A. Theft, murder, and idolatry
B. Taking an oath, effecting substitution (temurah), and cursing another using God's name
C. Eating pig, mixing wool and linen, and working on Shabbat
D. Not eating matzah, not sitting in a sukkah, and not hearing the shofar
Correct: B — Rabbi Yosei HaGelili's list (via Rabbi Yoḥanan) names one who swears falsely, one who effects temurah, and one who curses another with God's name — though Rabbi Yoḥanan later says temurah should not really be listed, since speech itself constitutes an act there.
