Temurah 2 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to Rav Yehuda's resolution of the mishna's apparent contradiction, what does 'everyone substitutes' mean?
A. It is fully permitted to substitute
B. Everyone can apply consecrated status to a non-sacred animal via substitution, though it is forbidden and punished
C. Only priests may substitute
D. Substitution never actually takes effect
Correct: B — Rav Yehuda reads the mishna as describing the outcome (the status takes effect) while the act itself remains forbidden and flogged, resolving the apparent contradiction with the following clause.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
What verse does the Gemara cite to show that substitution is forbidden?
A. "You shall not muzzle an ox"
B. "He shall not alter it, nor substitute it" (Leviticus 27:10)
C. "An eye for an eye"
D. "You shall not boil a kid in its mother's milk"
Correct: B — Leviticus 27:10, 'לֹא יַחֲלִיפֶנּוּ וְלֹא יָמִיר אֹתוֹ', explicitly prohibits substitution, which is why 'everyone substitutes' cannot mean it is permitted ab initio.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
What is Rabbi Yehuda's reasoning for ruling that an inheritor cannot effect substitution?
A. He derives the beginning of consecration (temurah) from its end (semikha): just as an heir does not place hands, he cannot substitute
B. He says women cannot inherit sacrifices
C. He holds inheritors are exempt from all mitzvot
D. He derives it from the word 'ish ish'
Correct: A — Rabbi Yehuda reasons from the end of consecration (placing hands before slaughter, which an heir cannot do) to its beginning (substitution), concluding an heir cannot do either.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
How does Rabbi Meir derive that a woman is included in the prohibition against substitution?
A. From the doubled verb 'אִם הָמֵר יָמִיר'
B. From the extra conjunction 'וְאִם' ("and if")
C. From Numbers 5:6 alone
D. He does not include women at all
Correct: B — Rabbi Meir derives a woman's inclusion from the added 'and' (וְאִם), since he uses the doubled verb 'hamer yamir' instead to include the inheritor.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
What does the baraita that Rava cites conclude about a gentile who consecrates an animal?
A. A gentile's consecration is entirely void
B. A gentile effects substitution just like a Jew
C. Gentiles do not effect substitution (אֵין עוֹשִׂין תְּמוּרָה) for what they consecrate
D. Only Rabbi Yosei says gentiles cannot substitute
Correct: C — The baraita states plainly that consecrated animals of gentiles do not undergo temurah, which Rava brings as evidence resolving Rami bar Ḥama's dilemma in the case where the gentile both consecrates and seeks atonement himself.
