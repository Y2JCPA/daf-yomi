📖 Overview: A Mishna of Non-Propagating Sanctities

Temurah 12a–12b
וְאֵין תְּמוּרָה עוֹשָׂה תְּמוּרָה, וְלֹא הַוָּלָד עוֹשֶׂה תְּמוּרָה
The scene: A new mishna groups together a striking list of halakhot that share one structure: a status, once diluted or transferred, does not simply keep propagating at full strength into further mixtures — teruma mixed again only counts proportionally, leavening agent works only if enough survives, drawn water invalidates a ritual bath only by a threshold count, the red heifer's purifying water requires ash placed onto water (not the reverse), and a beit haperas (a plowed grave-field) does not contaminate a second field the same way. The mishna's final and most direct application to our tractate: a substitute animal does not itself generate a further substitute, and the offspring of a consecrated animal does not become a substitute either — a point Rabbi Yehuda disputes. The Gemara identifies which tanna each halakha follows and works through the reasoning behind several of them.

🌾 The Mishna's Chain: Six 'Non-Propagating' Halakhot

Each shares the same structure: a status doesn't simply spread at full strength
CaseRule
Teruma mixed, then mixed again elsewhereOnly according to the proportional calculation, not as full teruma
Dough leavened by teruma leaven, remixedOnly leavens the new batch according to calculation
Drawn water (3 log) added to a ritual bathInvalidates it only per a specific threshold, not automatically
Water of the red heifer (mei chatat)Becomes purifying water only when ash is placed ONTO water, not water onto ash
A beit haperas (plowed grave-field)Does not create a second beit haperas if plowed further
A substitute (temurah) — and an offspringDoes not itself generate another substitute; nor does an animal's offspring
Unifying theme: In every case, a derivative or diluted status stops propagating in full force — it does not automatically confer its own status on whatever it later touches.

⚖️ Teruma Falls Into a Mixture, Then Falls Again

Rabbi Eliezer vs. the Rabbis on a se'ah taken from an already-mixed batch
Case
A se'ah of teruma falls into fewer than 100 se'ah of non-sacred produce (not nullified). Then a se'ah is taken FROM that mixture and falls into yet more non-sacred produce.
Rabbi Eliezer
That se'ah renders the new batch forbidden as if it were pure teruma — perhaps the original teruma never truly dispersed and came out intact.
The Rabbis
No — it renders the new batch forbidden only according to the calculated proportion of real teruma actually present, assuming even distribution.
Ruling
Our mishna follows the Rabbis, not Rabbi Eliezer — diluted status carries forward only in proportion, not at full strength.
Same logic, different context: This is the very principle the mishna later applies to temurah: a derivative status (the substitute) does not carry the same generative power as the original.

💧 How Much Drawn Water Invalidates a Ritual Bath?

Three positions reconciled by Rabba
❓ The mishna says drawn water invalidates a mikveh 'only according to calculation' — what calculation?
↓
🅰️ R' Eliezer ben Yaakov: drawn water is purified by flowing WITH existing valid water — invalid only if drawn water is the majority
↓
🅱️ Difficulty: R' Yoḥanan elsewhere says water that flowed in, even entirely drawn, is pure — matching neither the Rabbis nor R' Eliezer ben Yaakov
↓
✅ Rabba's resolution: the calculation is of VESSELS — Yosef ben Ḥoni: 2-3 vessels of drawn water invalidate it, but spread across 4-5 vessels, it does not
Point: Even a small technical detail — how many vessels poured the water — determines whether the 'calculation' rule protects the mikveh's validity.

🌫️ Must the Water Go Into the Vessel First?

Rabbi Shimon derives a gezeirah shava between the red heifer and the sota
Mishna
If one places the ashes into the vessel before the water, the mixture is unfit for the red heifer's purification.
Rabbi Shimon
I deem it fit even so! The verse oddly calls the ashes 'עָפָר' (dust) — the same word used for the sota's dust, teaching a gezeirah shava.
Application
Just as for the sota, placing the dust first is valid after the fact (בְּדִיעֲבַד), so too here.
Our mishna's tanna
Reads the verse's final clause — 'running water in a vessel' — as specifying that the water must go in first; the earlier clause about ash 'placed on it' just means mixing thoroughly afterward.
General principle invoked: 'We find everywhere that the facilitating item goes on top' — supporting the requirement that water precede ash.

🔁 Can a Substitute — or an Offspring — Make Its Own Substitute?

The mishna's own halakhic contribution to the tractate

The Sages

  • A substitute animal does NOT generate a further substitute — it stays as it is
  • The offspring of a consecrated animal does NOT become a substitute either
  • Consecrated status itself can generate a substitute; nothing derivative from it can

Rabbi Yehuda

  • Agrees a substitute cannot make another substitute
  • But dissents on offspring: the offspring of a consecrated animal DOES render an exchanged animal a substitute
  • Position echoed in earlier sugyot (daf 10-11) discussing fetal/offspring sanctity
Why it fits the pattern: Like teruma-that-fell-twice or drawn water beyond its threshold, a substitute (or an offspring) is one step removed from the original act of consecration — and the Sages hold that removal caps its generative power.
