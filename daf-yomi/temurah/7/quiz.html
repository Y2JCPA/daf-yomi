QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to Rabbi Yoḥanan, why is one flogged for consecrating an animal that was blemished from birth, even though this differs from consecrating a mere date palm?
A. Because animals are always more valuable than plants
B. Because it is disgraceful (בִּזְיָא) — he had unblemished animals of the same kind available and chose a blemished one
C. Because date palms cannot be consecrated at all
D. Because the Torah never mentions blemished animals
Correct: B — Rabbi Yoḥanan explains that a date palm was never a candidate for the altar to begin with, but an animal blemished from birth is disgraceful specifically because unblemished animals of its kind exist and were passed over.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
In the dispute over burning a whole blemished offering vs. part of it, whose opinion does the Gemara ultimately uphold?
A. Abaye's — only one violation, since it's a general prohibition from one verse
B. Rava's — two distinct violations, since the Tosefta counts them separately
C. Neither — both are rejected
D. Rabbi Akiva's
Correct: B — The Gemara concludes תְּיוּבְתָּא (a conclusive refutation) of Abaye's position, based on the Tosefta's list treating 'burning all' and 'burning part' as two separate named violations.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to the new mishna, which offerings can a priest NOT substitute for?
A. Any offering whatsoever
B. A sin offering, a guilt offering, and a firstborn offering received from an Israelite
C. Only burnt offerings
D. Only offerings brought by women
Correct: B — The mishna states that priests do not effect substitution for a sin offering, guilt offering, or firstborn, since these come to them as gifts rather than through their own consecration.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
How does Rabbi Akiva prove that a priest cannot substitute for a firstborn he received, using temurah's own verse?
A. He cites a verse about teruma instead
B. 'Both it and its substitute shall be holy' implies sanctity must take hold in the house of the original owner — which for a firstborn is the Israelite, not the priest
C. He says priests can never perform any mitzva involving animals
D. He argues the firstborn is never truly consecrated
Correct: B — Rabbi Akiva reads 'הוּא וּתְמוּרָתוֹ' as requiring that the substitute be created by the same person in whose house the original consecration occurred — for a firstborn, that is the Israelite's house, not the priest's.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to Rav Naḥman, when does the halakha permitting sale of an unblemished firstborn while alive apply?
A. Only when the Temple stands
B. Only in the present era, when there is no Temple and the priest fully owns it
C. Never — it is always forbidden
D. Only to blemished firstborns
Correct: B — Rav Naḥman limits this ruling to the present, without a Temple, since when the Temple stood an unblemished firstborn was destined for the altar and could not be sold alive at all.
