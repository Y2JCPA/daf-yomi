QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to Rav Yitzḥak (via Rava bar Rav Adda), when does an animal set aside for idol worship (muktzeh) stop being prohibited for the altar?
A. It is always prohibited, permanently
B. Once it has been used for labor by idol-attendants, since it presumably won't be sacrificed to the idol afterward
C. The moment it is first set aside
D. Only after it dies of old age
Correct: B — Rav Yitzḥak holds that active use of the animal for labor by idol-attendants signals it will no longer be sacrificed to the idol, ending its muktzeh status — contrasted with Rabbi Yoḥanan's differing thresholds.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
Why does the Gemara conclude that a muktzeh or ne'evad animal must be PERMITTED for ordinary consumption, based on Ezekiel 45:15?
A. Because the verse says nothing about consumption
B. Because if they were already forbidden to eat, excluding them from the altar (via that same verse) would be obviously unnecessary to state — so the verse's exclusion only makes sense if they ARE permitted to eat
C. Because idol-worship animals are always kosher
D. Because the mishna states this explicitly
Correct: B — The logic is that a verse excluding these animals from sacrifice would be superfluous if they were already forbidden to eat entirely — its necessity implies they remain permitted for ordinary consumption, just barred from the altar.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to the new mishna, if a man agrees to pay a prostitute one lamb but actually gives her 100 lambs, what is the status of all 100?
A. Only the first lamb is forbidden; the rest are gifts
B. All 100 lambs are forbidden, since they all trace back to the same payment arrangement
C. None are forbidden since the amount exceeds the agreement
D. Only 50 are forbidden
Correct: B — The mishna teaches that even excess animals beyond the agreed fee are still considered part of the same forbidden payment (etnan), not treated as a separate gift.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Rabbi Elazar, why is a lamb NOT considered a prostitute's forbidden payment (etnan) if she sacrificed it before the encounter occurred?
A. Because sacrifice always nullifies any prior status
B. Because at the moment the forbidden act (or payment designation) occurs, the lamb no longer exists as an available object to serve as 'payment'
C. Because prostitutes cannot sacrifice animals
D. Because the lamb becomes non-sacred once sacrificed
Correct: B — Rabbi Elazar's principle is that etnan status requires the animal to still exist as the designated payment at the critical moment; having already been sacrificed, it can no longer retroactively become 'payment.'
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to Rava, in the dispute over whether payment to a gentile prostitute differs from payment to an unmarried Jewish prostitute, what is his position?
A. Only the gentile prostitute's payment is forbidden
B. Only the Jewish prostitute's payment is forbidden
C. There is no distinction — payment to EITHER is forbidden, since intercourse with both is forbidden to a priest by a Torah prohibition
D. Neither payment is ever forbidden
Correct: C — Rava rejects Abaye's distinction (based on whether betrothal can take effect), instead deriving both cases from their shared forbidden status for priests, concluding that both types of payment are equally prohibited as etnan.
