📖 Overview: The Boundaries of Etnan and Muktzeh

Temurah 29a–29b
אֵין מוּקְצֶה אָסוּר אֶלָּא עַד שֶׁיַּעַבְדוּ בּוֹ
The scene: The Gemara grounds the mishna's category of a muktzeh (idol-set-aside) animal in a verse about safeguarding offerings 'for Me' and not another master, then debates the precise moment such an animal stops being prohibited — whether it must be actively worked, handed to idolatrous priests, or fed 'vetches of idolatry.' A parallel discussion shows why a worshipped (ne'evad) animal remains permitted for ordinary consumption, and traces a separate, doubly-derived exclusion of a tereifa from the altar. A new mishna then defines a prostitute's payment (etnan) — even a hundred lambs given as one fee are all forbidden — before the Gemara works out exactly when payment and intercourse must occur relative to each other for the prohibition to apply, and finally debates whether payment to a gentile prostitute, an unmarried Jewish woman, or one's own menstruating wife falls under this prohibition at all.

📜 Muktzeh: Source and Expiration

When does 'set aside for idolatry' stop applying to an animal?
PositionMuktzeh ends when...Basis
Rav Yitzḥak (via Rava bar Rav Adda)It has been used for LABOR by idol-attendantsOnce worked, it won't be sacrificed to the idol afterward
Rabbi Yoḥanan (via Ulla)It has been HANDED OVER to idol-attendants for fatteningHandover alone signals it won't be sacrificed
Rabbi Yoḥanan (via Baha)It has been FED 'vetches of idolatry'A more specific marker of the animal's new, permanent status
Verse source (all agree)N/A'You shall safeguard it to sacrifice TO ME' (Num. 28:2) — 'to Me,' not to another master, i.e., not for idol worship
Abaye's clarifying challenge: The verse can't mean 'every offering needs careful handling' (a weak lamb would then be disqualified) — it specifically excludes offerings intended for AN IDOL, not poor treatment in general.

🐐 Why a Worshipped Animal Stays Edible, and the Tereifa Puzzle

Two separate derivations from the same broader passage
📖 'From the well-watered pastures of ISRAEL' (Ezekiel 45:15) — offerings come only from what's permitted TO an ordinary person
↓
💡 If muktzeh/ne'evad were ALREADY forbidden to eat, excluding them from the altar would be obvious — so the verse must mean they're actually PERMITTED to eat, yet still barred from the altar
↓
❓ But doesn't a tereifa (forbidden to eat) also need its own separate exclusion verse — even though 'forbidden to eat' should make altar-exclusion obvious?
↓
✅ Yes — because the extra verse teaches something NEW: even an animal that became a tereifa only AFTER being fit and consecrated (not just one born unfit) is still excluded — a genuine novelty requiring its own text
Layered necessity: Even after one verse excludes a tereifa, ANOTHER source (the animal-tithe 'passes under the rod' phrase) is needed to cover a tereifa that had a period of fitness before becoming disqualified — each layer closes a different loophole.

💰 New Mishna: What Counts as a Prostitute's Payment

Even scale doesn't matter, but intent does

Straightforward Case

  • 'Here is this lamb as your fee' — even if he actually hands over 100 lambs (having agreed to just one), ALL 100 count as payment and are forbidden
  • The novelty: extra animals aren't treated as a separate 'gift' — they all trace back to the same forbidden payment

The Maidservant Case

  • 'Take this lamb, and let your maidservant sleep with my slave' — is this 'payment for services' at all?
  • Rabbi Meir: NOT considered etnan (no human prostitution is technically occurring between the principals)
  • The Rabbis: IS considered etnan
Underlying theme: The category of etnan is defined by the TRANSACTION's structure and intent, not simply by the quantity of animals or the precise participants involved.

⏱️ When Must Payment and Intercourse Align?

Rabbi Elazar's timing rule, and Rav Oshaya's follow-up dilemma
1️⃣ Baraita: if payment was given but intercourse never happened, OR intercourse happened but payment was given only much LATER (unconnected), the animal is permitted — it doesn't retroactively become 'payment'
↓
2️⃣ Sharpened case: if he explicitly designated THIS lamb as her fee BEFORE intercourse, it IS forbidden — even if actually handed over later — since it was already earmarked
↓
3️⃣ Rabbi Elazar's resolution for a seeming exception: if the PROSTITUTE HERSELF already sacrificed the lamb (using her prior right to it) before the encounter occurred, it's not 'payment' at all — it no longer existed as such at the critical moment
↓
4️⃣ Rav Oshaya's follow-up: what if she only CONSECRATED it (not yet sacrificed) before the encounter — permitted, or still forbidden? Left unresolved (תֵּיקוּ), since consecration alone may or may not count as 'removing' it the same way sacrifice does
Underlying logic: 'Payment' requires the animal to genuinely belong to the transaction at the moment of the forbidden act — ownership technicalities (when exactly she acquires it) matter enormously.

⚖️ Whose Payment Counts as Etnan?

A four-way layered dispute over scope
Rav
Payment for ANY forbidden relation (including male-male) counts as etnan — EXCEPT payment to one's own menstruating wife, since the verse specifically says 'זוֹנָה' (a prostitute), and she isn't one.
Levi
Even payment to a menstruating wife IS forbidden — the verse also says 'תּוֹעֵבָה' (abomination), a term also used for forbidden relations like this one.
Abaye
Payment to a GENTILE prostitute is forbidden (derived from 'תּוֹעֵבָה,' linked to relations where betrothal can't take effect); payment to an UNMARRIED JEWISH prostitute is PERMITTED, since betrothal CAN take effect with her.
Rava
No distinction — payment to EITHER is forbidden, derived by analogy: since intercourse with either is forbidden to a priest by a Torah prohibition, both payments share the same forbidden status.
A key precision from Rava: Even for an unmarried woman, payment isn't automatically 'etnan' — the man must explicitly frame it as payment for services ('this lamb is for you'), just as certain forbidden relations require prior warning before liability applies.
