Temurah 10 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to the resolution of Rami bar Ḥama's dilemmas, when one person consecrates an animal for another's atonement, who performs temurah for it?
A. The consecrator
B. The one who achieves atonement with it
C. Neither can perform temurah
D. Both must agree together
Correct: B — Rabbi Abbahu, citing Rabbi Yoḥanan, rules that the one who achieves atonement (מִתְכַּפֵּר) — not the consecrator — is the one who can effect substitution, resolving the dilemma raised back on daf 2.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to Rabbi Yosei's dissenting view in the new mishna, in which case does substitution actually take effect?
A. Substituting a limb for a fetus
B. Substituting a non-sacred limb for a whole consecrated animal — the entire substitute becomes holy
C. Substituting a fetus for a limb
D. None of the cases work according to Rabbi Yosei
Correct: B — Rabbi Yosei holds that just as declaring an animal's leg a burnt offering consecrates the whole animal, declaring a non-sacred limb a substitute for a whole consecrated animal renders the entire substitute holy.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
What is the core dispute between bar Padda and Rabbi Yoḥanan?
A. Whether animals can be consecrated at all
B. Whether a fetus can be directly imbued with (inherent) sanctity when consecrated
C. Whether priests can perform temurah
D. Whether gentiles can consecrate animals
Correct: B — Bar Padda holds sanctity cannot take hold directly on a fetus; Rabbi Yoḥanan holds it can, citing the case of a consecrated pregnant sin offering where either the mother or the offspring can be brought for atonement.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
How does Rabbi Zeira defend bar Padda against the mishna about avoiding firstborn status (saying 'what's in the womb is a burnt offering')?
A. He says the mishna is incorrect
B. He explains it refers only to value-sanctity (kedushat damim), not inherent bodily sanctity
C. He says fetuses are never affected by that mishna
D. He agrees it refutes bar Padda
Correct: B — Rabbi Zeira consistently reinterprets each challenge as describing value-sanctity (sanctity of the fetus's monetary worth) rather than the inherent bodily sanctity that bar Padda denies can apply to a fetus.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to the mishna about animals blemished before consecration, what shows that value-sanctity (kedushat damim) alone is strong enough to have real legal force?
A. Nothing — value-sanctity has no legal force
B. Such animals are exempt from firstborn law and priestly gifts only until they are redeemed, after which those obligations apply
C. They are immediately eaten without restriction
D. They can never be redeemed
Correct: B — The mishna implies that before redemption, animals with only value-sanctity are exempt from firstborn status and gifts, proving that even this lesser sanctity is strong enough to override those obligations while it lasts.
