📖 Overview: Whose Offering Is It, and What Can Be Consecrated

Temurah 10a–10b
מַקְדִּישׁ מוֹסִיף חוֹמֶשׁ, וּמִתְכַּפֵּר עוֹשֶׂה תְּמוּרָה
The scene: The daf resolves two dilemmas of Rami bar Ḥama that trace back to the opening of the tractate: when one person consecrates an offering for another's atonement, the consecrator alone adds the redemption fifth, but the one achieving atonement is the one who performs substitution. A new mishna then turns to a different boundary of temurah: one cannot substitute non-sacred limbs for a consecrated fetus, or vice versa, or either for a whole animal (with Rabbi Yosei partly dissenting). This launches a long-running dispute between bar Padda and Rabbi Yoḥanan over whether a fetus can be directly imbued with sanctity at all — argued out through a marathon exchange between Rabbi Zeira and Rabbi Yirmeya, who test bar Padda's position against mishna after mishna, ultimately distinguishing sanctity that inheres in an animal's body from sanctity that inheres merely in its value.

🔑 Two Dilemmas About 'Whose' Offering It Is

One person consecrates; another achieves atonement with it
1️⃣ Dilemma one: if it later becomes blemished and is redeemed, who adds the one-fifth — the consecrator or the one who atones?
↓
2️⃣ Answer: 'And if HE WHO CONSECRATED will redeem his house' (Lev. 27:15) — only the consecrator adds a fifth, never the one who merely atones
↓
3️⃣ Dilemma two: who can perform temurah for it — the consecrator or the one who atones?
↓
4️⃣ Answer (R' Abbahu, citing R' Yoḥanan): the one who ACHIEVES ATONEMENT performs temurah — not the consecrator
Full circle: This is the very question left open on daf 2 — whether ownership for temurah follows the consecrator or the one atoning. It is now resolved: it follows the one atoning.

Rabbi Yosei's proof: Just as saying 'this animal's leg is a burnt offering' consecrates the whole animal, saying 'this leg is a substitute' should make the whole animal a substitute.

🤰 Can a Fetus Itself Be Sanctified?

Bar Padda and Rabbi Yoḥanan disagree at the root
Bar Padda
Sanctity does not take hold on a fetus directly — it isn't yet a complete 'animal.'
Rabbi Yoḥanan
Sanctity does take hold on a fetus. Proof: if one consecrates a pregnant sin offering and it gives birth, he may choose to bring either the mother OR the offspring for atonement — the other then grazes until blemished.
Question
Why state this twice — once for consecrating the mother while pregnant, once for consecrating the fetus alone? Either case should teach the same halakha!
Answer
Each case needs its own teaching: consecrating the mother might extend to the fetus only because the mother herself is fit; consecrating the fetus alone might not work since it isn't 'outside' yet. Both are necessary.
Stakes: This dispute over fetal sanctity directly explains the new mishna's rule that fetuses cannot serve as, or be substituted by, ordinary animals.

⚖️ Rabbi Zeira Defends Bar Padda, Mishna After Mishna

Rabbi Yirmeya presses with proof after proof; Rabbi Zeira distinguishes each
❓ R' Yirmeya: a mishna on avoiding firstborn status says 'what's in this womb, if male, is a burnt offering' — and it works when born! Doesn't that prove fetuses take sanctity?
↓
💬 R' Zeira: that is only value-sanctity (קְדוּשַּׁת דָּמִים) — sold, proceeds fund an offering — not inherent bodily sanctity
↓
❓ R' Yirmeya presses: is value-sanctity even strong enough to cancel firstborn status, or to forbid working the mother?
↓
✅ R' Zeira: yes — animals blemished before consecration are exempt from firstborn law and gifts only until redeemed, proving value-sanctity alone suspends both
↓
❓ R' Yirmeya: what about offspring already born of a consecrated mother (וַלְדֵי קָדָשִׁים) — clearly sanctified!
↓
✅ R' Zeira: different — sanctified through the mother's consecration, not through direct consecration of a fetus
Ongoing debate: The exchange runs through several more mishnayot, each requiring its own fine distinction to preserve bar Padda's position.

💰 Kedushat HaGuf vs. Kedushat Damim

Two levels of sanctity, with very different strength

קְדוּשַּׁת הַגּוּף (Inherent/Bodily)

  • The animal itself must be sacrificed
  • Applies to a properly consecrated unblemished offering
  • Bar Padda: cannot apply to a fetus directly

קְדוּשַּׁת דָּמִים (Value/Monetary)

  • Only the animal's sale-value is sacred; proceeds buy an offering
  • Can apply even to a fetus, per bar Padda
  • Still strong enough to cancel firstborn status and forbid labor before redemption
Key move: By conceding that value-sanctity can apply to a fetus, bar Padda answers every challenge — each proof-text turns out to describe value-sanctity, not the inherent sanctity Rabbi Yoḥanan claims.
