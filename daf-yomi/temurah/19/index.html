📖 Overview: Precisely When Does an Offspring Reach the Altar?

Temurah 19a–19b
מִיגּוֹ דְּנָחֲתָא לַהּ קְדוּשַּׁת דָּמִים — נָחֲתָא נָמֵי קְדוּשַּׁת הַגּוּף
The scene: The Gemara pins down exactly when Rabbi Elazar allows an offspring itself to be sacrificed: only when its mother herself carried that same offering's status. This is tested against a female Paschal offering's offspring before and after Passover (when a leftover Paschal animal becomes a peace offering), and against a female designated for a guilt offering — which, since 'guilt offering' status can never attach to any female, means even Rabbi Elazar concedes the offspring cannot be sacrificed as one. A new mishna then addresses exactly this case, with Rabbi Shimon dissenting on whether the disqualified female must first develop an actual blemish before being sold. The underlying principle driving the majority view — that value-sanctity automatically drags inherent sanctity along with it — becomes the subject of its own amoraic dispute between Rav Kahana and Rava.

🐐 Before and After Pesach: When Does the Mother's Status Matter?

Rava and Abaye refine exactly what makes an offspring sacrifice-eligible
Rava
Rabbi Elazar only lets an offspring be sacrificed directly when its mother carries the SAME offering-status — like a female burnt offering, since a bird burnt offering can be female.
Abaye challenges
But a female Paschal offering that gives birth AFTER Pesach — when the mother becomes a peace offering — has its offspring sacrificed as a peace offering too, per Rabbi Elazar! Yet the mother was never 'peace offering' from the start!
Rava responds
After Pesach, the leftover Paschal animal genuinely BECOMES a peace offering — so the mother DOES carry peace-offering status by the time of birth. Before Pesach, no such status exists, so they'd disagree there too.
Abaye's alternative
Or: nobody disagrees before Pesach — 'wherever the leftover goes, the offspring goes too' is a fixed tradition. Before Pesach, mother and offspring are both consecrated only 'for value,' so neither is sacrificed directly.
Ravina's resolution: The case must involve consecrating an ALREADY-pregnant animal — since a fetus is a separate 'body' (per Rabbi Yoḥanan), it can carry Paschal-offering status directly even while its mother carries only value-sanctity.

🚫 Why a Guilt Offering's Offspring Is Different

Rabbi Yosei b'Rabbi Ḥanina pins down Rabbi Elazar's true logic
❓ Does Rabbi Elazar's leniency (offspring sacrificed directly) ever apply to a female designated for a GUILT offering?
↓
💬 Rabbi Yosei b'Rabbi Ḥanina: No — Rabbi Elazar concedes here to the Rabbis. Its offspring is NOT sacrificed as a guilt offering
↓
🔑 Why not? Because a guilt offering can NEVER be brought from a female, unlike a burnt offering (which has a valid female bird form) — 'guilt offering' status can never attach to the mother at all
↓
✅ This confirms Rabbi Elazar's rule is precisely about the mother's STATUS, not merely whether the offspring happens to be a valid male animal
Why the teaching was needed: Without it, one might think Rabbi Elazar's leniency applies to ANY fit male offspring — the guilt-offering case proves it truly depends on the mother's own potential status.

💰 Two Theories of How Sanctity 'Descends'

Rav's principle vs. Rabbi Shimon's stricter fitness requirement

Rav's Principle (majority view)

  • Once value-sanctity (קְדוּשַּׁת דָּמִים) descends on an animal, inherent sanctity (קְדוּשַּׁת הַגּוּף) automatically comes with it
  • Applies even to the female guilt-offering candidate — it needs a real blemish before sale
  • Rava: this even explains why a MALE consecrated 'for its value' still gets inherent sanctity and is fit to be sacrificed

Rabbi Shimon's Principle

  • Inherent sanctity attaches only to what is CURRENTLY fit for the altar
  • The female guilt-offering candidate is never fit — so no inherent sanctity, sellable immediately
  • Exception: an animal 'lacking only time' (מְחוּסַּר זְמַן, like a firstborn before 8 days) IS sanctified, since it's fit tomorrow — like a firstborn
Precise limit of the exception: A guilt offering brought in the wrong YEAR (1st vs. 2nd) does NOT get the 'future fitness' exception, even though it will also be fit later — Rabbi Shimon's rule there is drawn specifically from the firstborn analogy, not simple future eligibility.

🔄 Rav Kahana vs. Rava — and Rava's Own Reversal

Does consecrating a male animal 'for its value' also confer inherent sanctity?
1️⃣ Rav Kahana: Yes — such a male becomes fully sanctified with inherent sanctity, since it IS fit to be sacrificed as the offering type intended
↓
2️⃣ Rava (initially): No — designating it 'for its value' means only value-sanctity applies, nothing more
↓
3️⃣ Rav Yehuda's citation of Rav settles it: value-sanctity always brings inherent sanctity with it, as a general principle
↓
4️⃣ Rava retracts his own position and accepts Rav Kahana's view — a fit male consecrated 'for value' does carry inherent sanctity and may be sacrificed
Why this matters for the mishna: This principle is exactly why the female guilt-offering candidate cannot simply be sold — even though she'll never be a guilt offering herself, her value-sanctity still pulls inherent sanctity in behind it.
