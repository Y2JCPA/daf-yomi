QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to Rabbi Yoḥanan, in what case do Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yosei both agree that only the FIRST sanctity takes effect?
A. Whenever any two sanctities are mentioned at all
B. When the speaker explicitly says 'this shall take effect, and AFTERWARD this shall take effect' — clear stated sequence
C. Never — they always disagree
D. Only for sin offerings
Correct: B — Rabbi Yoḥanan clarifies that explicit sequential language is agreed upon by all; the real dispute concerns only ambiguous phrasing like the mishna's 'substitute of the burnt offering, substitute of the peace offering' with no explicit sequence stated.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
What does the baraita's agreement — that saying 'half sin offering, half burnt offering' (sin offering FIRST) always results in death — prove about Rabbi Meir's reasoning?
A. That Rabbi Meir never really holds his stated position
B. That his rule is genuinely about which phrase was spoken FIRST, not about sin-offering sanctity being uniquely able to absorb a mixed designation
C. That sin offerings can always be split in half
D. Nothing significant
Correct: B — Since even Rabbi Meir agrees the animal dies when sin offering is named first, this confirms his general rule is 'the first-stated designation wins' — not some special rule about sin-offering sanctity 'catching' a mixed case.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
What three halakhic lessons does the Gemara derive from Rabbi Yoḥanan's case of two partners consecrating their respective halves of an animal at different times?
A. Nothing of significance; it's a marginal case
B. Value-sanctity alone can permanently defer an animal; even a LIVING animal can be permanently disqualified; deferral at the very outset counts as real deferral
C. Only that partners can never own consecrated animals together
D. That temurah never applies to jointly-owned animals
Correct: B — The Gemara draws three lessons: value-sanctity (from consecrating just half) is enough to permanently defer an animal, living (not just slaughtered) animals can be permanently disqualified, and deferral from the very start of consecration counts as genuine deferral.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
What is the outcome of Abaye's dilemma about declaring 'half substitute, half animal tithe' for a non-sacred animal?
A. The substitute-status always wins
B. The tithe-status always wins
C. The dilemma is left unresolved as teiku, since neither named sanctity technically applies to the situation at all
D. The animal is automatically non-sacred
Correct: C — Unlike the clear-cut 'half burnt offering, half tithe' case (where burnt offering plainly wins since tithe-consecration requires passing animals under a rod), the substitute/tithe case has neither sanctity technically applying, leaving the Gemara's dilemma unresolved.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
How does Abaye resolve the apparent contradiction between the mishna (where 'תַּחַת' creates a substitute) and a baraita (where 'תַּחַת' works for bedek habayit items, which can never generate a substitute)?
A. He says one of the two sources must be wrong
B. He explains that 'תַּחַת' is genuinely ambiguous — it can mean either associating one sanctity with another (substitution) or simple desacralization/redemption, depending on context
C. He says bedek habayit items don't really exist
D. He rejects the mishna's ruling entirely
Correct: B — Abaye resolves the contradiction by showing that the word 'תַּחַת' carries two distinct possible meanings — substitution-language in one context, redemption-language in another — so both sources can be correct without contradiction.
