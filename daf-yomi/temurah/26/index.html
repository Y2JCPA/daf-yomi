📖 Overview: What Exact Words Make a Substitute?

Temurah 26a–26b
תַּחַת זוֹ, תְּמוּרַת זוֹ, חֲלִיפַת זוֹ — הֲרֵי זוֹ תְּמוּרָה
The scene: Rabbi Yoḥanan clarifies exactly where Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yosei's dispute lives: everyone agrees that explicit sequential language ('this takes effect, then this') follows the first statement, and explicit conditional language ('this only if that too') gives both — the argument is only about ambiguous back-to-back phrasing like the mishna's own case. A series of baraitot test this through half-burnt/half-peace and half-burnt/half-sin offering declarations, revealing that Rabbi Meir's rule is really about which phrase came first, not about sin-offering sanctity mixing freely. Rabbi Yoḥanan's case of two partners consecrating their respective halves at different times yields three important principles about deferred sanctity. Abaye then raises (and partly leaves unresolved) a further dilemma about mixing substitute-status with animal-tithe status. The daf closes with a new mishna cataloging exactly which Hebrew phrases create a valid substitute and which merely describe ordinary redemption.

🗣️ Where Everyone Agrees, and Where the Real Dispute Lives

Rabbi Yoḥanan narrows the Rabbi Meir/Rabbi Yosei disagreement to one ambiguous phrasing
Explicit sequence
'This will take effect [as substitute for the burnt offering], and AFTERWARD this will take effect [for the peace offering]' — everyone agrees: only the FIRST sanctity takes effect.
Explicit condition
'This won't take effect UNLESS that also takes effect' — everyone agrees: BOTH sanctities take effect together.
The disputed case
But what about the mishna's actual wording — 'the substitute of the burnt offering, the substitute of the peace offering' — with no explicit sequence OR condition stated?
Rabbi Meir
Since he didn't say 'and,' this reads like implicit SEQUENCE — only the first phrase (burnt offering) takes effect.
Rabbi Yosei
He avoided 'and' precisely because he WANTED both — saying 'and' would have implied a half-and-half split, which he mistakenly thought couldn't be sacrificed at all.
The insight: Both sages agree on the clear cases; they disagree only about how to read genuinely ambiguous back-to-back phrasing — a matter of interpreting silence, not explicit words.

🤝 Two Partners, One Animal — Three Lessons

Rabbi Yoḥanan's case: consecrating half now, the other half later
1️⃣ Case: two partners jointly own an animal. One consecrates HIS half; later, he buys out the OTHER half and consecrates that too. Now the whole animal is consecrated — but it can NEVER be sacrificed
↓
2️⃣ Lesson one: value-sanctity (קְדוּשַּׁת דָּמִים) alone — like consecrating just half an animal — is enough to permanently DEFER it from ever reaching the altar
↓
3️⃣ Lesson two: even a LIVING animal (not just one already slaughtered) can become permanently disqualified through such deferral
↓
4️⃣ Lesson three: deferral that happens right at the START (the very moment of consecration) counts as real, permanent deferral — just as much as deferral that happens later
Ripple effect: Because the animal itself can never be sacrificed, even its SUBSTITUTE inherits that same permanent limitation — it too can only graze until blemished.

🌾 Half-Substitute, Half-Tithe: Which Wins?

Abaye's dilemma, only partly resolved
✅ Clear case: 'half burnt offering, half animal tithe' — EVERYONE agrees the burnt offering wins entirely, since tithe-sanctity only comes from passing animals under the rod, which isn't happening here at all
↓
❓ Harder case: 'half SUBSTITUTE, half animal tithe' — substitute-sanctity doesn't apply either (no consecrated animal was standing before him for substitution)
↓
🔀 Does 'substitute' win, since it applies broadly to ALL consecrated categories? Or does 'tithe' win, since tithe-consecration is uniquely powerful (even catching the animal counted BEFORE and AFTER the actual tenth)?
↓
⏳ תֵּיקוּ — the dilemma stands unresolved
Why the difference: Unlike the clear-cut burnt-offering case, here NEITHER named sanctity technically applies at all — making the comparison genuinely uncertain.

📜 New Mishna: The Exact Words That Create a Substitute

Testing 'תַּחַת' against a seemingly contradictory baraita
Mishna
'This is IN PLACE OF that' (תַּחַת), 'the SUBSTITUTE of that,' or 'the EXCHANGE for that' — all three create a valid substitute. But 'this is DESACRALIZED ONTO that' (מְחוּלֶּלֶת) does NOT.
Contradicting baraita
For bedek habayit (Temple upkeep) items — which can never generate a substitute — 'exchange' and 'substitute' language accomplish NOTHING. But 'תַּחַת' and 'מְחוּלֶּלֶת' DO work — as ordinary desacralization/redemption!
The puzzle
If 'תַּחַת' always means SUBSTITUTION (as the mishna implies), how can the same word also work for bedek habayit, where substitution is impossible?
Abaye's resolution
'תַּחַת' is genuinely AMBIGUOUS — sometimes it means associating one sanctity with another (substitution), and sometimes it simply means desacralization/redemption. Context (and which type of consecrated item is involved) determines which sense applies.
Why this matters: The mishna's precise vocabulary for temurah isn't purely mechanical — the same Hebrew word can carry two different legal meanings depending on what kind of consecrated item stands opposite it.
