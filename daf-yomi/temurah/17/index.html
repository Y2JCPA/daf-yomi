📖 Overview: Closing Chapter Two, Opening Chapter Three

Temurah 17a–17b
וְלַד שְׁלָמִים וּתְמוּרָתָן... עַד סוֹף כׇּל הָעוֹלָם — הֲרֵי הֵן שְׁלָמִים
The scene: The daf catalogs several amoraic examples of temurah's unique stringency — that an unwitting substitution is treated like an intentional one, incurring lashes, while unwitting consecration is not. It then returns to Rabbi Elazar's list of animals (hybrids, a tereifa, one born by caesarean, a tumtum, a hermaphrodite) that carry no inherent sanctity, with Rava working through several failed comparisons before settling on the right analogy to a non-kosher species. Chapter Two closes and Chapter Three opens with a new mishna: the offspring and substitutes of peace offerings retain peace-offering status through endless future generations — a rule the Gemara derives from a doubled 'male/female' in Leviticus 3:1, and from Deuteronomy 12:26-27's requirement that these animals actually be sacrificed, not merely kept until they die of neglect.

🎯 Five Ways 'Unwitting Equals Intentional' Only for Temurah

The Amoraim each supply a scenario testing Rabbi Yosei b'Rabbi Yehuda's stringency

ḤizkiyyaThought substitution was permittedTemurah: flogged and consecrated; ordinary consecration: not consecrated at all
Reish Lakish/R' YoḥananMeant 'substitute for my burnt offering,' said 'peace offering'Temurah takes effect; equivalent consecration error does not
Another versionMeant to name a black animal, said 'white' insteadThe white animal becomes the (unintended) substitute; not so for consecration

🐐 Can These Animals Even Sanctify Something Else?

Rabbi Meir presses Rabbi Elazar's ruling to its logical edge
R' Elazar
Kilayim, tereifa, yotzei dofen, tumtum, and androginos are neither sacred through consecration, nor can they sanctify a substitute.
R' Meir
But if they're never sacred to begin with, how could the ruling about substitutes even apply? You can't substitute FOR something that was never consecrated!
Resolution
It must mean: an animal consecrated WHILE fit, which only later became a tereifa or was born by caesarean after its mother's consecration — that's when the 'no substitute' half of the rule bites.
For kilayim/tumtum/androginos
These can only ever arise as the OFFSPRING of an already-consecrated animal (following Rabbi Yehuda, who holds offspring can generate temurah) — never through direct consecration.
Shmuel's clarification: 'Neither sacred nor sanctify' means specifically: not sacred FOR PURPOSES OF SUBSTITUTION, and cannot GENERATE a substitute — not that they lack all sanctity whatsoever.

🐕 Rava's Search for the Right Comparison

Multiple attempts to explain why these animals lack inherent sanctity
1️⃣ Rav Pappa's first attempt: like a non-kosher animal — never sacrificed, never inherently sanctified
↓
2️⃣ Challenge: but a BLEMISHED animal also can't be sacrificed, yet it DOES carry inherent sanctity! What's different?
↓
3️⃣ Rava's 2nd attempt: a blemished animal's SPECIES is still sacrificed (unblemished ones); these categories never are — but then why does even a tereifa (whose species IS sacrificed) fail?
↓
4️⃣ Rava's 3rd attempt: these are INHERENT disqualifications (like non-kosher status), unlike a blemish, which is merely a missing part — but wait, some listed blemishes (שָׂרוּעַ וְקָלוּט) are also inherent!
↓
5️⃣ Final answer: compare to FOOD status — a non-kosher animal and a tereifa are both forbidden to EAT; a blemished animal remains permitted to eat. That distinction holds
Method on display: Rava doesn't settle for the first plausible analogy — he tests it against every counter-example until one survives.

👪 New Mishna: Peace Offerings Propagate Forever

Unlike sin offerings, a peace offering's line never dies out

The Mishna's Rule

  • Offspring of a peace offering = a peace offering
  • Its substitute = a peace offering
  • Even THEIR offspring, and so on — 'until the end of all time' — remain peace offerings, requiring semikha, libations, and the waving of breast and thigh

Verse Source (Lev. 3:1)

  • 'זָכָר' ('male') — includes the OFFSPRING (masculine word)
  • 'נְקֵבָה' ('female') — includes the SUBSTITUTE (feminine word)
  • Rav Safra questioned reversing this; Abaye defends the pairing by grammatical gender
Why 'until the end of the world' is needed: The mishna anticipates Rabbi Elazar's later view (that a peace offering's direct offspring should NOT be sacrificed) and pointedly rejects it for every future generation, not just the first.

📜 Must the Substitute Actually Be Sacrificed?

Deuteronomy 12:26-27 settles the question — they aren't simply left to starve
❓ 'רַק קָדָשֶׁיךָ אֲשֶׁר יִהְיוּ לָךְ... תִּשָּׂא וּבָאתָ' — bring substitutes ('your holy things') and offspring ('that you have') to the Temple. But then what — leave them to die of neglect?
↓
📖 'וְעָשִׂיתָ עוֹלוֹתֶיךָ' — treat them the way you treat the offering they came from: a burnt-offering's substitute is sacrificed as a burnt offering; a peace-offering's is sacrificed as a peace offering
↓
R' Yishmael
'רַק' (only) limits this to SOME offerings, not all — a guilt offering's substitute, for instance, is excluded
R' Akiva
No limiting word is even needed — 'אָשָׁם הוּא' ('it is a guilt offering') already teaches: only IT is sacrificed, not its substitute
Why this matters: Without this verse, one might assume all substitutes and offspring — even a sin offering's — must be brought to the Temple and starved there; the Torah instead directs that eligible ones (like a peace offering's) are actually sacrificed.
