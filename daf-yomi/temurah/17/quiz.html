QUESTION 1 OF 5
What is the common thread across the Amoraim's various examples of 'unwitting equals intentional' for temurah?
A. Unwitting substitution never takes effect
B. In every case, the same kind of mental error that would void ordinary consecration still allows the substitution to fully take effect and incur lashes
C. Only Rabbi Yosei b'Rabbi Yehuda's own examples count
D. Unwitting acts are always exempt from lashes
Correct: B — Each scenario (Ḥizkiyya's, Reish Lakish/R' Yoḥanan's, Rav Sheshet's, etc.) shows that an unwitting act of substitution still consecrates the animal and incurs lashes, unlike the parallel case of unwitting consecration, which has no effect at all.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to Shmuel's clarification of Rabbi Elazar's ruling, what does 'neither sacred nor sanctify' mean for animals like kilayim, tereifa, and tumtum?
A. They have absolutely no sanctity of any kind, ever
B. They cannot become a valid substitute, and cannot generate one either — not that they lack all sanctity in every sense
C. They may never be eaten
D. They are automatically considered non-sacred forever
Correct: B — Shmuel specifies the ruling is about temurah specifically: these animals cannot serve as, or generate, a substitute — leaving room for other forms of sanctity (like value-sanctity) to still apply.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
In Rava's final, successful comparison, what distinguishes a tereifa and a non-kosher animal (no inherent sanctity) from a blemished animal (which DOES carry inherent sanctity)?
A. Their price
B. Both a non-kosher animal and a tereifa are forbidden to eat, while a blemished animal remains permitted to eat
C. Their color
D. Nothing distinguishes them
Correct: B — After several rejected comparisons, Rava settles on the food-status distinction: non-kosher animals and tereifot are forbidden for consumption, unlike a blemished (but still kosher) animal, which may still be eaten.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to the new mishna, how far does peace-offering status extend through a line of offspring and substitutes?
A. Only to the first generation of offspring
B. Only to the first substitute, not further
C. Indefinitely — 'until the end of all time' — every subsequent offspring or substitute remains a peace offering
D. It never applies to offspring, only substitutes
Correct: C — The mishna explicitly states that offspring, substitutes, and their offspring's offspring 'until the end of all time' all retain full peace-offering status, requiring semikha, libations, and the breast/thigh waving.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to Rabbi Akiva, what verse teaches that a guilt offering's (asham) substitute is NOT sacrificed on the altar?
A. 'רַק קָדָשֶׁיךָ' ('only your holy things')
B. 'אָשָׁם הוּא' — 'it is a guilt offering' — the word 'הוּא' (it) implies only the original, not its substitute, is sacrificed
C. 'זָכָר' ('male')
D. There is no such verse; Rabbi Akiva disagrees with the entire premise
Correct: B — Rabbi Akiva derives from the emphatic 'הוּא' in 'it is a guilt offering' (Lev. 5:19) that only the guilt offering itself is sacrificed, excluding its substitute — making Rabbi Yishmael's broader limiting word 'רַק' unnecessary in his view.
