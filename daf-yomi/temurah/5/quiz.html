5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to Rava, why is the Israelite rapist not flogged for divorcing his victim, even though he transgressed a prohibition?
A. Because rape carries no prohibition
B. Because 'all his days' (Deut. 22:29) shows the divorce never fully took hold, since he remains bound to remarry her
C. Because he is exempt as a minor
D. Because only priests are flogged for this
Correct: B — Rava explains that 'all his days' teaches the obligation to remarry persists indefinitely, meaning the divorce is treated as not having fully severed the marriage — hence no lashes.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
What verse does the Gemara use to show that separating teruma from poor-quality produce to cover choice produce is nonetheless effective?
A. "You shall bear no sin by reason of it" (Num. 18:32)
B. "It is most holy" (Lev. 27:28)
C. "He shall not profane his seed" (Lev. 21:15)
D. "For a vow it shall not be accepted" (Lev. 22:23)
Correct: A — Rabbi Ile'a derives from 'you shall bear no sin by reason of it' that the act must have consecrated the produce as teruma — otherwise there would be no sin to bear.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
Which verse does Rava cite as proof that temurah is a Torah-stated exception where a forbidden act does take effect?
A. "Reishit" — the first part of them
B. "He shall not exchange it, nor substitute it"
C. "Both it and that for which it is substituted shall be holy" (הוּא וּתְמוּרָתוֹ)
D. "It shall not be redeemed"
Correct: C — Rava points to the mishna's own verse, 'both it and its substitute shall be holy,' as explicit textual proof that this particular forbidden act (substitution) is effective — not evidence for a general rule.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
In the case of consecrating a blemished animal for the altar, how does Rava explain why the mishna calls the consecration effective, seemingly against his general rule?
A. He says the mishna is simply wrong
B. "For a vow it shall not be accepted" (לֹא יֵרָצֶה) implies only the sacrifice fails, so the consecration itself succeeded
C. Blemished animals were never really forbidden
D. He agrees with Abaye entirely here
Correct: B — Rava reads the specific wording — that only its acceptance/sacrifice (ritzui) is barred — as proof that consecration itself took effect, making this a limited exception rather than a refutation of his rule.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
What is the general pattern the Gemara follows throughout this sugya when a mishna seems to contradict either Abaye or Rava?
A. The Gemara simply declares the mishna corrupt
B. It finds a verse specific to that halakha that limits the ruling to its own case, preserving the sage's general theory elsewhere
C. It always sides with Abaye
D. It ignores mishnayot entirely
Correct: B — Time and again, whichever sage's rule a mishna appears to contradict, the Gemara locates language in that specific verse that explains the ruling as a unique exception, leaving the broader theory intact.
