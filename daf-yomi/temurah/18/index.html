📖 Overview: Which Offspring and Substitutes Actually Reach the Altar

Temurah 18a–18b
וְלַד שְׁלָמִים לֹא יִקְרַב שְׁלָמִים / יִקְרַב
The scene: The daf finishes tracing exactly what a verse adds beyond the orally-transmitted rule that a guilt offering's substitute merely grazes rather than dies, landing on Rav Huna's teaching about an unspecified guilt offering slaughtered after being consigned to grazing. It then turns to a real tannaitic dispute: does the direct offspring of a peace offering get sacrificed as one (the Rabbis) or not (Rabbi Eliezer, fearing owners will breed entire flocks from delayed offerings)? Rabbi Shimon narrows the disagreement to only the first offspring, not further generations, and Rabbi Pappeyas testifies to the practice. Two more mishnayot follow: a thanks offering's line of offspring and substitutes counts as a thanks offering but without the accompanying bread, and a burnt offering's substitute-line counts fully as a burnt offering — except for the special case of a female designated for a burnt offering that gives birth to a male, which becomes its own dispute.

🐏 What Does the Verse Add to an Already-Known Tradition?

The Gemara searches for the real purpose of a limiting verse
❓ We already know by oral tradition that a guilt offering's substitute grazes (rather than dies, like a sin offering's). Why then does a verse ALSO exclude it from being sacrificed?
↓
💬 First answer: since 'grazing' and 'not sacrificed' are logically linked, this must be redundant — reconsider
↓
💬 Second answer: the verse teaches an EXTRA consequence — one who sacrifices it anyway violates a prohibition derived from a positive mitzva
↓
✅ Final answer (Rav Huna): the verse is needed for a different case entirely — a guilt offering consigned to graze, but slaughtered unspecified before it developed a blemish, is valid as a BURNT offering
The verse's real payoff: "אָשָׁם הוּא" — 'it is a guilt offering' — teaches the animal 'remains as it is': if never consigned to graze, it CANNOT default to burnt-offering status even when slaughtered unspecified.

📜 Why Would the Offspring NOT Be Sacrificed?

Rabbi Eliezer's reasoning shifts from wordplay to policy

First Attempt: A Wordplay

  • 'וְאִם' (and if) read as 'וְאֵם' (and a mother)
  • Teaches: the MOTHER may be a peace offering, not the offspring
  • Refuted: the same logic would wrongly exclude a thanks offering's own offspring too, contradicting an accepted baraita

Final Answer: A Rabbinic Decree

  • גְּזֵירָה — a preventive decree
  • Fear: an owner might delay sacrificing his obligatory peace offering in order to breed 'entire flocks' from it for profit
  • Applies specifically to peace offerings, where delay is most tempting
Underlying value: Even a a fully consecrated, sacrifice-eligible animal can be barred from the altar by rabbinic policy aimed at preventing economic exploitation of consecrated status.

🍞 New Mishna: A Thanks Offering's Line — Minus the Bread

Offspring and substitutes of a todah retain its status, with one exception
FeatureThanks offering (todah) itselfIts offspring/substitute/replacement line
Sacrificed as a thanks offering?YesYes — 'until the end of all generations' (וְאִם עַל תּוֹדָה)
Requires accompanying loaves?YesNo — derived from 'עַל זֶבַח הַתּוֹדָה,' limiting the bread requirement to the original animal alone
Derivation: The verse's extra 'יַקְרִיבֶנּוּ' (he shall offer IT) teaches one must choose only ONE of two recovered animals, while 'וְאִם עַל תּוֹדָה' expands the rule to cover offspring, substitutes, and replacements alike.

🐑 A Female Designated for a Burnt Offering Gives Birth

New mishna: substitute-of-a-burnt-offering's line vs. this special case
Mishna
A burnt offering's SUBSTITUTE line (and its offspring, forever) is fully sacrificed as a burnt offering — flayed, cut into pieces, entirely burned.
But here
If one designates a FEMALE for a burnt offering (burnt offerings must be male) and it gives birth to a male — is the male itself sacrificed, or does it merely graze until sold to fund a burnt offering?
Rabbis
It grazes and is redeemed — since the MOTHER herself could never be a valid burnt offering, being female.
Rabbi Elazar
The male offspring itself IS sacrificed as a burnt offering directly.
Rava's distinction
This explains why the mishna's FIRST clause (substitute's offspring) has no dispute at all: there, the 'mother' (the substitute) COULD be sacrificed, so even the Rabbis agree its offspring can too. Only the female-designated-for-burnt-offering case is disputed.
A parallel challenge resolved: Elsewhere Rabbi Elazar seems to require even a guilt offering's leftover money to buy an individual (not communal) burnt offering — Rava limits Rabbi Elazar's lenient 'sacrifice it directly' ruling to precisely this narrow female-burnt-offering scenario.
