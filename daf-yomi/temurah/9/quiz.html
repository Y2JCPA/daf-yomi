Temurah 9 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to the mishna, which type of blemished animal can NEVER be validly substituted for (or used as a substitute)?
A. Any blemished animal whatsoever
B. One that was blemished from birth, before it could have been consecrated unblemished
C. Only female blemished animals
D. Only blemished cattle
Correct: B — The mishna teaches that 'good for bad' substitution works only when the blemish came after consecration; an animal blemished from the very start cannot generate a valid temurah at all.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
What is Rava's core objection to Abaye's attempt to derive 'good-for-good is flogged' through a kal vachomer (a fortiori inference)?
A. He says the inference is logically invalid
B. One does not administer punishment (lashes) based on a logical inference alone — אֵין עוֹנְשִׁין מִן הַדִּין
C. He says good-for-good substitution is actually permitted
D. He agrees entirely with Abaye
Correct: B — Rava's principle is that corporal punishment requires an explicit verse; a logical derivation, however compelling, cannot by itself generate liability for lashes.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to Rabbi Shimon in the second mishna, how must a substitution be structured?
A. Any number of animals may be substituted for any number of others
B. Strictly one non-sacred animal for one consecrated animal
C. Only females may be substituted
D. Only for guilt offerings
Correct: B — Rabbi Shimon derives from 'וְהָיָה הוּא וּתְמוּרָתוֹ' — since 'it' (הוּא) is singular — that the substitute must likewise be a single, specific animal, disputing the first tanna's allowance of any ratio.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
What is the core disagreement between Reish Lakish and Rabbi Yoḥanan regarding Rabbi Shimon's opinion?
A. Whether Rabbi Shimon permits any substitution at all
B. Whether Rabbi Shimon, who bars substituting two animals for one, also bars substituting twice in sequence for the same animal
C. Whether Rabbi Shimon agrees with the first tanna entirely
D. Whether Rabbi Shimon applies only to blemished animals
Correct: B — Reish Lakish holds Rabbi Shimon permits sequential substitution for the same consecrated animal (its sanctity never left), while Rabbi Yoḥanan holds he forbids it, just as he forbids substituting two-for-one.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi, who must add the extra one-fifth (ḥomesh) upon redeeming a blemished consecrated animal?
A. Anyone who redeems any consecrated item
B. Only the one who directly consecrated it originally (מַקְדִּישׁ), not one whose animal received transferred sanctity (מַתְפִּיס)
C. Only priests
D. No one is ever required to add a fifth
Correct: B — Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi derives from 'and if he who consecrated it will redeem' that the added fifth applies only to the original consecrator, not to someone whose animal became holy through a transfer of sanctity (like a substitute).
