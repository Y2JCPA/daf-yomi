Berakhot 16 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
One recited Shema, erred, and does not know where — but realizes it in the middle of a paragraph. According to the Tosefta, what does he do?
A. Returns to the beginning of the entire Shema
B. Returns to the beginning of that paragraph
C. Continues from where he is
D. Returns to 'and you shall write them'
Correct: B — The Tosefta recited before Rabbi Yoḥanan rules: in the middle of a paragraph, return to the beginning of that paragraph; between paragraphs, to the first break; between 'writing' and 'writing', to the first 'writing'. Rabbi Yoḥanan added that once one has begun 'in order to lengthen your days' he simply continues.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
How does the Gemara reconcile Rav Sheshet's rule that laborers recite Shema only when idle from work with Beit Hillel's statement that laborers work and recite?
A. Rav Sheshet follows Beit Shammai
B. Idle from work for the first paragraph; working is permitted during the second paragraph
C. Idle when atop a tree, working when on the ground
D. Beit Hillel refer to the homeowner, not to laborers
Correct: B — The Gemara answers: this (idle) refers to the first paragraph of Shema, which requires intent, while that (working) refers to the second paragraph.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
Why is one who marries a widow obligated in Shema on his wedding night, even though marrying is also a mitzva?
A. Marrying a widow is not considered a mitzva
B. He is not preoccupied, whereas one marrying a virgin is
C. A widow's marriage takes place on a different night
D. The exemption is only for a Torah scholar
Correct: B — The Gemara explains the distinction as one of preoccupation: 'here he is preoccupied, there he is not.' Only preoccupation with a mitzva, such as a groom's, exempts.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
The Gemara asks: if preoccupation exempts a groom, why is a mourner obligated in all mitzvot (except tefillin)? What is its answer?
A. A mourner is in fact exempt from Shema
B. A mourner's preoccupation is with a voluntary matter; a groom's preoccupation is with a mitzva
C. A mourner has already accepted the Kingdom of Heaven
D. Mourning at night is only rabbinic
Correct: B — Rabbi Abba bar Zavda cited Rav that a mourner is obligated in all mitzvot except tefillin, called 'splendor' (Ezekiel 24:17). The Gemara distinguishes: there the preoccupation is voluntary; here, for a groom, it is preoccupation with a mitzva.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
How does the Gemara explain Rabban Gamliel bathing on the first night after his wife died?
A. Bathing was permitted for anyone in acute mourning at night
B. Acute mourning at night is only rabbinic, and the Sages did not decree it for a delicate person (istenis)
C. His wife's death occurred on a festival, which cancels mourning
D. He held that a mourner is only prohibited from bathing in hot water
Correct: B — Rabban Gamliel called himself an istenis, a delicate person. The Gemara explains he held that aninut at night is only rabbinic — 'the end will be like a bitter day' (Amos 8:10) — and the Sages did not extend the decree to a delicate person.
