📖 Overview: Shema in the Tree, Shema at the Wedding

Berakhot 16a–16b
אֵינִי שׁוֹמֵעַ לָכֶם לְבַטֵּל הֵימֶנִי מַלְכוּת שָׁמַיִם אֲפִילּוּ שָׁעָה אַחַת
The scene: After a last word on tents of Torah and streams, the Gemara finishes the previous mishna with a practical Tosefta: what to do when you err in Shema and cannot remember where. Then a new mishna moves from precision to concentration. Laborers may recite Shema perched on a tree or a wall, though they may not pray there, and a groom is exempt from Shema on his wedding night — yet Rabban Gamliel recited it anyway. The Gemara probes how much intent Shema needs, how a laborer's prayer and Grace after Meals are shortened, and why a groom's preoccupation exempts him while a mourner's does not. A second mishna records Rabban Gamliel bathing on the night his wife died and accepting condolences for his slave Tavi, leading to the laws of mourning for slaves. The daf ends with the personal prayers the Sages added after their Amida.

🌳 What May a Laborer Do Without Climbing Down?

The mishna and its Tosefta on workers atop trees and walls
SituationRulingReason
Reciting Shema atop a tree or a course of stonesPermittedShema needs less concentration than the Amida
Praying the Amida atop a treeOnly atop an olive or fig tree; on all other trees, climb downThose trees have dense branches, so one can stand and focus
The homeowner (self-employed) praying atop any treeAlways climbs downHis mind is not settled, and it is his prerogative to descend
Intent — Rav Sheshet's conditionIdle from work for the first paragraph; Beit Hillel's 'work and recite' is the second paragraph'Hear, Israel' is compared to 'Pay attention, and hear, Israel' (Deut. 27:9)
Leading the prayer or the Priestly BlessingNot permittedSo as not to neglect the work they were hired for
Prayer and Grace shortened: One baraita has laborers pray the full Amida, another an abridged one. Rav Sheshet first split them between Rabban Gamliel and Rabbi Yehoshua, but since Rabbi Yehoshua permits everyone to abridge, the Gemara concludes both follow Rabban Gamliel: workers paid a wage abridge, workers who work only for their meal pray in full. Similarly, wage-laborers skip the blessing before bread and say two Grace blessings, folding 'Who builds Jerusalem' into the blessing of the land — unless the homeowner reclines with them.

💍 Why Is the Groom Exempt but the Mourner Obligated?

The Gemara derives the exemption and then tests its limits
Baraita
'When you sit in your home' excludes one engaged in a mitzva; 'when you walk along the way' excludes a groom. Hence: one who marries a virgin is exempt, one who marries a widow is obligated.
Rav Pappa
How is it derived? 'Like the way' — just as a journey is voluntary, so all who are obligated are engaged in voluntary matters.
Gemara
But surely the verse also covers one walking to perform a mitzva, and still the Torah says: recite!
Answer
Then it should say 'when walking'. Why בְּלֶכְתְּךָ, 'when you walk'? On your walking you are obligated; on a mitzva's walking you are exempt.
Gemara
Then why only a virgin? Marrying a widow is a mitzva too! — Here he is preoccupied; there he is not. But if preoccupation exempts, one whose ship sank at sea should be exempt too — yet Rav says a mourner is obligated in every mitzva except tefillin, called 'splendor' (Ezekiel 24:17).
Answer
There it is preoccupation with a voluntary matter; here it is preoccupation with a mitzva.
May he recite anyway? The mishna says a groom who wishes to recite Shema the first night may. Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel says: not everyone who wishes to assume the name of piety may assume it.

🛁 Rabban Gamliel Breaks His Own Rules — Three Times

Each time his students object, and each time he explains why he is the exception

Shema on his wedding night

  • Students: didn't you teach us a groom is exempt?
  • 'I will not listen to you and remove the Kingdom of Heaven from myself even for one hour'

Bathing the night his wife died

  • Students: didn't you teach us a mourner may not bathe?
  • 'I am not like other people — I am delicate (istenis)'
  • Gemara: acute mourning at night is only rabbinic — 'the end will be like a bitter day' (Amos 8:10) — and the Sages did not decree it for a delicate person

Condolences for his slave Tavi

  • Students: didn't you teach us one does not accept condolences for slaves?
  • 'Tavi my slave is not like other slaves — he was virtuous'
  • Baraita: for slaves no row of comforters, no mourners' blessing; Rabbi Yosei allows for a virtuous slave: 'Alas, a good and loyal man who enjoyed his labor'
Rabbi Eliezer's maidservant: When students followed him from the upper floor to the gatehouse to the banquet hall to console him, he said: you are not burned even by boiling water! For a slave one says only what one says for an ox or donkey: 'May the Omnipresent replenish your loss.' Likewise only three are called Patriarchs and four Matriarchs, and slaves are not called 'Father so-and-so' — except Rabban Gamliel's, who were significant.

🙏 What the Sages Said After Their Amida

Rabbi Elazar's reading of Psalms 63 and the personal prayers that follow
1️⃣ Rabbi Elazar: 'So I will bless You as I live' (Psalms 63:5) is Shema; 'to Your name I will raise my hands' is the Amida. One who does both is satisfied 'as with fat and marrow' and inherits two worlds — 'lips of joys', plural
↓
2️⃣ Rabbi Elazar: may love, brotherhood, peace and friendship dwell in our lot; enlarge our borders with disciples; give us a good companion and a good inclination
↓
3️⃣ Rabbi Yoḥanan: look upon our shame, clothe Yourself in Your mercy, wrap Yourself in Your loving-kindness, and let Your goodness and humility come before You
↓
4️⃣ Rabbi Zeira: that we not sin, nor be ashamed, nor be disgraced before our forefathers. Rabbi Ḥiyya: that Your Torah be our vocation, and our heart not faint nor our eyes dim
↓
5️⃣ Rav: long life — of peace, goodness, blessing, sustenance, vigor, fear of sin, no shame, wealth and honor, love of Torah and fear of Heaven
↓
6️⃣ Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi: save us from the arrogant, from a bad man, a bad mishap, an evil inclination, a bad companion, a bad neighbor, the destructive Satan, a harsh trial and a harsh opponent — even though royal guards stood watch over him
Pattern: Each sage's prayer reflects his own concern — disciples, humility, Torah as a livelihood, a long good life, protection from enemies — showing that fixed prayer leaves room for the personal.
