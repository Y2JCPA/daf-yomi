📖 Overview: The Cup of Blessing and a New Chapter

Berakhot 51a–51b
עֲשָׂרָה דְּבָרִים נֶאֶמְרוּ בְּכוֹס שֶׁל בְּרָכָה
The scene: The daf finishes the chapter on blessings over food. It rules on one who forgot to bless before eating, wanders into the folk-medicine of asparagus (an early-morning wine or beer drink) and the warnings of angels, and then lays out the ten requirements of the cup of blessing used for Grace after Meals. The story of Ulla refusing to send the cup to Yalta ends in four hundred smashed barrels. A new chapter then opens: the mishna lists every dispute between Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel concerning a meal, beginning with which comes first in kiddush, the blessing over the day or over the wine.

🧄 Ate Without a Blessing: Can He Still Bless?

Rav Ḥisda's answer, Ravina's extension, and where the Gemara draws the line
❓ They asked Rav Ḥisda: one who ate and drank and did not recite a blessing, does he go back and recite it now?
↓
🧄 Rav Ḥisda: Should one who ate garlic and now smells go eat another garlic so he smells more? He must bless before continuing to eat.
↓
➕ Ravina: therefore even if he finished his meal, he goes back and blesses. Proof: one who immersed recites עַל הַטְּבִילָה only after emerging.
↓
Immersion
Before immersing the man was unfit to bless (he was impure), so blessing afterward is the only option.
Eating
Before eating he was fit to bless. Since he was excluded once the meal ended, he stays excluded: הוֹאִיל וְאִידְּחִי — אִידְּחִי.
↓
✅ Result: bless before continuing a meal, but once it is over the opportunity is lost.
Also on the daf: Why must one spit out food in his mouth rather than push it aside to bless? Rabbi Yoḥanan (via Rav Yitzḥak Kaskesa'a): יִמָּלֵא פִי תְּהִלָּתֶךָ, 'My mouth will be filled with Your praise' (Psalms 71:8), so praise is said with the whole mouth.

🌿 Asparagus: Every Contradiction Is Wine vs. Beer

The Sages' six rules for the morning drink, and how the Gemara reconciles clashing baraitot
QuestionMade of wineMade of beer
Good for L-E-T (heart, eyes, spleen) or R-M-T (head, intestines, hemorrhoids)?Good for L-E-T, harmful for R-M-TGood for R-M-T, harmful for L-E-T
Good for the intestines?Yes, when made with old wine (proof: the konam vow in Nedarim)Not the subject of that baraita
What may follow it?Only breadOnly its own kind (e.g. dates after date beer)
Spit after drinking?If he spits, he is harmedIf he does not spit, he is harmed
Rav Ashi's conclusion—Its saliva may be expelled even before the king, since not spitting is dangerous
The six rules: Drink it undiluted and from a full cup; receive it in the right hand and drink with the left; do not converse after it and do not pause during it; return the cup only to the one who handed it to you; spit after it; and supplement it only with its own kind.

🏆 Ten Things Said About the Cup of Blessing

Rabbi Zeira in the name of Rabbi Abbahu, and how much of the list Rabbi Yoḥanan kept

The full list of ten

  • Rinsing (inside) and washing (outside)
  • Undiluted wine, and a full cup
  • Adorning and wrapping
  • Take it in both hands, then place it in the right
  • Lift it a handbreadth from the ground; fix your eyes on it
  • Some say: also send it as a gift to one's household

Rabbi Yoḥanan: only four

  • Rinsing
  • Washing
  • Undiluted
  • Full — and whoever blesses over a full cup receives 'a boundless inheritance' (Deut. 33:23); Rabbi Yosei bar Ḥanina: he inherits both worlds

How the Sages did it

  • Adorning: Rav Yehuda surrounded the cup with students; Rav Ḥisda with other cups
  • Wrapping: Rav Pappa wrapped himself in his cloak; Rav Asi spread a cloth on his head
  • Both hands: 'Lift your hands in holiness and bless the Lord' (Psalms 134:2)
  • Lift a handbreadth: 'I will lift the cup of salvation' (Psalms 116:13)
  • Left hand assisting the right? Unresolved by the early Sages, so Rav Ashi rules stringently
Rav Asi adds: One does not speak over the cup of blessing, and one does not bless over a 'cup of punishment' — Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak: a second cup. A baraita agrees: one who drinks in pairs should not bless, for 'Prepare to meet your God, O Israel' (Amos 4:12), and such a person is not prepared.

🍇 Ulla, Rav Naḥman, and Yalta's Four Hundred Barrels

Who must receive the cup of blessing for the household to be blessed?
Rav Naḥman
Master, please send the cup of blessing to Yalta.
Ulla
No need. Rabbi Yoḥanan said: the fruit of a woman's body is blessed only through the fruit of a man's body, as it says וּבֵרַךְ פְּרִי בִטְנְךָ — 'the fruit of your body' (Deut. 7:13), not 'her body'. Rabbi Natan taught the same in a baraita.
Yalta
(Hearing this, she rose in a rage, went into the wine store, and broke four hundred barrels of wine.)
Rav Naḥman
Let the Master send her another cup.
Ulla
(Sending a different cup:) All the wine of this barrel is wine of blessing.
Yalta
From itinerant peddlers come empty words, and from rags come lice.
Why the cup mattered: The tenth item on the list — sending the cup to one's household — is 'so that his wife will be blessed'. Ulla held that giving it to the husband already accomplishes that.

The mishna's list of disputes, beginning with kiddush

Forgot Grace after MealsReturn to where he ate and blessBless where he remembered (both: until the food is digested)

'The halakha follows Beit Hillel': The Gemara asks why the Tosefta needs to say so, since a Divine Voice already proclaimed it. Either the Tosefta predates the Divine Voice, or it was taught afterward — and the answer continues on the next daf. The mishna also rules that one answers amen after a Jew's blessing but after a Samaritan's only once the whole blessing is heard.
