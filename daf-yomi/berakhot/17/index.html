📖 Overview: Prayers, Sayings, and the Onen

Berakhot 17a–17b
מִי שֶׁמֵּתוֹ מוּטָּל לְפָנָיו — פָּטוּר מִקְּרִיאַת שְׁמַע וּמִן הַתְּפִלָּה וּמִן הַתְּפִילִּין
The scene: The daf continues the string of personal prayers the Sages said after the Amida — Rav Safra's plea for peace among the disciples, Rabbi Alexandri's 'yeast in the dough', Rava's confession later adopted for Yom Kippur, Mar son of Ravina's 'guard my tongue', and Rav Sheshet's fast-day prayer. Then come sayings each sage was wont to repeat, the blessing students gave on leaving the study hall, and a dispute over who is 'stubborn-hearted'. The Gemara returns to the mishna to ask why Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel fears presumptuousness for a groom reciting Shema while the Rabbis fear it for idleness on the Ninth of Av. The second chapter closes, and a third opens with the mishna of one whose dead lies before him, who is exempt from Shema, prayer, tefillin and all the mitzvot.

🙏 Five More Prayers After the Amida

Each sage's closing words reflect a different concern
SageOccasionCore of the prayer
Rav SafraAfter prayerPeace in the heavenly and earthly entourages and among the disciples — and may those who study not for its own sake come to study לִשְׁמָהּ
Rabbi AlexandriAfter prayerStation us in a lighted corner, not a dark one; 'our will is to do Your will — what prevents us? The yeast in the dough and subjugation to the kingdoms'
RavaAfter prayer'Before I was created I was worthless… I am dust in life, all the more so in death, a vessel full of shame' — cleanse my sins, but not through suffering and illness. This became Rav Hamnuna Zuti's Yom Kippur confession
Mar son of RavinaConcluding prayer'My God, guard my tongue from evil… may my soul be like dust to all; open my heart to Your Torah' — thwart the plans of those who plot against me
Rav SheshetOn a fast dayIn Temple times a sinner offered only fat and blood and was atoned; may my fat and blood diminished by fasting be as if offered on the altar
Rabbi Yoḥanan on finishing Job: A person will ultimately die and an animal will be slaughtered — all stand to die. Happy is one who grew up in Torah, labored in Torah, pleased his Creator, and left the world with a good name: 'A good name is better than fine oil, and the day of death than the day of birth' (Ecclesiastes 7:1).

💬 What the Sages Were Wont to Say

Sayings 'fluent in the mouth' of Rabbi Meir, the Sages of Yavne, Abaye, Rava and Rav

On Torah study

  • Rabbi Meir: study with all your heart to know My ways; keep My Torah in your heart; guard your mouth from sin — and I will be with you everywhere
  • Sages of Yavne: I am a creature and my fellow is a creature; my work is in the city, his in the field. One who does much and one who does little are equal, provided he directs his heart to Heaven

On peace and humility

  • Abaye: be shrewd in fear of Heaven; 'a soft answer turns away wrath' (Proverbs 15:1); increase peace with all, even a gentile in the marketplace
  • Rabban Yoḥanan ben Zakkai was never preceded in greeting, not even by a gentile in the marketplace
  • Rava: the goal of wisdom is repentance and good deeds — not to study and then spurn one's father, mother or teacher. 'All who fulfill them' (Psalms 111:10): for its own sake; otherwise, better not to have been created

On the World-to-Come

  • Rav: there is no eating, drinking, procreation, business, jealousy, hatred or competition; the righteous sit with crowns on their heads enjoying the splendor of the Divine Presence — 'they beheld God, and ate and drank' (Exodus 24:11)
  • Greater is God's promise to women than to men: 'Rise up, women at ease…' (Isaiah 32:9)
  • Rav asked Rabbi Ḥiyya: by what merit? Bringing their children to read in the synagogue, sending their husbands to learn, and waiting for them to return
Farewell from the study hall: Leaving Rabbi Ami (some say Rabbi Ḥanina), students would bless: 'May you see your world in your lifetime, and your end be life in the World-to-Come… your eyes shine in the light of Torah, your face radiate like the brightness of the firmament.'

💪 'Hear Me, Stubborn-Hearted, Far From Charity'

Two readings of Isaiah 46:12 — praise for the righteous, or rebuke for the obstinate?
❓ Who are the 'stubborn-hearted who are far from charity'? Rav and Shmuel (some say Rabbi Yoḥanan and Rabbi Elazar) disagree
↓
One said
The whole world is sustained by God's charity, but the righteous are sustained by force — their deeds entitle them
The other said
The whole world is sustained by their merit, yet they are not sustained even by their own merit
↓
📜 Rav Yehuda in the name of Rav: every day a Divine Voice from Mount Horeb declares — the whole world is sustained for the sake of Ḥanina my son, and Ḥanina my son suffices with a kav of carobs from one Shabbat eve to the next
↓
🔄 Rav Yehuda himself disagrees: the 'stubborn-hearted' are the foolish heathens of Gova'ei — Rav Yosef: no convert ever came from them. Rav Ashi: the people of Mata Meḥasya, who see the glory of Torah twice a year and still do not convert
'Our leaders are laden' (Psalms 144:14): Said on leaving Rav Ḥisda's study hall. One view: leaders in Torah, laden with mitzvot; another: leaders in Torah and mitzvot, laden with suffering. 'No breach' — not like David's circle that produced Ahitophel; 'no going forth' — not like Saul's that produced Doeg; 'no outcry' — not like Elisha's that produced Geihazi; 'in our open places' — no son or student who spoils his dish in public.

😇 Who Fears Presumptuousness — Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel or the Rabbis?

The groom's Shema versus idleness on the Ninth of Av
Gemara
Our mishna: a groom who wishes may recite Shema; Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel says not everyone who wishes to assume the name may. So he fears yuhara and the Rabbis do not?
Objection
But in the mishna on the Ninth of Av it is reversed! Where the custom is to work, one works — yet Torah scholars are everywhere idle. Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel: everyone should conduct himself as a Torah scholar. Rabbis contradict Rabbis, and Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel contradicts himself.
R' Yoḥanan
The attribution is reversed in one of the sources.
Rav Sheisha
No need to reverse. For the Rabbis: with Shema, everyone is reciting and he recites too — it does not look presumptuous. On the Ninth of Av everyone works and he does not — that is conspicuous.
Rav Sheisha
For Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel: Shema depends on intent, and we all know a groom cannot concentrate — so reciting is a show. On the Ninth of Av an onlooker just says he has no work: 'Go see how many idlers there are in the marketplace.'
Chapter boundary: With this the second chapter, Haya Korei, concludes, and the third chapter, Mi SheMeto, begins.

⚰️ The New Mishna: Who Is Exempt Around a Funeral?

Mi SheMeto — from the bereaved to the pallbearers to the row of comforters
1️⃣ One whose dead lies before him is exempt from Shema, the Amida, tefillin, and all mitzvot in the Torah
↓
2️⃣ Pallbearers, their replacements, and their replacements' replacements: those before the bier who are still needed are exempt from Shema; those after the bier, even if needed, are obligated
↓
3️⃣ Both groups are exempt from the Amida prayer, which requires concentration
↓
4️⃣ Returning from the burial: if they can begin and finish Shema before reaching the row of comforters, they begin; if not, they do not
↓
5️⃣ In the row: the inner row, facing the mourners, is exempt; the outer row is obligated
↓
6️⃣ Women, slaves and minors are exempt from Shema and tefillin but obligated in prayer, mezuza and Grace after Meals
↓
7️⃣ Gemara: 'before him' implies not before him — not exempt. But a baraita says the bereaved eats in another room, or a friend's house, or behind a partition, or turns his face — without reclining, meat, wine, blessing or zimmun
Left open: The tension between the mishna's 'laid out before him' and the baraita's broad exemption is the question the daf ends on.
