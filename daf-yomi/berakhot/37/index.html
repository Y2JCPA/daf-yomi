📖 Overview: When Is Grain 'Bread'?

Berakhot 37a–37b
כֹּל שֶׁהוּא מֵחֲמֵשֶׁת הַמִּינִים מְבָרְכִין עָלָיו בּוֹרֵא מִינֵי מְזוֹנוֹת
The scene: The daf opens by finishing the analysis of Rav and Shmuel's twin rulings about the five species of grain: one statement covers mixtures, the other excludes rice and millet. But a Tosefta says that rice which was ground, baked and cooked gets borei minei mezonot, and after several attempts to pin that Tosefta on Rabbi Yoḥanan ben Nuri, the Gemara declares Rav and Shmuel conclusively refuted. From there the daf turns to the blessing after eating: Rabban Gamliel requires the full Grace after Meals for the seven species, the Rabbis one abridged blessing, and Rabbi Akiva boldly follows the majority in Rabban Gamliel's presence. The last section asks when bread that has been broken, soaked or cooked still deserves hamotzi, and closes with odd flatbreads and their status for ḥalla.

🍚 Rice: A Refutation of Rav and Shmuel

Every proof that rice is 'not grain' meets a baraita that treats it like grain
📜 Rav and Shmuel: 'anything that has of the five grains' teaches mixtures; 'anything that is of the five grains' excludes rice and millet, even pure
↓
❓ But a baraita: rice bread or millet bread — bless before and after 'like a cooked dish', and a cooked dish gets mezonot before and me'ein shalosh after!
↓
💡 'Like a cooked dish' in having blessings before and after; not like it in which blessings: here shehakol before and borei nefashot after
↓
❓ But another baraita lists rice among the cooked dishes (ḥilka, teraggis, flour, zariz, arsan, and rice)!
↓
💡 That is Rabbi Yoḥanan ben Nuri, who says rice is grain: karet for its leaven, and matza from it fulfills the Passover obligation. The Rabbis disagree
↓
❓ But the Tosefta: rice ground, baked and cooked gets mezonot — if that were Rabbi Yoḥanan ben Nuri it would say hamotzi and three blessings. So it must be the Rabbis!
↓
⛔ תְּיוּבְתָּא — a conclusive refutation of Rav and Shmuel

🌾 The Tosefta: Wheat and Rice From Kernel to Dish

What form the grain is in decides both the blessing before and the blessing after
FormBlessing beforeBlessing after
Chewing raw wheatבּוֹרֵא פְּרִי הָאֲדָמָה (the Rabbis); Rabbi Yehuda: borei minei zera'im, 'various kinds of seeds'—
Wheat ground, baked, then cooked — slices intactהַמּוֹצִיאThree blessings of Grace after Meals
Same, slices dissolvedבּוֹרֵא מִינֵי מְזוֹנוֹתOne blessing abridged from three (me'ein shalosh)
Chewing raw riceבּוֹרֵא פְּרִי הָאֲדָמָה—
Rice ground, baked, then cooked — even with pieces intactבּוֹרֵא מִינֵי מְזוֹנוֹתMe'ein shalosh as taught — but the Gemara emends: 'nothing', i.e. only borei nefashot
Why emend? Rav Sheshet first assigns 'me'ein shalosh after rice' to Rabban Gamliel. But the Tosefta's wheat clause (dissolved slices → me'ein shalosh) cannot be Rabban Gamliel, who requires three blessings even for dates. So the whole Tosefta is the Rabbis, and its rice ending is corrected to match their view that no blessing of the Land follows rice.

🍯 After Dates in Jericho: Rabban Gamliel vs. Rabbi Akiva

The dispute over the blessing after the seven species, and a student who follows the majority
Baraita
Anything from the seven species: Rabban Gamliel says three blessings of Grace after Meals; the Sages say one blessing abridged from three.
Story
Rabban Gamliel and the elders reclined in an upper room in Jericho, ate dates, and Rabban Gamliel gave Rabbi Akiva permission to bless. Rabbi Akiva hurried and recited me'ein shalosh.
R' Gamliel
Akiva! How long will you stick your head into the dispute?
R' Akiva
Our teacher, though you say this and your colleagues say that, you taught us: individual and many — the halakha follows the many.
R' Yehuda
A variant in Rabbi Akiva's name: seven species that are not grain, or grain not made into bread — Rabban Gamliel: three blessings; Sages: one. Neither seven species nor grain, such as rice or millet bread — Rabban Gamliel: me'ein shalosh; Sages: nothing.
Rav Sheshet
So a baraita saying 'nothing after rice' and a Tosefta saying 'me'ein shalosh after rice' need not conflict — one is the Rabbis, the other Rabban Gamliel.
In the end: The Gemara prefers to keep the Tosefta entirely with the Rabbis and emend its rice clause, rather than split one Tosefta between two tannaim.

🍞 When Do Bread Crumbs Still Get Hamotzi?

Rav Yosef's olive-bulk rule is tested against the meal-offerings and finally replaced
1️⃣ Rav Yosef: a cooked dish (ḥavitza) with bread pieces of an olive-bulk — hamotzi and three blessings; smaller pieces — mezonot and me'ein shalosh
↓
2️⃣ His proof: a priest eating meal-offerings in Jerusalem says hamotzi (after sheheḥeyanu the first time), and 'he crumbles them all into olive-bulk pieces'
↓
3️⃣ Abaye: then for the school of Rabbi Yishmael, who crush the offering back to flour, no hamotzi? Yet a baraita says one who gathers an olive-bulk of those crumbs is liable to karet if leavened and fulfills matza if unleavened — crumbs are still bread!
↓
4️⃣ Answer: the crumbs were kneaded back into one mass. Rejected: the baraita says 'ate them' within the time of eating a half-loaf, not 'ate it'
↓
5️⃣ Better answer: the crumbs came from a large loaf that already had bread status
↓
6️⃣ Conclusion — Rav Sheshet: even without olive-bulk pieces, hamotzi. Rava: provided it still has תּוֹרִיתָא דְנַהְמָא, the appearance of bread
Ḥalla footnote: Terokanin (a watery batter roasted in a hole in the ground, per Abaye) are said to be obligated in ḥalla, but Ravin reports Rabbi Yoḥanan exempting them. Terita, variously a batter poured on a hot stove, Indian skewer-bread, or bread made for kutaḥ, is exempt. Rabbi Ḥiyya exempts kutaḥ-bread; Rabbi Yehuda says the shape tells: thick loaves obligated, board-shaped exempt.

🥣 Rava's Rihata: Farmers vs. Meḥoza

A flour-and-honey porridge, blessed by its primary ingredient — until Rava changes his mind

Farmers' Rihata

  • They add extra flour
  • The flour (סְמִידָא) is primary
  • בּוֹרֵא מִינֵי מְזוֹנוֹת

Meḥoza Rihata

  • They do not add extra flour
  • The honey (דּוּבְשָׁא) is primary
  • שֶׁהַכֹּל נִהְיֶה בִּדְבָרוֹ — Rava's first ruling

Rava's Retraction

  • Both this and that: mezonot
  • Because Rav and Shmuel said anything containing the five species of grain gets mezonot
  • The grain's presence, not its proportion, decides
Note: Rava relies on the very rule of Rav and Shmuel that the daf's opening refuted with regard to rice. The refutation concerned rice and millet; the principle that grain in a mixture governs the blessing stands.
