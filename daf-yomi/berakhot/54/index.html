📖 Overview: Blessings for What One Sees and Survives

Berakhot 54a–54b
חַיָּיב אָדָם לְבָרֵךְ עַל הָרָעָה כְּשֵׁם שֶׁמְּבָרֵךְ עַל הַטּוֹבָה
The scene: A new chapter opens with one long mishna of blessings triggered by experience rather than by the clock: seeing a place of miracles, a storm, the great sea, hearing good or bad news, building a house. It adds the rule that one blesses for the bad as for the good, derived from 'with all your heart, soul, and might', and closes with laws of reverence for the Temple and two enactments of the Sages. The Gemara derives the blessing over miracles from Jethro, shows that individuals bless over their own miracles, walks through a baraita's list of miracle sites with the midrash behind each, and ends with Rav Yehuda's four who must give thanks — seafarers, desert travelers, the healed, and the freed prisoner.

🌧️ Bless for the Bad, Do Not Pray for the Past

Three more rulings from the mishna, and its Temple-related laws

Vain prayer

  • Crying out over the past is a vain prayer (תְּפִלַּת שָׁוְא)
  • His wife is pregnant and he prays 'may she bear a boy'
  • He hears a scream in the city and prays 'may it not be from my house'

Entering a city

  • Two prayers: on entering and on leaving
  • Ben Azzai: four — two on entering, two on leaving
  • He gives thanks for the past and cries out for the future

Bless for bad as for good

  • 'With all your heart' — with both inclinations, good and evil
  • 'With all your soul' — even if He takes your soul
  • 'With all your might' — with all your money; or: for every measure He metes out to you, thank Him
Temple reverence and two enactments: No levity facing the eastern gate, aligned with the Holy of Holies; no entering the Temple Mount with staff, shoes, money belt, or dust on one's feet; no shortcut through it, and spitting all the more so. Temple blessings ended 'until everlasting'; when the heretics claimed there is only one world, the Sages instituted 'from everlasting to everlasting'. They also enacted greeting a friend with God's name, as Boaz did (Ruth 2:4) — 'It is time to work for the Lord; they have made void Your Torah' (Psalms 119:126).

🦁 Does an Individual Bless Over His Own Miracle?

The mishna speaks of Israel's miracles; the Gemara tests the private case
❓ For a miracle of the many we bless — but for a miracle of an individual we do not?
↓
🦁 A man walking by the Euphrates was attacked by a lion and miraculously saved. Rava told him: whenever you reach that spot, bless 'Who performed a miracle for me in this place'.
↓
💧 Mar son of Ravina, thirsty in a valley of willows, had a spring created for him. Later in the market of Meḥoza a wild camel attacked; a wall split open and he took shelter.
↓
🔁 At the willows he blessed 'Who did a miracle for me in the willows and with the camel'; at Meḥoza, 'with the camel and in the willows'.
↓
Miracle of the many
Everyone is obligated to bless
Miracle of an individual
He alone is obligated to bless

🗺️ The Baraita's Tour of Miracle Sites

Each place on the list comes with a verse or a midrash
1️⃣ Crossings of the sea and of the Jordan — explicit verses (Exodus 14:22; Joshua 3:17)
↓
2️⃣ Streams of Arnon — 'Vahev in Sufa' (Numbers 21:14): Emorites hid in caves to ambush Israel; the Ark flattened the mountains, crushing them; the lepers Et and Hev saw the blood flowing and Israel sang
↓
3️⃣ Stones of Elgavish — stones that stopped 'because of a man' (Moses, when the hail ceased) and fell 'because of a man' (Joshua, at the descent of Beit Ḥoron)
↓
4️⃣ Og's rock — he uprooted a mountain three parasangs wide to hurl at Israel's camp; God sent grasshoppers to bore through it and it fell on his neck; his teeth grew out and held it ('You lengthened the teeth of the wicked', Reish Lakish on Psalms 3:8); Moses, ten cubits tall, leapt ten cubits with a ten-cubit axe and struck his ankle
↓
5️⃣ The rock Moses sat on (Exodus 17:12); Lot's wife — a tragedy, so one says 'the true Judge', and for Lot 'Who remembers the righteous', for even in His anger God remembered Abraham (Genesis 19:29)
↓
6️⃣ Jericho's wall 'swallowed in its place' — the verse says it fell, but since its width equaled its height, merely falling would have changed nothing; it sank

🚢 Four Who Must Give Thanks

Rav Yehuda's list from Psalm 107, and how the thanksgiving is to be said
Rav Yehuda
In Rav's name: four must give thanks — seafarers, desert travelers, one who was sick and recovered, and one released from prison. Each is in Psalm 107: they cry to the Lord in their trouble, He delivers them, and 'they thank the Lord for His kindness'.
Rav Yehuda
The blessing: בָּרוּךְ גּוֹמֵל חֲסָדִים טוֹבִים — Blessed is He Who bestows good kindnesses.
Abaye
And he must give thanks before ten, for 'let them exalt Him in the congregation of the people' (Psalms 107:32).
Mar Zutra
And two of them must be Sages: 'and praise Him in the assembly of elders'.
Rav Ashi
Say all of them must be Sages! — The verse says 'congregation of the people', not 'of elders'. Then say ten ordinary people plus two Sages! — The difficulty stands.
Story
Rav Yehuda fell ill and recovered. Rav Ḥana of Baghdad and the Sages said: 'Blessed is the Merciful Who gave you to us and not to the dust.' He replied: you have exempted me. There were ten present, and he answered amen — which counts as blessing himself.
Rav Yehuda's other lists: Three need protection: the sick, a bridegroom, and a bride (a baraita adds a woman in childbirth; some add a mourner, and Torah scholars at night). Three things that, prolonged, lengthen one's days: prayer, the meal at one's table, and time in the bathroom — and the Gemara immediately asks whether prolonging prayer is really a virtue.
