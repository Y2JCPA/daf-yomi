Berakhot 31 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
Why did Rav Ashi break a cup of valuable white glass at his son's wedding feast?
A. To fulfill the custom of breaking a glass under the wedding canopy
B. Because he saw the Sages were excessively joyous and wanted to sober them
C. As a sign of mourning for the Temple during the meal
D. Because the cup had become ritually impure
Correct: B — The Gemara relates that Rav Ashi saw the Sages 'excessively joyous' and broke the cup before them, and they became sad — a way of restraining unbounded laughter in this world.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to the baraita, from what atmosphere should one begin to pray?
A. Directly from a court case, so justice is fresh in mind
B. From deliberation over an unresolved halakhic question
C. From a conclusive halakha (halakha pesuka) and the joy of a mitzva
D. From lighthearted conversation that relaxes the mind
Correct: C — One may not pray directly from judgment or from an unsettled halakhic debate, nor from sorrow, laziness, laughter or idle matters, but from a conclusive halakha that leaves no room for further deliberation and from the joy of a mitzva.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
What does the Tosefta derive from Solomon's phrase 'to hear the song and the prayer' (I Kings 8:28)?
A. That prayer must be sung aloud in the Temple
B. That one should first praise God and only afterward request his needs
C. That personal requests may never be added to prayer
D. That the Amida must be recited three times a day
Correct: B — 'Song' is praise and 'prayer' is request; hence one does not insert requests after emet veyatziv, but begins with praise and only then asks for his needs. After the Amida, however, one may add even the equivalent of the Yom Kippur confession.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
How does Rabbi Akiva refute Rabbi Yishmael's reading that a sota found innocent, if barren, will be granted children?
A. Barren women would deliberately seclude themselves in order to be granted children
B. The verse speaks only of the priest, not the woman
C. Hannah was never actually barren
D. The sota waters were never administered in practice
Correct: A — Rabbi Akiva argues that if innocence alone brought children, every barren woman would go seclude herself; rather the verse promises easier childbirth — tall instead of short, fair instead of dark, two instead of one. On his view Hannah's double 'im ra'o tireh' is simply ordinary human language.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
What did Shmuel say Hannah meant by praying for 'an offspring of men' (zera anashim)?
A. A son who would be inconspicuous among men
B. A son equal to Moses and Aaron
C. A son who would anoint two men as kings — Saul and David
D. A son who would be neither too tall nor too short
Correct: C — Rav said 'a man among men'; Shmuel said an offspring who would anoint two men, Saul and David; Rabbi Yoḥanan said one weighed against Moses and Aaron (Psalms 99:6); the Rabbis said one inconspicuous among men, which Rav Dimi spelled out as neither too tall nor short, smart nor foolish.
