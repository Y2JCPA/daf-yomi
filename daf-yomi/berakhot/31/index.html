📖 Overview: How to Approach Prayer

Berakhot 31a–31b
וְחַנָּה הִיא מְדַבֶּרֶת עַל לִבָּהּ, רַק שְׂפָתֶיהָ נָּעוֹת, וְקוֹלָהּ לֹא יִשָּׁמֵעַ
The scene: The daf opens with sages breaking precious cups at weddings to check unrestrained laughter, and Rabbi Shimon ben Yoḥai's rule that one may not fill his mouth with mirth in this world. From there the Gemara turns to the atmosphere from which one rises to pray: not judgment or open halakhic debate, not sorrow, laziness or frivolity, but a conclusive halakha and the joy of a mitzva. A Tosefta then derives the structure of prayer from Daniel, David and Solomon, and Rav Hamnuna shows how many halakhot flow from the single verse describing Hannah's silent prayer. The amud closes with Hannah's bold words to Eli and to God, and the story of young Samuel ruling in the presence of his teacher.

🙏 From What State Does One Rise to Pray?

Two baraitot on the atmosphere that should precede the Amida, and how to take leave of a friend

Not from…

  • Judgment (דִּין) or deliberation over an unsettled halakha — they distract
  • Sorrow (עַצְבוּת) or laziness
  • Laughter, conversation, frivolity, or purposeless matters

Rather from…

  • A conclusive halakha (הֲלָכָה פְּסוּקָה) that leaves no room for further debate
  • The joy of a mitzva (שִׂמְחָה שֶׁל מִצְוָה)
  • The Sages acted like our mishna (gravity); Rav Ashi acted like the baraita (a conclusive halakha)

Taking leave of a friend

  • Not from conversation, laughter or idle talk, but from a matter of halakha
  • The early prophets ended with words of praise and consolation
  • Mari, grandson of Rav Huna: this way he will remember you
  • Rav Kahana escorted Rav Shimi bar Ashi to the palm grove of Babylonia and learned there that the palms date from Adam's decree (Jeremiah 2:6)
The sages and laughter: Rav Ashi broke a cup of costly white glass at his son's wedding when the Sages grew too merry. Rav Hamnuna Zuti, asked to sing, sang 'Woe unto us, for we shall die,' with the chorus 'Where is Torah and where is mitzva that protect us?' Rabbi Yoḥanan in the name of Rabbi Shimon ben Yoḥai: one may not fill his mouth with laughter in this world — only 'then' (Psalms 126:2), when the nations say 'the Lord has done great things.' Reish Lakish never did so again after hearing this.

⚖️ What Counts as a 'Conclusive Halakha'?

Three amoraim offer a model ruling that settles the mind before prayer
1️⃣ The Gemara asks: what is an example of a הֲלָכָה פְּסוּקָה?
↓
2️⃣ Abaye: the ruling of Rabbi Zeira — the daughters of Israel were stringent upon themselves, so that even for a drop of blood the size of a mustard seed a woman sits seven clean days
↓
3️⃣ Rava: the ruling of Rav Hoshaya — a person may use artifice and bring his grain into the courtyard still in its chaff, so his animal may eat it exempt from tithes
↓
4️⃣ If you wish: the ruling of Rav Huna citing Rabbi Zeira — one who lets blood from a consecrated animal, benefit from that blood is forbidden and one who uses it commits me'ila
↓
5️⃣ Each is a settled, universally accepted ruling — it focuses the mind without dragging it into argument
Rabbi Akiva's custom: Rabbi Yehuda taught: with the congregation Rabbi Akiva would shorten his prayer and finish, to avoid burdening the public. Alone, one would leave him in one corner and find him in another — because of his many bows and prostrations.

🪟 The Tosefta Learns Prayer From Daniel, David, Solomon and Hannah

A series of 'I might have thought…' resolved by the prayers of biblical figures
I might have thought…Already articulated by…Halakha
One may pray anywhereDaniel: 'open windows facing Jerusalem' (Daniel 6:11) — Rabbi Ḥiyya bar AbbaAlways pray in a house with windows
One may pray all day longDaniel: 'three times a day he knelt'There are fixed prayers
Fixed prayer began only in exileDaniel: 'just as he had done before'It predates the exile
One may face any directionDaniel: 'facing Jerusalem'Pray toward Jerusalem
All three prayers may be said at onceDavid: 'evening and morning and noon' (Psalms 55:18)Each prayer at its time
One may pray aloudHannah: 'her voice could not be heard' (I Samuel 1:13)The Amida is silent
Ask one's needs first, praise afterSolomon: 'to hear the song and the prayer' (I Kings 8:28) — song = praise, prayer = requestPraise first; no requests after emet veyatziv until the Amida's request blessings
After the Amida: Rav Ḥiyya bar Ashi citing Rav: although personal requests belong in 'Who listens to prayer', after finishing the Amida one may add requests without limit — even the equivalent of the Yom Kippur confession.

🕯️ Rav Hamnuna: 'How Many Great Halakhot From the Verses of Hannah!'

Each phrase of I Samuel 1:13–17 yields a rule of prayer or of human conduct
📜 וְחַנָּה הִיא מְדַבֶּרֶת עַל לִבָּהּ — 'Hannah spoke in her heart': one who prays must focus his heart
↓
👄 'Only her lips moved': one must enunciate the words with his lips, not merely think them
↓
🤫 'Her voice could not be heard': it is forbidden to raise one's voice in the Amida
↓
🍷 'Eli thought her to be drunk': a drunk person is forbidden to pray — Rabbi Elazar adds: one who prays drunk is like an idolater, from bat beliya'al / benei beliya'al (Deut. 13:14)
↓
Rabbi Elazar on Eli
'How long will you be drunk?' — one who sees something unseemly in another must rebuke him. 'Go in peace… may God grant your request' — one who suspected wrongly must appease and even bless the other.
Rabbi Elazar on Hannah
'I have drunk neither wine nor liquor' — one falsely suspected must inform the suspecter of his innocence. 'No, my master' (Ulla): you are no master in this matter, and the Divine Spirit does not rest on you.
Lord of Hosts: Rabbi Elazar: from Creation no one called God צְבָאוֹת until Hannah. She argued: of all the hosts You created, is it hard to give me one son? Like a poor man who pushes into the king's feast: from this whole banquet, is one slice of bread too much?

👦 Samuel Rules Before His Teacher — and Hannah Intervenes

Rabbi Elazar reads 'for this youth I prayed' (I Samuel 1:27) as a plea for the boy's life
Eli
They slaughtered the bull — call a priest to come and slaughter it.
Samuel
Why search for a priest? Slaughter of an offering by a non-priest is valid! שְׁחִיטָה בְּזָר כְּשֵׁרָה
Eli
How do you know this?
Samuel
Does it say 'and the priest shall slaughter'? It says 'and the priests shall offer' — only from receiving the blood onward is it a priestly mitzva.
Eli
You have spoken well. But you have ruled in the presence of your teacher, and whoever rules before his teacher is liable to death.
Hannah
'I am the woman who stood here with you' — do not punish the child born of my prayer.
Eli
Let me punish him, and I will pray for mercy that God give you a greater son than this one.
Hannah
'For this youth I prayed' — I want no other.
Also from Hannah: Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi: 'who stood here with you' teaches that it is forbidden to sit within four cubits of one who is praying. And Rabbi Elazar: Hannah spoke impertinently toward Heaven — 'she prayed on the Lord' — as did Elijah at Carmel: 'You have turned their hearts backward.'
