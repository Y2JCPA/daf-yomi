📖 Overview: Afflictions of Love

Berakhot 5a–5b
חֲבִיבִין עָלֶיךָ יִסּוּרִין? לֹא הֵן וְלֹא שְׂכָרָן. הַב לִי יְדָךְ
The scene: The daf continues the theme of Shema on one's bed: Abaye requires even a Torah scholar to say at least one verse of mercy, and Rabbi Yitzḥak compares the bedtime Shema to a double-edged sword that keeps demons at bay. Reish Lakish's four-step program for subduing the evil inclination leads into the daf's central subject: suffering. If afflictions come, examine your deeds; if nothing is found, suspect neglect of Torah; if still nothing, these are afflictions of love, which cleanse like salt and through which Torah, Eretz Yisrael, and the World-to-Come are given. Three sickbed scenes with Rabbi Yoḥanan and the story of Rav Huna's soured wine show how the Sages lived these ideas. The daf ends with Abba Binyamin's practices concerning prayer and the bed.

⚔️ Reish Lakish: Four Weapons Against the Evil Inclination

Rabbi Levi bar Ḥama in the name of Rabbi Shimon ben Lakish, reading Psalm 4:5 phrase by phrase
1️⃣ 'Tremble, and do not sin' (רִגְזוּ וְאַל תֶּחֱטָאוּ) — always incite the good inclination against the evil one. If it wins, good; if not...
↓
2️⃣ 'Say to your heart' (אִמְרוּ בִלְבַבְכֶם) — engage in Torah study. If it wins, good; if not...
↓
3️⃣ 'Upon your bed' (עַל מִשְׁכַּבְכֶם) — recite Shema, accepting the yoke of Heaven and reward and punishment. If it wins, good; if not...
↓
4️⃣ 'And be still, Selah' (וְדֹמּוּ סֶלָה) — remind yourself of the day of death
Same teacher, same daf: Reish Lakish also reads Exodus 24:12 — 'the tablets' are the Ten Commandments, 'the Torah' is Scripture, 'the mitzva' is Mishna, 'that I have written' is Prophets and Writings, 'to teach them' is Talmud — teaching that all of these were given to Moses at Sinai. And Rabbi Yitzḥak: one who recites Shema on his bed holds a double-edged sword (Ps. 149:6), and demons keep away from him (Job 5:7).

🔍 If Suffering Comes: Rava's Diagnostic

Rava (some say Rav Ḥisda) on how to read one's own afflictions
😣 A person sees that suffering has come upon him
↓
🔎 Let him examine his deeds — 'Let us search and examine our ways and return to the Lord' (Lam. 3:40)
↓
Found a transgression
The suffering is punishment for it — and a call to return
Found nothing
Attribute it to neglect of Torah study (בִּטּוּל תּוֹרָה) — 'Happy is the man whom You afflict, Lord, and teach from Your Torah' (Ps. 94:12)
↓
💝 Attributed it to neglect of Torah and still found nothing? Then know these are afflictions of love — 'For whom the Lord loves, He rebukes' (Prov. 3:12)
↓
Rav Huna: whom God delights in, He crushes with suffering (Isa. 53:10) — but only if accepted knowingly, like a guilt-offering brought willingly. The reward: 'he shall see offspring, lengthen his days', and his learning endures
Rabbi Yoḥanan's warning: One who could study Torah and does not — God brings hideous afflictions that trouble him: 'I was silent from good, and my pain was strong' (Ps. 39:3), and 'good' means Torah (Prov. 4:2). Rabbi Zeira adds: unlike a human seller, God gave Israel the Torah and rejoiced.

💝 What Are 'Afflictions of Love'?

Two definitions from a pair of amoraim, then Rabbi Yoḥanan's broader view and his exclusions

No neglect of Torah

  • One of Rabbi Ya'akov bar Idi and Rabbi Aḥa bar Ḥanina
  • Afflictions of love are those that still allow Torah study
  • 'Happy is the man whom You afflict... and teach from Your Torah' (Ps. 94:12)

No neglect of prayer

  • The other of the pair
  • Afflictions of love are those that still allow one to pray
  • 'Blessed is God Who did not turn away my prayer' (Ps. 66:20)

Rabbi Yoḥanan: both

  • Even afflictions that stop Torah and prayer are of love — 'whom the Lord loves, He rebukes'
  • Read 'teach him' as 'teach us': the Torah teaches their value a fortiori from a slave freed for a tooth or an eye
  • But leprosy and children are not afflictions of love — leprosy when visible or in Eretz Yisrael (though still an 'altar of atonement'); children meaning one who has none, for children who died can be afflictions of love
Reish Lakish and Rabbi Shimon ben Yoḥai: 'Covenant' is said of salt (Lev. 2:13) and of afflictions (Deut. 28:69): as salt sweetens meat, afflictions cleanse a person's sins. And three good gifts were given to Israel only through suffering — Torah (Ps. 94:12), Eretz Yisrael (Deut. 8:5–7), and the World-to-Come (Prov. 6:23).

🤝 'Is Your Suffering Dear to You?'

Three sickbed visits with the same script — and one unexpected reason to weep
R' Yoḥanan
To his sick student Rabbi Ḥiyya bar Abba: Is your suffering dear to you (חֲבִיבִין עָלֶיךָ יִסּוּרִין)?
R' Ḥiyya
Neither it nor its reward (לֹא הֵן וְלֹא שְׂכָרָן).
R' Yoḥanan
Give me your hand (הַב לִי יְדָךְ). — He gave it, and Rabbi Yoḥanan stood him up.
Gemara
When Rabbi Yoḥanan himself fell ill, Rabbi Ḥanina asked him the same question and raised him. Why could Rabbi Yoḥanan not raise himself? A prisoner cannot free himself from prison (אֵין חָבוּשׁ מַתִּיר עַצְמוֹ מִבֵּית הָאֲסוּרִים).
R' Yoḥanan
Visiting Rabbi Elazar in a dark room, he bared his arm and light filled the house. Seeing him weep: Why cry? If for Torah you did not master — 'one who does much and one who does little' are equal if the heart is directed to Heaven. If for livelihood — not everyone merits two tables. If for children — this is the bone of my tenth son.
R' Elazar
I weep for this beauty of yours that will decay in the earth (לְהַאי שׁוּפְרָא דְּבָלֵי בְּעַפְרָא).
R' Yoḥanan
For that you may certainly weep. — And they both wept. Then: Is your suffering dear to you? Neither it nor its reward. Give me your hand — and he stood him up.
Rav Huna's vinegar: Four hundred barrels of Rav Huna's wine soured. The Sages told him to examine his deeds — 'Is God suspected of judging without justice?' They had heard he withheld his tenant farmer's share of the vines. 'He steals everything from me!' 'One who steals from a thief still tastes theft.' He undertook to give the share — and the vinegar turned back into wine, or the price of vinegar rose to that of wine.

🛏️ Abba Binyamin: Prayer, the Bed, and Waiting for a Friend

'All my life I took pains over two things' — and a third teaching about two who enter to pray
TeachingWhat it meansSource and consequence
'My prayer should be before my bed'Not literally in front of the bed — one prays with nothing between himself and the wall, like Hezekiah (Isa. 38:2). Rather: prayer adjacent to going to bedRav Yehuda in the name of Rav, or Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi, on facing the wall
'My bed should lie north to south'The Shekhina rests along the east–west axis of the Temple, so the bed is turned away from that lineRabbi Yitzḥak: he will have sons — 'whose belly You fill with Your treasure [tzfunekha]' (Ps. 17:14); Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak: his wife will not miscarry (Gen. 25:24)
Two enter to pray; one finishes and leaves the other aloneHis prayer is torn up before his face — 'You who tear your soul in your anger' (Job 18:4) — and he causes the Shekhina to depart from Israel: 'the Rock will be moved from its place''Rock' is the Holy One (Deut. 32:18). And if he waits for his fellow? The daf ends by asking what his reward is
Takeaway: Abba Binyamin's private disciplines — when to pray, how to sleep, and not abandoning a fellow at prayer — all orient daily life toward the Divine Presence.
