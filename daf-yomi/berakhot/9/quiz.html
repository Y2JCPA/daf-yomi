Berakhot 9 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to Rav Yitzḥak bar Yosef, how did Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi actually treat Rabbi Shimon's view that Shema may be recited just after dawn?
A. He ruled it as the halakha for everyone
B. He rejected it entirely
C. He relied on it only in exigent circumstances, for Sages who had slept through the night after a wedding
D. He permitted it only if one also recites hashkivenu
Correct: C — The ruling was inferred from an incident: two Sages got drunk at his son's wedding and woke after dawn, and he said Rabbi Shimon is worthy to rely upon in exigent circumstances.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
Why does Rabbi Elazar ben Azarya hold that the Paschal lamb may be eaten only until midnight?
A. Because 'in haste' refers to Israel leaving in the morning
B. Because of a verbal analogy: 'on that night' in the eating verse parallels 'on that night' when the firstborn were struck at midnight
C. Because it may not be eaten on a second night
D. Because sacrifices are normally eaten by day
Correct: B — He links 'they shall eat the meat on that night' (Exodus 12:8) to 'I will pass through Egypt on that night' (12:12), and the plague of the firstborn occurred at midnight.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to the school of Rabbi Yannai, why did God use the pleading word 'na' in asking Israel to borrow silver and gold from Egypt?
A. Because Israel was afraid of the Egyptians
B. So that Abraham would not say God kept the promise of enslavement but not the promise of great possessions
C. Because the Egyptians refused to give the vessels
D. Because Moses did not want to ask
Correct: B — God 'begged' so that the righteous Abraham could not claim that only the harsh part of the Covenant between the Pieces was fulfilled; Israel itself would have gladly left empty-handed.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
What does the mishna's sign 'between sky-blue and white' refer to, according to the Gemara?
A. Piles of white wool and sky-blue wool
B. The sky-blue and white strings within one's ritual fringes
C. The color of the sky at dawn
D. A wolf and a dog
Correct: B — Piles of wool can be told apart even at night, so the mishna must mean distinguishing the tekhelet strings from the white strings in the tzitzit.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
How does Rav Ashi resolve the problem that 'Lord, open my lips' seems to interrupt between redemption and prayer?
A. It should be said only in the evening prayer
B. It should be said only in the afternoon prayer
C. Since the Sages fixed it as part of prayer, it counts as an extended prayer, just as hashkivenu is an extended redemption
D. It should be omitted entirely
Correct: C — Rav Ashi says the verse may be said before every prayer; once instituted by the Sages it is part of the prayer itself, exactly as hashkivenu is treated as an extension of the redemption blessing.
