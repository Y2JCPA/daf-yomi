📖 Overview: Until Dawn, the Paschal Lamb, and the Morning Shema

Berakhot 9a–9b
מֵאֵימָתַי קוֹרִין אֶת שְׁמַע בְּשַׁחֲרִית? מִשֶּׁיַּכִּיר בֵּין תְּכֵלֶת לְלָבָן
The scene: The daf finishes the discussion of reciting the evening Shema around dawn: Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi relied on Rabbi Shimon only in exigent circumstances, for two Sages who slept through the night after his son's wedding. The Gemara then reads the story of Rabban Gamliel's sons closely and asks why the mishna omits eating the Paschal lamb from the mitzvot that extend until dawn, which opens the classic dispute between Rabbi Elazar ben Azarya and Rabbi Akiva over 'on that night' and 'in haste'. Aggada on the Exodus follows: why God 'begged' Israel to borrow Egyptian vessels, and what 'I will be that I will be' meant. Amud bet brings the new mishna on the morning Shema — from when one can tell sky-blue from white — and the practice of the vatikin, who finish Shema at sunrise so that redemption is juxtaposed to prayer.

🍷 Rabbi Shimon Is Worthy to Rely On — in a Pinch

Closing the question of Shema just before and after dawn or sunrise
1️⃣ The time just before sunrise is really day; it is called 'night' only because some people are still asleep — so it can count as 'when you lie down'
↓
2️⃣ Rabbi Aḥa son of Rabbi Ḥanina in the name of Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi: the halakha follows Rabbi Shimon in the name of Rabbi Akiva. Rabbi Zeira adds: provided he does not say הַשְׁכִּיבֵנוּ, 'help us lie down', in the morning
↓
3️⃣ Rav Yitzḥak bar Yosef arrived from Eretz Yisrael: this ruling was never said explicitly — it was inferred from a story
↓
4️⃣ A pair of Sages got drunk at the wedding of Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi's son and slept past dawn without reciting the evening Shema
↓
5️⃣ He told them: כְּדַאי הוּא רַבִּי שִׁמְעוֹן לִסְמוֹךְ עָלָיו בִּשְׁעַת הַדְּחָק — Rabbi Shimon is worthy to rely upon in exigent circumstances
Rabban Gamliel's sons: They surely knew their father's view. Their real question: do the Rabbis fundamentally disagree, so that the halakha follows the many? Or do they agree, saying 'until midnight' only to distance people from transgression? He answered: they agree with me, and you are obligated. Even according to the Rabbis, the mitzva lasts until dawn.

🐑 Until When May the Paschal Lamb Be Eaten?

Our mishna omits it from the list — Rav Yosef: the mishna is Rabbi Elazar ben Azarya, the baraita is Rabbi Akiva

Rabbi Elazar ben Azarya — until midnight

  • 'They shall eat the meat on that night' (Exodus 12:8)
  • Verbal analogy: 'I will pass through Egypt on that night' (12:12) — the firstborn were struck at midnight
  • Second night excluded by 'it shall not remain until morning' (12:10); every plain 'morning' is the first morning
  • 'Haste' = the haste of the Egyptians at midnight
  • Parallel tanna: Rabbi Eliezer — slaughter in the evening, eat from sunset, burn at 'the time you left Egypt'

Rabbi Akiva — until dawn

  • 'You will eat it in haste' (Exodus 12:11) — until the time of haste, i.e., morning
  • 'That night' teaches it is eaten at night, not by day like other offerings
  • 'That' excludes a second night, lest one compare it to peace-offerings eaten over two days
  • 'Haste' = the haste of Israel in the morning
  • Parallel tanna: Rabbi Yehoshua — keep eating until 'the time you left Egypt'
Rabbi Abba: All agree Israel was redeemed in the evening ('took you out of Egypt at night', Deuteronomy 16:1) and actually left by day ('on the day after the Paschal offering… with a high hand', Numbers 33:3). The redemption began for them in the evening.

💰 'Speak, Please' — Why God Begged Israel to Borrow

Aggada of the Exodus: the school of Rabbi Yannai, Rabbi Ami, and Reish Lakish
R' Yannai
'Speak, please (נָא) in the ears of the people… borrow silver and gold vessels' (Exodus 11:2). Na is only supplication. Why would God plead?
Answer
So that Abraham, that righteous man, would not say: 'they will be enslaved and afflicted' You fulfilled, but 'afterward they will leave with great possessions' (Genesis 15:13–14) You did not.
Israel
If only we could get out ourselves! Like a prisoner told 'tomorrow we free you with much money' who says: please, release me today and I ask for nothing.
R' Ami
'They gave them what they requested' (Exodus 12:36) — against their will. Some say against Egypt's will ('she who tarries at home divides the spoils', Psalms 68:13); some say against Israel's will, because of the burden of carrying it.
R' Ami
'They emptied Egypt' — they made it like a trap with no grain for bait.
Reish Lakish
Like an abyss in the sea with no fish.
Double language: 'I will be that I will be' (Exodus 3:14): I was with you in this enslavement and I will be with you in the enslavement of the kingdoms. Moses: Master of the Universe, enough for the trouble in its hour! So God said: tell them only 'I will be has sent me'. Likewise Rabbi Abbahu on Elijah's 'Answer me, Lord, answer me' (I Kings 18:37): answer me with fire from heaven, and answer me by turning their hearts so they will not call it sorcery.

🌅 From When Is the Morning Shema Recited?

The new mishna and the baraita's signs of daybreak
OpinionSign that it is dayNote
Mishna (first tanna)When one can distinguish sky-blue (תְּכֵלֶת) from whiteGemara: not piles of wool, which one can tell apart at night — the blue and white strings of one's tzitzit
Rabbi EliezerBetween sky-blue and leek-greenA finer distinction
Rabbi Meir (baraita)Between a wolf and a dogTwo similar animals
Rabbi Akiva (baraita)Between a donkey and a wild donkeyTwo similar animals
Aḥerim (baraita)When one can recognize an acquaintance at four cubitsRav Huna: the halakha follows Aḥerim; Abaye: for tefillin — but for Shema, follow the vatikin
Until when?Mishna: until sunrise; Rabbi Yehoshua: until three hours, when kings riseAfter that one 'loses nothing' — he is like one reading Torah
The vatikin: Rabbi Yoḥanan: the vatikin would finish Shema exactly with sunrise, to juxtapose redemption to prayer and pray by day. Rabbi Zeira's verse: 'They shall fear You with the sun' (Psalms 72:5).

🔗 Juxtaposing Redemption to Prayer

A promise, a complaint, and a technical problem with 'Lord, open my lips'
📜 Rabbi Yosei ben Elyakim in the name of the holy community of Jerusalem: one who juxtaposes redemption to prayer is not harmed all day
↓
❓ Rabbi Zeira: I did, and I was harmed — I had to bring a myrtle to the king's palace! Reply: that was a privilege — Rabbi Yoḥanan says run to greet even gentile kings, to learn to distinguish them from the kings of Israel. And Rav Beruna once juxtaposed them and laughed with joy all day
↓
❓ But Rabbi Yoḥanan says prayer opens with 'Lord, open my lips' (Psalms 51:17) — isn't that an interruption between redemption and prayer?
↓
R' Elazar (1)
Say it only in the evening prayer. Rejected: Rabbi Yoḥanan calls one who juxtaposes evening redemption to evening prayer a 'son of the World-to-Come'
R' Elazar (2)
Say it only in the afternoon prayer
Rav Ashi
Say it in all of them: since the Sages fixed it in prayer, it is like an extended prayer — just as hashkivenu is an extended redemption
↓
✅ Why is 'May the words of my mouth' (Psalms 19:15) said after the eighteen blessings? Rabbi Yehuda son of Rabbi Shimon ben Pazi: David said it only after eighteen psalms — 'Happy is the man' and 'Why are the nations in an uproar' are one chapter, as also shown by David's 103 chapters before his first 'Halleluya' (Psalms 104:35)
