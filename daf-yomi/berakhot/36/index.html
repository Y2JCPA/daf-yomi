📖 Overview: Borderline Foods

Berakhot 36a–36b
כֵּיוָן דְּאִית לֵיהּ הֲנָאָה מִינֵּיהּ — בָּעֵי בָּרוֹכֵי
The scene: This daf is a parade of hard cases. Olive oil swallowed in beet water for a sore throat, plain flour, the soft heart of a young palm, the leaves and buds of a caper-bush, dry peppers and ginger, a pot of pounded grain sweetened with honey: for each, the Amoraim argue which blessing fits, or whether any is needed. Two principles keep surfacing. First, wherever a person derives pleasure, a blessing is required, even if his motive was medicinal. Second, what people plant a tree for decides whether a part of it is a fruit. The caper-bush pulls the discussion into the laws of orla, where Rava works through three definitions of 'protection for the fruit' before an experiment settles the matter.

🌴 Heart of Palm: Is It a Fruit?

Shmuel compliments his student, then the Gemara sides against the student
Rav Yehuda
בּוֹרֵא פְּרִי הָאֲדָמָה — it is a fruit.
Shmuel
שֶׁהַכֹּל נִהְיֶה בִּדְבָרוֹ — since it will ultimately harden into the tree.
Shmuel
Shinnana! Your view is the reasonable one: a radish also hardens eventually, yet we say ha'adama over it.
Gemara
Not so. People plant a radish for the soft radish; nobody plants a palm for its heart.
Challenge
Then anything not planted for that purpose gets no proper blessing? The caper-bush is planted for its fruit, yet the mishna gives ha'adama over its leaves and young fronds!
R' Naḥman b.Y.
People do plant a caper-bush with its leaves in mind; nobody plants a palm with its heart in mind. Although Shmuel praised Rav Yehuda, the halakha follows Shmuel.
The test: A part of a plant is 'fruit' for blessing purposes when people cultivate the plant with that part in mind — נָטְעִי אִינָשֵׁי אַדַּעְתָּא.

🌿 Rav on the Caper-Bush: Why Not Just Say 'Halakha Like Rabbi Akiva'?

Every extra word in Rav's ruling turns out to be load-bearing
📜 Rav Yehuda in Rav's name: an orla caper-bush outside Eretz Yisrael — throw away the berries (אֲבִיּוֹנוֹת) and eat the buds (קַפְרֵיסִין)
↓
❓ So berries are fruit and buds are not? But the mishna says ha'etz over both berries and buds!
↓
💡 Rav follows Rabbi Akiva: R' Eliezer tithes fronds, berries and buds; R' Akiva tithes only the berries, 'because it alone is fruit'
↓
Say 'halakha like R' Akiva'?
Then one might apply it even in Eretz Yisrael. Rav teaches: whoever is lenient in the Land, the halakha follows him outside the Land — but not inside
Say 'like R' Akiva outside the Land'?
One might limit that to tree-tithes, which are rabbinic even in the Land, and still decree on orla, which is Torah law in the Land. Rav teaches: even for orla
↓
🧑‍⚖️ Ravina to Mar bar Rav Ashi, seen eating buds: if you follow lenient R' Akiva, follow Beit Shammai, who are more lenient still — they treat the caper as uncertain orla, and uncertain orla outside the Land is permitted
↓
✅ Answer: Rabbi Akiva against Rabbi Eliezer — we follow him. Beit Shammai against Beit Hillel — 'it is not a mishna' at all
Beit Shammai's oddity: They call the caper-bush a diverse kind in a vineyard (a vegetable) yet obligated in orla (a tree). The Gemara explains: they are uncertain, and rule stringently in both directions.

🛡️ Rava's Three Attempts to Define 'Protection for the Fruit'

Leviticus 19:23 'et piryo' includes what is secondary to the fruit — so why are caper buds permitted?
1️⃣ Rava: a 'shomer' must exist both while attached and after picking; the caper bud falls off when picked. Abaye objects: a pomegranate flower does not join the fruit for impurity, yet rinds, flower, nutshells and pits are all obligated in orla — the criteria differ
↓
2️⃣ Rava: a 'shomer' must still be present when the fruit ripens; the bud drops earlier. Objection: Rav Naḥman in Rabba bar Avuh's name forbids orla date-coverings, which protect only the young fruit
↓
3️⃣ Deflection: Rav Naḥman follows Rabbi Yosei, who calls even the grape-bud (סְמָדַר) a fruit. Rav Shimi of Neharde'a: but in the Sabbatical-year mishna even the Rabbis forbid cutting 'all other trees from when fruit emerges'
↓
4️⃣ Rava, finally: a 'shomer' is something whose removal kills the fruit. Remove a caper bud and the fruit lives
↓
5️⃣ The experiment: they removed a pomegranate's flower and it withered; they removed the caper's flower and the fruit survived
↓
6️⃣ Halakha like Mar bar Rav Ashi: discard the berries, eat the buds. Since buds are not fruit for orla, they are not fruit for blessings either — בּוֹרֵא פְּרִי הָאֲדָמָה, not ha'etz
Rav Asi's gloss: Beit Hillel's 'vines from when they yegaru' means the unripe grape the size of a white bean — a stage the Rabbis count as fruit, unlike the earlier grape-bud.

🌶️ Three Late Disputes: Peppers, Ginger, and Sweetened Grain

Two are resolved by 'fresh vs. dry', the third by Rav and Shmuel's rule

Peppers

  • Rav Sheshet: shehakol
  • Rava: no blessing at all — consistent with his ruling that chewing peppers on Yom Kippur is exempt
  • Objection: Rabbi Meir reads 'any tree for food' to include the pepper tree, whose wood tastes like its fruit — proof that Eretz Yisrael 'lacks nothing' (Deut. 8:9)
  • Resolution: fresh peppers are food; dry ones are not

Ginger

  • Rava: chewing ginger on Yom Kippur is exempt
  • The Sages to Mareimar: but Rava himself permits Indian ginger and rules ha'adama over it!
  • Same resolution: fresh ginger gets ha'adama; dry gets nothing

Ḥavitz Kedera / Daysa

  • Plain pounded grain: all agree mezonot
  • Mixed with honey: Rav Yehuda says shehakol — the honey is primary; Rav Kahana says mezonot — the grain is primary
  • Rav Yosef: Rav Kahana is right, for Rav and Shmuel said כֹּל שֶׁיֵּשׁ בּוֹ מֵחֲמֵשֶׁת הַמִּינִין — anything containing the five grains — is mezonot
Why two statements? Rav and Shmuel said both 'anything that has of the five grains' and 'anything that is of the five grains'. Had they said only the second, one might think mixtures are excluded; the daf ends mid-explanation of why the first is needed too.
