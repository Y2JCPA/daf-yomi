QUESTION 1 OF 5
How does Rav Ashi reconcile the mishna's 'his dead laid out before him' with the baraita exempting one who eats in another house?
A. The baraita is a minority opinion and is rejected
B. 'Before him' is not literal — as long as it is incumbent on him to bury the dead, it is as if the dead lies before him
C. The exemption applies only when he turns his face and eats
D. The baraita refers to Shabbat
Correct: B — Rav Pappa limited the baraita to the case of turning one's face, but Rav Ashi read 'before him' from Abraham: 'Abraham rose from before his dead' and 'that I may bury my dead from before me' (Genesis 23:3–4) — whenever burial is incumbent on him, it is as if the dead lies before him.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
Why may one not walk within four cubits of a grave wearing tefillin and reading from a Torah scroll?
A. Because the cemetery is ritually impure
B. Because it is considered mocking the dead, who cannot perform mitzvot — 'He who mocks the poor blasphemes his Creator'
C. Because one might drop the Torah scroll
D. Because prayer is forbidden in a cemetery entirely
Correct: B — The baraita applies Proverbs 17:5 — performing a mitzva in front of the dead mocks those who can no longer perform mitzvot. The Gemara limits this to within four cubits; beyond that one is obligated.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to Ravina, what is the practical difference between the first tanna (two watchers alternate) and ben Azzai (on a boat both may pray in another corner)?
A. Whether a boat counts as a private domain
B. Whether one is concerned about mice on a boat
C. Whether prayer may be recited while seated
D. Whether a corpse may be moved on a boat
Correct: B — Ravina explains the dispute turns on mice: the first tanna fears mice everywhere, so the corpse must never be left unwatched; ben Azzai holds there is no concern for mice on a boat.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
What conclusion did Rabbi Ḥiyya's sons reach about whether their deceased father knew of their anguish?
A. The dead know nothing at all (Job 14:21)
B. The dead know everything that happens to their children
C. The dead know their own pain but not the pain of others
D. Only righteous fathers know of their sons' troubles
Correct: C — One son cited 'His sons are honored yet he shall not know it' (Job 14:21); the other cited 'in his flesh does he feel pain' (Job 14:22) and Rabbi Yitzḥak's statement that maggots pain the dead like a needle. They reconciled: the dead know their own pain but not others'.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
From which verse did Rabbi Yonatan, after retracting, derive that the dead converse with one another?
A. 'And the dead know nothing' (Ecclesiastes 9:5)
B. 'This is the land that I swore to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, saying' (Deuteronomy 34:4)
C. 'His sons are honored yet he shall not know it' (Job 14:21)
D. 'Son of a living man' (II Samuel 23:20)
Correct: B — Rabbi Shmuel bar Naḥmani reported that Rabbi Yonatan asked what 'saying' adds in Deuteronomy 34:4 — God told Moses: go tell Abraham, Isaac and Jacob that the oath I swore to you I have fulfilled for your children. This shows the dead converse.
