📖 Overview: The Havdala Flame and Ending the Chapter

Berakhot 53a–53b
אֵין מְחַזְּרִין עַל הָאוּר כְּדֶרֶךְ שֶׁמְחַזְּרִים עַל הַמִּצְוֹת
The scene: The daf is almost entirely about the havdala blessing over fire and spices. Which flame counts as having 'rested' on Shabbat, whose candle may be used, what to do with a fire glimpsed outside a city, and which fires exist for light rather than heat or honor. Spices follow the same logic: no blessing unless they are meant for fragrance. The mishna's 'until one benefits from its light' splits Rav from Rava. The Gemara then returns to the mishna's last cases — one who forgot Grace after Meals, how long digestion lasts, answering amen — and closes the chapter with oil for the hands and the verse 'you shall sanctify yourselves'.

🕯️ Which Flame 'Rested' on Shabbat?

Defining the baraita's term, then the puzzle of a flame passed from one candle to another
❓ 'Fire that rested' — rested from all labor, even permitted labor? But a baraita blesses over fire lit on Shabbat for a woman in childbirth or a dangerously ill person!
↓
✅ Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak: 'rested' means rested from labor of transgression. A lantern that burned the whole of Shabbat may be blessed at its conclusion.
↓
📜 Baraita: a gentile who lit from a Jew, or a Jew who lit from a gentile — bless. A gentile who lit from a gentile — do not bless.
↓
❓ If the problem is a flame that did not rest, a Jew lighting from a gentile also did not rest! Is the new flame a different entity? No — one who carries a flame into the public domain on Shabbat is liable, so the flame is one entity.
↓
Jew from gentile
The forbidden flame is still there, but one blesses over the permitted addition that came into being in the Jew's hand.
Gentile from gentile
By right the same should apply — but the Sages decreed because of the first gentile and the first pillar of flame kindled on Shabbat.

🏙️ Fire Seen From Outside the City

A string of baraitot on when a passer-by may use a flame for havdala
CaseRulingReason / resolution
City with a majority of gentilesDo not blessPresumed lit on Shabbat
Majority Jews, or half and halfBlessThe baraita should have said 'even half'; it said 'majority Jews' only to match the first clause
A child holding a torchCheck — bless if the child is JewishRav: it is right after sunset; an adult with fire is surely a gentile, but a child may be a Jew who just picked it up
A fire at least as thick as a furnace openingBlessA fire that size is kindled for light too
Fire of a furnace, oven, or stoveOne baraita: bless; another: do notAt the start it is only for heating; at the end its light is used
Light of a synagogue or study hallOne baraita: bless; another: do notDepends whether an important person is present (lit for honor), whether there is a caretaker who uses it, or whether there is moonlight
One blessing or many? Fire brought into the study hall: Beit Shammai — each person blesses for himself, to avoid interrupting study (the house of Rabban Gamliel would not even say 'good health' after a sneeze there). Beit Hillel — one blesses for all, for 'the splendor of the King is in the multitude of the people' (Proverbs 14:28).

🌿 Spices: Only If Meant for Fragrance

The mishna bans the candle and spices of the dead; the Gemara extends the principle

No blessing

  • Candle of the dead — lit for honor, not light (Rav: if it is carried out by day and night it is for honor; only at night, it is for light and one may bless)
  • Spices of the dead — to remove the odor
  • Rav Huna: bathroom spices and oil that removes filth
  • Rabbi Yoḥanan: incense smelled in Tiberias on Shabbat eve or Tzippori after Shabbat — presumed for perfuming garments
  • Rabbi Yosei: even a majority-Jewish town, since some burn incense for witchcraft and some for garments — the majority is not for fragrance

Blessing

  • Baraita: a scent outside a town with a Jewish majority
  • The perfumer's shop — the scent is for fragrance, so customers will come and buy
  • Sitting there all day: bless once; entering and leaving repeatedly: bless each time

Forbidden to smell

  • One walking through an idolaters' market who wants to smell the incense is a sinner

👀 'Until They Benefit From Its Light' — Literally or Potentially?

Rav and Rava read the mishna differently, and the amora'im act accordingly
Rav
Not literal benefit: if standing close he could use its light, he may bless even from a distance. Rav Ashi agrees: 'from a distance' is what we learned.
Objection
Tosefta: a candle hidden in one's lap or in a lantern, or seeing a flame without using its light, or using its light without seeing the flame — no blessing until he both sees the flame and uses its light. 'Sees but does not use' — isn't that distance?
Rav's side
No, it is a flame that is gradually dimming (עוֹמְמוֹת, spelled with an ayin, from 'the cedars could not dim it', Ezekiel 31:8). Rav Ḥisda: coals are 'glowing' if a chip placed among them ignites by itself.
Rava
'Benefit' is literal. How close? Ulla: enough to tell an issar from a pundeyon. Ḥizkiya: to tell a Tiberian weight from a Tzippori weight.
Practice
Rav Yehuda blessed on the light of Adda the servant's house, far away; Rava on Gurya bar Ḥama's house, next door; Abaye on the house of bar Avuh.
Rav
One does not seek out fire the way one seeks out other mitzvot. Rabbi Zeira: I used to search; once I heard this, I bless only if a flame comes my way.

💰 Forgot Grace: Go Back, or Bless Where You Are?

The Houses argue by parable, and the Gemara tells two stories that settle the mood
1️⃣ Rav Zevid (or Rav Dimi bar Abba): the dispute is only about one who forgot; if he skipped the blessing deliberately, all agree he must return — the mishna's word 'forgot' is precise
↓
2️⃣ Beit Hillel: by your view, one who ate atop the Temple Mount and came down must climb back up? Beit Shammai: if he forgot his purse up there he would climb for his own honor — all the more so for the honor of Heaven!
↓
3️⃣ Two students: one forgot, went back like Beit Shammai, and found a purse of gold; one acted deliberately, did not return, and was eaten by a lion
↓
4️⃣ Rabba bar bar Ḥana, traveling with a caravan, told them 'I forgot a golden dove' so they would wait; he went back, blessed, and found a golden dove — Israel is likened to a dove (Psalms 68:14), saved only by its wings, i.e. by mitzvot
Closing the chapter: Digestion: Rabbi Yoḥanan — as long as he is not hungry; Reish Lakish — as long as he is thirsty from eating, or four mil after a large meal. Amen: Rav told his son 'seize the chance and bless', yet Rabbi Yosei says the one who answers amen is greater (Rabbi Nehorai: the mighty who finish the battle) — a tannaitic dispute. No amen after schoolchildren practicing. Oil for the hands: Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak knows only the baraita on 'you shall sanctify yourselves' (Leviticus 20:26) — first waters, final waters, oil, and the blessing.
