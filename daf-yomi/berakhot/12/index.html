📖 Overview: Openings, Conclusions, and What Shema Contains

Berakhot 12a–12b
כְּלָלוֹ שֶׁל דָּבָר: הַכֹּל הוֹלֵךְ אַחַר הַחִתּוּם
The scene: The daf opens by finishing the analysis of Reish Lakish's 'single blessing' and recalling that the Ten Commandments were once read daily but were abolished to deny the heretics a talking point. The mishna's rule about long and short blessings sparks a famous dilemma: if one began a blessing thinking he held wine and concluded it correctly for beer, does the opening or the conclusion decide? The baraita's 'everything follows the conclusion' is examined and the question is left standing. A chain of teachings from Rav follows — the wording of Emet VeYatziv, how to bow, the Ten Days of Repentance, praying for others, and the atoning power of shame, illustrated by King Saul. The Gemara then explains why the Sages did not add the portion of Balak to Shema and why they did add tzitzit, and a new mishna records Ben Zoma's derivation that the Exodus must be mentioned at night.

🍷 A Blessing Begun for Wine and Finished for Beer

Do we follow the essence of the blessing or its conclusion?
✅ Obvious case: held wine, thought it beer, began intending 'by Whose word all things came to be', concluded 'fruit of the vine' — he fulfilled his obligation, since even shehakol would have worked for wine after the fact
↓
❓ Held beer, thought it wine, began intending 'fruit of the vine', concluded shehakol — do we follow the essence of the blessing (עִיקַּר בְּרָכָה) or the conclusion (חֲתִימָה)?
↓
📜 Come and hear: morning — opened 'Who forms light', closed 'Who brings on evenings' — not fulfilled; opened 'brings on evenings', closed 'forms light' — fulfilled. Evening: the reverse. General rule: everything follows the conclusion
↓
Rejection
The light blessing is different — it ends with its own full blessing, 'Blessed… Who forms the lights'. Short food blessings have no such closing
But per Rabbi Yoḥanan?
He says a blessing without mention of God's kingship is no blessing, and the closing lacks it. Answer: since (Rabba bar Ulla) the blessing mentions day and night together, the kingship at the opening covers both
↓
❓ Then what does 'general rule' add? Not our case — it adds bread and dates: ate dates thinking bread, closed with the dates blessing — fulfilled, since even the bread blessing works for dates, which also sustain. The wine/beer dilemma stays unresolved
Method: A proof from a long blessing with its own concluding formula cannot settle a question about a short one-line blessing.

😳 King Saul: The Shame That Won Forgiveness

Why the proof from Ezekiel is not enough, and where the Gemara finds a better one
כָּל הָעוֹשֶׂה דְּבַר עֲבֵירָה וּמִתְבַּיֵּישׁ בּוֹ — מוֹחֲלִין לוֹ עַל כָּל עֲוֹנוֹתָיו
The argument: Ezekiel's verse speaks to the community, and 'perhaps a community is different' — it is forgiven more easily. So the Gemara turns to Saul before his last battle: he tells Samuel that God no longer answers him 'by prophets or by dreams' (I Sam. 28:15) — but he does not mention the Urim VeTummim. Why? Because he had killed Nov, the city of priests, and was ashamed to consult an oracle that works through a priest. And the shame worked: Samuel says 'tomorrow you and your sons will be with me' — Rabbi Yoḥanan: 'in my company' among the righteous. The Rabbis find the pardon elsewhere: the Gibeonites, who hated Saul, would never have called him 'the chosen of the Lord' (II Sam. 21:6) — a Divine Voice said it.

🦁 Three Portions and the Shape of Shema

What was removed, what was never added, and what was included — and why

Ten Commandments — removed

  • Read daily by the priestly watch in the Temple
  • Shmuel and Rabbi Natan: outside the Temple they sought to read them too, but abolished it because of the grievance of the heretics (תַּרְעוֹמֶת הַמִּינִין)
  • Rabba bar bar Ḥana in Sura and Ameimar in Neharde'a tried to reinstate them; Rav Ḥisda and Rav Ashi each answered: already abolished

Balak — never added

  • The Sages wanted to include it — not for its mention of the Exodus (usury and weights mention it too)
  • Rabbi Yosei bar Avin: because of 'He couched, He lay down like a lion… who shall rouse Him?' (Num. 24:9) — an echo of 'when you lie down and when you rise'
  • Why not that one verse? Tradition: we do not split a portion Moses did not split — and the whole portion would burden the congregation (טוֹרַח צִבּוּר)

Tzitzit — included

  • Rabbi Yehuda bar Ḥaviva: it contains five things — the mitzva of tzitzit, the Exodus, the yoke of mitzvot, and warnings against heresy, licentious thoughts, and idolatry
  • 'After your hearts' = heresy ('the fool said in his heart, there is no God')
  • 'After your eyes' = licentious thoughts (Samson: 'she is upright in my eyes')
  • 'You stray after' = idolatry ('they strayed after the Be'alim')
Bonus: Rabbi Ḥelbo: the extra Shabbat blessing was the outgoing watch's wish to the incoming one — 'May He who caused His Name to dwell in this house cause love, brotherhood, peace and friendship to dwell among you'.

🌙 Mentioning the Exodus at Night: Ben Zoma vs. the Sages

The mishna and its baraita on 'all the days of your life'
R' Elazar b. A.
I am like a man of seventy, and I never merited to have the Exodus mentioned at night — until Ben Zoma expounded it.
Ben Zoma
'That you may remember the day you went out of Egypt כֹּל יְמֵי חַיֶּיךָ' (Deut. 16:3). 'The days of your life' — the days; 'all the days of your life' — the nights.
The Sages
'The days of your life' — this world; 'all' — to include the days of the Messiah.
Ben Zoma
Is the Exodus mentioned in the days of the Messiah? Jeremiah says they will 'no longer say: the Lord lives Who brought Israel up from Egypt', but rather Who brought them from the north country (Jer. 23:7–8)!
The Sages
Not that the Exodus will be uprooted — rather, redemption from the kingdoms will be primary and the Exodus secondary. Likewise 'your name shall no longer be called Jacob, but Israel' (Gen. 35:10)…
Where it stands: The Sages read 'no longer' as a shift in emphasis, not erasure; the daf ends mid-sentence with the Jacob/Israel parallel.
