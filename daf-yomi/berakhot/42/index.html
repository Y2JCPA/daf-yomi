📖 Overview: When One Blessing Covers Another

Berakhot 42a–42b
בֵּרַךְ עַל הַיַּיִן שֶׁלִּפְנֵי הַמָּזוֹן — פָּטַר אֶת הַיַּיִן שֶׁלְּאַחַר הַמָּזוֹן
The scene: The daf opens by completing Ben Zoma's answer: wine is not exempted by bread because it causes a blessing itself. Stories follow about dessert bread eaten in meal-sized quantities, and about whether one may keep eating after 'finishing' — Rav Pappa insists the rule speaks of the table being removed, and Rava keeps eating at the Exilarch's because the host's meal is not over. The halakha of when a meal ends is fixed by Rav's three 'immediately' pairs, the last being washing followed by Grace. A new mishna then lists which blessings exempt others: wine before the meal covers wine after it, bread covers appetizers, and a reclining group shares one blessing. The Gemara limits the wine rule to Shabbat and festivals, leaves Beit Shammai's position and the 'wine during the meal' question partly open, and asks whether sitting together, without reclining, makes a joint meal.

🥐 When Dessert Bread Counts as a Meal

Pat haba'ah bekisanin in two stories
1️⃣ Rav Huna ate thirteen sweet loaves, three to a kav of flour, and did not recite Grace — they were not real bread
↓
2️⃣ Rav Naḥman: עָדֵי כַּפְנָא — 'that is hunger!' Rather, anything on which others base a meal requires Grace
↓
3️⃣ At his son's wedding, Rav Yehuda heard guests reciting hamotzi over dessert bread: 'What is this tzitzi sound I hear?'
↓
4️⃣ They answered: Rabbi Mona in Rabbi Yehuda's name says hamotzi over dessert bread, and Shmuel ruled like Rabbi Mona
↓
5️⃣ Rav Yehuda: it was stated that the halakha is not like Rabbi Mona. But didn't you say in Shmuel's name that wafers can form an eiruv and get hamotzi?
↓
6️⃣ Answer: there he based his meal on them; where one has not based his meal on them, no hamotzi
Why wine is different: Ben Zoma's students asked why bread does not exempt wine. The answer: wine causes a blessing itself — it accompanies kiddush and havdala even when one does not want to drink — so it keeps its own blessing during a meal.

🍽️ When Is the Meal Over?

Three attempts to mark the end, and the one that becomes halakha

'Finished' vs. 'Removed'

  • At Rav Huna son of Rav Natan's house, Rav Pappa ate what was brought after the meal without a blessing
  • Challenged — once one finished, isn't further eating forbidden? — he replied: the rule says סִלֵּק, once the table was removed
  • At the Exilarch's, after the table was removed, Rava ate the portion sent from the house and Rabbi Zeira did not; Rava: 'we rely on the Exilarch's table' — the host's meal is not yet over

Oil After Eating

  • Rav: one accustomed to fragrant oil — the oil delays the end of his meal
  • Rav Kahana: 'we who are accustomed to oil, the oil delays it for us'
  • The Gemara: the halakha is not like any of these statements

Rav's Three 'Immediately's

  • Immediately after semikha — slaughter
  • Immediately after the blessing of redemption — the Amida
  • Immediately after washing the hands — Grace after Meals: this defines the end
  • Abaye adds: immediately after Torah scholars enter — blessing, as with Laban (Gen. 30:27) and Joseph (Gen. 39:5)
Bottom line: Neither 'finishing' nor oil marks the end of the meal; the final washing followed by Grace does.

🍷 One Blessing for All the Wine?

The Gemara narrows the mishna's first rule
R' Yoḥanan
The mishna speaks only of Shabbat and festivals, when one bases his meal on wine. The rest of the year, bless each and every cup.
R' Yehoshua b.L.
Also when leaving the bathhouse and after bloodletting, when one drinks a lot. Otherwise, every cup.
Rabba b. Mari
On a weekday I saw Rava bless wine before the meal and again after it. I told him: well done — and so said Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi.
R' Yitzḥak b.Y.
Abaye, on a festival you bless every single cup? Don't you accept Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi?
Abaye
נִמְלָךְ אֲנָא — I keep changing my mind. I never intended to base my meal on wine, so each cup is a new decision.
Dilemma
Does wine during the meal exempt wine after it? Before-meal wine is 'to drink' like after-meal wine; mid-meal wine is 'to moisten the food'. Rav and Rav Naḥman: it exempts. Rav Kahana, Rav Sheshet, Rav Huna, Rav Yehuda and all of Rav's students: it does not.
Rava
But the mishna says wine during the meal — each blesses; after the meal — one blesses for all. So after-meal wine needs its own blessing! Rav Naḥman: two independent cases — if wine came only after the meal, one blesses for all.
Key idea: Exemption depends on intent: only when one sets out to base the meal on wine does the first cup cover the last.

🛋️ Sitting Together or Reclining Together?

What turns several diners into one meal with one blessing
📜 Mishna: they sat — each blesses for himself; they reclined — one blesses for all
↓
❓ Baraita: ten walking on the road, even eating one loaf — each for himself; if they sat to eat, even each from his own loaf — one for all. Sitting suffices?!
↓
💡 Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak: the travelers had said נֵיזִיל וְנֵיכוֹל לַחְמָא בְּדוּךְ פְּלָן — 'let us go and eat in such-and-such a place'. Designating a place in advance makes it a joint meal
↓
🕯️ When Rav died, his students returned from the burial and said: let us eat bread by the Dannak River
↓
Reading A
The mishna means reclining specifically — merely sitting does not count
Reading B
Since they said 'let us go and eat in that place', it is as if they reclined
↓
🤷 It was not within their ability to resolve — until Rav Adda bar Ahava stood up (the story continues on the next daf)
The living question: Rav's own students, at their teacher's funeral meal, faced the very question his mishna raised.
