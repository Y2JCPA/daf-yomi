📖 Overview: Vegetables, Loaves, and the Etiquette of Blessing

Berakhot 39a–39b
מְבָרֵךְ וְאַחַר כָּךְ בּוֹצֵעַ
The scene: The daf opens by settling the size of the halakhic olive: a medium olive, so an olive whose pit was removed still counted. It then tells the sobering story of two students before bar Kappara who quarreled over which blessing to say first, and neither lived out the year. A cluster of amoraic rulings follows on cooked turnips, beets, dill water and the flour added to a dish. The second half turns to bread: does the blessing have to end as the loaf is broken (Rabbi Ḥiyya) or is the blessing said over a whole loaf first (Rava, whose view is the halakha)? Should one bless over a piece or a whole loaf? The daf closes with the two loaves of Shabbat, the broken matza of Pesaḥ, and Rav Ami and Rav Asi using the eiruv bread for a second mitzva.

🍗 Bar Kappara's Students: Whose Blessing Comes First?

Cabbage, Damascene plums and pullets were set before the teacher
1️⃣ Bar Kappara gave one student permission to bless. He hurried and blessed the pullet first
↓
2️⃣ His fellow student ridiculed him for grabbing the meat before the vegetables
↓
3️⃣ Bar Kappara: 'I am not angry at the one who blessed but at the one who ridiculed. If your friend is like one who never tasted meat, why mock him?'
↓
4️⃣ Then he turned: 'No, I am angry at the one who blessed. אִם חׇכְמָה אֵין כָּאן, זִקְנָה אֵין כָּאן?! If there is no wisdom here, is there no elder here to ask?'
↓
5️⃣ It was taught: both students did not live out the year
↓
6️⃣ The Gemara's lesson: was this a dispute over the blessing itself (shehakol on both, so the favorite goes first vs. ha'adama on vegetables, so the 'fruit' goes first)? No — all agree both get shehakol; they argued whether the favorite or the nourishing cabbage takes precedence
Why the story is cited: The Gemara wanted to prove that tanna'im disputed whether boiled vegetables get 'Who creates fruit of the ground'. It concludes the story proves nothing about that; the students argued only about precedence.

🍞 When Must the Blessing Over Bread End?

Dry bread in a bowl sparks a dispute about the order of blessing and breaking
R' Ḥiyya b. Ashi
Over dry bread soaking in a bowl one recites hamotzi — it is bread in every respect, even if a whole loaf is also on the table.
Rabbi Ḥiyya
I disagree. צָרִיךְ שֶׁתִּכְלֶה בְּרָכָה עִם הַפַּת — the blessing must conclude together with the breaking of the loaf. Dry crumbs were sliced off long ago.
Rava
What is different about dry bread? Because the blessing ends on a slice? When you bless over a loaf too, the blessing ends on a slice, since one cuts before the blessing!
Rava
Rather: מְבָרֵךְ וְאַחַר כָּךְ בּוֹצֵעַ — one recites the blessing over the whole loaf and only afterward breaks it.
Gemara
The Sages of Neharde'a acted like Rabbi Ḥiyya; the Rabbis acted like Rava. Ravina: 'My mother told me your father acted like Rabbi Ḥiyya.'
Halakha
The halakha is in accordance with Rava: bless first, then break.
Practical result: Because the blessing is completed over an intact loaf, one should not cut into it before finishing the words of hamotzi.

🥖 Pieces and Whole Loaves on the Same Table

Which one receives the blessing?

Rav Huna

  • Bless over the pieces and exempt the whole loaves
  • Parallel: the first tanna in teruma — separate teruma from a whole small onion, not half a large one? No, the Gemara rejects that mapping

Rabbi Yoḥanan

  • שְׁלֵמָה מִצְוָה מִן הַמּוּבְחָר — the whole loaf is the optimal way
  • Everyone agrees: a wheat piece beats a barley whole loaf; quality wins

The God-fearing way

  • Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak: a God-fearing person satisfies both — Mar son of Ravina placed the piece inside the whole loaf and broke them together
  • A tanna named Shalman taught the same baraita; Rav Naḥman: 'You are peace (shalom) and your teaching is whole (shelema), for you made peace among the students'
Why the onion parallel fails: Where a priest is present, everyone agrees the better half-onion is given. The tanna'im disagree only when no priest is there: separate from what will endure (first tanna) or always from the best (Rabbi Yehuda). So it is not a 'whole vs. significant' dispute at all.

🕯️ Shabbat Loaves, Pesaḥ Matza, and the Eiruv Bread

Special occasions change how bread is broken
❓ Does the 'piece inside the whole' method ever become obligatory?
↓
Pesaḥ
Rav Pappa: everyone agrees one places the broken matza inside the whole and breaks — לֶחֶם עֹנִי, 'bread of affliction' (Deut. 16:3), and the poor eat pieces
Shabbat
Rabbi Abba: one must break bread over two loaves — לֶחֶם מִשְׁנֶה, 'twice as much bread' (Exodus 16:22) of the manna
↓
👀 Rav Ashi saw Rav Kahana hold two loaves and break one. Rabbi Zeira broke off one piece big enough for the whole Shabbat meal
↓
🤔 Ravina: doesn't that look gluttonous? Rav Ashi: since he does not do so on other days and only does so today, it shows honor for the mitzva, not gluttony
↓
✅ Rav Ami and Rav Asi would say hamotzi over the eiruv bread: 'since one mitzva was done with it, let us do another mitzva with it'
Pattern: Each Shabbat and festival practice is anchored in a verse: the manna's double portion, and the affliction bread of the Exodus.
