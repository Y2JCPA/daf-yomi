📖 Overview: Cleanliness, Coverings and the Chamber Pot

Berakhot 25a–25b
כִּי יְיָ אֱלֹהֶיךָ מִתְהַלֵּךְ בְּקֶרֶב מַחֲנֶךָ … וְהָיָה מַחֲנֶיךָ קָדוֹשׁ וְלֹא יִרְאֶה בְךָ עֶרְוַת דָּבָר
The scene: This daf is the halakhic heart of 'your camp shall be holy'. Rav Huna and Rav Ḥisda disagree about feces on the skin and about how far to distance oneself from a smell, each armed with a verse from Psalms. Abaye and Rava argue about feces being carried past, Rav Yehuda distinguishes doubtful feces from doubtful urine, and a long chain of Amoraim asks when dried feces or absorbed urine stop being a problem — Rava rules that feces as dry as earthenware still forbid, urine only while it is wet. Amud bet returns to the mishna's bather: he may recite Shema in murky water because it acts like solid ground, his heel may see his nakedness but not touch it, and 'the Torah was not given to ministering angels'. The daf closes with the rules for urine that had water added to it, the chamber pot before or behind the bed, and a wedding-night story about a Torah scroll left in the bedroom.

🗣️ Rav Huna vs. Rav Ḥisda: Which Verse Governs Shema?

The same two Amoraim disagree in two cases, and a baraita sides with Rav Ḥisda

Rav Huna — lenient

  • Feces on his skin, or his hand inside the bathroom: he may recite Shema
  • Rava explains: כֹּל הַנְּשָׁמָה תְּהַלֵּל יָהּ, 'Let every soul praise the Lord' (Psalms 150:6) — read as 'every breath': only the mouth must be in a clean place
  • Foul odor with a visible source: distance four cubits from the source and recite

Rav Ḥisda — strict

  • Feces on his skin or a hand in the bathroom: he may not recite Shema
  • כׇּל עַצְמוֹתַי תֹּאמַרְנָה, 'All my bones shall say: Lord, who is like You' (Psalms 35:10) — praise is with the whole body
  • Foul odor with a source: distance four cubits from where the smell ceases
  • A baraita agrees: no Shema opposite human, dog, pig or chicken excrement or a stinking dung heap; ten handbreadths above or below is a separate domain; otherwise move out of sight — and the same for prayer

Rava's ruling

  • The halakha does not follow that baraita in all its rulings
  • Rather: human excrement, pig excrement, and dog excrement only when skins were placed in it for tanning
  • Rav Sheshet on a smell with no source: students sleep on the study-hall mats while others learn — Torah study is permitted (if the odor is another's), but Shema is not
Forgot tefillin in the bathroom? Rav Huna: place your hand on them until you finish — Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak: until the first mass is discharged. Why not stop at once? Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel taught that holding back feces brings dropsy and holding back urine brings jaundice.

🐖 Feces Passing By, and Feces in Doubt

Abaye vs. Rava, then Rav Yehuda on uncertainty
❓ Feces being carried past him (צוֹאָה עוֹבֶרֶת) — may he recite Shema?
↓
Abaye: permitted
From the mishna on leprosy: a pure person standing under a tree stays pure when a leper merely passes beneath it; only a stationary source spreads impurity. Likewise a leprous stone
Rava: forbidden
Leprosy depends on permanence — 'he shall dwell alone; outside the camp shall his dwelling be' (Leviticus 13:46). Here the Torah said 'your camp shall be holy' (Deuteronomy 23:15), and this is not holy
↓
🐷 Rav Pappa: a pig's mouth is like passing feces — even after it climbed out of the river
↓
🤔 Rav Yehuda: doubtful feces — forbidden; doubtful urine — permitted. Another version: doubtful feces in the house — permitted, in a dung heap — forbidden; doubtful urine — permitted even in a dung heap
↓
✅ Why is urine lighter? Rav Hamnuna: the Torah forbade only opposite the stream itself. Rabbi Yonatan: 'a place outside the camp' (Deut. 23:13) needs no covering — that is urine; 'you shall cover your excrement' (Deut. 23:14) is feces. Urine on the ground is only a rabbinic decree, and the Sages decreed only where its presence is certain
Two models of holiness: Abaye reasons from the mechanics of impurity; Rava from the purpose of the verse. For Rava the question is not whether the filth 'contaminates' the spot but whether the camp is presently holy.

Ravina relates that Rav Yehuda of Difti told him to check whether feces had crusted, or, some say, cracked.

🌊 The Mishna's Bather: Reciting Shema in the Water

From the timing question to the heel that sees his nakedness
1️⃣ The mishna says he must recite before sunrise if he can get out and cover himself. Is that Rabbi Eliezer's 'until sunrise'? No — even Rabbi Yehoshua, for the mishna may speak of the vatikin, who finish Shema at sunrise (Rabbi Yoḥanan)
↓
2️⃣ If he cannot get out, he covers himself in the water and recites. But his heart sees his nakedness! Rabbi Elazar (or Rav): the mishna means murky water, which is like solid earth
↓
3️⃣ Baraita: in clear water, sit up to the neck and recite; some say, muddy it with your foot. The first tanna holds that the heart seeing the nakedness, and the heel seeing the nakedness, are permitted
↓
4️⃣ Heel touching the nakedness: Abaye forbids, Rava permits — לֹא נִתְּנָה תּוֹרָה לְמַלְאֲכֵי הַשָּׁרֵת, the Torah was not given to ministering angels (Rav Ḥinnana's version puts the dispute on seeing, with touching forbidden by all)
↓
5️⃣ Halakha: touching is forbidden, seeing is permitted
↓
6️⃣ Foul water or flax-steeping water: he may not cover himself in them at all. Urine: he adds water — any amount, or a quarter-log per Rabbi Zakkai. Rav Naḥman and Rav Yosef differ on whether the dispute is about water added before or after; Rav Yosef told his attendant, 'Bring me a quarter-log, like Rabbi Zakkai'
Rava's lantern: Feces behind a transparent lantern-like cover — permitted, since for feces the Torah demands covering ('you shall cover your excrement') and it is covered. Nakedness behind such a cover — forbidden, since 'no indecent thing shall be seen in you' and it is seen. Abaye: a tiny bit of feces can be nullified with spittle; Rava: thick spittle. Feces in a hole: put your sandal over it. Stuck to the sandal? Teiku.

🛏️ The Chamber Pot: In Front of the Bed or Behind It?

Two baraitot swap the names of Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel and Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar
Baraita
A pot for excrement or urine forbids Shema even when empty. Urine itself — until he adds water. Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel: behind the bed he recites; in front of the bed he does not, but moves four cubits and recites. Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar: even in a house of a hundred cubits, not until he removes it or puts it under the bed.
Dilemma
What does Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel mean? Behind the bed — recite at once, in front — four cubits? Or behind — four cubits, in front — not at all?
Second baraita
Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar: behind the bed he recites at once; in front, he moves four cubits. Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel: even in a house of a hundred cubits, not until he removes it or puts it under the bed. So 'behind the bed' means immediately — the dilemma is solved, but the names contradict!
Resolution
Reverse the second baraita. Why that one and not the first? Whom have you heard say that an entire house is like four cubits? Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar — so the strict 'hundred cubits' opinion is his.
Rav Yosef
I asked Rav Huna: a bed less than three handbreadths off the ground is obviously lavud, joined to the ground. But three through nine handbreadths? He said: I do not know. Ten I did not need to ask — Abaye: well done, ten handbreadths is a separate domain.
Ruling
Rava: under three is lavud, ten is a separate domain, three to ten is Rav Huna's unresolved question. Rav (and Bali in the name of Rav Ya'akov): the halakha follows Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar. Rava: it does not.
A Torah scroll in the bedroom: Rav Aḥai's son could not consummate his marriage; his father found a Torah scroll in the room and said, 'Had I not come, you would have endangered my son.' A baraita forbids relations in a room with a Torah scroll or tefillin until they are removed or placed in a vessel within a vessel. Abaye: their regular vessel does not count — even ten such vessels are like one. Rava begins: 'A cloak…' (continued on the next daf).
