Berakhot 25 Quiz

QUESTION 1 OF 5
Which verse does Rava cite as the basis for Rav Huna permitting Shema when there is feces on one's skin?
A. 'All my bones shall say: Lord, who is like You' (Psalms 35:10)
B. 'Let every soul praise the Lord' (Psalms 150:6), read as 'every breath'
C. 'And your camp shall be holy' (Deuteronomy 23:15)
D. 'Guard your foot when you go to the house of God' (Ecclesiastes 4:17)
Correct: B — Rav Huna reads 'kol haneshama' as 'every breath': as long as the mouth that praises is in a clean place, the rest of the body is irrelevant. Rav Ḥisda's 'all my bones' requires the whole body to be clean.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
How does Rava reject Abaye's proof from the leper who passes under a tree?
A. A leper is impure only in the Land of Israel
B. Leprous impurity depends on permanence ('he shall dwell alone'), whereas here the Torah demands that 'your camp shall be holy', and it is not
C. The mishna about the leper follows Rabbi Yehuda, whose view is rejected
D. Feces are more severe than a leper
Correct: B — Rava distinguishes the sources: for leprosy the verse ties impurity to a fixed dwelling, but the requirement for Shema is simply that the camp be holy, which it is not while feces pass by.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
What is Rava's final halakha regarding dried feces and dried urine?
A. Both are permitted once they no longer moisten
B. Feces are permitted once crusted; urine is forbidden as long as its mark is visible
C. Feces are forbidden even when as dry as earthenware; urine is forbidden only as long as it moistens
D. Both are forbidden as long as any mark is visible
Correct: C — After the dispute between Ameimar and Mar Zutra, Rava rules that feces as dry as earthenware are forbidden, while urine forbids only while it still moistens.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
Why does the mishna permit reciting Shema while covered by water, given that 'his heart sees his nakedness'?
A. Because the heart seeing the nakedness is never a problem
B. Because the mishna speaks of murky water, which is like solid earth and forms a barrier
C. Because he holds his hand over his heart
D. Because water is a separate domain of ten handbreadths
Correct: B — Rabbi Elazar, or Rabbi Aḥa bar Abba bar Aḥa in the name of Rav, explains that the mishna refers to murky water, which counts as solid ground. Clear water requires sitting up to the neck or muddying it with the foot.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
Why does the Gemara reverse the second baraita rather than the first when the names of Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel and Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar conflict?
A. Because the second baraita was taught later
B. Because Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel is always lenient
C. Because Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar is known to hold that an entire house is like four cubits, so the 'hundred cubits' stringency must be his
D. Because Rav ruled like Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar
Correct: C — The Gemara asks 'what did you see to reverse the latter?' and answers that Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar is the one who holds the whole house is like four cubits, matching the view that even a house of a hundred cubits requires removing the pot.
