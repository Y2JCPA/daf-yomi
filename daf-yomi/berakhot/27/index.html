📖 Overview: Deadlines, Early Shabbat, and a Nasi Deposed

Berakhot 27a–27b
שְׁמַע מִינַּהּ ״עַד וְעַד בַּכְּלָל״ — הֲלָכָה כְּרַבִּי יְהוּדָה הוֹאִיל וּתְנַן בִּבְחִירָתָא כְּווֹתֵיהּ
The scene: The daf opens by settling whether Rabbi Yehuda's 'until' (four hours, seven hours, the midpoint of the afternoon) includes the last hour or not, and proves from tractate Eduyyot that it does. From there it asks whether the halakha follows Rabbi Yehuda for the afternoon prayer too, and finds the evidence split: Rav prayed the Shabbat prayer while it was still day, while Rav Huna and his colleagues waited for night. A story of Rav praying early at Geniva's house yields three halakhot and a discussion of what an early Shabbat or early motzaei Shabbat prayer commits one to. The daf closes with the famous confrontation over whether the evening prayer is optional, which ends with Rabban Gamliel removed and Rabbi Elazar ben Azarya proposed in his place.

⏳ Does 'Until' Include the Last Hour?

The Gemara tests Rabbi Yehuda's 'until seven hours' for Mussaf against a baraita
❓ Mishna: Mussaf may be recited all day; Rabbi Yehuda says until seven hours. Is 'until' inclusive (עַד וְעַד בַּכְּלָל) or exclusive?
↓
📜 Baraita: if Mussaf and Minḥa are both pending, the Rabbis say Minḥa first (it is frequent, תְּדִירָה); Rabbi Yehuda says Mussaf first, because its time is about to elapse (עוֹבֶרֶת)
↓
If exclusive
Mussaf would end at the start of the seventh hour (noon), before Minḥa begins half an hour later — the two could never overlap. Rabbi Yehuda's ruling would be impossible.
If inclusive
Mussaf runs through the seventh hour, so it overlaps with Minḥa. But then what separates Rabbi Yehuda's 'until the midpoint of the afternoon' from the Rabbis' 'until evening'?
↓
💡 'Midpoint of the afternoon' means the first half of the afternoon period: it ends at eleven hours minus a quarter after sunrise. So Rabbi Yehuda's 'until' is always inclusive.
↓
✅ Rav Naḥman: Eduyyot proves it — Rabbi Yehuda ben Bava testified that the daily morning offering was brought at four hours. Rav Kahana: the halakha follows Rabbi Yehuda, since the 'preferred' tractate Eduyyot rules like him.
Practical result: The morning prayer may be recited until the end of the fourth hour, and Mussaf until the end of the seventh.

🍞 When Did the Manna Melt? Reading the Hours of the Morning

Exodus 16:21 says 'when the sun grew hot it melted' — the Gemara fits that into both views of 'morning'
Phrase or opinionHour of the dayHow it is derived
'In the heat of the day' (Genesis 18:1)Six hoursThe Torah's term for full midday heat, when even the shade is hot
'When the sun grew hot it melted' (Exodus 16:21)Four hoursRabbi Aḥa bar Ya'akov: the hour when the sun is hot but the shade is still cool
Morning according to the RabbisUntil noon'Morning by morning' (בַּבֹּקֶר בַּבֹּקֶר) splits it into two mornings, so gathering ended at the end of the third hour
Morning according to Rabbi YehudaUntil four hoursThe extra 'morning' moves the end of gathering one hour earlier
ConclusionEveryone agreesThe manna's melting at four hours fits both tannaitic clocks; the mishna need not be a third opinion
Why this matters: The Gemara first suspected the manna mishna was a 'new position' that fit neither Rabbi Yehuda nor the Rabbis. Reading the doubled 'morning' rescues it for both.

🌇 Minḥa Until Evening or Until the Midpoint? Nobody Rules

Rav Ḥisda looks for a decision and finds the practice of the Sages divided
Rav Ḥisda
For the morning prayer Rav Kahana ruled like Rabbi Yehuda because of Eduyyot. Here, for the afternoon prayer, what is the ruling?
Rav Yitzḥak
He was silent and said nothing — he knew of no established ruling.
Rav Ḥisda
Let us see for ourselves: Rav prayed the Shabbat prayer on Shabbat eve while it was still day. Learn from this that the halakha follows Rabbi Yehuda, and Minḥa ends at the midpoint of the afternoon.
Gemara
On the contrary! Rav Huna and the Sages would not pray until evening. Learn from that that the halakha does not follow Rabbi Yehuda.
Conclusion
Since the halakha was stated neither like this Master nor like that Master: one who acts like this one has acted legitimately, and one who acts like that one has acted legitimately (דַּעֲבַד כְּמָר עֲבַד).
Story
Rav visited Geniva's house and prayed the Shabbat prayer on Shabbat eve. Rabbi Yirmeya bar Abba prayed behind him, and Rav did not step back to interrupt him. Three halakhot: one may pray Shabbat's prayer on Shabbat eve; a student may pray behind his rabbi; it is forbidden to pass before one who is praying.
Two objections answered: Rabbi Ami and Rabbi Asi did pass before people praying — but beyond four cubits. And Rabbi Yirmeya prayed behind Rav even though Rav taught never to pray next to or behind one's rabbi (Rabbi Eliezer adds that this drives away the Divine Presence) — because Rabbi Yirmeya was a disciple-colleague (תַּלְמִיד חָבֵר), which is why he could ask Rav directly, 'Did you separate from labor?'

🕯️ Praying Shabbat In and Out Before Its Time

What does an early Shabbat or early motzaei Shabbat prayer commit you to?

Early Shabbat prayer (Rav)

  • Rav prayed the Shabbat prayer on Shabbat eve while it was still day
  • Rav told Rabbi Yirmeya he had separated from labor
  • Rav Naḥman in Shmuel's name: one may also recite kiddush over the cup before nightfall — and the halakha follows him
  • Rabbi Yishmael (or, per Ulla, Rabbi Elazar) son of Rabbi Yosei did the same beside a pillar (or a palm tree)

Early motzaei Shabbat prayer (Rabbi Yoshiya)

  • Rabbi Yoshiya would pray the motzaei Shabbat evening prayer while it was still Shabbat
  • Rav Yehuda in Shmuel's name: one may recite havdala over the cup after that early prayer
  • Ulla's version of the pillar story is exactly this case: motzaei Shabbat prayed on Shabbat

Prayed early by mistake

  • Rav Dimi bar Liva'ei prayed Shabbat's prayer on a cloudy day thinking it was night; Abaye let him fumigate baskets afterward — an error does not bind him
  • A community prayed the motzaei Shabbat prayer on Shabbat when clouds darkened the sky; when the sun reappeared Rabbi ruled: since they prayed, they prayed
  • Why the difference? A community is different — we do not burden them to pray again
Side question: Rabbi Avin reported that Rabbi prayed the Shabbat prayer early, then entered the bathhouse and taught afterward. Rava: he entered only to perspire, and this was before the Sages decreed against that on Shabbat.

🌙 'Is the Evening Prayer Optional?' — The Day Rabban Gamliel Was Deposed

The mishna's 'evening prayer has no fixed time' follows the view that it is optional (רְשׁוּת)
1️⃣ Rav Yehuda in Shmuel's name: Rabban Gamliel says the evening prayer is obligatory (חוֹבָה); Rabbi Yehoshua says optional. Abaye rules obligatory; Rava rules optional
↓
2️⃣ A student asks Rabbi Yehoshua: optional. He asks Rabban Gamliel: obligatory. 'But Rabbi Yehoshua told me optional!' — 'Wait until the masters of the shields (בַּעֲלֵי תְּרִיסִין) enter the study hall'
↓
3️⃣ In public, Rabban Gamliel asks: does anyone dispute this? Rabbi Yehoshua, in deference to the Nasi, says no. 'Yehoshua, stand on your feet and they will testify against you!'
↓
4️⃣ Rabbi Yehoshua: 'If I were alive and he dead, the living could contradict the dead. Now that both are alive, how can the living contradict the living?'
↓
5️⃣ Rabban Gamliel keeps lecturing while Rabbi Yehoshua stands, until the people tell Ḥutzpit the disseminator: 'Stop!' — and he stops
↓
6️⃣ 'How long will he afflict him? Rosh HaShana last year, the firstborn case of Rabbi Tzadok, now this. Let us remove him!'
↓
7️⃣ Not Rabbi Yehoshua (party to the incident), not Rabbi Akiva (no ancestral merit to protect him). Rabbi Elazar ben Azarya: wise, rich, tenth generation from Ezra. He goes to consult his wife
Why each qualification: Wise — he can answer Rabban Gamliel's challenges. Rich — he can afford to represent Israel at Caesar's court. Descendant of Ezra — ancestral merit shields him from being punished through Rabban Gamliel's resentment.
