📖 Overview: Bedrooms, Bare Skin and Filthy Streets

Berakhot 24a–24b
טֶפַח בָּאִשָּׁה עֶרְוָה … שׁוֹק בָּאִשָּׁה עֶרְוָה … קוֹל בָּאִשָּׁה עֶרְוָה … שֵׂעָר בָּאִשָּׁה עֶרְוָה
The scene: The daf begins by completing Rava's ruling that tefillin may be kept under one's head at night because protecting them outweighs the indignity, and Rabbi Yirmeya locates the spot between the pillow and the mattress. It then turns to reciting Shema in bed: Shmuel permits two people sharing a bed to turn away and recite, even a husband and wife, while Rav Yosef insists only a wife is 'like his own flesh'. That leads into a string of definitions of nakedness (erva): Rav Huna on buttocks, the ages at which children count as adults, and the famous four statements that a handbreadth, a leg, a voice and hair of a woman are all erva. Amud bet reconciles Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi's belching, yawning, sneezing and spitting during prayer with a baraita condemning each, records the prayer said after flatulence, and closes with the debate over whether one may recite Shema while walking through filthy alleyways.

👥 Two People in One Bed: Can Each Turn Away and Recite Shema?

Rav Yosef son of Rav Neḥunya asks Rav Yehuda; Shmuel's answer sparks an objection
Shmuel
Two who are sleeping in one bed — this one turns his face and recites Shema, and that one turns his face and recites — even if his wife is with him.
Rav Yosef
His wife — and needless to say another person?! On the contrary: אִשְׁתּוֹ כְּגוּפוֹ, his wife is like his own flesh, so he will not have lustful thoughts; another is not like his own flesh.
Objection
One baraita: two in one bed, each turns away and recites. Another: one sleeping with his children and household beside him may not recite unless a garment separates them — but if they are minors, it is permitted. For Rav Yosef this is easy: the first is about his wife, the second about others. But for Shmuel?
Shmuel
Does it work for Rav Yosef? The second baraita also says his household — which includes his wife — needs a garment between them! You must say that the case of a wife is a tannaitic dispute for Rav Yosef; for me too it is a tannaitic dispute, and I follow one baraita.
Gemara
'This one turns his face' — but there are still bare buttocks! This supports Rav Huna: עֲגָבוֹת אֵין בָּהֶם מִשּׁוּם עֶרְוָה, buttocks are not nakedness. Shall we add the mishna that a woman may separate ḥalla naked because she can cover her 'face' in the ground?
R. Naḥman b.Y.
No proof: that mishna is a case where her 'face' was pressed fully into the ground so her posterior was covered too.
How old is a minor? Rav Ḥisda: a girl until three years and one day, a boy until nine years and one day. Some say: eleven and a day for a girl, twelve and a day for a boy. Either way, the real line is puberty — 'Your breasts were formed and your hair was grown' (Ezekiel 16:7).

🙈 What Is Erva? Four Statements About a Woman

Each Amora brings a verse; the Gemara narrows Rabbi Yitzḥak to one's own wife and Shema

Not nakedness

  • Rav Huna: buttocks are not erva
  • Rav Mari asked Rav Pappa about pubic hair protruding from a garment; Rav Pappa dismissed it — 'a hair, a hair', you are splitting hairs

A handbreadth (Rabbi Yitzḥak)

  • טֶפַח בָּאִשָּׁה עֶרְוָה
  • Not about gazing — Rav Sheshet already taught that the list of jewelry in Numbers 31:50 means whoever gazes at a woman's little finger is as if he gazed at her genitals
  • Rather: even his own wife, and with regard to reciting Shema

Leg, voice, hair

  • Rav Ḥisda: a leg is erva — 'Uncover the leg, pass through the rivers' followed by 'your nakedness shall be revealed' (Isaiah 47:2–3)
  • Shmuel: a voice is erva — 'For your voice is sweet and your countenance alluring' (Song of Songs 2:14)
  • Rav Sheshet: hair is erva — 'Your hair is like a flock of goats' (Song of Songs 4:1)
Hanging tefillin: Rabbi Ḥanina saw Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi hang his tefillin, though a baraita warns that one who hangs his tefillin will have his life hang in the balance (Deuteronomy 28:66). Either he hung them by the box, not the strap — or both are forbidden and Rabbi hung them in their pouch, teaching that tefillin need not be laid flat like a Torah scroll.

🤧 Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi Belched, Yawned, Sneezed and Spat in Prayer

Rabbi Ḥanina's report versus a baraita that condemns all four
👀 Rabbi Ḥanina: I saw Rabbi belch, yawn, sneeze, spit, and feel for a louse in his garment while praying — but he would not wrap himself in his fallen prayer shawl, and when he yawned he put his hand on his chin
↓
❓ Baraita: sounding one's voice — little faith; raising it — like the false prophets; belching and yawning — uncouth; sneezing — a bad omen (some say: clearly repulsive); spitting — like spitting before the king
↓
Belch / yawn
Rabbi did so involuntarily (לְאוֹנְסוֹ); the baraita condemns doing it deliberately
Sneeze
Rabbi sneezed from above; the bad omen is from below. Rav Zeira: 'One who sneezes in prayer — a good omen; as they give him relief below, so above' — worth all my learning
Spit
Rav Yehuda: absorb it in your garment, or in your head covering if the garment is fine. Ravina spat behind him instead: 'I am delicate'
↓
✅ Rav Huna on the voice: only one who can concentrate silently is of little faith; if he cannot, he may sound his voice — but not in a congregation, where he would disturb others
Rabbi Abba's baraita: While quietly avoiding Rav Yehuda (who held that ascending from Babylonia to Eretz Yisrael violates Jeremiah 27:22), Rabbi Abba heard this taught: one who breaks wind in prayer waits until the odor dissipates and resumes; some say he steps back four cubits and says, 'Master of the universe, You formed us with orifices and cavities; our disgrace and shame are revealed before You…' and resumes where he stopped. Rabbi Abba: had I come only for this, it would have been enough.

🚶 Walking Through Filthy Alleyways: Recite or Stop?

A lenient tradition, a fierce dissent, and verses for both outcomes
1️⃣ Rav Huna in the name of Rabbi Yoḥanan (some say Rabba bar bar Ḥana in the name of Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi): walking through filthy alleyways, place your hand over your mouth and recite Shema
↓
2️⃣ Rav Ḥisda: 'By God! Had Rabbi Yoḥanan said it to me with his own mouth, I would not obey him'
↓
3️⃣ But Rav Huna also said a Torah scholar may not stand in a place of filth, since he cannot avoid Torah thoughts. Answer: standing is forbidden, walking is permitted
↓
4️⃣ Rabbi Yoḥanan permits contemplating Torah everywhere except the bathhouse and bathroom — and when Rabbi Abbahu fell silent in a filthy alley, Rabbi Yoḥanan told him to return to the beginning if he had paused long enough to finish. Answer: 'I do not hold so; but for you, who do — return to the beginning'
↓
5️⃣ A baraita supports Rav Huna (hand over mouth and recite) and a baraita supports Rav Ḥisda (do not recite; if already reciting, stop)
↓
6️⃣ If he did not stop: 'I gave them statutes that were not good' (Ezekiel 20:25), 'Woe to those who draw iniquity with cords of vanity' (Isaiah 5:18), 'He has shown contempt for the word of the Lord' (Numbers 15:31). If he stopped: 'Through this matter you will prolong your days' (Deuteronomy 32:47)
Covered below the waist: Baraita: one sleeping unclothed under his garment who cannot expose his head because of the cold makes a barrier with the garment at his neck and recites (some say: at his heart) — the first tanna holds that 'his heart seeing his nakedness' is permitted. Rav Huna: if his garment is tied around his waist, he may recite Shema, whether it is cloth, leather or sackcloth.
