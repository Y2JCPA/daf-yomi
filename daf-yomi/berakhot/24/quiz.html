Berakhot 24 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
Why does Rava rule in accordance with Shmuel that tefillin may be placed under one's head at night, even though a baraita refuted Shmuel?
A. Because the baraita was taught by Beit Shammai
B. Because whatever offers the tefillin more protection is preferable, even at some cost to their dignity
C. Because a pouch of a handbreadth creates a separate domain
D. Because tefillin are not worn at night in any case
Correct: B — The daf opens with Rava's reason: 'whatever protects them more is preferable.' Rabbi Yirmeya then specifies the place — between the pillow and the mattress, not directly under his head.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
What was Rav Yosef's objection to Shmuel's ruling that two people in one bed may each turn away and recite Shema 'even with his wife'?
A. A wife is more of a problem than a stranger because of her nakedness
B. The phrase 'even his wife' is backwards: his wife is like his own flesh and permitted, whereas another person is not like his own flesh
C. One may never recite Shema in bed at all
D. Only minors may share a bed with one reciting Shema
Correct: B — Rav Yosef argued that 'ishto kegufo' — a man will not have lustful thoughts about his wife — so the wife is the easier case and another person the harder one, the reverse of Shmuel's phrasing.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
Regarding which halakha does the Gemara conclude Rabbi Yitzḥak said 'an exposed handbreadth in a woman is nakedness'?
A. The prohibition of gazing at a woman
B. Reciting Shema in the presence of even his own wife
C. Separating ḥalla while unclothed
D. Sleeping beside minors
Correct: B — It cannot be about gazing, since Rav Sheshet already taught that gazing at a woman's little finger with impure intent is like gazing at her nakedness. So Rabbi Yitzḥak must be speaking of one's wife and the recitation of Shema.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
How does the Gemara reconcile Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi sneezing during prayer with the baraita that calls sneezing a bad omen?
A. Rabbi sneezed only between blessings
B. The baraita refers to a deliberate sneeze
C. Rabbi sneezed 'from above' (his nose), while the bad omen refers to sneezing 'from below' (flatulence)
D. The baraita follows a minority opinion that Rabbi rejected
Correct: C — Belching and yawning are distinguished as involuntary versus deliberate, but for sneezing the Gemara distinguishes above from below, citing Rav Zeira's tradition that a sneeze during prayer is in fact a good omen.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to the baraita that supports Rav Ḥisda, what should one do if he is in the middle of reciting Shema and reaches a filthy alleyway?
A. Place his hand over his mouth and continue
B. Stop reciting
C. Recite the rest silently in his heart
D. Walk faster and finish
Correct: B — The baraita in accordance with Rav Ḥisda states that one walking in filthy alleyways may not recite Shema, and if he was already reciting, he stops. Rabbi Abbahu rewards him with 'through this matter you will prolong your days'.
