📖 Overview: Hearing Your Own Shema

Berakhot 15a–15b
הַקּוֹרֵא אֶת שְׁמַע וְלֹא הִשְׁמִיעַ לְאׇזְנוֹ — יָצָא. רַבִּי יוֹסֵי אוֹמֵר: לֹא יָצָא
The scene: The daf opens by finishing a thought: the full acceptance of the Kingdom of Heaven is relieving oneself, washing, donning tefillin, reciting Shema and praying, and Rabbi Yoḥanan compares one who does so to a person who built an altar and offered a sacrifice. From washing the Gemara turns to a new mishna: does one who recites Shema without hearing his own voice fulfill his obligation? The first tanna says yes, Rabbi Yosei says no, and a second dispute asks whether sloppy enunciation invalidates the recitation. The Gemara then lines up three other sources — a deaf person separating teruma, reciting Grace after Meals silently, and reading the Megilla — and works hard to match each one to Rabbi Yosei, Rabbi Yehuda or Rabbi Meir, before Rav Ḥisda gives the practical ruling. The daf closes with the importance of enunciating every letter of Shema.

🧼 Five Steps to a Complete Kingdom of Heaven

Rabbi Ḥiyya bar Abba in the name of Rabbi Yoḥanan, and what to do when there is no water
1️⃣ Relieve yourself, wash your hands, don tefillin, recite Shema and pray — וְזוֹ הִיא מַלְכוּת שָׁמַיִם שְׁלֵמָה, this is acceptance of the complete Kingdom of Heaven
↓
2️⃣ Rabbi Yoḥanan: the verse credits such a person as if he built an altar and offered a sacrifice — 'I will wash in purity my hands, and I will encircle the altar of the Lord' (Psalms 26:6)
↓
3️⃣ Rava presses further: the verse says 'I will wash' (אֶרְחַץ), not 'I will wash my hands' — so it is as if he immersed his whole body
↓
4️⃣ Ravina reports a scholar from Eretz Yisrael: with no water, one may wipe his hands with earth, a rock or a sliver of wood
↓
5️⃣ Rava approves: is it written 'I will wash with water'? It says 'in purity' (בְּנִקָּיוֹן) — anything that cleans. Rav Ḥisda even cursed one who went hunting for water at the time of prayer
The limits: Not searching for water applies only to Shema, whose time is limited. For prayer, which can be said all day, one must seek water — up to a parsa ahead of him, though he need not turn back even one mil (less than a mil, he does turn back).

👂 Three Tannaim, Three Levels of 'Audible'

The mishna's dispute, and the two additional positions the Gemara uncovers
TannaInaudible recitationSource / reasoning
Rabbi YoseiNot valid even after the fact'שְׁמַע' — make your ear hear what your mouth utters (he also learns 'any language' from the same word)
Rabbi Yehuda in the name of Rabbi Elazar ben AzaryaMust be audible ab initio; valid after the fact'Hear, Israel, the Lord is our God, the Lord is One' — a requirement, but not a condition
Rabbi MeirValid even ab initio'Which I command you this day, upon your heart' — the words follow the intention of the heart
First tanna of our mishnaFulfilled his obligation'שְׁמַע' teaches only that Shema may be recited in any language one hears and understands
Second dispute in the mishna: One who was not precise with the letters — Rabbi Yosei says he fulfilled his obligation, Rabbi Yehuda says he did not. One who recited out of order did not fulfill it; one who erred returns to the place of the error.

🌾 Which Tanna Says the Deaf Person's Teruma Is Valid?

The Gemara tests three sources against Rabbi Yosei and Rabbi Yehuda
📜 Mishna in Terumot: a deaf person who speaks but cannot hear may not separate teruma ab initio, but after the fact his teruma is valid. Who is the tanna?
↓
Rav Ḥisda: it is Rabbi Yosei. He invalidates only Shema, which is Torah law; teruma depends on a blessing, which is rabbinic, and the separation does not hinge on the blessing
↓
❓ But a baraita says one may not recite Grace after Meals silently, yet if he did he fulfilled it — neither Rabbi Yosei (never valid) nor Rabbi Yehuda (valid even ab initio, as we assumed)
↓
Rabbi Yehuda himself
A deaf person may separate teruma even ab initio — the baraita of Rabbi Yehuda son of Rabbi Shimon ben Pazi
His teacher, R' Elazar ben Azarya
Must be audible ab initio, valid after the fact — the Grace after Meals baraita
↓
✅ Final resolution: even if Rabbi Yehuda agrees with his teacher, the 'ab initio' baraita is simply Rabbi Meir's view and the 'after the fact' baraita is Rabbi Yehuda's
The Megilla too: Rav Mattana attributed the mishna 'all are fit to read the Megilla except a deaf-mute, an imbecile and a minor' to Rabbi Yosei, since a deaf-mute is grouped with those whose reading is never valid. The Gemara answers that the list may be mixed, and that Rabbi Yehuda's 'deems a minor fit' can be elucidation, not dispute — a minor who reached the age of training is fit even ab initio.

⚖️ Rav Ḥisda Rules — and Rav Yosef Draws the Boundary

Why the halakha needed two statements, and whether other mitzvot follow Shema
Rav Ḥisda
Rav Sheila said: the halakha follows Rabbi Yehuda who spoke in the name of Rabbi Elazar ben Azarya, and the halakha follows Rabbi Yehuda.
Gemara
Why both? Had he said only 'like Rabbi Yehuda', I would think inaudible recitation is fine even ab initio. Had he said only 'like Rabbi Yehuda in the name of Rabbi Elazar ben Azarya', I would think 'must make it audible' means there is no remedy after the fact.
Rav Yosef
The dispute is only about Shema. For the rest of the mitzvot all agree he did not fulfill his obligation — 'Pay attention, and hear, Israel' (Deuteronomy 27:9).
Objection
But the baraita: one may not recite Grace after Meals in his heart, yet if he did, he fulfilled it! Other mitzvot are only ab initio.
Emended
Rather: the dispute is only about Shema, where it says 'Hear, Israel'; for the rest of the mitzvot all agree he did fulfill his obligation.
Gemara
And 'Pay attention, and hear'? That verse is written about matters of Torah study.
Bottom line: Ab initio one must hear his own Shema; after the fact the recitation is valid. Rabbi Tavi in the name of Rabbi Yoshiya adds: in both disputes of the mishna the halakha follows the lenient view.

🔤 Complete Writing, Complete Teaching, Careful Letters

Two baraitot taught before Rava, and Rabbi Ḥama's reward for precise enunciation

וּכְתַבְתָּם — write them 'tam', complete

  • Rabbi Oshaya: in mezuza and tefillin everything is written, even the commands to write them
  • Rava: this is Rabbi Yehuda, who writes only the curses in the sota scroll — 'and he shall write these curses' (Numbers 5:23)
  • Why the extra verse? Otherwise a verbal analogy 'writing–writing' from sota would exclude the commands here too

וְלִמַּדְתֶּם — teach them 'tam', complete

  • Rav Ovadya: leave a space between adjacent words whose letters run together
  • Rava's examples: עַל לְבָבֶךָ, בְּכָל לְבַבְכֶם, עֵשֶׂב בְּשָׂדְךָ, וַאֲבַדְתֶּם מְהֵרָה, הַכָּנָף פְּתִיל, אֶתְכֶם מֵאֶרֶץ
  • Run them together and the meaning is obscured

The reward

  • Rabbi Ḥama son of Rabbi Ḥanina: one punctilious with the letters of Shema — Gehenna is cooled for him
  • 'When the Almighty scatters (befares) kings over it, it will snow in Tzalmon' (Psalms 68:15)
  • Read befaresh, when he enunciates; read betzalmavet, in the shadow of death
Also on the daf: Rabbi Tavi in the name of Rabbi Yoshiya on 'the grave and the barren womb' (Proverbs 30:15–16): just as a womb takes in and gives forth, so the grave takes in and gives forth — a Torah refutation of those who deny the resurrection of the dead.
