📖 Overview: Three Who Ate as One

Berakhot 45a–45b
שְׁלֹשָׁה שֶׁאָכְלוּ כְּאַחַת, חַיָּיבִין לְזַמֵּן
The scene: After closing the previous chapter with the blessing over water — where the Gemara's ruling is simply 'go out and see what the people do' — the daf opens the chapter on zimmun. The mishna lays out who joins the three: eaters of doubtfully tithed produce, redeemed tithes, a waiter who ate an olive-bulk, and a Samaritan count; eaters of untithed produce, a gentile, women, slaves and minors do not. The Gemara finds the source in Psalms and Deuteronomy, adds that a respondent's amen and a translator's voice must not overpower the leader, and then argues at length whether two who ate together may form a zimmun voluntarily. It closes with the practicalities: the diner who stepped out, the one who interrupts his meal for the others, three equal Sages who lost their zimmun by blessing separately, and what a latecomer answers when he finds the group already blessing.

Before the chapter began: The mishna's Rabbi Tarfon says the blessing over water is 'Who creates many forms of life and their needs'. Asked which opinion is the halakha, Abaye (or Rav Yosef) answered: פּוֹק חֲזִי מַאי עַמָּא דָּבַר — go out and see what the people do. And one who drank water only because a piece of meat choked him recites no blessing at all.

📖 Where Does Zimmun Come From — and Why Not Shout Amen?

Two verses for the mitzva, and two lessons about the volume of a response
גַּדְּלוּ לַה׳ אִתִּי וּנְרוֹמְמָה שְׁמוֹ יַחְדָּו
The sources: Rav Asi derives zimmun from 'Praise God with me, and we will exalt His name together' (Psalms 34:4): the leader turns to at least two others. Rabbi Abbahu derives it from 'When I call the Name of the Lord, give [plural] praise to our God' (Deuteronomy 32:3). From the word 'together' Rav Ḥanan bar Abba adds that one who answers amen should not raise his voice above the one blessing. Rabbi Shimon ben Pazi learns the same for the translator of the public Torah reading: 'Moses spoke, and God responded in a voice' (Exodus 19:19) — 'in a voice' is superfluous unless it means in Moses' voice, no louder. A baraita adds the reverse: if the translator cannot match the reader, the reader lowers his voice.

✌️ Can Two Who Ate Together Choose to Form a Zimmun?

Rav and Rabbi Yoḥanan disagree; the Gemara tests four proofs and identifies who said what
Proof 1
Mishna: three who ate as one must form a zimmun — three yes, two no!
Reply
There the mishna speaks of an obligatory zimmun; our dispute is about an optional one.
Proof 2
Baraita: three may not disperse. If two could form a zimmun, why forbid one of the three from leaving?
Reply
Different — from the outset they were established as an obligatory three and may not trade it for an option. The same answers the waiter who may join two uninvited but not three, and Rav's rule about calling back a diner who left.
Proof 3
Baraita: women form a zimmun among themselves, though a hundred women are like two men for a quorum. So two men may too!
Reply
Different — there are three separate minds (דֵּעוֹת). And women and slaves may not join together only because of concern for promiscuity.
Conclusion
It is Rabbi Yoḥanan who forbids: he taught that of two who ate together, one fulfils his duty through the other's blessing — and Rabbi Zeira explained this teaches there is no zimmun blessing between them. The Western Sages who permit heard it from Rav before he descended to Babylonia (Rav Huna).
Abaye's tradition: Two who ate together — it is a mitzva to separate and each bless for himself. A baraita agrees, but only when both are learned; if one is learned and one an ignoramus, the learned one blesses and the other fulfils his obligation.

🏃 One Stepped Out, One Still Eating, Three Who Blessed Alone

Practical rulings on holding a zimmun together
1️⃣ Rav (via Rav Dimi bar Yosef): three ate together and one went out to the market — they call him and include him in the zimmun
↓
2️⃣ Abaye: only if they call and he answers
↓
3️⃣ Mar Zutra: that suffices for three; for ten they wait until he comes and sits
↓
4️⃣ Rav Ashi objects: the opposite is logical — nine look like ten, but two do not look like three
↓
5️⃣ Halakha follows Mar Zutra: with ten, God's Name is invoked, and it is not proper conduct to do so with fewer than ten present
↓
6️⃣ Rava (and Rabbi Zeira): one interrupts his meal to join the two, but two do not interrupt for one — Rav Pappa did so for his son Abba Mar only beyond the letter of the law
↓
7️⃣ Yehuda bar Mareimar, Mar bar Rav Ashi and Rav Aḥa of Difti, none greater than the other, each blessed alone. Mareimar: you fulfilled Grace but not zimmun — and אֵין זִימּוּן לְמַפְרֵעַ, there is no retroactive zimmun

🙏 What Does a Latecomer Answer — and Amen After Your Own Blessing?

Rav Zevid and Rav Pappa do not really disagree; two baraitot are reconciled

Found them saying 'Nevarekh'

  • He answers בָּרוּךְ וּמְבוֹרָךְ — 'Blessed is He and blessed is His Name' (Rav Zevid)

Found them saying 'Barukh'

  • He answers amen (Rav Pappa)

Amen after one's own blessing

  • One baraita: praiseworthy; another: reprehensible
  • Resolution: praiseworthy after Who builds Jerusalem; reprehensible in other blessings
  • Abaye answered it aloud so his workers would hear and rise to work, since 'Who is good and does good' is not Torah law
  • Rav Ashi answered it in a whisper so that people would not belittle 'Who is good and does good'
Pattern: The same move resolves both questions: two rulings that look contradictory are each speaking about a different moment in the blessing.
