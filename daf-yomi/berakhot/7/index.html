📖 Overview: God Prays, God's Anger, and Moses' Questions

Berakhot 7a–7b
יְהִי רָצוֹן מִלְּפָנַי שֶׁיִּכְבְּשׁוּ רַחֲמַי אֶת כַּעֲסִי
The scene: This daf is a chain of aggadic teachings of Rabbi Yoḥanan, first in the name of Rabbi Yosei and then in the name of Rabbi Shimon ben Yoḥai. It opens with the startling idea that God Himself prays — that His mercy overcome His anger — and that He asked Rabbi Yishmael the High Priest for a blessing. From there it explores how brief God's anger is, why Balaam's curses failed, and the lesson Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi learned when he tried to curse a heretic. Moses' three requests lead to the classic question of why the righteous suffer and the wicked prosper. The second amud turns to Abraham calling God 'Lord', Leah's thanksgiving, the meaning of the names Reuben and Ruth, the pain of wayward children, whether one may provoke the wicked, and the value of fixing a place for prayer and serving Torah scholars.

🙏 Does God Pray? And What Does He Pray?

Rabbi Yoḥanan in the name of Rabbi Yosei opens the daf
R' Yoḥanan
From where do we know that the Holy One, Blessed be He, prays? Isaiah 56:7 says בְּבֵית תְּפִלָּתִי, 'the house of My prayer', not 'the house of their prayer'.
Gemara
What does He pray?
Rav
'May it be My will that My mercy overcome My anger, that My mercy prevail over My other attributes, that I conduct Myself with My children with the attribute of mercy, and that I enter before them beyond the letter of the law.'
Baraita
Rabbi Yishmael ben Elisha entered the Holy of Holies to offer incense and saw Akatriel Ya, Lord of Hosts, on a high and exalted throne. He said: 'Yishmael, My son, bless Me!'
R' Yishmael
'May it be Your will that Your mercy overcome Your anger… and that You enter before them beyond the letter of the law.' And God nodded His head.
Lesson
שֶׁלֹּא תְּהֵא בִּרְכַּת הֶדְיוֹט קַלָּה בְּעֵינֶיךָ — do not take the blessing of an ordinary person lightly.
Big idea: God's own 'prayer' is a wish that mercy win out over strict justice. If God accepts a human blessing, all the more so must we value one another's blessings.

⏱️ How Long Is God Angry, and Who Can Find That Moment?

'God is furious every day' (Psalms 7:12) — but only for a rega
❓ Is there really anger before the Holy One? Yes: 'God is furious every day' (Psalms 7:12)
↓
⏱️ How long? A moment (רֶגַע) — 1/58,888 of an hour; Rabbi Avin: as long as it takes to say the word. Proof: 'His anger is but for a moment' (Psalms 30:6)
↓
🔮 No creature can pinpoint that moment except Balaam the wicked — 'who knows the knowledge of the Most High' (Numbers 24:16)
↓
Objection
He could not even know the mind of his donkey — did he know the mind of the Most High?
Answer
It means only that he could time the instant God is angry, and curse at that moment
↓
✅ Rabbi Elazar: 'Know the righteous acts of the Lord' (Micah 6:5) — God did not become angry at all in the days of Balaam, so the curse failed: 'How can I condemn whom God has not condemned?' (Numbers 23:8)
When is it? Abaye: in the first three hours of the day, when the rooster's crest turns white and it stands on one leg with no red streaks in its crest. Rabbi Meir: it is the moment the kings of East and West put on their crowns and bow to the sun.

🐓 Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi Tries to Curse a Heretic

A story that ends in a different lesson than it began with
כִּי מְטָא הַהִיא שַׁעְתָּא — נָיֵים. אֲמַר: שְׁמַע מִינַּהּ, לָאו אוֹרַח אַרְעָא לְמֶעְבַּד הָכִי
The story: A heretic in Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi's neighborhood kept tormenting him with challenges to verses. One day he tied a rooster between the legs of his bed and watched it, planning to curse the heretic at the exact moment of God's anger. When the moment came — he had dozed off. He concluded: it is not proper conduct to do this, even to the wicked. 'His mercy is over all His creations' (Psalms 145:9), and 'Punishment, even for the righteous, is not good' (Proverbs 17:26). The same daf adds Rabbi Yoḥanan's teaching that a single pang of remorse in the heart is better than many lashes (Hosea 2:9), and Reish Lakish: better than a hundred lashes, for 'a rebuke enters deeper into a man of understanding than a hundred lashes to a fool' (Proverbs 17:10).

⚖️ Moses' Three Requests and the Righteous Who Suffer

Rabbi Yoḥanan in the name of Rabbi Yosei: all three were granted; Rabbi Meir: only two
RequestSource verseGranted?
That the Divine Presence rest upon Israel'Is it not in that You go with us' (Exodus 33:16)Yes (all agree)
That the Divine Presence not rest upon the nations'So that we are distinguished, I and Your people' (Exodus 33:16)Yes (all agree)
To know God's ways — why the righteous suffer and the wicked prosper'Show me Your ways' (Exodus 33:13)R' Yoḥanan: yes; R' Meir: no — 'I will be gracious to whom I will be gracious' (Exodus 33:19)
First answer: righteous who prospers = righteous son of a righteous manRejected: 'children shall not be put to death for the fathers' (Deut. 24:16) — ancestors' sins count only when one adopts their deedsRetracted
Final answer: prospering righteous = completely righteous; suffering righteous = not completely righteous; prospering wicked = not completely wicked; suffering wicked = completely wickedGemara's conclusionAccepted
Seeing God's back: Rabbi Yehoshua ben Korḥa: 'You cannot see My face' (Exodus 33:20) — when I wanted at the burning bush, you did not; now that you want, I do not. Rabbi Yonatan disagrees: for hiding his face Moses earned a glowing countenance, for 'fearing' Israel feared to approach him, for not 'gazing' he beheld the likeness of the Lord. What did he see? Rav Ḥana bar Bizna: the knot of God's tefillin. And every good promise of God is fulfilled — 'I will make from you a mighty nation' came true through Reḥaviya's very many descendants.

😤 May One Provoke the Wicked?

Rabbi Shimon ben Yoḥai says yes; Rabbi Yitzḥak says no — the Gemara offers three resolutions

Permitted to provoke

  • Rabbi Yoḥanan in the name of Rabbi Shimon ben Yoḥai and the baraita of Rabbi Dostai son of Rabbi Matun
  • 'The keepers of the Torah will fight them' (Proverbs 28:4)
  • 'Do not compete with evil-doers' (Psalms 37:1) means only: do not become like them
  • Only one whose own conscience troubles him says otherwise

Do not provoke

  • Rabbi Yitzḥak: if the hour is smiling on a wicked man, do not provoke him
  • 'His ways prosper at all times' (Psalms 10:5)
  • He even wins in judgment and sees his enemies fall
  • Rav Huna: the wicked 'swallows the man more righteous than he' (Habakkuk 1:13) — but not the completely righteous

Three resolutions

  • 1️⃣ Personal matters: don't provoke; matters of Heaven: do
  • 2️⃣ Both about Heaven: provoke only when the hour is not smiling on him
  • 3️⃣ Both when the hour smiles: a completely righteous person may; one not completely righteous may not
  • Or: when the hour smiles on the wicked, even the righteous can be harmed
Also on 7b: Abraham was the first to call God 'Lord' (Genesis 15:8), and Daniel's prayer was answered 'for the sake of the Lord' — for Abraham's sake. Leah was the first to thank God ('This time I will give thanks', Genesis 29:35). Reuben = 'See the difference between my son and my father-in-law's son' Esau. Ruth = she merited David, who inundated God with song. Wayward children at home are harder than the war of Gog and Magog. One who fixes a place for prayer sees his enemies fall (II Samuel 7:10), and serving Torah is greater than studying it — Elisha 'poured water' over Elijah's hands (II Kings 3:11).
