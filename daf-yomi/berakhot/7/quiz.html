Berakhot 7 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
How does Rabbi Yoḥanan in the name of Rabbi Yosei derive that God prays?
A. From God asking Rabbi Yishmael to bless Him
B. From Isaiah 56:7, which says 'the house of My prayer' rather than 'the house of their prayer'
C. From 'God is furious every day' (Psalms 7:12)
D. From Moses' request 'Show me Your ways'
Correct: B — The verse says 'the house of My prayer' (בְּבֵית תְּפִלָּתִי), implying that God Himself prays. Rabbi Yishmael's story is a supporting baraita, not the source of the derivation.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
What lesson did Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi draw when he fell asleep at the moment of God's anger?
A. That the rooster is not a reliable sign of God's anger
B. That Balaam alone could find that moment
C. That it is not proper conduct to curse people, even the wicked, since 'His mercy is over all His creations'
D. That one should pray at sunrise before the kings bow to the sun
Correct: C — He concluded from his nodding off that it is improper to curse even a heretic, citing Psalms 145:9 and 'Punishment, even for the righteous, is not good' (Proverbs 17:26).
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to the Gemara's final explanation, who is 'the righteous person who suffers'?
A. A righteous person who is the son of a wicked person
B. A completely righteous person being tested
C. A righteous person who is not completely righteous
D. A wicked person who is the son of a righteous person
Correct: C — The first explanation (son of a wicked father) is rejected because children are punished for ancestors only when they adopt their deeds. The Gemara concludes the sufferer is a righteous person who is not completely righteous.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
On which of Moses' three requests do Rabbi Yoḥanan and Rabbi Meir disagree?
A. Whether the Divine Presence would rest upon Israel
B. Whether the Divine Presence would not rest upon the nations
C. Whether God revealed to Moses the ways in which He conducts the world
D. Whether Moses could see God's face
Correct: C — Rabbi Meir holds two requests were granted and one was not: God did not reveal His ways, as it says 'I will be gracious to whom I will be gracious' (Exodus 33:19), even to one who is not worthy.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
How does the Gemara resolve the tension between 'the keepers of the Torah will fight them' and Rabbi Yitzḥak's 'do not provoke a wicked person upon whom the hour is smiling'?
A. It rules entirely like Rabbi Yitzḥak: never provoke the wicked
B. It distinguishes personal matters from matters of Heaven, whether the hour is smiling, and whether the provoker is completely righteous
C. It says Rabbi Yitzḥak referred only to heretics
D. It says Proverbs 28:4 refers to the World-to-Come
Correct: B — The Gemara offers several resolutions: one may provoke in matters of Heaven but not personal matters; or only a wicked person on whom the hour is not smiling; or only a completely righteous person may provoke one on whom the hour smiles.
