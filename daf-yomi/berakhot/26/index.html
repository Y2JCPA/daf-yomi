📖 Overview: Chapter Three Ends, Chapter Four Begins

Berakhot 26a–26b
תְּפִלַּת הַשַּׁחַר עַד חֲצוֹת. רַבִּי יְהוּדָה אוֹמֵר: עַד אַרְבַּע שָׁעוֹת
The scene: The daf finishes chapter three with two loose ends: how to shield a Torah scroll during marital relations (a cloak over a chest counts as a vessel within a vessel, and a ten-handbreadth partition is only for one who has no other room), and how far one distances himself from feces — four cubits behind, but out of sight in front, with a chain of challenges about empty bathrooms. A final mishna rules that a zav who experienced a seminal emission still needs immersion, while Rabbi Yehuda exempts him. Chapter four, Tefillat HaShaḥar, then opens with the mishna's deadlines for each prayer: morning until noon (Rabbi Yehuda: four hours), afternoon until evening (Rabbi Yehuda: pelag haminḥa), evening with no fixed time, and Musaf all day (Rabbi Yehuda: seven hours). The Gemara asks whether a missed prayer can be made up at the next one, distinguishes error from deliberate omission, and traces the whole system to either the Patriarchs or the Temple offerings.

🚽 May One Pray Opposite a Bathroom?

Rav Huna's 'out of sight in front' meets Rav Ḥisda's leniency
📜 Mishna: distance four cubits from urine and feces. Rav Huna: only when they are behind him; in front, he moves until they are out of sight (מְלֹא עֵינָיו) — and the same for prayer
↓
❓ But Rav Ḥisda said one may stand opposite a bathroom and pray!
↓
💡 Answer: a bathroom with no feces in it
↓
❓ But Rav Yosef bar Ḥanina said 'the bathroom the Sages spoke of' is even one with no feces, and 'the bathhouse' is even one with no one in it!
↓
New structure
Rav Ḥisda means a building made as a bathroom but never yet used
Ravina's dilemma
Ravina asked whether designating a place as a bathroom takes effect — but only about praying inside it, not opposite it
↓
✅ Rava: Persian bathrooms, though they contain feces, are considered sealed, since the waste rolls away underground
Closing chapter three: A cloak on a chest is a vessel within a vessel. Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi requires a ten-handbreadth partition before a Torah scroll — but when Mar Zutra saw one in Mar bar Rav Ashi's bedroom he objected: that is for someone with no other room; you have another room! ('It did not enter my mind.') The chapter's last mishna: a zav who had a seminal emission, a menstruating woman who discharged semen, and a woman who saw menstrual blood after relations all need immersion; Rabbi Yehuda exempts — and, per Rabbi Ḥiyya, he exempts even one who had the emission first and then became a zav.

🔁 Can a Missed Prayer Be Made Up?

Rabbi Yoḥanan's rulings, a dilemma about minḥa, and a verse from Ecclesiastes
Gemara
Does everyone really hold the morning prayer ends at noon? Rabbi Yoḥanan said: one who erred and skipped the evening prayer prays twice in the morning; skipped the morning — prays twice at minḥa!
Answer
One may keep praying all day. Until noon he receives the reward of prayer at its time; afterward, the reward of prayer but not of prayer at its time.
Dilemma
Skipped minḥa — may he pray twice in the evening? Evening and morning are one day, 'there was evening and there was morning, one day' (Genesis 1:5). But minḥa and ma'ariv are two days, and prayer stands in place of a sacrifice: כֵּיוָן דְּעָבַר יוֹמוֹ בָּטַל קׇרְבָּנוֹ, once its day passed, its offering is void. Or is prayer mercy, offered whenever one wishes?
R' Yoḥanan
Rav Huna bar Yehuda in the name of Rabbi Yitzḥak in the name of Rabbi Yoḥanan: one who erred and skipped minḥa prays twice in the evening, and there is no 'its day passed, its offering is void'.
Objection
'That which is crooked cannot be made straight' (Ecclesiastes 1:15) — this is one who omitted the evening or morning Shema or prayer. 'That which is wanting cannot be numbered' — one whose friends were counted for a mitzva and he was not among them.
Resolution
Rabbi Yitzḥak in the name of Rabbi Yoḥanan: that baraita is about one who omitted deliberately (בְּמֵזִיד). Rav Ashi: the wording proves it — it says 'omitted' (בִּטֵּל), not 'erred' (טָעָה).
Bottom line: An accidental omission is repaired by doubling the next Amida; a deliberate one is 'crooked' and cannot be straightened.

🕯️ Making Up Minḥa on Shabbat and the Havdala Puzzle

The baraita's rule and the Tosefta that contradicts it
1️⃣ Missed minḥa on Friday afternoon: pray two Amidas on Shabbat night
↓
2️⃣ Missed minḥa on Shabbat: pray two weekday Amidas on Saturday night
↓
3️⃣ Say havdala (in 'Who graciously grants knowledge') in the first, the real evening prayer — not in the second, which stands in for minḥa
↓
4️⃣ If he said havdala in the second but not the first: the second counts, the first does not
↓
5️⃣ So omitting havdala is like not praying at all, and he must repeat?
↓
6️⃣ But the Tosefta: one who forgot 'the might of the rains' or the request for rain must repeat; one who forgot havdala in 'Who grants knowledge' does not, since he can say it over the cup. קַשְׁיָא — the contradiction stands
Rabbi Yehuda's 'until': Does 'until four hours' include the fourth hour or not? The Gemara begins to answer from 'until pelag haminḥa': if 'until' excludes, Rabbi Yehuda differs from the Rabbis; if it includes… the answer continues on the next daf.

👴 Who Instituted the Three Daily Prayers?

Both views have a baraita behind them — and they end up reconciled

R' Yosei son of R' Ḥanina: the Patriarchs

  • Abraham — Shaḥarit: 'Abraham rose early in the morning to the place where he had stood' (Genesis 19:27); standing means prayer, as 'Pinehas stood up and prayed' (Psalms 106:30)
  • Isaac — Minḥa: 'Isaac went out to converse (לָשׂוּחַ) in the field toward evening' (Genesis 24:63); conversing means prayer, as 'a prayer of the afflicted… pours out his complaint (שִׂיחוֹ)' (Psalms 102:1)
  • Jacob — Ma'ariv: 'He encountered (וַיִּפְגַּע) the place and slept there' (Genesis 28:11); encountering means prayer, as 'do not encounter Me' (Jeremiah 7:16)

R' Yehoshua ben Levi: the daily offerings

  • The prayers parallel the tamid offerings in time and character
  • Shaḥarit until noon, or four hours — like the morning tamid
  • Minḥa until evening, or pelag — like the afternoon tamid
  • Ma'ariv unfixed — like the limbs and fats burning all night
  • Musaf all day, or seven hours — like the Musaf offering

Reconciliation

  • Is the offerings baraita a refutation of Rabbi Yosei son of Rabbi Ḥanina?
  • He can answer: the Patriarchs instituted the prayers, and the Sages based them on the offerings (וְאַסְמְכִינְהוּ רַבָּנַן אַקׇּרְבָּנוֹת)
  • Proof: otherwise, who instituted Musaf according to him? No Patriarch did
Why it matters: The Gemara links this dispute to the mishna's deadlines: if prayer times mirror the offerings, then Rabbi Yehuda and the Rabbis are really arguing about when each tamid may be brought.
