📖 Overview: The Closing Amud of Berakhot

Berakhot 64a
תַּלְמִידֵי חֲכָמִים מַרְבִּים שָׁלוֹם בָּעוֹלָם — אַל תִּקְרֵי ״בָּנָיִךְ״ אֶלָּא ״בּוֹנָיִךְ״
The scene: The final amud of Berakhot finishes the count of Oved-edom's blessing — sixty-two descendants for having honored the ark — and then gathers a set of teachings from Rabbi Avin HaLevi: one who forces the hour is pushed aside by it, illustrated by Rav Yosef's refusal to head the yeshiva ahead of Rabba; why Psalm 20 invokes 'the God of Jacob'; a meal with a Torah scholar is like basking in the Shekhina; and the precise wording of a farewell. Rabbi Levi bar Ḥiyya praises going straight from synagogue to study hall, Rav says scholars have no rest in either world, and the tractate closes with Rabbi Elazar in the name of Rabbi Ḥanina: Torah scholars increase peace in the world. הדרן עלך הרואה וסליקא לה מסכת ברכות.

🔢 Counting Oved-edom's Blessing

The Gemara finishes the a fortiori from the end of 63b: what exactly was the blessing?
❓ 'The Lord blessed the house of Oved-edom because of the ark' — what was the blessing?
↓
👩‍👧 Rav Yehuda bar Zevida: Ḥamot and her eight daughters-in-law, each bearing six in a single womb
↓
📜 Proof: 'Peulletai the eighth… for God blessed him' (I Chron. 26:4–5) — the eight sons are the blessing; 'sixty-two of Oved-edom' (I Chron. 26:8)
↓
The arithmetic
9 women × 6 sons = 54
Plus
The original 8 sons → 62 in all
The point: If sweeping and sprinkling before an ark that neither ate nor drank earned this, how much more so hosting and feeding a Torah scholar.

⛰️ Sinai or Uprooter of Mountains?

Rabbi Avin HaLevi's rule about the hour, proved from Rabba and Rav Yosef
R' Avin HaLevi
כׇּל הַדּוֹחֵק אֶת הַשָּׁעָה — שָׁעָה דּוֹחַקְתּוֹ. Whoever forces the moment, the moment forces him aside; whoever yields to the moment, the moment yields to him.
The case
Rav Yosef was Sinai — vastly learned in the sources. Rabba was an uprooter of mountains — razor sharp. One of them had to be chosen as head of the yeshiva.
Babylonia
They sent to Eretz Yisrael: Sinai or uprooter of mountains — which takes precedence?
Eretz Yisrael
Sinai takes precedence, for everyone needs the owner of the wheat (מָרֵי חִטַּיָּא).
Rav Yosef
Still he declined — the Chaldean astrologers had told him: 'You will reign for two years.'
Outcome
Rabba presided twenty-two years; afterwards Rav Yosef presided two and a half years. In all Rabba's years, Rav Yosef did not even summon a bloodletter to his house — he assumed no airs of authority.
Lesson: Rav Yosef relinquished the opportunity and still ultimately received what was destined for him.

🪵 More From Rabbi Avin HaLevi

Three further teachings and their verses
TeachingVerseExplanation
Why 'the God of Jacob' and not of Abraham and Isaac?'The Lord will answer you on the day of distress; the name of the God of Jacob set you on high' (Ps. 20:2)The owner of the beam should go in with its thick end — Jacob, who fathered the nation, must carry its heaviest load and keep praying for his children
A meal with a Torah scholar present'Aaron and all the elders of Israel came to eat bread with Moses' father-in-law before God' (Ex. 18:12)They ate before Moses, not literally before God — so one who enjoys such a meal is as if he enjoyed the radiance of the Shekhina
Taking leave of the living: say לֵךְ לְשָׁלוֹם, 'go to peace'Jethro to Moses, 'Go to peace' (Ex. 4:18)Moses went up and succeeded
Never לֵךְ בְּשָׁלוֹם, 'go in peace', to the livingDavid to Absalom, 'Go in peace' (II Sam. 15:9)Absalom went and was hanged
Taking leave of the dead: say 'go in peace''You shall come to your fathers in peace' (Gen. 15:15)The reverse of the farewell for the living

🏃 From Strength to Strength

Two readings of Psalms 84:8 — and what Torah scholars are and are not granted
1️⃣ R' Levi bar Ḥiyya: one who leaves the synagogue and enters the study hall to engage in Torah merits to receive the Divine Presence
↓
2️⃣ Source: יֵלְכוּ מֵחַיִל אֶל חָיִל יֵרָאֶה אֶל אֱלֹהִים בְּצִיּוֹן — 'They go from strength to strength; each appears before God in Zion' (Ps. 84:8)
↓
3️⃣ R' Ḥiyya bar Ashi in the name of Rav: the same verse teaches that Torah scholars have no rest, neither in this world nor in the World-to-Come
↓
4️⃣ Why? Because in both worlds they are always going 'from strength to strength' — constant ascent, never standing still
Connection: Prayer leads into study, and study never ends — the daf's build-up to the tractate's closing praise of scholars.

🕊️ Torah Scholars Increase Peace in the World

The final teaching of Masechet Berakhot
אָמַר רַבִּי אֶלְעָזָר אָמַר רַבִּי חֲנִינָא: תַּלְמִידֵי חֲכָמִים מַרְבִּים שָׁלוֹם בָּעוֹלָם, שֶׁנֶּאֱמַר: ״וְכׇל בָּנַיִךְ לִמּוּדֵי ה׳ וְרַב שְׁלוֹם בָּנָיִךְ״ — אַל תִּקְרֵי ״בָּנָיִךְ״ אֶלָּא ״בּוֹנָיִךְ״
Rabbi Elazar in the name of Rabbi Ḥanina: Torah scholars increase peace in the world, as it says, 'And all your children shall be taught of the Lord, and great shall be the peace of your children' (Isaiah 54:13). Do not read banayikh, your children, but bonayikh, your builders — scholars are the builders of their generation's peace. The Gemara strings together four more verses of peace: 'Those who love Your Torah have great peace; there is no stumbling block for them' (Ps. 119:165); 'May there be peace within your walls, prosperity within your palaces' (Ps. 122:7); 'For the sake of my brothers and friends I shall say: Peace be within you; for the sake of the House of the Lord our God I will seek your good' (Ps. 122:8–9); 'May the Lord give strength to His people; the Lord will bless His people with peace' (Ps. 29:11). With that, the tractate ends: הדרן עלך הרואה וסליקא לה מסכת ברכות.
