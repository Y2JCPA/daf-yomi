📖 Overview: Primary Foods, the Seven Species, and What Comes After

Berakhot 44a–44b
כׇּל שֶׁהוּא עִיקָּר וְעִמּוֹ טְפֵלָה — מְבָרֵךְ עַל הָעִיקָּר וּפוֹטֵר אֶת הַטְּפֵלָה
The scene: Two mishnayot anchor this daf. The first sets the principle of primary and secondary foods: bless the primary and exempt the secondary — a rule the Gemara can only illustrate with the impossibly sweet fruits of Genosar, which leads into hyperbolic tales of Rabbi Yoḥanan's feasts and King Yannai's estates. The second mishna asks what blessing follows the fruits for which Eretz Yisrael is praised: Rabban Gamliel demands the full Grace after Meals, the Rabbis one abridged blessing, and Rabbi Akiva extends Grace even to a filling boiled vegetable. The Gemara supplies the text of the abridged blessing, debates its closing formula, then turns to what is said after eggs, vegetables, and water, and ends with a baraita on foods that heal and foods that harm.

🧂 When Is Bread Secondary to Salted Food?

The mishna's principle and the Gemara's unusual case
📜 Mishna: they brought salted food first and bread with it — bless the salted food and exempt the bread, for the bread is secondary (טְפֵלָה)
↓
❓ Is there any case where salted food is primary and bread secondary?
↓
💡 Rav Aḥa son of Rav Avira in Rav Ashi's name: it was taught about those who eat fruits of Genosar — so sweet that salted food is eaten to cut the sweetness, and bread only accompanies the salted food
↓
Rav
A meal without salt is not a meal
Rabbi Yoḥanan
A meal without a cooked dish with gravy (שְׂרִיף) is not a meal
Genosar hyperbole: Rabba bar bar Ḥana: when a hundred of us followed Rabbi Yoḥanan to eat Genosar fruit, each brought him ten; when ten of us, each brought a hundred — and he swore he had tasted no sustenance. Rabbi Abbahu ate until a fly slid off his forehead; Rav Ami and Rav Asi until their hair fell out; Reish Lakish until he became confused and Rabbi Yehuda Nesia's men carried him home. Rav Dimi added King Yannai's city that supplied 600,000 bowls of sardines a week to the fig-cutters, and a tree yielding forty se'a of pigeons a month.

🍞 Grace After Grapes? Rabban Gamliel vs. the Rabbis

Mishna: after grapes, figs, and pomegranates — three blessings or one abridged blessing?
R' Gamliel
After the fruits for which the land is praised, recite the three blessings of Grace after Meals.
The Rabbis
One blessing abridged from the three (בְּרָכָה אַחַת מֵעֵין שָׁלֹשׁ).
R' Gamliel
My source: 'A land of wheat and barley, vines, figs and pomegranates…' (Deut. 8:8), 'a land in which you will eat bread…' (8:9), 'and you will eat and be satisfied and bless' (8:10). The fruits are inside the passage that commands blessing.
The Rabbis
The word 'land' in verse 9 interrupts the matter; 'you will eat and bless' refers only to the bread that follows it.
Gemara
But for Rabban Gamliel too, 'land' should interrupt! — No: for him that verse excludes one who chews raw wheat, which is not bread even for him.
R' Akiva
Even boiled vegetables, if they are one's sustenance, require three blessings. Rav Ashi: this means a cabbage stalk, which is filling.
Two rules of thumb: Rabbi Ḥanina: anything of the five grains — 'Who creates kinds of nourishment' before, me'ein shalosh after. Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi: anything of the seven species — 'fruit of the tree' before, me'ein shalosh after.

📜 The Text of Me'ein Shalosh — and How to End It

Abaye asked Rav Dimi: what is the one blessing abridged from three?
עַל הָעֵץ וְעַל פְּרִי הָעֵץ, וְעַל תְּנוּבַת הַשָּׂדֶה, וְעַל אֶרֶץ חֶמְדָּה טוֹבָה וּרְחָבָה… רַחֵם ה׳ אֱלֹהֵינוּ עַל יִשְׂרָאֵל עַמֶּךָ, וְעַל יְרוּשָׁלַיִם עִירֶךָ… כִּי אַתָּה טוֹב וּמֵטִיב לַכֹּל
The blessing: For tree fruit: 'For the tree and the fruit of the tree, and for the produce of the field, and for the desirable, good and spacious land You gave our ancestors to eat of its fruit and be satisfied with its goodness. Have compassion, Lord our God, on Israel Your people, on Jerusalem Your city, on Your Temple and Your altar. Rebuild Jerusalem Your holy city swiftly in our days, bring us up into it and gladden us in it, for You are good and do good to all.' For the five grains it opens 'For the nourishment and sustenance and for the produce of the field' and closes 'for the land and for the nourishment'. How does the fruit version close? One normally does not seal a blessing with two themes, yet Rav Dimi reported that Rav sealed the New Moon blessing 'Who sanctifies Israel and the New Moons'. So: Rav Ḥisda said 'for the land and for its fruits'; Rabbi Yoḥanan said 'for the land and for the fruits'. Rav Amram: no dispute — one is for us in Babylonia, one for them in Eretz Yisrael. Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak objected: shall they eat and we bless for their land's fruit? Reverse it: Rav Ḥisda (in Babylonia) says 'the fruits'; Rabbi Yoḥanan (in Eretz Yisrael) says 'its fruits'.

🥬 The Sages' Food Baraita: Helpful, Harmful, Remedied

'Cabbage for nourishment, beets for healing; woe to the house through which the turnip passes'

Beneficial

  • Spleen — good for the teeth (but bad for the intestines: chew it and spit it out)
  • Leeks — good for the intestines (but bad for the teeth: boil and swallow whole)
  • Any whole creature restores the soul — even tiny reed-fish (Rav Pappa)
  • What is near the vital organs restores the soul — the neck (Rav Aḥa bar Ya'akov); Rava asked for meat 'from where the blessing is made'
  • Cabbage: even for nourishment, and also healing; six healers: cabbage, beets, chamomile water, honey, stomach, heret, liver

Harmful

  • Raw vegetables make one pale — Rabbi Yitzḥak: at the first meal after bloodletting
  • Raw vegetables before the fourth hour: forbidden to eat, and one who does may not be spoken to because of the smell — Mar Zutra declined while Ameimar and Rav Ashi ate
  • Anything undersized stunts growth — even a kid worth a zuz, unless it reached a quarter of its size (Rav Ḥisda)
  • A small salted fish, poorly roasted and without beer after it, can kill on the 7th, 17th, or 27th day (some say 23rd)

The turnip

  • Yet Rava told his servant: if you see a turnip in the market, don't ask what to eat with bread today
  • So when is it harmful? Abaye and Rav: without meat; Rava and Rabbi Yoḥanan: without wine; Shmuel: without wood, i.e. undercooked
  • Rav Pappa's wife broke it with eighty palm logs after cooking, since Rav Pappa's household lacked wine
Closing thread: The mishna's 'one who drinks water for his thirst' is examined at the end of the daf: Rav Idi bar Avin says the phrase comes to exclude one who drinks for another reason.
