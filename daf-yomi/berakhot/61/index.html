📖 Overview: Inclinations, Love of God, and Rabbi Akiva's Death

Berakhot 61a–61b
אוֹי לִי מִיּוֹצְרִי וְאוֹי לִי מִיִּצְרִי
The scene: The daf begins with aggada on the creation of the human being: the double yod in וַיִּיצֶר, the two inclinations, and the long dispute between Rav and Shmuel over whether the tzela from which Eve was formed was a whole face or a tail. That leads to modesty in conduct — not walking behind a woman, and where one may or may not walk — and then to an anatomy of the inner life: the evil inclination as a fly at the entrances of the heart, the two advising kidneys, and Rabbi Yosei HaGelili's division of people into righteous, wicked and middling. The mishna's phrase with all your soul is then unpacked, and the daf reaches its climax in the story of Rabbi Akiva, who taught Torah under Roman decree, told the parable of the fox and the fish, and died reciting Shema. It closes on the mishna's rules of reverence toward the Temple's Eastern Gate.

✌️ Why Is 'Vayyitzer' Spelled With Two Yods?

A derasha proposed, attacked, and replaced by two alternatives
📜 Rav Naḥman bar Rav Ḥisda: 'Then the Lord God formed [וַיִּיצֶר] man' (Genesis 2:7) is written with a double yod — God created two inclinations, one good and one evil
↓
❗ Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak objects: an animal, where vayyitzer is not written with a double yod, would then have no inclination — yet we see it damages, bites and kicks!
↓
Answer 1
Rabbi Shimon ben Pazi: the doubling voices what a person says in every situation — אוֹי לִי מִיּוֹצְרִי וְאוֹי לִי מִיִּצְרִי, 'woe unto me from my Creator and woe unto me from my inclination'
Answer 2
Rabbi Yirmeya ben Elazar: God created Adam with two faces (du partzufin), male and female in one body — 'You have formed me behind and before' (Psalms 139:5)
↓
🪰 Elsewhere on the daf, the evil inclination is described: Rav — like a fly sitting between the two entrances of the heart (Ecclesiastes 10:1); Shmuel — like a grain of wheat, from ḥatat/ḥitta in 'transgression couches at the door' (Genesis 4:7)
And in the body: The Sages taught that a person has two kidneys, one advising good and one advising evil — reasonably the good one on the right, since 'a wise man's understanding is at his right hand, but a fool's at his left' (Ecclesiastes 10:2).

👥 Was the Tzela a Face or a Tail?

Rav and Shmuel on 'And the Lord God built the tzela' (Genesis 2:22)
Rav/Shmuel
One said the tzela means a face — Eve was originally one whole side of Adam. The other said it means a tail, an appendage.
For 'face'
That explains 'You have formed me behind and before', the double yod of vayyitzer, 'male and female He created them', and 'He closed up the place with flesh' — the open side had to be sealed.
For 'tail'
'Behind and before' is Rabbi Ami's reading: Adam was behind, last in creation, and before, first for punishment. Not the punishment of the snake — Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi taught that honor begins with the greatest and cursing with the least (snake, then Eve, then Adam) — but the flood, which blotted out 'both man and cattle' (Genesis 7:23).
For 'tail'
The double yod is Rabbi Shimon ben Pazi's 'woe from my Creator, woe from my inclination'; 'male and female' is Rabbi Abbahu's resolution — at first the thought arose to create two, and in the end only one was created; 'closed up with flesh' refers only to the place of the incision.
For 'tail'
And 'He built' is straightforward — a whole new building was needed.
For 'face'
'He built' is Rabbi Shimon ben Menasya's derasha: God braided Eve's hair, since in the coastal towns braiding is called building. Alternatively (Rav Ḥisda): He built her like a storehouse, narrow above and wide below, to hold a fetus.
And who walked in front? Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak: reasonably the man, as a baraita teaches that one should not walk behind a woman on a path, even his own wife. Rabbi Yoḥanan ranked it: behind a lion rather than behind a woman, behind a woman rather than behind idolatry, and behind idolatry rather than behind a synagogue while the congregation is praying.

🫀 Who Judges a Person — the Good Inclination or the Evil One?

Rabbi Yosei HaGelili's three categories, and the Sages' reactions

The Righteous

  • Ruled by the good inclination
  • 'And my heart is dead within me' (Psalms 109:22)
  • The evil inclination has been banished from the heart entirely

The Wicked

  • Ruled by the evil inclination
  • 'Transgression speaks to the wicked, there is no fear of God before his eyes' (Psalms 36:2)

The Middling (Beinonim)

  • Ruled by both
  • 'Because He stands at the right hand of the needy, to save him from them that rule his soul' (Psalms 109:31)
  • Rabba: People like us are middling
  • Abaye to Rabba: if you are middling, you leave no room for any creature to live
Rava and Rav: Rava said the world was created only for the fully wicked or the fully righteous, and that a person should know of himself whether or not he is completely righteous. Rav said the world was created only for Ahab ben Omri and for Rabbi Ḥanina ben Dosa — this world for Ahab, the World-to-Come for Rabbi Ḥanina.

🦊 The Fox, the Fish, and the Iron Combs

Rabbi Akiva lives out 'with all your soul' — even if He takes your soul
1️⃣ On 'with all your heart, all your soul and all your might' (Deut. 6:5), Rabbi Eliezer taught: for one whose body is dearer than his money — 'with all your soul'; for one whose money is dearer than his body — 'with all your might'. Rabbi Akiva: 'with all your soul' means even if He takes your soul
↓
2️⃣ Rome decreed that Israel may not engage in Torah. Pappos ben Yehuda found Rabbi Akiva convening public assemblies and asked: are you not afraid of the empire?
↓
3️⃣ 🦊 A fox saw fish fleeing the nets and invited them onto dry land to live together. They answered: you are called the cleverest of animals, but you are a fool — if we fear here in the water that gives us life, how much more so on land, where we die
↓
4️⃣ So too, said Rabbi Akiva: if we fear the empire while we sit and study Torah, of which it says 'for that is your life and the length of your days' (Deut. 30:20), how much more so if we abandon it
↓
5️⃣ Soon Rabbi Akiva was imprisoned, and Pappos alongside him. Pappos said: Happy are you, Akiva, seized for words of Torah; woe to Pappos, seized for idle matters
↓
6️⃣ Led out to execution at the time of Shema, his flesh raked with iron combs, he accepted the yoke of Heaven. To his students' 'Our teacher, even now?' he answered: all my days I was troubled by 'with all your soul' — now that the chance has come, shall I not fulfil it? He prolonged אֶחָד until his soul departed on that word
The angels' protest: 'This is Torah and this its reward?' God answered with the end of the verse — 'whose portion is in this life' (Psalms 17:14). A Divine Voice proclaimed: happy are you, Rabbi Akiva, that your soul departed on 'One', and that you are destined for the life of the World-to-Come.

🧭 Reverence Toward the Temple: Where and Which Way

The mishna's ban on irreverence at the Eastern Gate, and the baraitot on direction
OpinionThe rulingPractical upshot
Rav Yehuda in Rav's name; Rabbi Yoḥanan (via Rabbi Abba)The mishna's ban applies only from Mount Scopus (Tzofim) inward, only where one can see the Temple, only where no fence blocks the view, and only while the Divine Presence rests thereIt is a rule about visible proximity to the Temple, not about the whole land
First tannaIn Judea one relieves oneself facing north and south; in the Galilee only east and westOne must never face toward Jerusalem
Rabbi YoseiProhibited only where one can see the Temple, where there is no fence, and while the Divine Presence rests thereA lenient, situational rule
Rabbi YehudaProhibited while the Temple is standing; permitted once it is notThe obligation lapses with the destruction
Rabbi AkivaProhibited everywhere — the difference from the first tanna being lands outside Eretz YisraelRabba arranged the bricks in his bathroom east–west; when Abaye moved them, Rabba reset them, saying: I hold like Rabbi Akiva, who prohibits it everywhere
A textual note: The Gemara asks why the anonymous Rabbis differ from the first tanna at all, and answers that they disagree about the sides — places in Judea not directly east or west of Jerusalem, or in the Galilee not directly north of it.
