📖 Overview: Honor at the Table and the Boundaries of a Zimmun

Berakhot 47a–47b
אֵין מְכַבְּדִין אֶלָּא בְּפֶתַח שֶׁיֵּשׁ בָּהּ מְזוּזָה
The scene: The daf opens with stories about honor: Ravin only defers to Abaye at the synagogue door, diners wait for the one who breaks bread, and the one who breaks bread waits for the amen to finish. The Gemara then defines a proper amen and warns against the hasty or 'orphaned' amen. Returning to zimmun, it explains why the mishna bothered to list obviously permitted and forbidden foods (demai, first tithe, redeemed second tithe), why a Kuti is counted but an am ha'aretz is not, and how a group completes a quorum of ten — with a minor, a slave, or even two scholars who sharpen each other in halakha.

🚪 Ravin and Abaye on the Road: Where Does One Defer?

A small slight on a donkey ride leads to a rule about doorways
Abaye
Ravin's donkey went ahead of mine and he did not say 'Let the Master go first'. Ever since he came up from the West he has become arrogant!
Ravin
At the synagogue door: Let the Master enter first.
Abaye
Until now was I not Master? Why defer to me only here and not on the road?
Ravin
Rabbi Yoḥanan said: אֵין מְכַבְּדִין אֶלָּא בְּפֶתַח שֶׁיֵּשׁ בָּהּ מְזוּזָה — one defers only at a doorway that has a mezuza.
Gemara
Only where there is a mezuza? Then at a synagogue or study hall, which have no mezuza, would one not defer?
Answer
Say rather: at a doorway worthy of a mezuza — but not on a road or a bridge.
Say it in your teacher's words: Rav said diners may not eat until the one who breaks bread has tasted. Rav Safra insisted the word was 'taste', not 'eat'. There is no practical difference — the lesson is that one must repeat a teaching in the exact language of one's teacher.

🍞 Rules of the Table and the Proper Amen

Small courtesies that the Sages turned into halakha
RuleSourceDetail
Diners wait for the one who breaks bread to tasteRav (via Rav Yehuda b. Rav Shmuel bar Sheilat)No one at the table may taste anything before him

The one who breaks bread reaches out firstBaraitaBut he may defer to his teacher or to someone greater than he

No amen ḥatufa, ketufa, or yetomaBaraitaNot with the first syllable swallowed, not with the second cut off, not without knowing which blessing you answer
Do not toss a blessing from your mouthBaraitaA blessing should not be discharged quickly and indifferently
Measure for measureBen AzzaiAn orphaned amen — his children orphaned; an abbreviated amen — his days abbreviated; one who extends his amen — his days and years are extended
Joining late: Rav Shimi bar Ḥiyya arrived late and ate hurriedly to join Rav and Shmuel's zimmun. Rav said: we have finished. Shmuel replied: if they brought me truffles, or a young pigeon for Abba, wouldn't we still eat? The meal is not over. When Rav Aḥa arrived at the students' meal he claimed a main participant should recite, but the halakha is that the greatest person recites, even if he came at the end.

🌾 Why Does the Mishna Teach the Obvious?

Each 'peshita' hides a borderline case
❓ The mishna lists foods whose eaters join a zimmun — but each case seems obvious. What is it really teaching?
↓
Demai
Forbidden to him? He could declare his property ownerless, become poor, and the poor may eat demai (though Beit Shammai disagree, per Rav Huna).
First tithe, teruma taken
The Levite took it while the grain was still on the stalks and separated only teruma of the tithe. Reish Lakish: 'a tenth of the tithe' (Num. 18:26) — no teruma gedola needed.
Redeemed second tithe / hekdesh
He paid the principal but not the added fifth. Lesson: the missing fifth does not invalidate the redemption. Likewise a waiter who ate an olive-bulk counts as a participant.
↓
🔁 Rav Pappa to Abaye: then even a Levite who took his tithe from the pile should be exempt! Abaye: 'From all your tithes you shall set apart' (Num. 18:29) — once threshed and piled it is 'grain', and the priest's share applies.
↓
Tevel
Only rabbinic tevel: grown in an unperforated flowerpot.
Improperly redeemed
Second tithe redeemed with an unminted coin — 'bind up the money' (Deut. 14:25) means stamped coin; hekdesh exchanged for land, not money (Lev. 27:19).
Gentile
A convert who was circumcised but has not immersed — Rabbi Yoḥanan: until he immerses he is a gentile.
Method: Whenever the Gemara says 'peshita' (obvious), the answer is a narrower case the mishna must have had in mind.

🧑‍🌾 Who Is an Am Ha'aretz — and Why a Kuti Still Counts

The mishna counts a Kuti; a baraita excludes an am ha'aretz

Defining the am ha'aretz (tithes and purity)

  • Rabbi Meir: one who does not eat ordinary food in ritual purity
  • The Rabbis: one who does not tithe his produce properly
  • Rava: the exclusion follows the Rabbis — and Kutim tithe scrupulously, being exacting in any mitzva they embraced, even more than Jews
  • Abaye's alternative: the mishna speaks of a Kuti who is a ḥaver

Defining the am ha'aretz (the baraita)

  • Rabbi Eliezer: does not recite Shema evening and morning
  • Rabbi Yehoshua: does not don tefillin
  • Ben Azzai: no tzitzit on his garment
  • Rabbi Natan: no mezuza on his door
  • Rabbi Natan bar Yosef: has children but does not raise them to Torah study
  • Aḥerim: even one who read Bible and studied Mishna but did not serve Torah scholars — Rav Huna: the halakha follows Aḥerim

A cautionary tale

  • Rami bar Ḥama refused to include Rav Menashya bar Taḥlifa, who had studied Sifra, Sifrei, and halakhot, in a zimmun
  • Rava: Rami bar Ḥama died only because of that
  • Answer 1: Rav Menashya did serve the Sages; Rami bar Ḥama simply failed to check
  • Answer 2: one who hears halakhot from the Sages and reviews them is like a Torah scholar
Takeaway: Exclusion from a zimmun is serious; a person's Torah standing must be examined carefully before he is left out.

🔟 Completing a Quorum of Ten

Who — or what — may serve as the tenth?
1️⃣ Rabbi Yosei: a minor in a cradle is counted. The Gemara reconciles this with the mishna via Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi: he is not counted for a zimmun of three, but may be an adjunct to complete ten
↓
2️⃣ Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi: nine and a slave join. Objection: Rabbi Eliezer freed his slave to complete a minyan! Answer: two were missing — he freed one and used the other unfreed
↓
3️⃣ But Rav Yehuda says freeing a Canaanite slave violates 'they shall serve you forever' (Lev. 25:46)! Answer: a mitzva that benefits the many is different
↓
4️⃣ Rav Huna: nine and the ark join. Rav Naḥman: is an ark a man? Rather — nine that appear like ten, whether gathered or scattered
↓
5️⃣ Rav Ami: two and Shabbat join. Rav Naḥman: is Shabbat a man? Rather — two scholars who sharpen each other in halakha count as three. Rav Ḥisda: like me and Rav Sheshet; Rav Sheshet: like me and Rav Ḥisda
↓
6️⃣ Rabbi Yoḥanan: a mature minor who shows signs of puberty is counted; a baraita adds that one is not exacting about a minor
↓
7️⃣ Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi: rise early to be among the first ten in the synagogue — even if a hundred come after, you receive a reward equivalent to them all
Note: The Gemara emends 'receives the reward of them all' to 'a reward equivalent to them all' — no one takes away another's reward.
