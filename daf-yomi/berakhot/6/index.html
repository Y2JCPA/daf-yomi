📖 Overview: The Shekhina in the Synagogue

Berakhot 6a–6b
וּמִנַּיִן לַעֲשָׂרָה שֶׁמִּתְפַּלְּלִין שֶׁשְּׁכִינָה עִמָּהֶם — שֶׁנֶּאֱמַר: אֱלֹהִים נִצָּב בַּעֲדַת אֵל
The scene: The daf opens with the reward for waiting for one's fellow in the synagogue, then plunges into a startling baraita of Abba Binyamin: if the eye could see, no creature could withstand the demons. Abba Binyamin's next statement — prayer is heard only in a synagogue — leads Ravin bar Rav Adda to map where the Divine Presence rests: with ten who pray, three who judge, two who learn, even one alone. The same tradition asks what is written in God's own tefillin. The second amud is a string of teachings about synagogue conduct: God asks after a regular who is missing, one should fix a place for prayer like Abraham, run to a lecture even on Shabbat, and never pray behind the synagogue with one's back to it. It closes with Rabbi Ḥelbo's chain of Rav Huna's sayings on Mincha, weddings, fear of Heaven, and greeting.

🕍 Where Does the Shekhina Rest?

Ravin bar Rav Adda in the name of Rabbi Yitzḥak — and why each smaller group still needs its own verse
GroupVerseWhy say it, if fewer already suffice?
Ten who pray'God stands in the congregation of God' (Ps. 82:1)The Shekhina arrives before the ten; for three it comes only once they sit
Three who sit in judgment'In the midst of the judges He judges' (Ps. 82:1)You might think judgment merely keeps the peace and is not Torah — it teaches that judgment is also Torah
Two who study Torah'Then those who feared the Lord spoke one with another... and a book of remembrance was written' (Mal. 3:16)Two have their words written in the book of remembrance; one does not
One who studies alone'In every place where I cause My name to be mentioned, I will come to you and bless you' (Ex. 20:21)Even a single learner is blessed
Rav Ashi on 'those who think upon His name': One who intended to do a mitzva and was prevented by circumstances beyond his control — Scripture credits him as if he did it. And Abba Binyamin: a person's prayer is fully heard only in a synagogue — 'to hear the song and the prayer' (I Kings 8:28): in a place of song, there let prayer be.

📿 What Is Written in God's Tefillin?

Rabbi Avin bar Rav Adda: 'The Lord has sworn by His right hand and by the arm of His strength' (Isa. 62:8) — His right hand is Torah, the arm of His strength is tefillin
R' Naḥman
Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak to Rav Ḥiyya bar Avin: these tefillin of the Master of the world — what is written in them?
R' Ḥiyya
'Who is like Your people Israel, one nation in the land' (I Chron. 17:21).
R' Naḥman
Does the Holy One really glory in the praise of Israel?
R' Ḥiyya
Yes. 'You have affirmed the Lord this day' and 'the Lord has affirmed you this day' (Deut. 26:17–18): You made Me a single entity in the world — שְׁמַע יִשְׂרָאֵל ה׳ אֱלֹהֵינוּ ה׳ אֶחָד — and I will make you a single entity in the world: 'Who is like Your people Israel, one nation.'
Rav Aḥa
Rav Aḥa son of Rava to Rav Ashi: fine for one compartment — what is in the other compartments of the head tefillin?
Rav Ashi
'For who is a great nation', 'And who is a great nation' (Deut. 4:7–8), 'Happy are you, Israel' (33:29), 'Or has God attempted' (4:34), 'And to elevate you above all nations' (26:19).
Rav Aḥa
Then there are too many compartments! Rather: the two 'great nation' verses share one; 'Happy are you' and 'Who is like Your people' share one; 'Or has God attempted' is one; 'And to elevate you' is one — and on the arm, all are written together.
Tefillin as strength: 'All the nations shall see that the name of the Lord is called upon you, and they will fear you' (Deut. 28:10) — Rabbi Eliezer the Great: these are the tefillin of the head.

🙅 Praying Behind the Synagogue

Rav Huna reads Psalm 12:9 — 'The wicked walk round about, when vileness is exalted among the sons of men'
❌ Rav Huna: one who prays behind the synagogue is called wicked — 'the wicked walk round about'
↓
🔄 Abaye: only if he does not turn his face toward the synagogue; if he faces it, there is no objection
↓
⚔️ A man prayed behind a synagogue with his back to it. Elijah, appearing as an Arab, said: 'Is this how you stand before your Master?' — drew a sword and killed him
↓
❓ And the end of the verse — 'when vileness [kerum zulut] is exalted among the sons of men'?
↓
Rav Beivai's answer
Things that stand at the height of the world (רוּמוֹ שֶׁל עוֹלָם) — yet people treat them with contempt
R' Yoḥanan & R' Elazar
One who needs other people — his face changes like the kerum, a seabird (Rav Dimi) that shifts colors as the sun rises
R' Ami & R' Asi
It is as if he is judged with two judgments, fire and water — 'we came through fire and water' (Ps. 66:12)
Result: Facing away from the congregation is contempt for the synagogue; the verse's second half turns to the shame of dependence on others.

🚶 Synagogue Habits: Praised and Faulted

Rabbi Yitzḥak, Rabbi Yoḥanan, and Rav Huna on showing up, staying put, and how to walk

Praised

  • A fixed place for prayer — the God of Abraham helps him; at his death they say 'Where is the humble one, the pious one, of Abraham's disciples?' Abraham 'rose early to the place where he had stood' (Gen. 19:27), and standing means prayer
  • Entering: it is a mitzva to run — 'let us pursue to know the Lord' (Hos. 6:3)
  • Rabbi Zeira: I once thought sages running to the Shabbat lecture were desecrating Shabbat — until I heard Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi: always run to a matter of halakha, even on Shabbat (Hos. 11:10). Now I run too

Faulted

  • A regular who misses a day — God asks after him (Isa. 50:10): if he went for a mitzva, he has light; for an optional matter, no light, because he should have trusted in the name of the Lord
  • Rabbi Yoḥanan: when God enters a synagogue and finds no ten, He is immediately angry — 'Why, when I came, was there no one?' (Isa. 50:2)
  • Leaving: do not take large strides (Rav Huna; Abaye limits this to leaving)
'The reward for...' (אַגְרָא): Rabbi Zeira — the lecture: the running. Abaye — the kalla: the crowding. Rava — halakhic tradition: the reasoning. Rav Pappa — a house of mourning: the silence. Mar Zutra — a fast: the charity. Rav Sheshet — a eulogy: raising voices. Rav Ashi — a wedding: the words.

⏰ Rabbi Ḥelbo's Chain of Rav Huna's Sayings

Four teachings transmitted 'Rabbi Ḥelbo said that Rav Huna said'
1️⃣ Be vigilant in the afternoon prayer: Elijah was answered only at Mincha — 'answer me' that fire descend, 'answer me' that they not call it sorcery (I Kings 18:36–37). Rabbi Yoḥanan adds the evening prayer (Ps. 141:2); Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak adds the morning (Ps. 5:4)
↓
2️⃣ One who enjoys a groom's feast and does not gladden him violates five voices (Jer. 33:11). Gladden him and — Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi — merit the Torah given with five voices; Rabbi Abbahu — as if he offered a thanks-offering; Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak — as if he rebuilt a ruin of Jerusalem
↓
3️⃣ One who has fear of Heaven — his words are heeded: 'the end of the matter, all having been heard... for this is all of man' (Eccl. 12:13). Rabbi Elazar: the whole world was created only for him; Ben Azzai (or Ben Zoma): created to be his companions
↓
4️⃣ If you know your fellow usually greets you, greet him first — 'seek peace and pursue it' (Ps. 34:15). Fail to return a greeting and you are called a robber: 'the spoils of the poor are in your houses' (Isa. 3:14)
Objection on the five voices: 'All the people saw the voices' (Ex. 20:15) suggests more than five at Sinai — but those sounds came before the giving of the Torah and are not counted.
