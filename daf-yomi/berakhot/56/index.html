📖 Overview: Bar Haddaya and the Power of the Interpreter

Berakhot 56a–56b
כׇּל הַחֲלוֹמוֹת הוֹלְכִין אַחַר הַפֶּה
The scene: The daf opens with two kings who dreamed exactly what a sage told them they would see, proving that dreams come from daytime thoughts. Then comes the long story of bar Haddaya, who interpreted identical dreams favorably for Abaye, who paid, and disastrously for Rava, who did not — until Rava found the interpreter's book reading 'all dreams follow the mouth' and cursed him. Rabbi Yishmael and Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi show how sages decode dream images, including a heretic whose every dream exposed a sin. The second amud lists symbols with double meanings and Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi's advice: rise early and recite the good verse before the bad one can take hold.

⚖️ The Fall of Bar Haddaya

Rava pays, the readings flip, and the interpreter's own book condemns him
💵 Rava finally pays. Now a fallen wall = property without limit; Abaye's house collapsing on him = Abaye's yeshiva will come to you; his own house's bricks carried off = your teachings spread through the world; the Egyptian Hallel = miracles will be done for you
↓
📕 On a ship, bar Haddaya's book falls. Rava reads in it: כׇּל הַחֲלוֹמוֹת הוֹלְכִין אַחַר הַפֶּה. 'Scoundrel! It all depended on you, and you caused me such pain'
↓
😡 Rava forgives everything except the daughter of Rav Ḥisda, his wife: 'May this man be delivered to a kingdom that has no mercy on him'
↓
🚶 Bar Haddaya: the curse of a sage comes true even when baseless, all the more so Rava's, which was just. 'The Master said: exile atones for sin' — he flees to Rome
↓
The wardrobe keeper
A needle in my finger; a worm between two fingers — 'give me a zuz.' No coin, no answer. A worm over the whole hand — 'a worm has eaten all the silks!'
The verdict
The keeper, about to be executed: 'Bring the one who knew and did not say.' Because of your zuz the king's silks were ruined
↓
☠️ Two cedars bent together with a rope, a leg tied to each, the rope released — bar Haddaya split in two
The lesson: An interpreter who sells outcomes for a fee is not a bystander; when he withholds a reading for want of a coin, the ruin that follows is charged to him.

🧩 Sages Decode the Image

Rabbi Yishmael and Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi read dreams by wordplay and association
Ben Dama
I saw my two cheeks fall off.
R' Yishmael
Two Roman battalions plotted evil against you, and they died.
Bar Kappara
My nose fell off. My two hands were cut off. My two legs were cut off. They told me I would die in Adar and not see Nisan.
Rabbi
Anger (ḥaron af) has left you. You will not need the labor of your hands. You ride a horse. You will die in glory (adruta) and not come to temptation (nissayon).
A heretic
I was watering olives with oil. I plucked a star, then swallowed it. My eyes kissed each other. I kissed the moon. I was peeling eggs.
R' Yishmael
You had relations with your mother. You kidnapped an Israelite and sold him, spending the money. Relations with your sister. With an Israelite's wife. You strip the dead — and a woman arrived to say the cloak he wore belonged to a man who had died.
The heretic
They said my father left me property in Cappadocia — but I have none there and he never went.
R' Yishmael
Kappa is a beam, deka is ten. Look at the tenth beam of your house. It was full of coins.
Notice: Every image is decoded through a symbol the Gemara already uses: Israel as stars, siblings as two eyes, a woman as the moon, the egg of the mourner's meal. The heretic admitted all were true except the last — until the evidence walked in.

📜 Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi: Say the Good Verse First

For images that Scripture uses both ways, rise early and recite the favorable verse before the other can 'precede' it
1️⃣ Rabbi Ḥanan: three signs of peace — a river ('I will extend peace to her like a river', Isa. 66:12), a bird ('as birds hovering', Isa. 31:5), a pot ('You will establish [tishpot] peace', Isa. 26:12). Rabbi Ḥanina: only a pot without meat — with meat it is Micah 3:3
↓
2️⃣ But each has a dark verse too: 'distress will come in like a river' (Isa. 59:19), 'a bird that wanders from her nest' (Prov. 27:8), 'set on the pot' (Ezek. 24:3) — so recite the good one first
↓
3️⃣ Grapes: 'like grapes in the wilderness' (Hos. 9:10) before 'grapes of gall' (Deut. 32:32). A mountain: 'how beautiful upon the mountains' (Isa. 52:7) before 'for the mountains I will weep' (Jer. 9:9)
↓
4️⃣ A shofar: the great shofar of ingathering (Isa. 27:13) before the war horn of Giva (Hos. 5:8). A dog: 'no dog shall whet his tongue' (Ex. 11:7) before 'the dogs are greedy' (Isa. 56:11). A lion: 'the lion has roared' (Amos 3:8) before 'a lion is gone up from his thicket' (Jer. 4:7)
↓
5️⃣ A shave: Joseph 'shaved and changed his raiment' (Gen. 41:14) before Samson's 'if I be shaven my strength will go' (Judges 16:17). A well: 'a well of living waters' (Song 4:15) before Jer. 6:7. A reed: 'a bruised reed he shall not break' (Isa. 42:3) before 'the staff of this bruised reed' (II Kings 18:21)
↓
6️⃣ An ox: 'his firstling bullock, majesty is his' (Deut. 33:17) before 'if an ox gore a man' (Ex. 21:28)
Why a well is good: Rabbi Ḥanina: one who sees a well sees peace (Gen. 26:19). Rabbi Natan: he has found Torah, for 'whoever finds me finds life' (Prov. 8:35) matches 'a well of living water'. Rava: actual life.

🐂 A Field Guide to Dream Symbols

Baraitot and amoraic sayings on the second amud

Good Omens

  • A reed (kaneh): expect wisdom — 'get [kene] wisdom'; reeds: understanding (Prov. 4:7)
  • Rabbi Zeira: pumpkin, heart of palm, wax and reed are all good; a pumpkin is shown only to one who fears Heaven with all his might
  • A donkey: expect salvation — 'lowly, and riding upon a donkey' (Zech. 9:9)
  • A white horse, walking or running; a red horse walking
  • Ishmael son of Abraham (not any Arab): your prayer is heard; Pinehas: a wonder will be done for you

The Ox: Five Things

  • Ate its flesh — you will become wealthy
  • It gored you — sons who 'gore' each other in Torah
  • It bit you — suffering is coming
  • It kicked you — a long journey
  • You rode it — you rise to greatness; but 'rode it — he will die' refers to the ox riding him

It Depends

  • A cat: where it is called shunra, a nice song (shira na'a); where shinra, a change for the worse (shinui ra)
  • White grapes: always good; black grapes: good in season, bad out of season
  • A red horse running: bad
  • A camel (gamal): death was decreed and he was spared — 'I will also surely bring you up [gam alo]' (Gen. 46:4), or 'the Lord also [gam] has put away your sin' (II Sam. 12:13)
  • An elephant (pil): wonders (pelaot) — yet a baraita excludes elephant and monkey from good animal dreams; 'not difficult…'
Open question: The daf ends mid-resolution: the elephant that brings wonders and the elephant excluded from good omens must be different cases. The distinction is stated on the next daf.
