📖 Overview: The Reasons Behind Each Dispute

Berakhot 52a–52b
תָּדִיר וְשֶׁאֵינוֹ תָּדִיר — תָּדִיר קוֹדֵם
The scene: The Gemara works through the mishna's list of Beit Shammai–Beit Hillel meal disputes one by one. It first tests Beit Shammai's kiddush position against a baraita about havdala and answers that a day's arrival is unlike its departure. It then asks whether Beit Shammai even require a cup for Grace after Meals. The three purity disputes — washing before mixing, where to lay the cloth, and sweeping before washing — are each unpacked as competing rabbinic decrees, with Beit Hillel adding an 'alternatively' each time. The daf closes with Rava's spices-first custom, the meaning of bara versus boreh, and why spices of gentiles get no blessing.

🕯️ Do Beit Shammai Really Put the Day Before the Wine?

A baraita on havdala seems to have wine first — the Gemara must show it is Beit Shammai's, then explain it
❓ Baraita: entering one's house at the end of Shabbat, one blesses wine, then light, then spices, then havdala. Wine precedes the day's blessing!
↓
🤔 Maybe that baraita is Beit Hillel's? No — it puts light before spices, and only Beit Shammai hold that (per Rabbi Yehuda's Tosefta: the Houses dispute only light and spices).
↓
🤔 Maybe it is Beit Hillel per Rabbi Meir? No — in our mishna (Rabbi Meir) Beit Hillel say candle, spices, Grace, havdala, but the baraita puts everything after Grace. So it is Beit Shammai per Rabbi Yehuda.
↓
❗ Then the contradiction stands: kiddush — day before wine; havdala — wine before havdala.
↓
✅ Beit Shammai distinguish: at the day's arrival, the earlier we bring it in the better; at its departure, the later we send it off the better, so Shabbat is not a burden on us.
First, the Divine Voice: Why say the halakha follows Beit Hillel when a bat kol proclaimed it? Either the Tosefta was taught before the bat kol, or after it, following Rabbi Yehoshua, who said אֵין מַשְׁגִּיחִין בְּבַת קוֹל — one disregards a Heavenly Voice.

🍷 Do Beit Shammai Require a Cup for Grace After Meals?

The mishna's 'only that one cup' is pressed from every angle
Gemara
The mishna: if only one cup came after the meal, Beit Shammai bless the wine and then Grace. Doesn't that mean he drinks it and Grace has no cup?
Answer
No — he blesses over it and sets it down for after Grace.
Gemara
But the Master said one who blesses must taste!
Answer
He does taste it.
Gemara
But the Master said tasting it disqualifies it as a cup of blessing!
Answer
He tastes with his hand, or pours a little into another cup.
Gemara
But a cup of blessing needs a minimum measure, and he has reduced it! And the mishna says 'only that cup'!
Answer
The cup held more than the measure: not two cups, but more than one.
Gemara
But Rabbi Ḥiyya taught: Beit Shammai say he blesses the wine, drinks it, and then recites Grace!
Conclusion
Rather, two tanna'im differ about Beit Shammai's view.

🧼 Three Purity Disputes, Three Pairs of Decrees

Each House guards against a different impurity chain; the root is what each permits
DisputeBeit Shammai's concernBeit Hillel's concernRoot of the disagreement
Wash hands, then mix — or mix, then wash?Liquid on the outside of the cup may become impure from unwashed hands and then render the cup impureLiquid on the hands may become impure from the (outside-impure) cup and render the hands impureBS forbid using a cup whose outside is impure (decree due to drips); BH permit it — drips are uncommon. Alternatively: תֵּכֶף לִנְטִילַת יָדַיִם סְעוּדָה
Cloth on the table or the cushion?On the cushion, its liquid may become impure and then render the hands impureOn the table, its liquid may become impure and render the food impureBS forbid using a second-degree table (decree for teruma eaters); BH permit — teruma eaters are vigilant. Alternatively: hand-washing for ḥullin is not from the Torah, so better hands be impure than food
Sweep first or wash first?Washing first splashes the crumbs and ruins foodIf the attendant is a scholar he already removed olive-bulk crumbs; smaller crumbs may be destroyedBH forbid using an am ha'aretz attendant; BS permit it, so food may still be on the table
Principles invoked: Hands have second-degree impurity, and a second degree makes a third in ḥullin only through liquids. A vessel does not render a person impure, nor another vessel. A vessel whose outside became impure through liquid: its outside is impure but its inside, rim, ear, and handles are pure. Rabbi Yoḥanan: crumbs less than an olive-bulk may be destroyed by hand.

🌿 Rava Blesses the Spices First

Rav Huna bar Yehuda visits Rava and is surprised; Rabbi Yehuda's version explains it
1️⃣ Rav Huna bar Yehuda sees Rava bless over the spices before the light
↓
2️⃣ His objection: in our mishna both Houses put the candle first — Beit Shammai: candle, Grace, spices, havdala; Beit Hillel: candle, spices, Grace, havdala
↓
3️⃣ Rava: that is Rabbi Meir's version. Rabbi Yehuda says the Houses agree Grace is first and havdala last, and dispute only light and spices — Beit Shammai: light then spices; Beit Hillel: spices then light
↓
4️⃣ Rabbi Yoḥanan: the people practice like Beit Hillel according to Rabbi Yehuda — spices first
Rav Huna's ruling on the chapter: Rabbi Yosei bar Ḥanina in the name of Rav Huna: in this whole chapter the halakha follows Beit Hillel, except for the sweeping dispute, where it follows Beit Shammai. Rabbi Oshaya taught the mishna with the opinions reversed, so that here too the halakha follows Beit Hillel.

🔥 'Who Created' or 'Who Creates'? One Light or Many?

Two amora'im locate the real dispute in the flame blessing, then the spices of gentiles

Rava

  • All agree בָּרָא means 'created' (past)
  • The dispute is בּוֹרֵא: Beit Shammai — it means 'will create' (future); Beit Hillel — it also means 'has created'

Rav Yosef

  • Objection: 'Who forms light and creates [boreh] darkness' (Isaiah 45:7), 'forms mountains and creates wind' (Amos 4:13), 'creates the heavens' (Isaiah 42:5) — boreh clearly means past creation
  • So both words mean 'created'; the dispute is light vs. lights: Beit Shammai — one light in a fire; Beit Hillel — many lights (red, green, white)
  • Baraita: Beit Hillel told Beit Shammai, 'there are many lights in the fire'

Spices of gentiles

  • A gentile's candle: no blessing because it did not 'rest' on Shabbat — but why not their spices?
  • Rav Yehuda in the name of Rav: the mishna means a gentile party, which is presumed to be for idolatry
  • But the mishna's last clause separately bans idolatry's candle and spices! Rabbi Ḥanina of Sura: the mishna is giving the reason — gentiles' spices are banned because their parties are for idolatry
Leading into the next daf: A baraita: over fire that 'rested' one blesses; over fire that did not rest one does not. The Gemara asks what 'rested' means — and the answer continues.
