QUESTION 1 OF 5
Why is one who calls Sarah 'Sarai' not in violation, while one who calls Abraham 'Abram' transgresses according to bar Kappara and Rabbi Eliezer?
A. Because Sarah's name change was never recorded in the Torah
B. Because the instruction about Sarai was addressed to Abraham alone, whereas Abraham's renaming is stated in general terms
C. Because women's names are not subject to such rules
D. Because Scripture later reverts and calls her Sarai
Correct: B — The Gemara answers that God said to Abraham specifically 'you shall not call her name Sarai' (Genesis 17:15), while 'your name shall no longer be called Abram' is general. Jacob is different because Scripture itself later calls him 'Jacob, Jacob' (Genesis 46:2).
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi, what does the word 'Hear' (Shema) in Deuteronomy 6:4 teach?
A. That Shema may be recited in any language one understands
B. That one must not recite Shema out of order
C. That one should make one's ears hear what one's mouth utters
D. That the whole Torah must be read in Hebrew
Correct: C — Rabbi uses 'and they shall be' for Hebrew-only recitation, and 'Hear' to require audible recitation. The Rabbis use 'Hear' for any language and hold that inaudible recitation still fulfils the obligation.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
How does the Gemara deflect the proof that 'mitzvot require intent' from the mishna about reading Torah when Shema's time arrives?
A. It says the mishna follows Beit Shammai
B. It explains the reader was proofreading the text, so 'focused his heart' means intent to read the words, not intent to fulfil a mitzva
C. It says the reader had already recited Shema earlier
D. It says the mishna refers only to the evening Shema
Correct: B — The Gemara suggests the case is one reading to emend the text (בְּקוֹרֵא לְהַגִּיהַּ); such a person needs only intent to read, so no proof about intent for mitzvot can be drawn.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
What does Rabbi Meir hold about intent in Shema, and who rules in accordance with him?
A. The entire first paragraph requires intent; Rabbi Yoḥanan rules so
B. Intent is required only through 'the Lord is One'; Rava rules so
C. All three paragraphs require intent; Rabbi Akiva rules so
D. No intent is required at all; Rav Yosef rules so
Correct: B — The baraita states that intent of the heart is required until 'Hear, Israel, the Lord is our God, the Lord is One' — the statement of Rabbi Meir — and Rava says the halakha follows Rabbi Meir.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
What instruction did Rav Naḥman give his servant Daru about Shema?
A. Wake me for the entire Shema even if I have fallen asleep
B. Trouble me to recite the first verse; beyond that, do not trouble me
C. Never wake me once I have lain down
D. Recite Shema on my behalf if I fall asleep
Correct: B — Following Rav's ruling that one who recited the first verse and was overcome by sleep fulfilled his obligation, Rav Naḥman told Daru to bother him only for the first verse.
