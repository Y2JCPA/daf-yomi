📖 Overview: Immersion, Nine Kav, and Ezra's Decree

Berakhot 22a–22b
אֵין דִּבְרֵי תוֹרָה מְקַבְּלִין טוּמְאָה, שֶׁנֶּאֱמַר: ״הֲלֹא כֹה דְבָרִי כָּאֵשׁ נְאֻם ה׳״
The scene: The daf finishes explaining Rabbi Yehuda: he lets the ba'al keri recite blessings aloud because he treats them like Hilkhot Derekh Eretz — yet he immersed before teaching his own students. A baraita lists the tannaitic range on what a ba'al keri may study, and Rabbi Yehuda ben Beteira declares that words of Torah, like fire, cannot become impure; Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak reports that practice follows him. The Gemara then turns to the lighter alternative of nine kav of poured water: who whispered it, why, and to whom it applies. Abaye and Rava reconstruct Ezra's original decree, and Rava rules. A meal among Rav Pappa and colleagues tests whether nine kav suffices to bless for others. The new mishna adds rules about remembering an emission mid-prayer, reciting Shema while immersed, and distancing from filth.

📚 What May a Ba'al Keri Study?

The Sinai juxtaposition teaches reverence — so who is excluded, and how far?
SagePermitted for a ba'al keri
The Sages (from Deut. 4:9–10)Zavim, lepers, and those who lay with menstruants may read Scripture and study Mishna, Gemara, halakhot, and aggada — only the ba'al keri is barred, since his impurity comes from frivolity
Rabbi YoseiFamiliar mishnayot, but he may not expound a new mishna
Rabbi Yonatan ben YosefHe expounds the Mishna but not the Gemara
Rabbi Natan ben AvishalomEven the Gemara, as long as he does not utter God's names in it
Rabbi Yoḥanan the Cobbler (for R' Akiva)May not enter into midrash at all — or, may not enter the study hall at all
Rabbi YehudaOnly Hilkhot Derekh Eretz — hence Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak: he treats blessings like Hilkhot Derekh Eretz
Lenient for others, strict for himself: Rabbi Yehuda, a ba'al keri walking by the river, was asked by his students to teach Hilkhot Derekh Eretz. He immersed first. 'Didn't you teach us it is permitted?' — 'Although I am lenient with others, I am stringent with myself.'

🔥 Words of Torah Do Not Become Impure

Rabbi Yehuda ben Beteira, and the three elders whom practice follows
בְּנִי, פְּתַח פִּיךָ וְיָאִירוּ דְבָרֶיךָ
The student who mumbled: A student was reciting hesitantly before Rabbi Yehuda ben Beteira, not wanting to utter Torah clearly after an emission. He told him: My son, open your mouth and let your words illuminate — words of Torah do not become impure, as it says 'Is not My word like fire, says the Lord' (Jeremiah 23:29); just as fire cannot become impure, neither can Torah. Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak: the world follows three elders — Rabbi El'ai on the first shearing (only in Eretz Yisrael), Rabbi Yoshiya on kilayim (liable only for wheat, barley, and a grape seed sown in one hand motion), and Rabbi Yehuda ben Beteira on words of Torah. Ze'iri reported: 'They abolished immersion' (per Rabbi Yehuda ben Beteira) — or 'they abolished hand-washing' (per Rav Ḥisda, who cursed one who searches for water at prayer time). The parallel tannaitic dispute: Rabbi Meir permits expounding the Mishna but not the Gemara (the halakha per Rabbi El'ai in Rav's name); Rabbi Ḥanina ben Gamliel forbids both, or permits both.

🤫 Nine Kav — Whisper It or Teach It in the Market?

A ba'al keri over whom nine kav of drawn water is poured is pure
Baraita
Naḥum of Gam Zo whispered this halakha to Rabbi Akiva; Rabbi Akiva whispered it to ben Azzai; ben Azzai went out and taught it to his students in the market.
R' Yosei b. Avin / b. Zevida
One taught 'he taught it' openly — to prevent neglect of Torah and of procreation. The other taught 'he whispered it' — so scholars would not be with their wives like roosters.
Rabbi Yannai
I heard some are lenient and some are stringent. Whoever is stringent with himself, his days and years are lengthened.
R' Yehoshua b.L.
What is the nature of morning immersers? Not that they should stop — he himself bars a ba'al keri from Torah. Rather: why forty se'a when nine kav works? Why immerse when pouring works?
Rabbi Ḥanina
They built a great fence with it. A man solicited a woman to sin; she said: 'Good-for-nothing! Do you have forty se'a to immerse in?' He immediately desisted.
Rav Huna
Gentlemen, why disdain this immersion? If because of the cold, the bathhouses are possible. Rav Ḥisda: is there immersion in hot water? Rav Huna: Rav Adda bar Ahava agrees with you — but I stand by it.
Rabbi Zeira in the tub: Sitting in a bathhouse tub, he asked for nine kav to be poured on him. Rabbi Ḥiyya bar Abba: aren't you already sitting in water? Answer: forty se'a works by immersion, not pouring; nine kav works by pouring, not immersion.

📜 Reconstructing Ezra's Decree

Since all the tanna'im and amora'im dispute Ezra's enactment, let us see what Ezra actually decreed
🏺 Rav Naḥman prepared a nine-kav jug. Rav Dimi: Rabbi Akiva and Rabbi Yehuda Gelostera said nine kav is only for a sick person with an involuntary emission; a sick person with a normal one needs forty se'a. Rav Yosef: 'Rav Naḥman's jug is broken!'
↓
🔧 Ravin: in Usha, in Rav Oshaya's chamber, they asked Rav Asi, who said the opposite — nine kav for the sick with a normal emission; the sick with an involuntary one is exempt entirely. Rav Yosef: 'Rav Naḥman's jug is rejoined!'
↓
Abaye
Ezra decreed forty se'a for a healthy person with a normal emission and nine kav for a healthy person with an involuntary one; the amora'im disputed the sick
Rava
Ezra decreed only immersion (forty se'a) for the healthy with a normal emission; the Sages later added nine kav for the healthy with an involuntary one; the amora'im disputed the sick
↓
✅ Rava's ruling: healthy or sick with a normal emission — forty se'a; healthy with an involuntary emission — nine kav; sick with an involuntary emission — exempt from everything

🍽️ For Oneself vs. For Others — and the New Mishna

A Tosefta, a meal, and rules for prayer amid impurity and filth
1️⃣ Tosefta: nine kav purifies for one's own study; to teach others, forty se'a. Rabbi Yehuda: forty se'a in any case. Amora'im pair off: does 'for others' apply even to a sick involuntary emission? Does Rabbi Yehuda accept forty se'a in vessels, or only drawn water in the ground?
↓
2️⃣ Rav Pappa: 'Let me lead Grace — nine kav fell on me.' Rava bar Shmuel: 'For others you need forty se'a — I immersed, let me lead.' Rav Huna: 'Let me lead — I needed neither.' Rav Ḥama immersed on Passover eve to bless for the masses — but the halakha does not distinguish for oneself and for others
↓
3️⃣ Mishna: one who remembers mid-Amida that he is a ba'al keri does not stop but abridges. Baraita: reading the Torah, he does not stop but reads quickly; Rabbi Meir: no more than three verses
↓
4️⃣ Mishna: one immersing at dawn — if he can come up, cover himself, and recite before sunrise, he does; if not, he covers himself in the water and recites. Not in foul water or flax-water until fresh water is added. Four cubits from urine and feces
↓
5️⃣ Feces in front of him mid-prayer: walk until it is four cubits behind (if possible), otherwise four cubits to the side. Found feces afterward where he prayed — Rabba: he sinned, but his prayer stands. Rava: 'the sacrifice of the wicked is an abomination' (Proverbs 21:27) — his prayer is an abomination
↓
6️⃣ Urine flowing on his knees mid-prayer: stop until it ceases, then resume. Rav Ḥisda and Rav Hamnuna: return to the beginning, or to where he stopped? The dispute continues on the next daf
