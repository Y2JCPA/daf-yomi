📖 Overview: Prayers for Every Situation

Berakhot 60a–60b
כׇּל דְּעָבֵיד רַחֲמָנָא לְטָב עָבֵיד
The scene: The daf finishes the dispute over Sheheḥeyanu on new vessels, then works through the mishna's remaining clauses: blessing on bad that will turn good, vain prayers about a pregnancy or a scream in the city, and the prayers of one entering a large city. The Gemara adds a whole liturgy of daily life: entering a bathhouse, undergoing bloodletting, entering and leaving a bathroom, lying down to sleep, waking, and the chain of morning blessings from the rooster's crow to tefillin. It closes with Rava's reading of 'bless on the bad as on the good' — accept it with joy — and R' Akiva's night in the field, where every loss turned out to be salvation.

🏺 Sheheḥeyanu on New Vessels: Two Versions of One Dispute

Rav Huna and R' Yoḥanan, measured against R' Meir and R' Yehuda

Baraita: owns a similar one and buysR' Meir: no blessing. R' Yehuda: blesses either wayR' Meir: no blessing. R' Yehuda: blesses either way
Which tanna does each amora follow?Rav Huna = R' Meir; R' Yoḥanan = R' YehudaRav Huna = R' Yehuda; but R' Yoḥanan matches neither?!
R' Yoḥanan's defense: R' Yehuda would also require a blessing when one buys again; the tannaim framed their dispute around 'owns a similar one' only to show the reach of R' Meir's leniency — if he exempts even that case, all the more so buying again. Why not frame it to show the reach of R' Yehuda's stringency instead? כֹּחַ דְּהֶתֵּירָא עֲדִיף — the strength of a leniency is preferable.

🤰 'May She Bear a Son' — Really a Vain Prayer?

Leah, Dina, and the forty-day window
❓ Mishna: if one's wife is pregnant and he prays 'may she bear a male', it is a vain prayer. Is prayer truly useless here?
↓
📜 Rav Yosef objects: 'And afterwards she bore a daughter, Dina' (Gen. 30:21). Rav: Leah judged herself — twelve tribes are destined, ten already born; if this is a boy, my sister Rachel will count for less than a maidservant. At once the fetus became a girl
↓
Answer 1
One does not cite miracles to teach general halakha
Answer 2
Leah's prayer was within forty days of conception, when prayer can still help
↓
📜 Baraita of pregnancy prayers: days 1–3, that the seed not putrefy; day 3 to day 40, that it be male; 40 days to 3 months, that it not be a sandal (malformed); 3 to 6 months, not stillborn; 6 to 9 months, that it emerge safely
↓
❓ Rav Yitzḥak son of Rav Ami: gender is fixed at conception — if the man emits seed first, a girl; if the woman first, a boy: 'when a woman emits seed and bears a male' (Lev. 12:2). So what can prayer do?
↓
✅ Answer: when both emit seed at the same moment, gender is undetermined — there, prayer within forty days can help
The next clause — a scream in the city: Hillel the Elder heard a cry and said, 'I am certain it is not from my house' — 'he shall not fear evil tidings, his heart is steadfast, trusting in the Lord' (Ps. 112:7); Rava: read it either direction and it works. R' Yishmael son of R' Yosei told a frightened student, 'You are a sinner — the transgressors in Zion are afraid' (Isa. 33:14); 'happy is the man who fears always' (Prov. 28:14) refers only to Torah, lest one forget it. Rav Hamnuna said the same to Yehuda bar Natan when he sighed (Job 3:25).

🚫 'Do Not Say That' — Abaye Edits the Prayers

Three prayers of daily life, each corrected
Baraita
Entering a bathhouse: 'Save me from this and the like, let no ruin or sin befall me — and if ruin does befall me, let my death be atonement for all my sins.'
Abaye
Do not say that — never open your mouth to Satan (Reish Lakish; a baraita in R' Yosei's name). Rav Yosef's proof: Isaiah said 'we were almost like Sodom' (1:9), and the prophet answered, 'Hear the word of the Lord, rulers of Sodom' (1:10).
Rav Aḥa
Entering for bloodletting: 'May this be for healing and heal me, for You are a faithful Healer… for it is not the way of people to heal, but they have become accustomed.'
Abaye
Do not say that. The school of R' Yishmael taught from 'he shall cause him to be thoroughly healed' (Ex. 21:19) that permission is granted to the physician to heal.
Baraita
Entering a bathroom, say to the accompanying angels: 'Be honored, honored holy ones, servants of the Most High… leave me until I enter, do my will, and return to you.'
Abaye
Do not say that — they may leave him and go! Say instead: 'Guard me, guard me; help me, help me; support me, support me; wait for me, wait for me until I enter and come out, for this is the way of man.'
Rav
On exiting one says אֲשֶׁר יָצַר — 'Who formed man in wisdom and created in him orifices and cavities… were one opened or blocked, one could not stand before You' — and concludes 'Healer of the sick'.
Shmuel
Abba has made the whole world sick! Conclude 'Healer of all flesh'. Rav Sheshet: 'Who performs wonders'. Rav Pappa: say both — רוֹפֵא כׇל בָּשָׂר וּמַפְלִיא לַעֲשׂוֹת.
On the way out: Leaving the bathhouse: 'I thank You that You saved me from the fire' (Rav Aḥa). R' Abbahu confirmed it — the bathhouse floor collapsed beneath him, he stood on a pillar and saved one hundred and one men with one arm. Rising after bloodletting: 'Blessed… Who heals without payment' (רוֹפֵא חִנָּם).

🌅 From Bedtime to Tefillin: The Daily Sequence

Each act of waking and dressing has its own blessing
1️⃣ Lying down: recite Shema from Shema Yisrael through VeHaya Im Shamoa, then הַמַּפִּיל — 'Who casts the bands of sleep upon my eyes… enlighten my eyes lest I sleep the sleep of death… Who gives light to the whole world in His glory'
↓
2️⃣ Waking: אֱלֹהַי נְשָׁמָה — 'The soul You placed within me is pure… Blessed… Who restores souls to lifeless bodies'
↓
3️⃣ Rooster crows: 'Who gave the sekhvi understanding to distinguish day from night'; opening the eyes: 'Who gives sight to the blind'; sitting up: 'Who frees the bound'
↓
4️⃣ Dressing: 'Who clothes the naked'; standing: 'Who raises the bowed'; feet on the ground: 'Who spreads the earth over the waters'
↓
5️⃣ Walking: 'Who makes firm the steps of man'; shoes: 'Who provided me with all I need'; belt: 'Who girds Israel with strength'; head covering: 'Who crowns Israel with glory'
↓
6️⃣ Tzitzit: 'to wrap ourselves in tzitzit'; tefillin on the arm: 'to don tefillin'; on the head: 'concerning the mitzva of tefillin'
↓
7️⃣ Washing hands: 'concerning washing the hands'; washing the face: 'Who removes the bands of sleep from my eyes…', with a prayer to be attached to Torah and mitzvot and to find grace in all eyes, ending 'Who bestows loving-kindness on His people Israel'
Entering a city — Ben Azzai's four prayers: Before entering: 'bring me into this city in peace'; inside: 'I thank You that You brought me in'; before leaving: 'take me out in peace'; outside: 'I thank You that You took me out… lead me, support me, direct my steps, and rescue me from every enemy and ambush.' Rav Mattana: this applies only to a city without courts that try and execute; another version — even with courts, since one may not find someone to plead his case.

😊 'Whatever the Merciful One Does, He Does for the Best'

Rava reads the mishna, four sages bring verses, and R' Akiva spends a night in a field
לְעוֹלָם יְהֵא אָדָם רָגִיל לוֹמַר: ״כׇּל דְּעָבֵיד רַחֲמָנָא לְטָב עָבֵיד״
Bless on the bad as on the good: It cannot mean the same formula — the mishna itself gives HaTov veHaMeitiv for good tidings and Dayan HaEmet for bad. Rava: the mishna teaches to accept bad tidings with the same joy as good ones. Rav Aḥa in R' Levi's name: 'I will sing of loving-kindness and justice' (Ps. 101:1) — if kindness, I sing; if justice, I sing. R' Shmuel bar Naḥmani: 'In God I praise His word; in the Lord I praise His word' (Ps. 56:11) — the attribute of goodness and the attribute of punishment. R' Tanḥum: 'I lift the cup of salvation and call on the Lord'… 'I found trouble and sorrow and call on the Lord' (Ps. 116). The Rabbis: 'The Lord gave and the Lord took; blessed be the name of the Lord' (Job 1:21). Rav Huna in Rav's name from R' Meir, and a baraita from R' Akiva: accustom yourself to say whatever the Merciful One does, He does for the best. R' Akiva was refused lodging in a town and slept in a field with a rooster, a donkey and a candle. Wind put out the candle, a cat ate the rooster, a lion ate the donkey — and each time he said it. That night an army captured the town; with no light, no crowing and no braying, he alone went unnoticed.
