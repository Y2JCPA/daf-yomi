Berakhot 49 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
Why did Rav Sheshet become angry with Rav Ḥisda at the Exilarch's house?
A. Rav Ḥisda concluded a blessing with two themes
B. Rav Ḥisda omitted covenant, Torah, and sovereignty from Grace after Meals
C. Rav Ḥisda forgot to mention Shabbat
D. Rav Ḥisda recited Grace without having eaten grain
Correct: B — Rav Ḥisda relied on Rav's view that omitting covenant, Torah, and sovereignty still fulfills the obligation. Rabbi Zeira told him Rav Sheshet was right to be angry, since he had abandoned many tanna'im and amora'im to follow Rav.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
What reason does the Gemara give for Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi's rule that one does not conclude a blessing with two themes?
A. A long conclusion distracts those answering amen
B. One does not perform mitzvot in bundles
C. The Torah only mentions one theme per blessing
D. Two themes would require two mentions of God's sovereignty
Correct: B — The Gemara explains: 'ein osin mitzvot ḥavilot ḥavilot' — mitzvot are not bundled together; each blessing has its own single ending.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to Rav Naḥman, how does one conclude the third blessing of Grace even if he opened with 'Have compassion on Israel'?
A. 'Who redeems Israel'
B. 'Who bequeaths lands'
C. 'Who builds Jerusalem', because 'The Lord builds Jerusalem; He gathers in the exiles of Israel'
D. With both themes together
Correct: C — Rav Naḥman cites Psalms 147:2 to show that building Jerusalem and gathering Israel's exiles are one idea, so the ending is always 'Who builds Jerusalem'. Rav Sheshet, by contrast, matches the ending to the opening.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
Why, according to Shmuel as explained by Rav Naḥman, must one repeat the Amida but not Grace after Meals when Rosh Ḥodesh was forgotten?
A. Rosh Ḥodesh is only mentioned in the Amida, never in Grace
B. The Amida is an obligation, while Grace depends on eating, which is optional
C. Grace after Meals is only rabbinic
D. The Amida is shorter and easier to repeat
Correct: B — Rav Naḥman did not hear the reason from Shmuel but reasoned it out: prayer is obligatory, so one repeats; Grace depends on eating, which one may choose to skip. On Shabbat and Festivals, when eating is required, one does return to the beginning.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
How does Abaye resolve the apparent reversal of Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yehuda between zimmun (olive vs egg) and returning consecrated meat (egg vs olive)?
A. He reverses the opinions in the mishna about zimmun
B. He says the two mishnayot were taught by different tanna'im
C. For zimmun they interpret verses ('eat' vs 'eat and be satisfied'); for consecrated meat they reason by analogy to impurity or to the prohibition
D. He says Rabbi Meir retracted in one of the cases
Correct: C — Rabbi Yoḥanan would reverse one source. Abaye keeps both: in zimmun, Rabbi Meir reads 'eat' as an olive-bulk and 'be satisfied' as drinking, while Rabbi Yehuda reads 'eat and be satisfied' as an egg-bulk; for consecrated meat, Rabbi Meir compares return to impurity (egg-bulk) and Rabbi Yehuda to the prohibition (olive-bulk).
