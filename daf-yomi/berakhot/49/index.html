📖 Overview: Getting the Words of Grace Right

Berakhot 49a–49b
כׇּל שֶׁלֹּא אָמַר בְּרִית וְתוֹרָה בְּבִרְכַּת הָאָרֶץ וּמַלְכוּת בֵּית דָּוִד בְּ״בוֹנֵה יְרוּשָׁלָיִם״ — לֹא יָצָא יְדֵי חוֹבָתוֹ
The scene: The daf is about the precise wording of Grace after Meals. It lists the themes that must appear — covenant and Torah in the blessing of the land, the house of David in 'Who builds Jerusalem' — and shows Rav Ḥisda embarrassed at the Exilarch's table for leaving them out. It debates how many times God's sovereignty must be mentioned in the fourth blessing, whether a blessing may close with two themes, and what to do when Shabbat, a Festival, or Rosh Ḥodesh was forgotten. It ends by reconciling Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yehuda on olive-bulk versus egg-bulk, and opens a new mishna on how the zimmun formula grows with the size of the group.

📜 Themes That Must Appear in Grace after Meals

Requirements, blunders, and one lenient opinion

Required

  • Pelimu: mention the covenant before the Torah — the Torah was given with three covenants, circumcision with thirteen
  • Rabbi Abba: thanksgiving at the beginning and end of the blessing; never fewer than one, or it is reprehensible
  • Baraita, supporting Rabbi Il'a in the name of Rabbeinu: without covenant and Torah in the blessing of the land and the house of David in 'Who builds Jerusalem', one has not fulfilled his obligation

Marks of an ignoramus

  • Concluding the blessing of the land with 'Who bequeaths lands'
  • Concluding 'Who builds Jerusalem' with 'Who redeems Israel'
  • Both corrupt the intention of the blessing

Rav's leniency

  • Rav Ḥananel in Rav's name: one who omitted covenant, Torah, and sovereignty has still fulfilled his obligation
  • Reason: covenant does not apply to women; Torah and sovereignty apply neither to women nor to slaves
  • Rav Ḥisda relied on this at the Exilarch's house, and Rav Sheshet 'stiffened his neck over me like a snake'
  • Rabbi Zeira: you abandoned all those tanna'im and amora'im and followed Rav?!
Rav Ḥisda's humility: Asked by Rabbi Zeira to teach Mishna, he answered: I have not yet learned Grace after Meals, and I should teach Mishna?

🔚 May a Blessing Conclude with Two Themes?

Rabba bar Rav Huna's double ending sparks a review of every double-sounding conclusion
Rav Ḥisda
At the Exilarch's house Rabba bar Rav Huna opened 'Who builds Jerusalem' with one theme and closed with two — Jerusalem and the redemption of Israel. Great fortitude, to conclude with two! Rabbi taught: one does not conclude with two.
Levi
But we say 'for the land and for the food'!
Rabbi
That is one theme: the land that produces food. Likewise 'for the land and for the fruit' — the land that produces fruit.
Levi
'Who sanctifies Israel and the seasons'? 'Who sanctifies Israel and the New Moons'?
Rabbi
Israel, who sanctify the seasons; Israel, who sanctify the New Moons.
Levi
'Who sanctifies Shabbat, Israel, and the seasons'?
Rabbi
Except for that one — there it is all one matter, the sanctity of the day. Jerusalem and redemption are two distinct matters. And the reason: אֵין עוֹשִׂין מִצְוֹת חֲבִילוֹת חֲבִילוֹת, one does not perform mitzvot in bundles.
So how do we close the third blessing? Rav Sheshet: match the opening — 'have compassion on Israel' closes with 'Who redeems Israel'; 'have compassion on Jerusalem' closes with 'Who builds Jerusalem'. Rav Naḥman: always 'Who builds Jerusalem', for 'The Lord builds Jerusalem; He gathers in the exiles of Israel' (Ps. 147:2) — building Jerusalem is Israel's redemption. Rabbi Yosei son of Rabbi Yehuda permits even 'Who redeems Israel'.

👑 How Many Times Is God's Sovereignty Mentioned?

The status of 'Who is good and does good' decides everything
❓ Abba Yosei ben Dostai and the Rabbis: does 'Who is good and does good' require mention of God's sovereignty?
↓
Requires malkhut
It is rabbinic, an independent blessing — so it needs its own mention of sovereignty.
Does not require
It is Torah law, a continuation of the preceding blessings.
↓
💬 Rabbi Yoḥanan (via Rabba bar bar Ḥana): it requires sovereignty. But Rabbi Yoḥanan already said every blessing without sovereignty is no blessing — what is new?
↓
💡 Rabbi Zeira: it needs two mentions — one for itself and one for 'Who builds Jerusalem', which lacks it.
↓
🔁 Then it should need three, adding one for the blessing of the land! Answer: the land blessing is juxtaposed to a preceding blessing and needs none — and so, in principle, is 'Who builds Jerusalem'. But having mentioned the kingdom of David, it is not proper to omit the kingdom of Heaven.
↓
✅ Rav Pappa's version: 'Who is good and does good' needs two mentions of sovereignty besides its own — for Jerusalem and for the land.

🕯️ Forgot to Mention the Day? Rav Giddel's Fixes

Reported before Rav Huna, in Rav's name, with Rabbi Zeira listening behind
OmissionReplacement blessing (Rav)Must one start over?
Shabbat'Blessed… Who gave Shabbatot for rest to His people Israel with love, as a sign and a covenant; Blessed… Who sanctifies the Shabbat'Rav Naḥman started Grace over; Rabbi Menashya bar Taḥlifa in Rav's name: the short blessing works only before beginning 'Who is good and does good' — after that, return to the beginning
Festival'Blessed… Who gave Festivals to His people Israel for joy and for commemoration; Blessed… Who sanctifies Israel and the seasons'Same rule: Shabbat and Festivals are days one cannot refrain from eating, so an error sends one back
Rosh Ḥodesh'Blessed… Who gave New Moons to His people Israel for commemoration' — Rabbi Zeira did not know whether 'joy' was said, whether it closed with a blessing, or whether it was Rav Giddel's own or Rav'sShmuel: in the Amida one repeats; in Grace after Meals one does not
Why the difference for Rosh Ḥodesh? Rav Naḥman reasoned it out for Rav Amram: the Amida is an obligation, so an error requires repetition; Grace depends on eating, and if one wishes he does not eat — so it is not repeated. On Shabbat and Festivals, when one must eat, the error does require starting over.

🥚 Olive-bulk vs Egg-bulk: Did Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yehuda Switch Sides?

The end of the chapter and the opening of the next mishna
1️⃣ Our mishna: how much must one eat to obligate a zimmun? An olive-bulk (Rabbi Meir); Rabbi Yehuda says an egg-bulk
↓
2️⃣ Elsewhere: one who left Jerusalem with consecrated meat past Mount Scopus burns it in place; before that he returns — for an egg-bulk (Rabbi Meir) or an olive-bulk (Rabbi Yehuda). The opposite!
↓
3️⃣ Rabbi Yoḥanan: the opinions are reversed in one of the sources
↓
4️⃣ Abaye: do not reverse. Here they interpret verses — Rabbi Meir: 'you shall eat' is eating (an olive-bulk), 'and be satisfied' is drinking; Rabbi Yehuda: 'eat and be satisfied' is eating that satisfies, an egg-bulk
↓
5️⃣ There they reason logically — Rabbi Meir: returning is like impurity, which needs an egg-bulk; Rabbi Yehuda: returning is like the prohibition, which applies to an olive-bulk
↓
6️⃣ New mishna: with three say 'Let us bless'; with three and himself, 'Bless'; with ten, 'Let us bless our God'; with a hundred, 'the Lord our God'; with a thousand, 'the God of Israel'; with ten thousand, '…the God of Hosts, Who sits upon the cherubs, for the food that we have eaten'
↓
7️⃣ Rabbi Yosei HaGelili: the blessing follows the size of the crowd — 'In assemblies bless God' (Ps. 68:27). Rabbi Akiva: as in the synagogue, many or few say 'Bless the Lord'. Rabbi Yishmael: 'Bless the Lord, the blessed One'
Shmuel's principle: לְעוֹלָם אַל יוֹצִיא אָדָם אֶת עַצְמוֹ מִן הַכְּלָל — one should never exclude himself from the collective. The Gemara asks: but the mishna says 'with three and himself, he says Bless'! The answer begins: say that the mishna means…
