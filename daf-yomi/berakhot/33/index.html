📖 Overview: Danger, Additions, and Heresy in Prayer

Berakhot 33a–33b
אֵין עַרְוָד מֵמִית, אֶלָּא הַחֵטְא מֵמִית
The scene: The daf opens with the ḥasid's answer to the officer whose greeting he ignored: if you would not turn from a mortal king, how could I turn from the King of kings? The Gemara then limits the mishna's 'snake wrapped around his heel' — a scorpion or a charging ox does interrupt prayer — and tells how Rabbi Ḥanina ben Dosa killed a deadly arvad with his heel. A new mishna places the seasonal additions in the Amida: rain, the request for rain, and havdala. That last one launches a long discussion — the history of havdala moving between the Amida and the cup of wine, and what to do when a festival falls right after Shabbat. The final mishna lists the prayer leader who says 'Your mercy reaches the bird's nest', 'Your name be mentioned with the good', or 'modim, modim' — and is silenced.

🐍 Which Dangers Interrupt the Amida?

The mishna says a snake on the heel does not; the amoraim draw the line

Do not interrupt

  • A snake wrapped around the heel — if you do not provoke it, it will not bite
  • Objection: one who fell into a pit of snakes is presumed dead! Answer: there the pressure of his fall makes them bite
  • A ḥasid ignored a Roman officer's greeting and was threatened with death

Interrupt

  • Rav Sheshet: a scorpion — it stings even when undisturbed
  • Rabbi Yitzḥak: one who sees oxen coming stops; Rav Hoshaya: keep fifty cubits from an innocuous ox, and out of sight of a forewarned one
  • Rabbi Meir: while the ox's head is in the feed basket, climb to the roof and kick the ladder away. Shmuel: this is for a black ox in Nisan, when Satan dances between its horns

Rabbi Ḥanina ben Dosa

  • An arvad was harming people; he asked to see its hole and set his heel over it
  • It came out, bit him — and died
  • He carried it to the study hall: 'It is not the arvad that kills, but sin that kills'
  • 'Woe to the man who meets an arvad, and woe to the arvad that meets Rabbi Ḥanina ben Dosa'
The ḥasid's a fortiori: 'Had you been standing before a flesh-and-blood king and a friend greeted you, would you answer?' 'No — they would cut off my head.' 'You feared a king who is here today and in the grave tomorrow; I stood before the King of kings, who lives forever — all the more so!' The officer was appeased and the ḥasid went home in peace.

🌧️ Where Do the Seasonal Additions Belong?

Rav Yosef explains the mishna's placements, and the Tosefta tells us what happens when they are forgotten

Havdala — Rabbi EliezerThanksgiving (17th)——
Great is knowledge: Rav Ami: it heads the weekday blessings and sits between two Names — 'God of knowledge is the Lord' (I Samuel 2:3); one lacking knowledge may not be pitied (Isaiah 27:11). Rabbi Elazar: so does the Temple (Exodus 15:17), hence one with knowledge is as if the Temple were built in his days. Rav Aḥa Karḥina'a objected: then vengeance is great too — 'God of vengeance, Lord' (Psalms 94:1)! Answer: yes, in its place it is great; Ulla: one vengeance for good (Deut. 33:2), one for evil.

🍷 Havdala in the Amida, Over the Cup — or Both?

Rabbi Yoḥanan's history of the enactment and the rulings that follow from it
1️⃣ Rav Shemen bar Abba to Rabbi Yoḥanan: the Great Assembly instituted blessings, prayers, kiddushim and havdalot — where did they put havdala?
↓
2️⃣ Rabbi Yoḥanan: at first in the Amida; when the people grew wealthy, over the cup; when they became poor again, back in the Amida — and they said: one who says it in the Amida must also say it over the cup
↓
3️⃣ Rava objects from the Tosefta: omitting havdala does not require repeating the Amida 'because he can say it over the cup' — sounds optional! Answer: read 'because he says it over the cup'
↓
4️⃣ The reverse case: said it over the cup — must he say it in the Amida? Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak: a fortiori — if the Amida, the principal enactment, does not exempt the cup, the cup surely does not exempt the Amida
↓
5️⃣ Rav Aḥa Arikha's baraita: 'in this and that — blessings on his head' would be an unnecessary blessing, a violation of 'do not take the Name in vain' (Rav / Reish Lakish / Rabbi Yoḥanan). Emend: 'in this and not in that'
↓
6️⃣ Rav Ḥisda asked Rav Sheshet: erred in both? He returns to the beginning of both. Ravina asked Rava: the halakha? Like kiddush — said in the Amida, it is still said over the cup; so too havdala
Kiddush as the model: מָה קִידּוּשׁ אַף עַל גַּב דִּמְקַדֵּשׁ בִּצְלוֹתָא מְקַדֵּשׁ אַכָּסָא, אַף הַבְדָּלָה נָמֵי — the double recitation is the norm, not a redundancy.

🎉 A Festival Right After Shabbat — Whose Havdala?

Rabbi Zeira on his donkey, Rabbi Ḥiyya bar Avin walking behind, and the precise wording of a tradition
R' Ḥiyya b. Avin
Do you really say in Rabbi Yoḥanan's name that the halakha follows Rabbi Eliezer — havdala in Thanksgiving — when a festival falls after Shabbat? (There is no 'grants knowledge' in the festival Amida.)
R' Zeira
Yes.
Gemara
'Halakha' implies a dispute — who disputes? The Rabbis? They argue only the rest of the year. Rabbi Akiva's separate blessing? We never follow him: the Sages instituted eighteen, not nineteen — and here seven, not eight.
R' Zeira
It was not 'halakha' that was said, but 'one is inclined' (מַטִּין).
Gemara
Indeed the versions vary: Rav Yitzḥak bar Avdimi citing Rav — halakha, or some say inclined; Rabbi Yoḥanan — the Rabbis agree; Rabbi Ḥiyya bar Abba — his view appears correct.
R' Zeira
Hold on to Rabbi Ḥiyya bar Abba's version — he learns a tradition precisely from his master's mouth, like Raḥava of Pumbedita, who kept Rabbi Yehuda's very word stav for the Temple Mount's double colonnade.
Rav Yosef
I know neither this nor that — but I know that Rav and Shmuel fixed a pearl for us in Babylonia: 'Vatodi'einu'.
The pearl: וַתּוֹדִיעֵנוּ ה׳ אֱלֹהֵינוּ אֶת מִשְׁפְּטֵי צִדְקֶךָ… בֵּין קְדוּשַּׁת שַׁבָּת לִקְדוּשַּׁת יוֹם טוֹב הִבְדַּלְתָּ — 'You have made known to us Your righteous laws… You have distinguished between the holiness of Shabbat and the holiness of the festival' — a havdala woven into the festival's own blessing.

🐦 'Your Mercy Reaches the Bird's Nest' — Why Silence Him?

The final mishna of the chapter and Rabbi Ḥanina's warning against piling up praise
📜 Mishna: one who says 'Your mercy reaches the bird's nest', 'Your name be mentioned with the good', or 'modim, modim' — they silence him
↓
✅ 'Modim, modim' looks like two authorities. 'With the good' thanks only for good — but one must bless for the bad as for the good
↓
❓ But the bird's nest — God's mercy on the mother bird (Deut. 22:6–7) — what is wrong with invoking it?
↓
One amora
It casts jealousy among the works of Creation — as if God favors one creature
The other
It makes God's attributes into mercy, when they are only decrees
↓
🧪 A leader before Rabba said 'You pitied the bird's nest, pity us.' Rabba: how well he knows to appease his Master! Abaye: but the mishna silences him! — Rabba only wanted to sharpen Abaye
↓
👑 A leader before Rabbi Ḥanina said 'the great, mighty, awesome, powerful, strong, fearless…' — 'Have you finished all your Master's praises? Even the three we say we could not, had Moses not said them and the Great Assembly not fixed them. A king with a million gold dinars praised for silver — is that not an insult?'
Also on the daf: Rabbi Ḥanina: everything is in the hands of Heaven except fear of Heaven (Deut. 10:12) — a small thing only for Moses, like a man asked for a large vessel he already owns. Rabbi Zeira: saying 'Shema, Shema' is like 'modim, modim'; repeating word by word is merely reprehensible, repeating a whole verse gets him silenced. Rav Pappa asked Abaye: perhaps he lacked focus the first time?
