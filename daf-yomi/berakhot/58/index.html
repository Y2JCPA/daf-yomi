📖 Overview: Blessings on Crowds, Kings, Ruins, and Stars

Berakhot 58a–58b
מַלְכוּתָא דְאַרְעָא כְּעֵין מַלְכוּתָא דִרְקִיעָא
The scene: The daf continues the blessings recited on sights. A multitude of Israel earns 'Who knows all secrets', since every mind and face differs; Ben Zoma adds a blessing of gratitude for the labor of others and contrasts the good guest with the bad one. Blessings for Sages and kings lead to two stories about earthly royalty mirroring Heaven: blind Rav Sheshet knowing the king arrives in silence, and Rabbi Sheila escaping a Persian court by praising God's kingdom. The second amud covers houses of Israel inhabited or ruined, Rav Ḥisda's sigh at a fallen house of charity, the blessing over Jewish graves, seeing a friend after thirty days or a year, unusual creatures, and finally the comet and the constellations.

👥 Ben Zoma on the Temple Mount

Why a multitude gets 'Who knows all secrets', and what a good guest says
Rav Hamnuna
One who sees multitudes of Israel says: Blessed… חֲכַם הָרָזִים, Who knows all secrets. Over multitudes of gentiles: 'Your mother shall be sore ashamed' (Jeremiah 50:12).
Tosefta
Why? Their minds are unlike each other and their faces are unlike each other — only He Who knows secrets knows every heart.
Ben Zoma
Seeing a crowd from a step on the Temple Mount he added a second blessing: Blessed… Who created all these to serve me.
Ben Zoma
How much did Adam toil before he found bread: plowed, sowed, reaped, sheaved, threshed, winnowed, separated, ground, sifted, kneaded, baked. And I rise and find it all ready. For a garment: sheared, laundered, combed, spun, wove. All nations come to my door.
Good guest
How much trouble the host took for me — so much meat, so much wine, so many loaves — and all of it for me! 'Remember that you magnify his work' (Job 36:24).
Bad guest
What trouble did this host take? I ate one piece of bread, one piece of meat, one cup. Whatever he did, he did for his wife and children. 'Men do therefore fear him' (Job 37:24).
Numbers: A multitude is no fewer than six hundred thousand. Ulla: there is no multitude in Babylonia. Yishai, David's father, 'came among men' (I Samuel 17:12) — he went out, came in, and taught with multitudes.

🤫 Rav Sheshet and the Heretic: When Does the King Arrive?

A blind sage proves that earthly royalty mirrors the royalty of Heaven
❓ Everyone goes to greet the king; blind Rav Sheshet goes too. A heretic mocks: 'Whole jugs go to the river — where do broken jugs go?'
↓
🗣️ Rav Sheshet: 'Come and see that I know more than you.' First troop passes with noise — 'the king is coming!' — 'No.' Second troop, louder — 'now!' — 'No.'
↓
🤫 Third troop passes and there is silence. Rav Sheshet: 'Certainly now the king is coming.'
↓
📜 Proof: מַלְכוּתָא דְאַרְעָא כְּעֵין מַלְכוּתָא דִרְקִיעָא. At Horeb the Lord was not in the wind, not in the earthquake, not in the fire — 'and after the fire a still small voice' (I Kings 19:11–13)
↓
The blessing
When the king came, Rav Sheshet blessed him. The heretic: 'You bless someone you cannot see?'
The heretic's end
Some say his friends gouged out his eyes; some say Rav Sheshet set his eyes on him and he became a heap of bones
The idea: God's revelation came in the silence after the tumult; so the king appears when the noise of the escort stops.

⚖️ Rabbi Sheila Before the Persian Court

A flogging, an informer, Elijah's testimony, and a verse turned into a homily
1️⃣ Rabbi Sheila flogs a man who had relations with a gentile woman. The man informs: 'A Jew judges without the king's authority (harmana).' A messenger summons Rabbi Sheila
↓
2️⃣ 'Why did you flog him?' — 'He had relations with a female donkey.' — 'Witnesses?' Elijah appears as a man and testifies
↓
3️⃣ 'Then he deserves death!' — 'Since our exile we have no authority to execute; you do with him as you wish'
↓
4️⃣ While they deliberate he says 'Yours, O Lord, is the greatness…' (I Chronicles 29:11). Asked what he said: 'Blessed is the Merciful One Who gives kingdom on earth like the kingdom of Heaven, and gave you dominion and love of justice.' They hand him a staff: 'Judge!'
↓
5️⃣ Leaving, the informer sneers, 'Does God do miracles for liars?' — 'Scoundrel, are gentiles not called donkeys? "Whose flesh is as the flesh of donkeys" (Ezekiel 23:20).' Seeing him go to report this, Rabbi Sheila rules him a rodef — 'if one comes to kill you, kill him first' — and strikes him dead with the staff
↓
6️⃣ Since the verse saved him, he expounds it: greatness = Creation; power = Exodus; glory = sun and moon for Joshua; triumph = fall of Rome; majesty = wars of Arnon; 'all in heaven and earth' = Sisera; kingdom = Amalek; 'exalted' = Gog and Magog; 'head above all' = Rabbi Yoḥanan: even the water-distributor is appointed from Heaven
Rabbi Akiva's baraita: Greatness = splitting of the sea; power = plague of the firstborn; glory = giving of the Torah; triumph = Jerusalem; majesty = the Temple.

🏚️ Two Conversations on the Road, and the Sky Above

Ulla and Rav Ḥisda at a ruined house; Shmuel and colleagues on comets and constellations

Rav Ḥisda's Sigh

  • At the door of Rav Ḥana bar Ḥanilai's house, Rav Ḥisda sighed. Ulla: Rav says a sigh breaks half the body (Ezek. 21:11); Rabbi Yoḥanan, the whole body (Ezek. 21:12)
  • 'How can I not sigh?' Sixty bakers by day and sixty by night for anyone in need; his hand never left his purse lest a well-born pauper be shamed; four doors to four winds; grain scattered outside in famine years for those ashamed to take by day
  • Ulla: Rabbi Yoḥanan — since the Temple fell, the houses of the righteous were decreed to fall (Isa. 5:9), and God will restore them as He will restore Mount Zion (Ps. 125:1)
  • Still unsatisfied: 'It is enough for a servant to be like his master'

Zikin: The Comet

  • The mishna's zikin — Shmuel: a comet. 'The paths of heaven are as clear to me as the paths of Neharde'a, except the comet'
  • Tradition: a comet never crosses Orion (Kesil); if it did, the world would be destroyed. But we see it cross! Only its glow crosses
  • Rav Huna son of Rav Yehoshua: the curtain (vilon) tears and rolls, showing the light of the firmament
  • Rav Ashi: a star uprooted on one side of Orion; a star on the other side sees it, is startled, and seems to move

Orion, Pleiades, and Ash

  • Job 9:9 puts Orion before Pleiades; Amos 5:8 reverses. Without Orion's heat the world could not stand the cold of Pleiades, and vice versa
  • If Scorpio's tail did not rest in the River of Fire, no one stung by a scorpion would live — 'Can you bind the chains of Pleiades or loosen the bands of Orion?' (Job 38:31)
  • Pleiades (Kima): Shmuel — about a hundred (keme'a) stars, clustered or scattered
  • Ash: Rav Yehuda — Yota, the tail of Aries or the head of Taurus; the tail fits 'Ash with her sons' (Job 38:32)
Also on the amud: Rabbi Yirmeya ben Elazar: when Babylonia was cursed, its neighbors were cursed — 'pools of water' for the bittern (Isa. 14:23); when Samaria was cursed, its neighbors were blessed — 'a place for planting vineyards' (Micah 1:6).
