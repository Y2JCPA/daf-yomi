📖 Overview: Interruptions, Wrong Blessings, and Blessings in Any Language

Berakhot 40a–40b
כׇּל בְּרָכָה שֶׁאֵין בָּהּ הַזְכָּרַת הַשֵּׁם — אֵינָהּ בְּרָכָה
The scene: The daf begins with talk between hamotzi and the first bite: Rav, Rabbi Yoḥanan and Rav Sheshet draw the line in different places, with Rav Sheshet allowing even 'mix the food for the oxen' because one must feed animals before eating. A run of health advice from Rabbi Ḥiyya and others follows. A new mishna then rules that 'fruit of the ground' works after the fact for tree fruit, 'fruit of the tree' does not work for ground produce, and shehakol works for everything. Rav Huna and Rabbi Yoḥanan argue whether 'everything' includes bread and wine, and Binyamin the shepherd's homemade Aramaic blessing tests what a blessing must contain. The daf ends with a second mishna on foods that do not grow from the ground, and a debate over what novelot are.

🧂 What May Be Said After Hamotzi and Before Eating?

Three amora'im, three thresholds for an interruption
Words spoken after the blessingRavRabbi YoḥananRav Sheshet
טוֹל בָּרוּךְ — 'Take it and bless'No new blessing needed — this serves the blessingNo new blessingNo new blessing
הָבֵא מֶלַח — 'Bring salt', 'bring relish'Must bless again — an interruptionNo new blessing — it serves the mealNo new blessing
גַּבֵּיל לְתוֹרֵי — 'Mix the food for the oxen'Must bless againMust bless againNo new blessing — one may not eat before feeding his animals, so this too serves the meal
Rav Sheshet's source: Rav Yehuda said in Rav's name: it is forbidden to eat before giving food to one's animals, as the verse says first 'I will give grass in your fields for your animals' and only then 'and you shall eat and be satisfied' (Deuteronomy 11:15). Rava bar Shmuel added in Rabbi Ḥiyya's name that one may not break bread until salt or relish is before everyone — yet at the Exilarch's house he broke bread at once, explaining that such refined bread needs no salt.

💊 The Daf's Medicine Cabinet

Habits the Sages recommend, and the cautions that go with them

Recommended

  • Rabbi Ḥiyya: after all eating, eat salt; after all drinking, drink water — and you will not be harmed
  • Baraita: one who inundates his food with water avoids intestinal illness — Rav Ḥisda: a jug per loaf
  • Rabbi Yoḥanan: lentils once in thirty days keep diphtheria from the house; mustard once in thirty days keeps illness away
  • Rav: small fish prevent intestinal illness and make the whole body flourish
  • Rabbi Ḥama son of Rabbi Ḥanina: black cumin prevents heart pain

Cautions

  • Skip the salt or the water: by day, worry about bad breath; by night, about diphtheria
  • Lentils every day — bad breath; mustard every day — weakness of the heart
  • Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel: black cumin is one of sixty deadly drugs; sleep east of its storehouse and your blood is on your own head
  • Resolution: its taste heals, its odor harms — Rabbi Yirmeya's mother stuck it to his bread for the taste, then peeled it off
Also from Rabbi Ḥiyya: Urine leaves the body completely only when seated; Rav Kahana adds that over loose soil, or standing on a high place and aiming downhill, one may stand.

🌳 Said the Wrong Blessing? The Mishna's After-the-Fact Rules

A more general blessing can cover a more specific food, but not the reverse
❓ 'Who creates fruit of the ground' over fruit of the tree — valid?
↓
✅ Valid. Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak: the mishna follows Rabbi Yehuda, who holds the land is primary — if the spring dried up and the tree was cut, he still brings first fruits and reads the declaration
↓
❓ 'Who creates fruit of the tree' over ground produce — obviously invalid, so why teach it?
↓
💡 Needed for Rabbi Yehuda, who says the tree Adam ate from was wheat (a child cannot say 'father' and 'mother' until tasting grain). One might think wheat gets 'fruit of the tree'
↓
Tree
Take the fruit and the branch remains and produces again → 'fruit of the tree'
Not a tree
Take the fruit and no branch remains to produce again → 'fruit of the ground'
↓
🍷 'By whose word all things came to be' over everything: Rav Huna — except bread and wine; Rabbi Yoḥanan — even bread and wine
Adam's tree, three views: Rabbi Meir: a vine, for nothing brings wailing like wine (Noah, Genesis 9:21). Rabbi Neḥemya: a fig, since they were repaired with fig leaves (Genesis 3:7). Rabbi Yehuda: wheat.

🍷 Does Shehakol Cover Bread and Wine?

The Gemara tries to map Rav Huna and Rabbi Yoḥanan onto Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yosei
Tosefta
Rabbi Meir: one who saw bread and said 'How pleasant is this bread, blessed is the Omnipresent Who created it' has fulfilled his obligation. Rabbi Yosei: whoever deviates from the formula the Sages coined has not fulfilled it.
Gemara
So Rav Huna (shehakol fails for bread and wine) is Rabbi Yosei, and Rabbi Yoḥanan (shehakol works even for them) is Rabbi Meir?
Rav Huna
I can hold even like Rabbi Meir. He validated a blessing that mentions bread by name; where the word bread is absent, even Rabbi Meir agrees one has not fulfilled his obligation.
R' Yoḥanan
And I can hold even like Rabbi Yosei. He rejected a blessing the Sages never instituted; shehakol is a blessing the Sages did institute, so even Rabbi Yosei agrees it works after the fact.
Gemara
Related: Rabbi Yehuda's 'Who creates various kinds of herbs' is not the halakha. His reason was 'Blessed is the Lord day by day' (Psalms 68:20) — give each day, and each food, its fitting blessing.
Aggadic aside: Rabbi Zeira (or Rabbi Ḥinnana bar Pappa): a human vessel holds only when empty, but the Holy One fills the one who is already full — 'if you will surely listen, you will listen' (Exodus 15:26): listen to the old, and you will hear the new.

🐑 Binyamin the Shepherd's Aramaic Blessing

What must a blessing contain to count?
1️⃣ Binyamin the shepherd ate bread and said in Aramaic: בְּרִיךְ מָרֵיהּ דְּהַאי פִּיתָּא — 'Blessed is the Master of this bread'. Rav: he fulfilled his obligation
↓
2️⃣ But Rav himself says a blessing without God's name is no blessing! Answer: he actually said 'Blessed is the All-Merciful, Master of this bread'
↓
3️⃣ But Grace after Meals needs three blessings! Answer: he fulfilled only the first blessing
↓
4️⃣ What is new? The mishna in Sota already lists Grace among things said in any language. Answer: one might think only a translation of the Sages' formula works — Rav teaches that even a different formula works after the fact
↓
5️⃣ The core dispute: Rav — a blessing without God's name is no blessing; Rabbi Yoḥanan — a blessing without God's sovereignty is no blessing
↓
6️⃣ Abaye sides with Rav: the tithe confession, 'I did not forget' (Deut. 26:13), means 'to mention Your name over it' — sovereignty is not taught. Rabbi Yoḥanan emends: 'Your name and Your sovereignty'
Second mishna: Foods that do not grow from the ground (meat, fish, milk, eggs, cheese), spoiled foods, salt, brine, truffles and mushrooms all get shehakol. Mushrooms do grow from the ground, says Abaye, but they do not draw sustenance from it, so the baraita is read: 'that which does not draw sustenance from the ground'.
