📖 Overview: The Words of the Zimmun and the Dignity of Food

Berakhot 50a–50b
וּמִבִּרְכוֹתָיו שֶׁל אָדָם נִיכָּר אִם תַּלְמִיד חָכָם הוּא אִם לָאו
The scene: The daf concludes that 'Let us bless' is the better zimmun formula because the leader includes himself, then lists phrasings that reveal whether the speaker is a Torah scholar or an ignoramus. It resolves the contradiction in the mishna about crowd size — Rabbi Yosei HaGelili scales the formula, Rabbi Akiva does not, and Rava rules like Rabbi Akiva. A new mishna teaches that three who ate together may not divide, six may divide until ten, and ten may not until twenty; Rava adds that once people were included in a zimmun their obligation 'flies away', proving it from a bed that was split and rejoined. The daf closes with wine that has not been diluted, four rules about honoring bread, and what to do with food already in one's mouth when a blessing was forgotten.

🗣️ Why 'Let Us Bless' Beats 'Bless'

Finishing Shmuel's rule: never exclude yourself from the collective
❓ The mishna says that with three and himself one says 'Bless'. Doesn't that exclude the leader from the group?
↓
💬 Answer: the mishna means he may say 'Bless' — but 'Let us bless' (נְבָרֵךְ) is preferable.
↓
📜 Proof — Rav Adda bar Ahava, from the school of Rav: six may divide into two zimmun groups, up to ten.
↓
If nevarekh is preferable
Then dividing loses nothing — each group of three can still say 'Let us bless'.
If barekhu were preferable
Why permit dividing? Neither group of three could say 'Bless' (which needs three besides the leader).
↓
✅ Conclude: 'Let us bless' is preferable. The Tosefta agrees — whoever says either is not reprimanded, though the punctilious do reprimand him.

🎓 A Man's Blessings Reveal Whether He Is a Scholar

Phrasings of the zimmun that mark a talmid ḥakham or a bur
Scholar saysIgnoramus saysWhy it matters
וּבְטוּבוֹ — 'and by His goodness we live' (Rabbi)וּמִטּוּבוֹ — 'and from His goodness''From' implies only part of God's goodness reached him — a denial of His kindness. David's 'from Your blessing' (II Sam. 7:29) is a request, which is different; 'open your mouth wide' (Ps. 81:11) applies to Torah
בְּטוּבוֹ חָיִינוּ — 'we live' (Rabbi)'they live''They' excludes the speaker from the collective. The Sages of Neharbela taught the reverse, but the halakha does not follow them
'Let us bless the One Whose food we have eaten' (Rabbi Yoḥanan)'Let us bless to Him from Whose food we have eaten''To him' sounds like a blessing for the host. At the seder 'to Him Who performed miracles' is fine — only God performs miracles (Rav Ashi to Rav Aḥa son of Rava)
'Blessed is the One from Whose food we have eaten' (Rabbi Yoḥanan)'For the food we have eaten'It again sounds as if he blesses the host
Rav Huna son of Rav Yehoshua: these rules apply only with three—With ten, God's name is invoked — 'Blessed is the Lord our God… for the food we have eaten' — so it is self-evident to Whom the blessing is directed

🔢 Does the Formula Grow with the Crowd?

The mishna says ten equals ten myriads — and then gives different words for a hundred, a thousand, and ten thousand
Gemara
The mishna contradicts itself: 'both ten and ten myriads' — so they are the same; then 'with a hundred he says… with a thousand… with ten thousand…' — so it depends on the number!
Rav Yosef
Not difficult. This is Rabbi Yosei HaGelili, that is Rabbi Akiva. Rabbi Yosei HaGelili: bless according to the size of the crowd — 'In assemblies bless God' (Ps. 68:27). Rabbi Akiva: as in the synagogue, many or few say 'Bless the Lord'.
Gemara
What does Rabbi Akiva do with 'In assemblies bless God'?
Answer
He needs it for Rabbi Meir's teaching: even fetuses in their mothers' wombs sang at the sea. Rabbi Yosei HaGelili derives that from 'from the source of Israel', an allusion to the womb.
Rava
The halakha follows Rabbi Akiva.
Ravina
At the Exilarch's banquet Rav Ḥama bar Buzi rose to gather a hundred diners. Ravina told him: no need — Rava ruled like Rabbi Akiva; the formula is the same from ten upward.
More from the Exilarch's house and the synagogue: Rava: at the Exilarch's we recited Grace in groups of three — groups of ten would anger the Exilarch, and the noise kept anyone from hearing his blessing. Rafram bar Pappa said 'Bless the Lord' without 'the blessed One', and the whole synagogue shouted the full formula; Rava told him, 'black pot, why involve yourself in the dispute? The world's custom follows Rabbi Yishmael.'

🔁 Three May Not Divide — and No Retroactive Zimmun

The new mishna and Rava's proof from a broken bed
1️⃣ Mishna: three who ate together may not divide; likewise four and five. Six may divide, up to ten; ten may not divide until twenty. Two groups in one house join if some of them can see each other
↓
2️⃣ What is new? We already learned that three must form a zimmun! Rabbi Abba in Shmuel's name: even three who merely sat down to eat and have not yet eaten may not divide — or, in another version, even if each eats from his own loaf
↓
3️⃣ Alternatively, Rav Huna's rule: three who came from three different groups may not divide. Rav Ḥisda: only if each came from a group of three, so the obligation never lapsed
↓
4️⃣ Rava: and only if their original groups did not already include them in a zimmun. If they did — פְּרַח זִימּוּן מִינַּיְיהוּ, the zimmun obligation has flown away from them
↓
5️⃣ Rava's proof: an impure bed half stolen, lost, or divided by brothers or partners is pure; if restored it becomes susceptible to impurity from now on, not retroactively. Once divided, the impurity left — so too, once included in a zimmun, the obligation left
↓
6️⃣ Rabba Tosefa'a: if one of three went ahead and recited Grace alone, the other two fulfill their zimmun with him, but he gains nothing from theirs — there is no retroactive zimmun
↓
7️⃣ Baraita on two groups: if a common waiter serves both, the waiter joins them into one group

🍷 Wine, Bread, and Food in the Mouth

The daf's closing halakhot on the dignity of what we eat

Undiluted wine

  • Rabbi Eliezer: before water is added, bless 'fruit of the tree' and one may wash hands with it; after — 'fruit of the vine', and no washing
  • The Rabbis: either way it is wine — 'fruit of the vine', and no washing
  • Shmuel's 'one may do all his needs with bread' follows Rabbi Eliezer
  • Rabbi Yosei bar Rabbi Ḥanina: the Rabbis concede for a cup of blessing — Rav Oshaya: we want the mitzva done in the best manner
  • Rabbi Zeira: undiluted wine is fit for koraiytei, a wine-and-oil remedy

Respect for bread

  • Four rules: no raw meat on bread, no passing a full cup over bread, no throwing bread, no propping a dish with bread
  • Mar Zutra tossed a pomegranate to Rav Ashi; the baraitot conflict about throwing other foods
  • Resolution: food that becomes disgusting when thrown may not be thrown; food that does not, may
  • Wine may be piped before a bride and groom; roasted grain and nuts thrown in summer but not the rainy season; cakes never

Food in the mouth without a blessing

  • Rav Yehuda: move it to one side of the mouth and recite the blessing
  • Three baraitot: swallow — liquids, no alternative
  • Spit out — food that does not become disgusting and can still be eaten
  • Move aside — food that would become disgusting
