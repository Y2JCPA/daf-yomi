Berakhot 34 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to the baraita, how should one respond when asked to serve as prayer leader?
A. Accept immediately, so as not to burden the congregation
B. Refuse the first time, waver the second, and go up the third
C. Refuse three times before accepting
D. Accept only if no priest is present
Correct: B — One who does not refuse at all is like a dish without salt; one who refuses too much is like a dish ruined by salt. So: refuse once, vacillate 'like a wick catching flame' the second time, and descend before the ark the third.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
What is the dispute between Rav Huna and Rav Asi about an error in the middle blessings of the Amida?
A. Rav Huna: return to the beginning of the Amida; Rav Asi: return to Avoda
B. Rav Huna: return to 'You grace humanity', since the middle blessings are one unit; Rav Asi: they have no fixed order, so insert the missed blessing wherever one realizes
C. Rav Huna: repeat the whole Amida; Rav Asi: do nothing
D. They dispute only the last three blessings
Correct: B — Rav Huna treats the thirteen middle blessings as a single blessing, so one returns to its start, Ata Ḥonen — which is how he answers Rav Sheshet's objection from the baraita. Rav Asi holds the middle blessings have no fixed order.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
How did Rabbi Eliezer defend the student whose prayer was excessively brief?
A. He said brevity is always preferable in public prayer
B. He cited Moses' five-word prayer for Miriam, 'Please, God, heal her, please'
C. He said the student had already prayed privately
D. He cited Hannah, whose voice was not heard
Correct: B — Just as he defended the long-winded student from Moses' forty days and nights of prostration, Rabbi Eliezer defended the brief one from Moses' prayer in Numbers 12:13 — no one is briefer than that.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
How did Rabbi Ḥanina ben Dosa know whether a sick person would recover?
A. He was a prophet, as he told Rabban Gamliel's messengers
B. By examining the patient in person
C. If his prayer was fluent in his mouth he knew it was accepted; if not, he knew it was rejected
D. By whether the fever broke while he was still praying
Correct: C — He explicitly denied being a prophet or a prophet's son; his received tradition was that fluency of the prayer signals acceptance, which Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi grounds in Isaiah 57:19, 'He who creates the expression of the lips… and I will heal him.'
QUESTION 5 OF 5
How did Rabbi Yoḥanan ben Zakkai explain why Rabbi Ḥanina ben Dosa's prayer for his son succeeded when his own would not?
A. Rabbi Ḥanina was the greater scholar
B. Rabbi Ḥanina is like a servant before the king, who may enter at any time, while he is like a minister, who enters only when summoned
C. Rabbi Ḥanina prayed in a house with windows
D. Rabbi Ḥanina's prayer was longer
Correct: B — When his wife asked whether Ḥanina was greater than he, Rabbi Yoḥanan ben Zakkai said no — but a servant has constant, informal access to the king, whereas a minister appears only on formal occasions.
