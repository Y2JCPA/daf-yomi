Berakhot 62 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
What justification did Rabbi Akiva, Ben Azzai and Rav Kahana all give for observing their teachers' most private conduct?
A. That their teachers had given them permission
B. That it is Torah, and they must learn it
C. That they were checking whether their teachers were truly modest
D. That no other student was available to do so
Correct: B — Each was rebuked for impertinence, and each answered with the same words: torah hi, velilmod ani tzarikh — it is Torah, and I must learn.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
Which reason for not wiping with the right hand is given by Rava?
A. Because one eats with the right hand
B. Because one writes with the right hand
C. Because the Torah was given with the right hand
D. Because one points to the cantillation notes with it
Correct: C — Rava cites 'At His right hand was a fiery law unto them' (Deuteronomy 33:2). The other reasons belong to Rabba bar bar Ḥana, Reish Lakish and Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak, and parallel the tannaitic dispute of Rabbi Eliezer, Rabbi Yehoshua and Rabbi Akiva.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
What tradition did Rabbi Ami and Rabbi Asi rely on when entering the dangerous bathroom in Tiberias alone?
A. To carry a lamp and recite a verse
B. That the bathroom's tradition is modesty and silence, and the tradition for suffering is silence and prayer
C. That two who enter together are protected while one alone is not
D. To enter only during daylight hours
Correct: B — They told the Sages they had received this tradition, and indeed they were unharmed where two people entering together had been harmed even by day.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Rabbi Elazar, why did David not kill Saul in the cave?
A. Because Saul was still the anointed king
B. Because David's men prevented him
C. Because the modesty Saul displayed there spared him, though by law a pursuer may be killed
D. Because David had sworn an oath to Jonathan
Correct: C — David told Saul that as a pursuer he was liable by Torah law, but the modesty Saul showed — a fence within a fence, a cave within a cave, covering himself like a sukka — is what saved him.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
How does Rava argue that spitting is permitted in a synagogue?
A. From the shortcut, which is permitted in a synagogue
B. From shoes, which are forbidden on the Temple Mount but permitted in a synagogue
C. From the verse about the king's gate and sackcloth
D. From the fact that the Divine Presence no longer rests in synagogues
Correct: B — Rava reasons by analogy to shoes. When challenged that one might instead derive it from the shortcut, which is forbidden in a synagogue, he answers that the tanna himself derived spitting from the case of the shoe.
