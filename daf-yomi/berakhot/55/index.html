📖 Overview: From Long Prayers to the Science of Dreams

Berakhot 55a–55b
חֶלְמָא דְּלָא מְפַשַּׁר כְּאִגַּרְתָּא דְּלָא מִקַּרְיָא
The scene: The daf opens by reconciling praise for one who prolongs his prayer, his meal, and his time in the bathroom with sources that condemn each. A series of 'threes' follows: three things that shorten life, three that need mercy, three that God proclaims Himself. The appointment of Bezalel teaches that a leader needs the community's consent and that his name reflects his wisdom. Then the Gemara turns to dreams: Rav Ḥisda's maxims, Joseph's dream that was only partly fulfilled, the formula for 'bettering' a troubling dream before three people, the prayer said during the Priestly Blessing, and the principle that all dreams follow the mouth of the interpreter.

🙏 Three Things Rav Yehuda Praised Prolonging

Each praise seems contradicted by another source; each is resolved by a distinction

Prayer

  • Objection: 'Hope deferred makes the heart sick' (Proverbs 13:12); Rabbi Yitzḥak lists expecting prayer to be answered among three things that recall one's sins
  • Resolution: bad when one expects to be answered (מְעַיֵּין בַּהּ); good when one simply increases supplication

The Table

  • Why is it good? Perhaps a poor person will come and the host can feed him at once
  • Ezekiel 41:22 begins 'the altar' and ends 'the table before the Lord'
  • Rabbi Yoḥanan and Rabbi Elazar: while the Temple stood the altar atoned; now a person's table atones for him

The Bathroom

  • Objection: a baraita lists ten causes of hemorrhoids, and some say 'one who suspends himself too much in the bathroom'
  • Resolution: harmful when he prolongs and suspends himself; healthy when he prolongs without suspending
  • Rabbi Yehuda son of Rabbi Ilai told the matron his healthy face came from examining himself in the 24 bathrooms between his lodging and the study hall
Method: The Gemara does not reject either source; it splits the case. The same act is a virtue in one form and a fault in another.

🛠️ Bezalel: Appointed by Consent, Named for Wisdom

Rabbi Yitzḥak and Rabbi Yonatan read Exodus 35:30 and the order of the Tabernacle's construction
R' Yitzḥak
One may appoint a leader over a community only after consulting the community: 'See, the Lord has called by name Bezalel' (Exodus 35:30).
God to Moses
Moses, is Bezalel suitable in your eyes? — Master of the universe, if he is suitable before You, all the more so before me! — Nevertheless, go and tell them.
Israel
If he is suitable before the Holy One and before you, all the more so before us!
R' Yonatan
Bezalel was named for his wisdom. God told Moses: make a Tabernacle, ark, and vessels. Moses reversed it: ark, vessels, and Tabernacle.
Bezalel
Moses our teacher, the way of the world is to build a house and then bring in the vessels. If I make the vessels first, where shall I put them? Perhaps God said: Tabernacle, ark, and vessels?
Moses
Perhaps you were in God's shadow (בְּצֵל אֵל) and knew! Rav adds: Bezalel knew how to join the letters with which heaven and earth were created — wisdom, understanding, knowledge (Ex. 31:3; Prov. 3:19–20).
Rabbi Yoḥanan: God grants wisdom only to one who already has wisdom: 'He gives wisdom unto the wise' (Daniel 2:21). Rabbi Abbahu derived the same from 'in the hearts of all who are wise-hearted I have placed wisdom' (Exodus 31:6).

💭 Rav Ḥisda's Rules of Dreaming

A string of maxims that set the daf's theory of dreams
1️⃣ Any dream — but not one during a fast
↓
2️⃣ חֶלְמָא דְּלָא מְפַשַּׁר כְּאִגַּרְתָּא דְּלָא מִקַּרְיָא — a dream not interpreted is like a letter not read
↓
3️⃣ Neither a good dream nor a bad dream is entirely fulfilled — Rabbi Berekhya proves it from Joseph: the moon bowed, yet his mother was already dead (Gen. 37:9)
↓
4️⃣ A bad dream is better than a good one (it brings remorse); its sadness suffices, just as a good dream's joy suffices. Rav Yosef: even for me, the joy of a good dream negates it
↓
5️⃣ A bad dream is worse than lashes: 'God has so made it that men should fear before Him' (Eccl. 3:14) — Rabbi Yoḥanan: that is a bad dream
↓
6️⃣ Rabbi Shimon bar Yoḥai: as grain cannot grow without straw, no dream is without idle matter (Jeremiah 23:28); Rabbi Levi: expect a good dream's fulfillment up to 22 years, Joseph's span from age 17 to the end of the famine
Rav Huna: A good person is not shown a good dream and a wicked person is not shown a bad dream. David never saw a good dream; Ahitophel never saw a bad one. Since Psalms 91:10 promises the righteous will not be frightened by bad dreams, the Gemara concludes David saw dreams but did not understand what he saw — and Rabbi Zeira says one who sleeps seven days without a dream is called 'evil'.

🗣️ What To Do With a Troubling Dream

Rabbi Yoḥanan's remedy before three, the prayer at Birkat Kohanim, and why the interpreter's mouth matters
❓ Rabbi Yoḥanan: one distraught by a dream should 'interpret it' before three. But Rav Ḥisda said an uninterpreted dream is an unread letter — why promote its fulfillment?
↓
💡 Read instead: he should better it (יְטִיבֶנּוּ) before three. He says 'I saw a good dream'; they answer 'it is good and let it be good, may the Merciful One make it good; seven times may Heaven decree that it be good'
↓
📜 They recite three 'transformations' (Ps. 30:12, Jer. 31:12, Deut. 23:6), three 'redemptions' (Ps. 55:19, Isa. 35:10, I Sam. 14:45), and three 'peaces' (Isa. 57:19, I Chr. 12:19, I Sam. 25:6)
↓
Don't know what you saw?
Ameimar, Mar Zutra and Rav Ashi: stand before the priests as they lift their hands and say 'Master of the Universe, I am Yours and my dreams are Yours…' finishing as the congregation answers amen
Shmuel's practice
After a bad dream: 'the dreams speak falsely' (Zech. 10:2); after a good one: 'I speak with him in a dream' (Num. 12:6). Rava: the first is by a demon, the second by an angel
↓
✅ Rabbi Bena'a went to 24 interpreters in Jerusalem; each gave a different reading and all came true — כׇּל הַחֲלוֹמוֹת הוֹלְכִים אַחַר הַפֶּה, all dreams follow the mouth (Gen. 41:13). Rava: only when the interpretation fits the dream (Gen. 41:12)
Rabbi Yonatan: A person is shown only the thoughts of his own heart (Daniel 2:29–30). Rava: that is why no one dreams of a golden palm tree or an elephant passing through the eye of a needle.
