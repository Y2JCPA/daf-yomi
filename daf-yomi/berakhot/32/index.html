📖 Overview: Moses Wrestles With Heaven

Berakhot 32a–32b
מְלַמֵּד שֶׁתְּפָסוֹ מֹשֶׁה לְהַקָּדוֹשׁ בָּרוּךְ הוּא, כְּאָדָם שֶׁהוּא תּוֹפֵס אֶת חֲבֵירוֹ בְּבִגְדוֹ
The scene: Continuing the theme of bold prayer, the daf presents Moses as the model of one who 'speaks impertinently toward Heaven' and wins. He blames the Golden Calf on the wealth God showered on Israel, seizes God as if by the garment, refuses the offer to become a new nation, and argues that the nations would call God powerless — until God says 'I have forgiven according to your word.' Rabbi Elazar then ranks prayer above good deeds and sacrifices and teaches that although the gates of prayer were locked when the Temple fell, the gates of tears were not. The amud closes with the practice of the early pious men, who waited an hour before and after prayer, and with the rule that one does not interrupt the Amida even for a king — a rule tested when a Roman officer greets a praying ḥasid.

🥇 Di Zahav — 'The Gold Made Them Do It'

The school of Rabbi Yannai reads a place-name in Deuteronomy 1:1 as Moses' defense of Israel
❓ 'And Di Zahav' — no such place exists. What does it mean?
↓
🗣️ Moses said: Master of the Universe, the silver and gold You lavished on Israel until they said dai (enough) — that is what caused them to make the Calf
↓
Rabbi Yannai's school
A lion does not roar over a basket of straw, only over a basket of meat
Rabbi Oshaya
A lean, big-limbed cow fed lupines kicks its owner — 'who caused you to kick but the lupines I fed you?'
Rabbi Yoḥanan
A father bathes, anoints and feeds his son, hangs a purse on his neck and seats him at a brothel door — what can the son do but sin?
↓
📜 Popular saying (Rav Sheshet): a full stomach breeds sin — 'they were sated, their hearts lifted, they forgot Me' (Hosea 13:6); 'Jeshurun grew fat and kicked' (Deut. 32:15)
↓
✅ Rabbi Yonatan: God conceded to Moses — 'I gave them abundant silver and gold, which they used for the Ba'al' (Hosea 2:10)
Three verses that hold Israel up: Rabbi Ḥama son of Rabbi Ḥanina: without them Israel's legs would collapse in judgment — 'those with whom I have dealt in wickedness' (Micah 4:6), 'like clay in the potter's hand, so are you in My hand' (Jeremiah 18:6), and 'I will remove the heart of stone' (Ezekiel 36:26). Rav Pappa adds 'I will place My spirit within you and cause you to walk in My statutes' (Ezekiel 36:27). Rabbi Elazar also read 'Moses prayed el the Lord' as al — 'onto' — since Rabbi Eliezer ben Ya'akov's school exchanged alef and ayin.

✊ 'Leave Me Be' — Moses Refuses to Let Go

Rabbi Elazar and Rabbi Abbahu reconstruct the argument after the Golden Calf (Exodus 32; Numbers 14)
God
'Go, descend' — Moses, descend from your greatness. I gave you prominence only for Israel's sake; now that they have sinned, why do I need you?
Moses
His strength waned… until God said 'Leave Me be, that I may destroy them.' Then: this matter depends on me! He rose and took hold of prayer — like a king's friend who hears 'were it not for you I would kill him' and steps in to save the son.
Rabbi Abbahu
Were the verse not written one could not say it: 'Leave Me be' teaches that Moses grabbed God as a man grabs his friend's garment — 'I will not let You go until You forgive and pardon them.'
God
'And I will make of you a great nation.'
Moses
If a chair with three legs — the three forefathers — cannot stand before You in Your anger, how could a chair with one leg? And I would be ashamed before my forefathers: 'see the leader who sought greatness for himself and did not ask mercy for them.'
Moses
'You swore to them by Yourself' — had You sworn by heaven and earth, the oath would pass like them; sworn by Your great Name, it endures forever like Your Name.
Moses
'For lack of the Lord's ability' (feminine form!) — the nations will say His strength weakened like a woman's. You say they saw the Sea? They will say: one king He can beat, thirty-one kings He cannot.
God
'I have forgiven according to your word' (Numbers 14:20). Rabbi Yoḥanan: God conceded to Moses. Rav Yitzḥak: 'As I live' — Moses, you have given Me life with your words.
Happy is the student whose teacher concedes to him: Rabbi Simlai draws the practical lesson from Deuteronomy 3:23–25: Moses first praised God — 'You have begun to show Your servant Your greatness' — and only then asked 'let me pass over and see the good land.' Always set forth God's praise, and then pray for your needs.

📖 Vayḥal — What Did Moses Do When He 'Beseeched'?

Six sages read the rare word וַיְחַל (Exodus 32:11) six different ways
SageReading of vayḥalMeaning
Rabbi Elazarheḥelahu — it made him illMoses prayed until he sickened from exertion
Ravalo yaḥel — annulling a vow (Numbers 30:3)He cannot annul his own vow, but others can annul for him — Moses annulled God's vow to destroy Israel
Shmuelḥalal — a corpseHe gave himself over to death: 'if not, erase me from Your book'
Rava citing Rav Yitzḥakheḥela — caused to take effectHe caused the Attribute of Mercy to take effect upon them
The Rabbisḥullin — profaneMoses said: it is sacrilege for You to do such a thing
Rabbi Eliezer the Greataḥilu seized himRabbi Elazar: 'fire of the bones'; Abaye: the Babylonian eshta degarmei — a fever
Whose words? 'All this land of which I have spoken' should read 'of which You have spoken.' Rabbi Elazar: up to there are the student's words, from there the Master's. Rabbi Shmuel bar Naḥmani: all are Moses' — 'what You told me to say in Your name I said; now what do I tell them?'

🙏 Rabbi Elazar Weighs Prayer, Deeds, Fasts and Sacrifices

The mnemonic: deeds, charity, offering, priest, fast, shoe, iron

Prayer is greater than…

  • …good deeds: none was greater in deeds than Moses, yet he was answered only through prayer — 'speak no more to Me' is followed by 'go up to the summit'
  • …sacrifices: 'To what purpose is the multitude of your sacrifices' (Isaiah 1:11), and only later 'when you spread your hands I will hide My eyes' (1:15)
  • Rabbi Yoḥanan: a priest who killed a person may not lift his hands — 'your hands are full of blood'

A fast is greater than charity

  • A fast is performed with one's body
  • Charity is performed only with one's money
  • Yet Rava would not decree a fast on a cloudy day: 'You covered Yourself in a cloud, through which prayer cannot pass' (Lamentations 3:44)

Since the Temple was destroyed

  • The gates of prayer were locked — 'though I plead and call out, He shuts out my prayer' (Lamentations 3:8)
  • But the gates of tears were not locked — 'keep not silence at my tears' (Psalms 39:13)
  • An iron wall separates Israel from their Father in Heaven — Ezekiel's iron griddle (Ezekiel 4:3)
Forsaken and forgotten? Reish Lakish: Zion complained that even a man who takes a second wife remembers the first — 'You have forsaken and forgotten me.' God answered: twelve constellations, thirty armies for each, thirty legions for each… and 365,000 myriads of stars on each — all created only for you. 'These too shall be forgotten' is the Calf; 'I (anokhi) will not forget you' is Sinai (Rabbi Elazar citing Rav Oshaya).

⏳ Prolonging Prayer, Praying Again, and Waiting an Hour

From bolstering one's prayer to the early pious men who spent nine hours a day at it
1️⃣ Rabbi Ḥanin citing Rabbi Ḥanina: one who prolongs his prayer is not turned away empty — Moses fell before God forty days and 'the Lord heard me that time as well'
↓
2️⃣ But Rabbi Yoḥanan: one who prolongs and expects an answer ends in heartache — 'hope deferred makes the heart sick' (Proverbs 13:12); the remedy is Torah, the 'tree of life'. Resolution: praise is for one who prolongs without demanding
↓
3️⃣ Rabbi Ḥama son of Rabbi Ḥanina: prayed and not answered? Pray again — 'Hope in the Lord… and hope in the Lord' (Psalms 27:14)
↓
4️⃣ Four things need bolstering (חִזּוּק): Torah, good deeds (Joshua 1:7 'be strong and courageous'), prayer (Psalms 27:14), and one's occupation (II Samuel 10:12)
↓
5️⃣ The early pious waited an hour before praying — 'happy are those who dwell in Your house' (Psalms 84:5) — and an hour after — 'the upright will sit before You' (Psalms 140:14)
↓
6️⃣ Nine hours a day! How was their Torah kept and their work done? Because they were pious, their Torah was preserved and their work was blessed
Even if the king greets him: Rav Yosef: the mishna's rule not to answer applies to kings of Israel; for a gentile king one interrupts. A baraita says one who sees a violent man or a wagon coming should abridge rather than stop — that is when abridging is possible; otherwise he interrupts. A ḥasid who ignored a Roman officer's greeting was asked afterward: 'Does your Torah not say guard your life? Had I cut off your head, who would avenge you?' — and asked for a moment to appease him.
