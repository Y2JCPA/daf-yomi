📖 Overview: Women's Mitzvot and the Ba'al Keri

Berakhot 20a–20b
נָשִׁים וַעֲבָדִים וּקְטַנִּים פְּטוּרִין מִקְּרִיאַת שְׁמַע וּמִן הַתְּפִילִּין, וְחַיָּיבִין בִּתְפִלָּה וּבִמְזוּזָה וּבְבִרְכַּת הַמָּזוֹן
The scene: The daf closes the human-dignity discussion: the Nazirite may bury a met mitzva, but only because 'sit and refrain from action' is different. Rav Pappa asks Abaye why miracles happened for earlier generations and not for us; the answer is self-sacrifice for God's name, illustrated by Rav Adda bar Ahava, Rav Giddel, and Rabbi Yoḥanan. A new mishna then exempts women, slaves, and minors from Shema and tefillin but obligates them in prayer, mezuza, and Grace after Meals. The Gemara explains why each ruling needed stating, debates whether women's kiddush and Grace are Torah law, and finally opens the laws of the ba'al keri: does contemplation count as speech?

✨ Why Did Miracles Happen for Them and Not for Us?

Rav Pappa's question to Abaye, and three stories of the earlier generations
Rav Pappa
In Rav Yehuda's day all their learning was Nezikin, while we learn all six orders — thirteen versions of Okatzin! Yet when Rav Yehuda removed one shoe, rain fell; we cry out and no one notices.
Abaye
The earlier generations gave their lives for the sanctification of God's name (קְדוּשַּׁת הַשֵּׁם); we do not.
Story 1
Rav Adda bar Ahava saw a woman in the market wearing a karbalta of kilayim. Thinking she was Jewish, he tore it off her. She was not — he was fined 400 zuz. 'What is your name?' 'Matun.' 'Matun, matun — worth 400 zuz.'
Story 2
Rav Giddel sat at the immersion gates instructing women how to immerse. 'Aren't you afraid of the evil inclination?' 'They are like white geese to me.'
Story 3
Rabbi Yoḥanan sat there so women would see him and bear beautiful children. 'Aren't you afraid of the evil eye?' 'I descend from Joseph, over whom the evil eye has no dominion.'
Joseph's immunity: Rabbi Abbahu: read עֲלֵי עָיִן (Genesis 49:22) as עוֹלֵי עָיִן, 'above the eye'. Rabbi Yosei b. Rabbi Ḥanina: 'may they multiply like fish' (Genesis 48:16) — water covers fish, so the evil eye cannot see them. Or: an eye that refused to feast on what was not his is immune to the evil eye.

👩 The Mishna's Five Rulings — Why Each Needed Saying

Women, slaves, and minors: each 'obvious' ruling blocks a wrong inference
MitzvaRulingWhy say it? (מַהוּ דְתֵימָא)
ShemaExemptIt contains acceptance of the yoke of Heaven — you might think women are obligated anyway
TefillinExemptTefillin is juxtaposed to mezuza (Deut. 6:8–9) — you might equate them
PrayerObligatedIt is a plea for mercy; but 'evening, morning, and afternoon' (Psalms 55:18) could make it look time-bound
MezuzaObligatedMezuza is juxtaposed to Torah study (Deut. 11:19–20), from which women are exempt
Grace after MealsObligated'Meat in the evening and bread in the morning' (Exodus 16:8) could make it look time-bound
The rule: מִצְוַת עֲשֵׂה שֶׁהַזְּמַן גְּרָמָא — women are exempt from any positive mitzva caused by time. Shema is bound to morning and evening; tefillin to weekdays and daytime.

🍷 Are Women Obligated in Kiddush by Torah Law?

Rav Adda bar Ahava's ruling and Rava's derivation
📜 Rav Adda bar Ahava: women are obligated in kiddush of Shabbat by Torah law
↓
❓ Why? Kiddush is a time-bound positive mitzva, and women are exempt from those!
↓
Abaye
By rabbinic law only
Rava's rejection
But he said 'by Torah law'! And if so, obligate them rabbinically in every time-bound mitzva
↓
✅ Rava: זָכוֹר וְשָׁמוֹר — 'Remember' (Exodus 20:8) and 'Observe' (Deut. 5:12). Whoever is included in observing Shabbat is included in remembering it. Women must observe, so they must remember (kiddush)
Method: The two versions of the Ten Commandments are read as one unit: the prohibitions (Observe) and the positive mitzva (Remember) share the same set of obligated people.

🍞 Can a Woman Recite Grace on Behalf of Others?

Ravina asks Rava: is women's Birkat HaMazon from the Torah or the Rabbis?
1️⃣ The practical difference: one obligated only rabbinically cannot fulfill the obligation of one obligated by Torah law — כׇּל שֶׁאֵינוֹ מְחוּיָּיב בַּדָּבָר אֵינוֹ מוֹצִיא אֶת הָרַבִּים
↓
2️⃣ Proof attempt: a baraita says a son may bless for his father, a slave for his master, a woman for her husband — though 'may a curse come to a man whose wife and children bless for him'
↓
3️⃣ If women's obligation were rabbinic, how could she fulfill her husband's Torah obligation? So it must be Torah law
↓
4️⃣ Rejected: by that logic a minor is Torah-obligated too! Rather, the father ate only a rabbinic measure (not to satiety), so rabbinic fulfills rabbinic. The question stands
Aggada: Rav Avira (in the name of Rabbi Ami or Rabbi Asi): the angels asked how God 'shows favor' to Israel (Numbers 6:26) when He 'favors no one' (Deut. 10:17). God answered: I wrote 'you shall eat and be satisfied and bless' (Deut. 8:10), yet they are exacting with themselves to bless even over an olive-bulk or an egg-bulk.

💭 Is Contemplation Like Speech?

The mishna: a ba'al keri contemplates Shema in his heart without blessings; blesses after food but not before

Ravina: הִרְהוּר כְּדִבּוּר דָּמֵי

  • Contemplation is like speech
  • Otherwise, why would the ba'al keri contemplate at all?
  • Then why not say it aloud? 'As we found at Sinai' — those impure had to immerse before hearing the spoken Torah

Rav Ḥisda: לָאו כְּדִבּוּר דָּמֵי

  • Contemplation is not like speech
  • If it were, he should simply utter the words with his lips
  • Then why contemplate? Rabbi Elazar: so he is not sitting idle while everyone else recites Shema

Why Shema specifically?

  • Let him study a different chapter instead!
  • Rav Adda bar Ahava: one should engage in the matter the community is engaged in
  • Rabbi Yehuda in the mishna: he blesses both before and after
Background: Ezra decreed that one impure from a seminal emission may not engage in Torah until he immerses. The mishna allows blessing after food (Torah law) but not before (rabbinic law).
