📖 Overview: Wine at the Table and Blessings on Fragrance

Berakhot 43a–43b
כֹּל הַנְּשָׁמָה תְּהַלֵּל יָהּ — אֵיזֶהוּ דָּבָר שֶׁהַנְּשָׁמָה נֶהֱנֵית מִמֶּנּוּ וְאֵין הַגּוּף נֶהֱנֶה מִמֶּנּוּ? הֱוֵי אוֹמֵר: זֶה הָרֵיחַ
The scene: The daf opens at the moment Rav's students learn of his death and fear they never learned the laws of Grace after Meals; an elder resolves their question. The Gemara then asks whether reclining lets one person bless wine for everyone (Rav vs. Rabbi Yoḥanan), walks through the Tosefta's order of a formal meal, and explains with Ben Zoma why wine during the meal gets individual blessings. From the blessing on incense it moves to a catalogue of blessings over scents, the verse that grounds them, and a cluster of Rav Zutra bar Toviya's teachings: torches and demons, and the famous rule that one should rather enter a furnace than shame another in public. It closes with the disputes over oil, myrtle, and wine, and the six things that disgrace a Torah scholar.

🍷 Does Wine Need Reclining? Rav vs. Rabbi Yoḥanan

The mishna: 'If they reclined, one blesses for all.' Is that only for bread?
Rav
The mishna speaks only of bread, which requires reclining for one to bless on behalf of all. Wine does not require reclining.
R' Yoḥanan
Even wine requires reclining.
Rav (v. 2)
Some say Rav meant: reclining is effective only for bread. For wine, reclining does not help; each drinker blesses for himself.
R' Yoḥanan
Even for wine, reclining is effective.
Objection
The Tosefta: before the meal the guests drink wine and each blesses for himself; once reclined, wine comes and one blesses for all. Version 1 of Rav is contradicted by the first clause; version 2 by the last.
Answer
Version 1: guests in the hall are different, since their mind is on leaving (דְּדַעְתַּיְיהוּ לְמִיעְקַר). Version 2: once reclining works for the bread, it works for the wine too (מִגּוֹ).
Backdrop: The daf begins with Rav's students tearing their garments a second time: 'Rav is dead, and we have not learned the halakhot of Grace after Meals.' An elder resolved their question: once diners say 'let us go and eat in such-and-such a place', it is as if they reclined.

🍽️ The Order of a Formal Meal (Tosefta)

Two rooms, two washings, two rounds of wine
1️⃣ Guests enter and sit on benches and chairs (קָתֶדְרָאוֹת) until everyone has arrived
↓
2️⃣ Water is brought; each washes one hand, to hold the cup
↓
3️⃣ Wine is served; each blesses for himself
↓
4️⃣ They go up and recline; water is brought again and each washes both hands
↓
5️⃣ Wine during the meal: one blesses for all (Tosefta) — though our mishna says each blesses for himself during the meal
↓
6️⃣ Ben Zoma explains the mishna: during the meal each blesses for himself because אֵין בֵּית הַבְּלִיעָה פָּנוּי — the throat is not free; after the meal, one blesses for all
And the incense: The one who blessed on the wine also blesses on the incense (mugmar) after the meal. Why him? Because he washed his hands first after the meal. This supports Rav: whoever washes first at the end is designated to lead Grace. Hence Rabbi told Rav 'stand and wash your hands' — and Rabbi Ḥiyya reassured the trembling Rav: 'Son of noblemen, he is telling you to review Grace after Meals.'

🌿 Which Blessing for Which Scent?

Rava bar Yirmeya: bless incense when its column of smoke rises, just as HaMotzi is said before eating, when one intends to smell

Why bless a smell at all? Rav Zutra bar Toviya in Rav's name: 'Let every soul praise the Lord' (Psalms 150:6). What does the soul enjoy but not the body? Scent. Rav also taught that the young men of Israel will one day give off fragrance like Lebanon (Hosea 14:7).

🔥 Oil, Myrtle, and Wine: Which Comes First?

Two baraitot on the order of blessings, and Rabban Gamliel's tie-break

Beit Shammai

  • Oil and myrtle: bless the oil first, then the myrtle
  • Oil and wine: hold oil in the right hand, wine in the left; bless oil, then wine

Beit Hillel

  • Oil and myrtle: bless the myrtle first, then the oil
  • Oil and wine: wine in the right hand, oil in the left; bless wine, then oil
  • Then wipe the oil on the servant's head — or on the wall if the servant is a Torah scholar, since it is disgraceful for a scholar to go out perfumed

Rabban Gamliel decides

  • 'I will decide': oil first
  • Oil — we enjoy its scent and its anointing; myrtle — scent only
  • Rabbi Yoḥanan: הֲלָכָה כְּדִבְרֵי הַמַּכְרִיעַ, the halakha follows the decisor
Rav Pappa's dodge: At Rav Huna son of Rav Ika's house, Rav Pappa blessed the myrtle first. Challenged, he claimed Rava ruled like Beit Hillel. The Gemara says it was not so; he said it only to extricate himself. Fittingly, this amud also teaches: better to throw oneself into a fiery furnace than to shame another in public — learned from Tamar, who did not expose Judah (Genesis 38:25).

🧑‍🎓 Six Things That Disgrace a Torah Scholar

A baraita, with the Gemara's limits on each
ConductThe Gemara's qualification
Going out perfumed to the marketOnly where people are suspected of homosexuality (Rabbi Yoḥanan); only on clothing, not on the body, since sweat removes it (Rav Sheshet); hair is like clothing — or like the body (Rav Pappa)
Going out alone at nightBecause of suspicion — but not if he has a set time to study, since everyone knows where he is going
Wearing patched shoesOnly a patch on a patch, on the upper, on the road, in summer (Mar Zutra son of Rav Naḥman, explaining why Rabbi Ḥiyya bar Abba himself did so)
Conversing with a woman in the marketEven his wife, daughter, or sister — not everyone knows his relatives (Rav Ḥisda)
Reclining at a meal with ignoramusesLest he be drawn after them
Entering the study hall lastThey will call him negligent (פּוֹשֵׁעַ)
Some say: long stridesA long stride takes 1/500 of one's eyesight; remedy: the kiddush wine of Shabbat eve
Some say: walking fully uprightEven four cubits — as if pushing away the feet of the Shekhina, 'the whole earth is full of His glory' (Isaiah 6:3)
Also from Rav: A torch on a dark road is like two people (including the bearer), moonlight like three — for a demon is seen and harms one, is seen but harmless to two, and is not seen at all by three.
