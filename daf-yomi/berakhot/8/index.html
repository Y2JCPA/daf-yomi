📖 Overview: The Synagogue, the Study Hall, and the Torah Portion

Berakhot 8a–8b
אֵימָתַי עֵת רָצוֹן — בְּשָׁעָה שֶׁהַצִּבּוּר מִתְפַּלְּלִין
The scene: The daf opens by defining the 'time of favor' in Psalms 69:14 as the hour when the congregation prays — God never despises the prayer of the many. Reish Lakish calls one who has a synagogue and does not enter it an 'evil neighbor', and the long lives of Babylonia's elders are credited to arriving early and leaving late. A playful chain of interpretations reads 'the time of finding' as a wife, Torah, death, burial, or a lavatory. Rav Ḥisda's teachings on the synagogue lead to Ulla's famous line that since the Temple's destruction God has only the four cubits of halakha, which changed where Abaye prayed. The amud ends with the obligation to complete the weekly portion with the congregation. Amud bet continues with Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi's and Rava's advice to their sons, the caveats on passing behind a synagogue, praise of the Medes and Persians, and finally a return to the mishna: the halakha follows Rabban Gamliel, and Rabbi Shimon ben Yoḥai's case of reciting Shema twice around dawn.

🕍 Why Pray With the Congregation?

The prayer of the many is never despised
❓ 'Let my prayer be unto You, Lord, in a time of favor' (Psalms 69:14) — when is a עֵת רָצוֹן?
↓
✅ When the congregation is praying. Rabbi Yosei son of Rabbi Ḥanina: 'In a time of acceptance I have answered you' (Isaiah 49:8). Rabbi Aḥa: 'God is mighty, He despises no one' (Job 36:5) + 'for there were many with me' (Psalms 55:19)
↓
📜 Rabbi Natan's baraita: God says — whoever engages in Torah, acts of kindness, and prays with the congregation, I credit him as if he redeemed Me and My children from among the nations
↓
Reish Lakish
One with a synagogue in his city who does not enter to pray is an 'evil neighbor' (Jeremiah 12:14) and causes exile for himself and his children
R' Yoḥanan
Puzzled that there are elders in Babylonia, since long life is promised 'upon the land' — until told they go early and late to the synagogue: that is what helped them
↓
🏃 Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi to his sons: 'Go early and go late and enter the synagogue, so that your lives will be extended' — 'watching daily at My gates… whoso finds Me finds life' (Proverbs 8:34–35)
Rav Ḥisda: A person should always enter 'two doorways' into the synagogue. Not literally — rather, walk in the distance of two doorways and only then pray.

🔍 What Is 'The Time of Finding'?

'For this, let every pious man pray to You in the time of finding' (Psalms 32:6) — six readings
Sage'The time of finding' is…Proof text
Rabbi ḤaninaA wife'He who finds a wife finds good' (Proverbs 18:22) — in Eretz Yisrael they asked a groom: matza or motzeh ('more bitter than death', Ecclesiastes 7:26)?
Rabbi NatanTorah'He who finds Me finds life' (Proverbs 8:35)
Rav Naḥman bar YitzḥakDeath — pray for an easy one'Issues [totzaot] of death' (Psalms 68:21); 903 kinds of death — the hardest is croup, the easiest the kiss, like drawing a hair from milk
Rabbi YoḥananBurial'Glad when they can find a grave' (Job 3:22); Rabba bar Rav Sheila: pray for mercy until the last shovelful
Mar ZutraA lavatoryIn the West they said: this explanation is the best of all
Method: Each sage anchors 'finding' (מְצֹא) to another verse with the same root, showing how one word can hold a whole range of human needs worth praying for.

📏 Four Cubits of Halakha

Rava asks Rafram bar Pappa for Rav Ḥisda's teachings on the synagogue
Rav Ḥisda
'The Lord loves the gates of Zion more than all the dwellings of Jacob' (Psalms 87:2) — He loves gates distinguished (הַמְצוּיָּינִים) by halakha more than synagogues and study halls.
Ulla
Since the day the Temple was destroyed, the Holy One has nothing in His world but the אַרְבַּע אַמּוֹת שֶׁל הֲלָכָה, the four cubits of halakha alone.
Abaye
At first I studied at home and prayed in the synagogue. Once I heard Ulla's teaching, I pray only where I study.
Gemara
Rabbi Ami and Rabbi Asi had thirteen synagogues in Tiberias, yet prayed only between the pillars where they studied.
Ulla
One who benefits from his own labor is greater than one who merely fears Heaven: of the God-fearing it says only 'happy is the man' (Psalms 112:1); of the laborer, 'you are happy and it is good for you' (Psalms 128:2) — happy in this world, good in the World-to-Come.
Ulla
One should always live where his teacher lives — as long as Shimi ben Gera lived, Solomon did not marry Pharaoh's daughter. The baraita saying not to live near one's teacher speaks of a student who will not submit to him.
Leaving during Torah reading: 'They who forsake the Lord will perish' (Isaiah 1:28) — this is one who leaves while the Torah scroll is out. Rabbi Abbahu would leave between readers; Rav Pappa asked about between verses, and the question stands (teiku). Rav Sheshet turned away and studied: 'we are engaged in ours, they in theirs'.

📜 Complete the Portion With the Congregation

Rabbi Ami's rule and the story of Rav Beivai bar Abaye
1️⃣ Rav Huna bar Yehuda in the name of Rabbi Ami: always complete your portions with the congregation — שְׁנַיִם מִקְרָא וְאֶחָד תַּרְגּוּם, twice the text and once the translation
↓
2️⃣ Even a verse like 'Atarot and Divon' (Numbers 32:3), which is only place names — and the reward is that one's days and years are extended
↓
3️⃣ Rav Beivai bar Abaye planned to catch up on the whole year's portions on the eve of Yom Kippur
↓
4️⃣ Ḥiyya bar Rav of Difti stopped him: 'You shall afflict your souls on the ninth' (Leviticus 23:32) — but the fast is on the tenth! Rather, one who eats and drinks on the ninth is credited as if he fasted on both the ninth and the tenth
↓
5️⃣ He then thought to read the portions early; an elder taught him: neither earlier nor later than the congregation
↓
6️⃣ Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi to his sons: complete your portions with the congregation; be careful with the jugular veins in slaughter like Rabbi Yehuda; and respect an elder who forgot his learning through no fault of his own — the tablets and the broken tablets both rest in the Ark
Rava to his sons: Do not cut meat on your hand (danger, or spoiling the meal); do not sit on the bed of an Aramean woman (read as: do not sleep without Shema, do not marry a convert, or literally — after Rav Pappa found a dead baby under such a bed); and do not pass behind a synagogue while the congregation prays.

🌅 Back to the Mishna: Rabban Gamliel and Shema Around Dawn

Plus Abaye's caveats and what Rabbi Akiva and Rabban Gamliel admired in other nations

Halakha follows Rabban Gamliel

  • Rav Yehuda in the name of Shmuel: evening Shema may be recited until dawn
  • Rabbi Shimon ben Yoḥai: sometimes one recites Shema twice 'at night' — just before and just after dawn — fulfilling both the night and day obligations
  • Contradiction: is after dawn night or day? Answer: it is still night, but called 'day' because some people rise then
  • Rav Aḥa bar Ḥanina in the name of Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi: the halakha follows Rabbi Shimon ben Yoḥai

Passing behind a synagogue

  • Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi: forbidden while the congregation prays — it looks like contempt
  • Abaye's exceptions: another entrance; another synagogue in town
  • Also permitted if carrying a burden, running, or wearing tefillin — no one will suspect him

Learning from other nations

  • Rabbi Akiva likes the Medes for three things: cutting meat only on the table, kissing only the back of the hand, taking counsel only in the field (Jacob called Rachel and Leah to the field, Genesis 31:4)
  • Rabban Gamliel likes the Persians for three: modesty in eating, in the lavatory, and in intimate matters
  • Yet Rav Yosef: 'My consecrated ones' (Isaiah 13:3) — the Persians consecrated and designated for Gehenna
Second version: Some attach Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi's ruling to a different baraita of Rabbi Shimon ben Yoḥai in the name of Rabbi Akiva: sometimes one recites Shema twice 'in the day', just before and just after sunrise, fulfilling both the night and day obligations — with the same internal contradiction about whether the time before sunrise is day or night.
