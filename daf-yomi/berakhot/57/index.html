📖 Overview: The Dream Dictionary and the Five Blessings on Babylonia

Berakhot 57a–57b
חֲלוֹם — אֶחָד מִשִּׁשִּׁים לַנְּבוּאָה
The scene: The first amud is a dictionary of dream images, each keyed to a verse or a wordplay: tefillin promise greatness, barley means sins are removed, a goose means wisdom, a snake means livelihood, a marsh means a yeshiva. Sages report their own dreams — Rabbi Zeira would not leave Babylonia until he saw barley; Rav Ashi dreamed of a drum in a marsh and became head of the yeshiva. The second amud sorts figures and books into triads (piety, wisdom, calamity), lists things that are one-sixtieth of their ideal, and gives medical lists for the sick. Finally, the Gemara returns to the mishna's blessings on sights: on Mercury, on a place cleared of idolatry, and Rav Hamnuna's five blessings for one who sees wicked Babylonia.

🌾 The Dream Dictionary: Image, Meaning, Source

How the Gemara reads produce, animals, and actions through verses and wordplay
If you see…It means…Because…
Tefillin on your headExpect greatness'All the peoples shall see that the name of the Lord is called upon you' (Deut. 28:10) — Rabbi Eliezer the Great: the head tefillin
Wheat / barleyPeace / your sins are removed'Fat of wheat' with 'peace' (Ps. 147:14); se'orim = sar avon (Isa. 6:7). Rabbi Zeira waited for barley before leaving Babylonia
A laden vine / a fig / pomegranatesYour wife will not miscarry / your Torah is preserved / business grows like seedsPs. 128:3; Prov. 27:18; slices mean Torah for a scholar, mitzvot for an am ha'aretz (Song 4:3, 8:2)
Olives / olive oil / datesDurable business (trees: many children) / light of Torah / your sins have endedPs. 128:3; Ex. 27:20; temarim as tam — 'your iniquity is finished' (Lam. 4:22)
Etrog / lulav / myrtleHonored (hadur) before your Creator / one heart for your Father / property prospersLev. 23:40; lulav as lo lev; Ulla: myrtle only on its stem
Goose / rooster / eggsWisdom (relations with it: head of yeshiva — Rav Ashi's own dream) / a son / a pending request; broken: granted'Wisdoms cry aloud in the streets' (Prov. 1:20); beya = request; also nuts, cucumbers, glass
A snakeLivelihood ready; bit you: doubled; killed it: lostRav Sheshet said killing it doubles it — but only because he had killed a snake in his own dream

🏞️ Marsh, Forest, and Drum: Which Dream Makes a Rosh Yeshiva?

Rav Pappa and Rav Huna son of Rav Yehoshua each dreamed, and each was appointed
🌿 Baraita: one who enters a marsh (agam) becomes head of a yeshiva — reeds large and small surround him like students. One who enters a forest becomes head of the advanced students (benei kalla) — only great trees around him
↓
Version 1
Rav Pappa dreamed of a marsh and became rosh yeshiva; Rav Huna son of Rav Yehoshua dreamed of a forest and became head of the kalla
Version 2
Both entered a marsh; Rav Pappa also had a drum hanging from his neck — the voice sounded in public — and so he became rosh yeshiva
↓
🥁 Rav Ashi: I entered a marsh, hung a drum, and beat it — and he became head of the yeshiva
↓
🏠 Other ascents: climbing to a roof means greatness; Abaye and Rava say once he went up, he went up, even if he came down. Entering a city: desires fulfilled (Ps. 107:30). A small boat riding high: a good name; a large boat: for the whole family
↓
🔓 Tearing clothes: the sentence is torn. Naked in Babylonia: free of sin; naked in Eretz Yisrael: bare of mitzvot. Seized by a soldier: heavenly protection; a neck-chain (kolar) adds more — but a mere rope does not
Bloodletting: The tanna before Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak taught it means sins are forgiven; the baraita saying sins are 'arranged' (סְדוּרִין) means arranged to be forgiven.

📚 Triads: Piety, Wisdom, or Calamity

The Sages sort kings, prophets, books, and scholars into three outcomes

Expect Piety (חֲסִידוּת)

  • King David
  • Book of Psalms
  • Song of Songs
  • Ben Azzai

Expect Wisdom (חׇכְמָה)

  • King Solomon
  • Ezekiel (the Chariot); Proverbs; Ecclesiastes
  • Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi
  • Ben Zoma

Fear Calamity (פּוּרְעָנוּת)

  • King Ahab
  • Jeremiah; Job; Lamentations
  • Rabbi Yishmael ben Elisha, one of the ten martyrs
  • Aḥer — Elisha ben Avuya
Outside the pattern: The book of Kings means greatness, Isaiah means consolation, Esther means a miracle, and Rabbi Elazar ben Azarya means wealth. Rabbi Yoḥanan adds: a verse that falls into one's mouth on waking is a minor prophecy. Seeing Huna hints at a miracle (nes — one nun); Ḥanina, Ḥananya, Yoḥanan: miracles upon miracles (two nuns).

🔢 Five, Six, and Ten: The Mnemonic Lists

The Gemara moves from dreams to the body, the sick, and what is one-sixtieth of its ideal
1️⃣ Exceptions: all animals are good except elephant, monkey, and long-tailed ape (an elephant is good when saddled); all metals except hoe, chisel, axe on their handles; all fruit except unripe dates; all vegetables except turnip heads — Rav grew rich after seeing them, but on their stems; all colors except tekhelet; all birds except night-birds
↓
2️⃣ Three enter the body without benefit: cherries, bad dates, unripe dates. Three benefit without entering: washing, anointing, tashmish. Three taste of the World-to-Come: Shabbat, sun, tashmish — meaning relieving oneself, since relations weaken the body
↓
3️⃣ Three ease the mind: voice, sight, smell. Three broaden it: a beautiful home, wife, and vessels
↓
4️⃣ Five are one-sixtieth: fire of Gehenna, honey of manna, Shabbat of the World-to-Come, sleep of death, and a dream of prophecy
↓
5️⃣ Six good signs for the sick: sneezing (Job 41:10), sweat (Gen. 3:19), diarrhea (Isa. 51:14), seminal emission (Isa. 53:10), sleep (Job 3:13), a dream (Isa. 38:16)
↓
6️⃣ Six cures: cabbage, beets, dried foley, stomach, placenta, diaphragm; some add small fish. Ten cause relapse: ox meat, fat, roast, poultry, roasted egg, shaving, cress, milk, cheese, bathhouse; some add nuts and cucumbers — large ones are 'as hard as swords', small ones graced the tables of Rabbi and Antoninus
A corpse in the house: Peace for the house; if it ate and drank, a good sign; if it took vessels, a bad sign — Rav Pappa: only a shoe or sandal. Whatever a corpse takes is fine except shoe and sandal; whatever it gives is fine except dust and mustard.

🏛️ Back to the Mishna: Blessings on Idols and on Babylonia

The Tosefta on uprooted idolatry, and Rav Hamnuna's five blessings
Tosefta
One who sees Mercury (Markulis) says: Blessed… Who has shown patience to those who violate His will. One who sees a place cleared of idolatry says: Blessed… Who uprooted idolatry from our land — and may it be uprooted from all places of Israel, and turn the hearts of their worshippers to serve You.
Tosefta
Outside the Land one need not say 'turn the hearts of their worshippers', since most inhabitants are gentiles.
R' Shimon b. Elazar
Even outside the Land one must say it, for they are destined to convert: 'Then I will turn to the peoples a pure language' (Zephaniah 3:9).
Rav Hamnuna
One who sees wicked Babylonia must recite five blessings: on its ruins, Who destroyed wicked Babylonia; on Nebuchadnezzar's house, Who destroyed his house; on the lions' den or the furnace, Who did miracles for our fathers in this place; on Mercury, Who shows patience; on a place from which earth is taken, Who speaks and acts, decrees and fulfills.
Rava
Seeing donkeys carrying earth from Babylonia, he slapped their backs: 'Run, righteous ones, to do the will of your Master!'
Mar b. Ravina
On reaching Babylonia he took earth in his kerchief and threw it outside — 'I will sweep it with the broom of destruction' (Isaiah 14:23).
Rav Ashi
I never heard Rav Hamnuna's teaching, but on my own reasoning I recited all of them.
Turning point: The chapter has been about dreams since 55a; here it returns to the mishna's list of sights that call for a blessing, which continues on the next daf.
