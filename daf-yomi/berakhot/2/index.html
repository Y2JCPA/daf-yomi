📖 Overview: The First Mishna in Shas

Berakhot 2a–2b
מֵאֵימָתַי קוֹרִין אֶת שְׁמַע בָּעֲרָבִין? מִשָּׁעָה שֶׁהַכֹּהֲנִים נִכְנָסִים לֶאֱכוֹל בִּתְרוּמָתָן
The scene: The Talmud opens with Shema because reciting it is the daily acceptance of the yoke of Heaven. The mishna asks from when the evening Shema may be said and answers with an unusual sign: the moment priests who were impure may eat their teruma. The Gemara then asks why the mishna assumes we already know there is an obligation, why evening precedes morning, and what exactly that priestly sign means. Along the way it untangles four separate baraitot that each define nightfall differently.

⏰ Until When? Three Opinions in the Mishna

The mishna gives one starting point but three end points
TannaLatest time for evening ShemaReasoning
Rabbi EliezerEnd of the first watch'When you lie down' = the time people go to bed
The RabbisMidnight (חצות)A rabbinic fence to keep people from delaying
Rabban GamlielDawn (עלות השחר)'When you lie down' = the whole time people are asleep
Rabban Gamliel's sons: They came home from a wedding after midnight and had not recited Shema. Their father ruled: if dawn has not come, you are still obligated. He explained that even the Rabbis agree by Torah law; 'until midnight' is only a safeguard, just like burning the fats of offerings and eating sacrifices, which are Torah-permitted all night but restricted by the Sages to midnight.

🤔 Why Does the Tanna Start With 'From When'?

The Gemara's two opening questions on the very first mishna
Gemara
On what basis does the tanna ask from when? He speaks as if the obligation is already known, but this is the first mishna in all of Shas!
Gemara
And why teach the evening Shema first? Most mitzvot are daytime mitzvot. Teach the morning first!
Answer
Both questions have one answer. The tanna stands on the verse: בְּשׇׁכְבְּךָ וּבְקוּמֶךָ, 'when you lie down and when you arise' (Deut. 6:7). The Torah itself states the obligation and puts lying down before rising.
Alt.
If you wish, say instead that he learns it from Creation: וַיְהִי עֶרֶב וַיְהִי בֹקֶר, 'there was evening and there was morning, one day'. A day begins at night.
Gemara
Then why does the later mishna list the morning blessings (two before, one after) before the evening ones (two before, two after)?
Answer
Having begun with evening and then reached morning, the tanna finished explaining morning while he was on it, and only then returned to the evening blessings.
Big idea: The Oral Torah opens by mirroring the order of the Written Torah — evening Shema first.

⭐ Why 'When Priests Eat Teruma' Instead of 'When the Stars Come Out'?

The mishna's roundabout sign teaches a second halakha in passing
❓ Priests eat teruma at the emergence of the stars. Why not just say 'from the emergence of the stars'?
↓
💡 The phrasing teaches something extra: an impure priest who immersed may eat teruma once the stars emerge, even before bringing his atonement offering
↓
📜 Baraita on Leviticus 22:7 — 'And the sun sets and it is purified (וְטָהֵר), and afterward he may eat of the holy things'
↓
Reading A
The day is purified: sunset is complete, the sky clears, stars emerge. Only nightfall is required.
Reading B
The person is purified: 'sunset' means the start of sunset, and the priest must also bring his offering.
↓
✅ Rabba bar Rav Sheila: had the Torah meant the person, it would say 'and he shall be purified' (וְיִטְהַר). 'The day is purified' — as people say, 'the sun has set and the day has cleared'. The Sages of Eretz Yisrael reached the same conclusion from our baraita.
Result: Sunset in the verse means complete nightfall, and the atonement offering is not a prerequisite for eating teruma.

🍞 Four Baraitot, Many Signs of Nightfall

The Gemara reconciles seemingly contradictory descriptions of when evening Shema begins

Priest's Teruma

  • Our mishna, Rabbi Yehoshua, the Rabbis of the Tosefta
  • Sign: emergence of the stars
  • Proof from Nehemiah 4:15–16: work went 'from dawn until the stars came out', and that span is called 'day'

Poor Man's Meal

  • Baraita: 'from when a poor person enters to eat bread with salt'
  • Rabbi Ḥanina's opinion
  • Conclusion: this is a separate, later time than the priest's — otherwise Rabbi Ḥanina would equal Rabbi Yehoshua

Other Opinions

  • Rabbi Meir (Tosefta): when people eat their Shabbat-eve meal
  • Rabbi Eliezer: when the day becomes sanctified on Shabbat eve (sunset)
  • Rabbi Meir (baraita): when priests immerse for teruma
  • Rabbi Aḥai: when most people recline for a weekday meal
Method: Whenever two named opinions would collapse into one, the Gemara concludes they must describe different times. That is how it proves the poor man's time is distinct from, and later than, the priest's.

🌗 Rabbi Yehuda vs. Rabbi Meir: Can Immersion Time Be 'Night'?

The dispute turns on how long twilight (bein hashemashot) lasts
1️⃣ Rabbi Meir: evening Shema may begin from when the priests immerse in order to eat teruma
↓
2️⃣ Rabbi Yehuda objects: priests immerse while it is still day. How can that be the time of 'lying down'?
↓
3️⃣ Background: Rabbi Yehuda holds twilight lasts a long while after sunset; Rabbi Yosei holds it is like the blink of an eye just before the stars emerge
↓
4️⃣ Rabbi Meir replies: I am not using your twilight. I follow Rabbi Yosei — priests immerse in that instant before the stars, which already counts as night
Takeaway: The halakhic definition of 'night' is not one fixed moment; several tannaitic clocks are running, and the Gemara maps each opinion onto its clock.
