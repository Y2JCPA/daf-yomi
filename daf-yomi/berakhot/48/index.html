📖 Overview: The Four Blessings and Their Sources

Berakhot 48a–48b
וְאָכַלְתָּ וְשָׂבָעְתָּ וּבֵרַכְתָּ אֶת ה׳ אֱלֹהֶיךָ עַל הָאָרֶץ הַטּוֹבָה אֲשֶׁר נָתַן לָךְ
The scene: The daf finishes the question of who may be counted in a zimmun: a minor who knows to Whom we bless, and a majority who ate grain. A story about King Yannai and Shimon ben Shataḥ raises whether one who ate no bread may recite Grace for others — Rabbi Yoḥanan says no, an olive-bulk of grain is required. The Gemara then turns to Grace after Meals itself: Rav Naḥman traces each of its four blessings to Moses, Joshua, David and Solomon, and the Sages at Yavne, and a Tosefta reads all of them out of a single verse, Deuteronomy 8:10. The same verse is mined for the blessing before eating, the blessing over the Torah, and the blessing over misfortune.

🧒 When Does a Child Count for a Zimmun?

From 'two hairs' to a single practical test
1️⃣ The baraita says a minor who grew two hairs is counted, one who did not is not — and then adds 'one is not exacting with regard to a minor'. What does that clause add?
↓
2️⃣ Answer: it includes a mature minor — one showing signs of puberty is counted, and we do not check whether he has actually reached majority
↓
3️⃣ The Gemara rejects all of these statements: וְלֵית הִלְכְתָא כְּכׇל הָנֵי שְׁמַעְתָּתָא
↓
4️⃣ The halakha follows Rav Naḥman: קָטָן הַיּוֹדֵעַ לְמִי מְבָרְכִין — מְזַמְּנִין עָלָיו, a minor who knows to Whom one recites a blessing is included
Abaye and Rava as boys: Rabba asked them: to Whom do we bless? 'To the All-Merciful.' And where does He reside? Rava pointed to the ceiling; Abaye went outside and pointed to the sky. Rabba said: you will both be Sages — as people say, a cucumber is recognized from its blossoming stage.

🥬 Nine Ate Grain, One Ate Vegetables

How large a grain-eating majority does a zimmun of ten need?
Rav
If nine ate grain and one ate vegetables, they join to form a zimmun of ten. (Reported by Rav Yehuda, son of Rav Shmuel bar Sheilat.)
R' Zeira
I asked Rav Yehuda: what if eight ate grain and two ate vegetables? What if seven?
Rav Yehuda
There is no difference — they still join.
R' Zeira
Six I did not even ask about; it was clear to me that six grain-eaters are insufficient.
R' Yirmeya
You did well not to ask — but for the opposite reason! Seven or eight work because they are a majority. Six is also a majority.
Gemara
Rabbi Zeira held that we require an obvious majority (רוּבָּא דְמִינְּכַר), which six out of ten is not.
The point of dispute: Everyone agrees a majority of grain-eaters is needed; Rabbi Zeira and Rabbi Yirmeya disagree only whether a bare majority of six suffices.

👑 Shimon ben Shataḥ at King Yannai's Table

Can someone who ate nothing recite Grace for others?
🍞 King Yannai and the queen ate bread. Since Yannai had executed the Sages, no one was left to recite the blessing for them.
↓
🤝 The queen made the king swear not to harass her guest and brought her brother Shimon ben Shataḥ, seating him between the two thrones. To the king's 'see how I honor you', he replied: it is the Torah that honors me — 'Extol her and she will exalt you' (Prov. 4:8).
↓
🍷 Given the cup: 'How shall I bless — Blessed is He from Whom Yannai and his companions have eaten?' He drank that cup, was given another, and recited Grace.
↓
Rabbi Yoḥanan
Shimon ben Shataḥ acted on his own. One cannot discharge others' obligation until he eats an olive-bulk of grain.
Objection: Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel
One who reclined with them and merely dipped in brine or ate a single dry fig joins them!
↓
✅ Resolution and halakha (Rava, via Rav Ḥana bar Yehuda): he joins for the count — even with a vegetable leaf and a cup of wine — but to discharge the many he must eat an olive-bulk of grain.
Two different thresholds: Joining a zimmun requires only a token participation in the meal; leading it for others requires real bread.

Shabbat in the third blessing: The baraita: on Shabbat one begins and ends the third blessing with consolation and mentions the sanctity of the day in the middle. Rabbi Eliezer allows placing it in the blessing of the land or the Yavne blessing as well; the Rabbis say only in the consolation blessing. The difference between the Rabbis and the first tanna is after the fact — the first tanna holds one who mentioned it elsewhere has fulfilled his obligation.

📖 One Verse, Many Blessings

The Sages extend Deuteronomy 8:10 beyond Grace after Meals

Blessing before eating

  • Tosefta: a fortiori — if one blesses when satisfied, all the more so when hungry
  • Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi: 'that He gave you' — from the moment He gave it
  • Rabbi Yitzḥak: 'And He will bless your bread' (Ex. 23:25) — read 'and you will bless'; it is called 'bread' before it is eaten
  • Rabbi Natan: the maidens told Saul the people would not eat until Samuel 'blesses the sacrifice' (I Sam. 9:13)

Blessing over the Torah

  • Rabbi Yishmael: a fortiori — one blesses over temporal life, all the more so over eternal life
  • Rabbi Ḥiyya bar Naḥmani: 'the good land that He gave [natan] you' paired with 'I will give [ve'etna] you the tablets and the Torah' (Ex. 24:12)
  • Rabbi Yehuda ben Beteira: 'tova' is Torah — 'I have given you a good teaching' (Prov. 4:2); 'hatova' is the building of Jerusalem

Blessing over the bad, and required themes

  • Rabbi Meir: 'that the Lord your God gave you' — your Judge, in whatever judgment He judges you, goodness or calamity
  • Rabbi Eliezer: one must say 'a desirable, good, and spacious land' and mention the house of David, or he has not fulfilled his obligation
  • Naḥum the Elder: mention the covenant; Rabbi Yosei: mention the Torah
  • Pelimu: covenant before Torah, for the Torah was given with three covenants
Why did the maidens talk so long to Saul? Because women are chatterers; Shmuel: to gaze longer at Saul's beauty, 'taller than the people from the shoulders up'; Rabbi Yoḥanan: because one sovereignty does not overlap its fellow even a hairbreadth — Samuel's leadership was not to be cut short.
