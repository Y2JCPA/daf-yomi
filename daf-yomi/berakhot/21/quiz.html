Berakhot 21 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
Why does the Gemara conclude that a ba'al keri may contemplate Shema and recite Grace after Meals, but need not begin the Amida?
A. Because prayer lacks the acceptance of the yoke of Heaven
B. Because Shema and Grace after Meals are Torah law, while prayer is rabbinic
C. Because prayer is too long to recite in a state of impurity
D. Because the community is not engaged in prayer
Correct: B — The 'yoke of Heaven' answer failed because Grace after Meals also lacks it; the Gemara settles on the Torah/rabbinic distinction.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
Why is Rabbi Yoḥanan's kal vaḥomer deriving a Torah-law blessing before food refuted?
A. Because Torah study gives eternal life, which food does not, and the mishna says the ba'al keri does not bless before food
B. Because the blessing before food was instituted by Ezra
C. Because food is eaten more often than Torah is studied
D. Because Rav Yehuda's verse contradicts it
Correct: A — Torah is special in providing eternal life, so its before-blessing cannot be transferred to food; and the mishna's ruling that the ba'al keri blesses after but not before food shows the before-food blessing is only rabbinic.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to Rav Yehuda, one who is unsure whether he recited Shema does not repeat it, but one unsure about emet veyatziv does. Why?
A. Emet veyatziv is shorter
B. Shema is rabbinic but emet veyatziv, commemorating the Exodus, is Torah law
C. Emet veyatziv contains the acceptance of the yoke of Heaven
D. Shema may only be recited once per day
Correct: B — Rav Yehuda holds that 'when you lie down and rise' refers to Torah study generally, so Shema is rabbinic and doubt is treated leniently, while remembering the Exodus is a Torah obligation treated stringently.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
What is the underlying dispute between Rav Huna (finish before Modim) and Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi (finish before Kedusha)?
A. Whether Modim or Kedusha is more important
B. Whether a latecomer must pray with the congregation at all
C. Whether an individual may recite kedusha on his own
D. Whether the prayer leader must repeat the Amida
Correct: C — Rav Huna holds an individual may say kedusha alone, so he need only reach Modim with the congregation; Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi holds kedusha requires the congregation, so he must finish before it.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
How does the Gemara explain that Rabbi Yehuda does expound juxtaposed verses in Deuteronomy, yet does not derive the ba'al keri prohibition from 'the day you stood at Horeb'?
A. He rejects the authority of Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi
B. He uses that juxtaposition to teach that one who teaches his son Torah is as if he received it at Horeb
C. He holds a ba'al keri is never prohibited from Torah
D. He derives the prohibition from the sorceress instead
Correct: B — Rabbi Yehuda needs the same adjacent verses for Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi's other teaching about teaching one's son, so they are unavailable for the ba'al keri derivation.
