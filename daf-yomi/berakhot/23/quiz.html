QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to Rav Ashi, what do Rav Ḥisda and Rav Hamnuna actually disagree about regarding one who needed to urinate mid-prayer?
A. Whether a delay long enough to finish the whole prayer sends him back to the beginning
B. Whether, when he did not delay that long, his prayer was valid at all — was he a fit person or a disqualified one?
C. Whether he may finish praying before relieving himself
D. Whether the rule applies to Shema as well as the Amida
Correct: B — Rav Ashi notes that the dispute never mentions the length of delay. Everyone agrees a long delay requires starting over; they argue whether, absent such a delay, the person was fit for prayer (prayer valid) or disqualified (prayer invalid).
QUESTION 2 OF 5
One who needs to relieve himself but can restrain himself may still pray. For how long must he be able to hold on?
A. Until he finishes the entire Amida
B. The time it takes to walk a parasang
C. Until midnight
D. The time it takes to walk four cubits
Correct: B — Rav Zevid (or Rav Yehuda) limits the baraita to one who cannot restrain himself, and Rav Sheshet (or Rav Zevid) sets the measure at the time it takes to walk a parasang.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
Why did Rava forbid entering a regular bathroom wearing tefillin even just to urinate?
A. Because Beit Shammai forbid bringing tefillin near a bathroom at all
B. Because urine splashes on the tefillin straps
C. Because we are concerned he will come to defecate, or break wind, while still wearing them
D. Because the tefillin would become impure in the bathroom
Correct: C — Ravina permitted and Rav Adda bar Mattana forbade; Rava ruled it forbidden lest he defecate in them, and some say lest he forget they are on his head and break wind in them.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
How does the Gemara finally explain 'matters which I permitted you here, I prohibited you there' with respect to tefillin?
A. Beit Shammai permit holding tefillin in a makeshift bathroom but not a regular one
B. In a regular bathroom one sits and there are no ricocheting drops, so holding tefillin is permitted; in a makeshift bathroom there are drops, so it is prohibited
C. It refers to baring one handbreadth behind and two in front
D. It refers to a man versus a woman
Correct: B — After the intermediate suggestions about handbreadths are rejected, the Gemara concludes the baraita is about tefillin and Rava's attribution to Beit Shammai is refuted. The distinction is the ricocheting drops in a makeshift bathroom, to be derived by reason rather than by kal vaḥomer.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to Abaye, who holds that designation is significant, when is it forbidden to bundle money in a cloth used for tefillin?
A. Only if the cloth was designated for tefillin and tefillin were already bundled in it
B. Only if tefillin were already bundled in it, regardless of designation
C. If the cloth was designated for tefillin, even if tefillin were never yet bundled in it
D. Never — money and tefillin may always be bundled together
Correct: C — Rav Ḥisda requires both designation and actual use. Abaye, for whom designation itself is significant, forbids as soon as the cloth was designated, while use without designation does not forbid.
