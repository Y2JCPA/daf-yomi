📖 Overview: Bodily Needs, Prayer, and Where Tefillin May Go

Berakhot 23a–23b
הַנִּצְרָךְ לִנְקָבָיו — אַל יִתְפַּלֵּל, וְאִם הִתְפַּלֵּל — תְּפִלָּתוֹ תּוֹעֵבָה
The scene: The daf opens by finishing a dispute about one who needed to urinate in the middle of the Amida: Rav Ashi explains that everyone agrees a long delay sends him back to the beginning, and the argument is only about whether his prayer was ever valid. From there the Gemara rules that one who needs to relieve himself may not pray at all, unless he can restrain himself for the time it takes to walk a parasang, and derives it from 'Prepare to greet your God, O Israel'. The rest of the daf is a practical guide to tefillin in undignified places: what to do with them at a regular or makeshift bathroom, the tragic incident that led the Sages to require holding them in one's hand, whether the baraita forbidding urination while wearing them follows Beit Shammai, and finally whether tefillin may be brought to a meal, bundled with coins, or placed under one's pillow.

🚻 May One Who Needs to Relieve Himself Pray?

The baraita, its qualification, and the verse behind it
❓ Rav Ḥisda and Rav Hamnuna disputed one who needed to urinate mid-prayer. Rav Ashi: if the issue were the length of the delay, they would have said so!
↓
💡 Rav Ashi: everyone agrees that a delay long enough to finish the whole prayer sends him back to the start. They argue only where he did not delay: is he גַּבְרָא דְחוּיָא (disqualified, prayer invalid) or גַּבְרָא חַזְיָא (fit, prayer valid)?
↓
📜 Baraita: one who needs to relieve himself may not pray, and if he prayed, his prayer is an abomination (תּוֹעֵבָה)
↓
Cannot restrain himself
The baraita applies: he may not pray (Rav Zevid, some say Rav Yehuda)
Can restrain himself
His prayer is valid. For how long? Rav Sheshet (or Rav Zevid): the time it takes to walk a parasang (עַד פַּרְסָה)
↓
✅ Rabbi Shmuel bar Naḥmani in the name of Rabbi Yonatan: the source is הִכּוֹן לִקְרַאת אֱלֹהֶיךָ יִשְׂרָאֵל, 'Prepare to greet your God, O Israel' (Amos 4:12)
Rav Ashi's one-liner: Rav Ashi (some say Rabbi Ḥanina bar Pappa) reads 'Guard your foot when you go to the house of God' (Ecclesiastes 4:17) as: שְׁמוֹר נְקָבֶיךָ בְּשָׁעָה שֶׁאַתָּה עוֹמֵד בִּתְפִלָּה לְפָנַי — mind your orifices when you stand before Me in prayer.

🐭 How the Enactment Evolved: Hold Them in Your Hand

Two baraitot tell the story, and the Amoraim fix the details
1️⃣ At first they placed tefillin in holes near the bathroom — mice came and took them
↓
2️⃣ They moved them to windows facing the public domain — passersby came and took them
↓
3️⃣ A student left his tefillin in such a hole; a prostitute took them and announced in the study hall, 'See what so-and-so gave me as my payment.' He went up to the roof, fell and died
↓
4️⃣ At that moment they instituted that one holds them in his garment and in his hand and enters
↓
5️⃣ Rabbi Meyasha son of Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi: roll them up like a scroll and hold them in the right hand opposite the heart; Rav Naḥman: no strap may hang more than a handbreadth below the hand
↓
6️⃣ Rabbi Zeira / Rabbi Yoḥanan: that is by day, when there is still time to don them; at night he makes a pouch of a handbreadth (כְּמִין כִּיס טֶפַח). Abaye: a handbreadth is needed only for their regular vessel — a different vessel may be smaller
Proof for Abaye: Mar Zutra (some say Rav Ashi): small sealed vessels of less than a handbreadth protect their contents even in a tent over a corpse — so a small space can serve as a barrier. And Rabbi Yoḥanan and Rav Naḥman would hand their students a book of aggada before entering the bathroom but kept their tefillin: 'since the Sages permitted it, they will protect me.'

💧 Is the Baraita Beit Shammai? 'What I Permitted Here I Prohibited There'

A baraita forbids urinating while holding tefillin — Rava says it is not the halakha
Baraita
One may not hold tefillin in his hand or a Torah scroll in his arm and pray; one may not urinate with them or sleep with them, neither a deep sleep nor a nap. Shmuel adds: a knife, money, a bowl or a loaf are the same — worry that they will fall distracts from prayer.
Rava
In the name of Rav Sheshet: the halakha does not follow this baraita, for it is Beit Shammai. If it were Beit Hillel — they permit holding tefillin in a regular bathroom to defecate; must it be said that one may hold them to urinate in a makeshift one?
Objection
The same baraita says, 'Matters which I permitted you here, I prohibited you there.' Is that not tefillin? For Beit Hillel it makes sense: permitted in the regular bathroom, prohibited in the makeshift one. But Beit Shammai permit nothing at all!
Defense
Perhaps 'here and there' refers to baring a handbreadth behind and two in front — one baraita for defecation, another for urination?
Rejection
For urination why bare anything behind? Rather both baraitot are about defecation: two handbreadths in front for a man, none for a woman. But then why call it 'a kal vaḥomer with no refutation'? That is simply how things are. So it must be tefillin — and Rava's statement is conclusively refuted (תְּיוּבְתָּא).
Gemara
Then how is the makeshift bathroom stricter? In a regular bathroom one sits and there are no ricocheting drops (נִיצוֹצוֹת), so holding tefillin is permitted; in a makeshift one there are drops he may touch, so it is prohibited. Derive it from this reason, not from a kal vaḥomer — because as a kal vaḥomer it could not be refuted.
Method: The Gemara ends up accepting the baraita as Beit Hillel: the leniency for the regular bathroom and the stringency for the makeshift one are both about keeping tefillin clean, not about which house of study one follows.

🛏️ Tefillin at Meals, With Money, and Under the Pillow

Three more practical questions close the daf

At a formal meal

  • Baraita: before a regular meal, pace four cubits ten times or ten cubits four times, relieve yourself, then enter
  • Rabbi Yitzḥak: remove tefillin before entering
  • Rabbi Ḥiyya disagrees: place them on the table — and that is admirable
  • Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak: he refrains until the time of the blessing after the meal

Bundled with coins

  • One baraita permits bundling tefillin with money in one's head covering; another forbids
  • Rav Ḥisda: forbidden only if the cloth was designated for tefillin and already used; designated only, or used only — permitted
  • Abaye, who holds designation is significant (הַזְמָנָה מִילְּתָא הִיא): designated alone is enough to forbid; used without designation — permitted

Under his head at night

  • Rav Yosef son of Rav Neḥunya asked Rav Yehuda: under the feet is obviously degrading — what about under the head?
  • Shmuel: permitted, even if his wife is with him
  • Baraita: under the head is fine, but if his wife is with him it is forbidden — unless the spot is three handbreadths higher or lower
  • A conclusive refutation of Shmuel — yet Rava rules like Shmuel anyway. Why? The daf ends on the question
Note: In two of these three cases the daf sides with dignity plus practicality: tefillin should be protected, but not treated as an ordinary object one bundles with cash or sleeps on.
