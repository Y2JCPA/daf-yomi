📖 Overview: Interruptions, Priorities, and Tefillin

Berakhot 14a–14b
כָּל הַקּוֹרֵא קְרִיאַת שְׁמַע בְּלֹא תְּפִילִּין, כְּאִילּוּ מֵעִיד עֵדוּת שֶׁקֶר בְּעַצְמוֹ
The scene: A baraita confirms the restored wording of Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yehuda on greetings during Shema, and the Gemara extends the question to Hallel and Megilla, then to tasting food on a fast day. Rav's striking teaching — that greeting a person before praying is like building an idolatrous altar — is qualified to one who goes early to another's door, and paired with the prohibition on doing one's business before prayer. Rabbi Yoḥanan rules like Rabbi Yehuda that 'your God' must run straight into 'True', but he and Rabba dispute whether Emet is repeated. The Eretz Yisrael custom of abbreviating the tzitzit portion at night is weighed against Rav's rule that one who begins it must finish it. Rabbi Shimon ben Yoḥai adds a second reason for the order of the portions, and the daf closes with Rav reciting Shema before donning tefillin — excused by a late messenger — and Ulla's warning that Shema without tefillin is false testimony.

🎵 May One Interrupt Hallel or the Megilla to Greet Someone?

Aḥai, the reciter of Rabbi Ḥiyya's academy, poses the dilemma
❓ Two directions: a fortiori — if Shema, a Torah obligation, may be interrupted, surely Hallel, which is rabbinic, may be! Or perhaps publicizing the miracle (פַּרְסוֹמֵי נִיסָּא) takes precedence and one may never interrupt
↓
✅ Rabbi Ḥiyya: one interrupts, and it is of no concern
↓
Rabba — full Hallel
On days when the individual completes the whole Hallel: interrupt between paragraphs, not in the middle of a paragraph
Rabba — partial Hallel
On days when the individual does not complete Hallel: interrupt even in the middle of a paragraph
↓
❓ But Rav bar Shaba came before Ravina on a partial-Hallel day and Ravina did not interrupt for him!
↓
💡 Rav bar Shaba is different — he was not considered important in Ravina's eyes
Same style of question: Ashyan, reciter of Rabbi Ami's academy, asked whether one who is fasting may taste food. Did he take on himself to avoid eating and drinking — which this is not — or to avoid enjoyment, which this is? Rabbi Ami: he tastes, and it is of no concern; a baraita agrees, and tasting requires no blessing. Rabbi Ami and Rabbi Asi tasted up to a quarter-log.

🛕 What Comes Before Prayer — and What May Not

Rav, Shmuel, Rabbi Zeira and Rav Yitzḥak bar Ashyan on the first act of the morning

Greeting a person first

  • Rav: whoever greets his fellow before praying is as if he made him an altar (בָּמָה) — 'Cease from man… for of what (בַּמֶּה) account is he' (Isa. 2:22): read not bameh but bama
  • Shmuel keeps the plain sense: 'in what did you consider him — and not God?'
  • Rav Sheshet objects from our mishna, which lets one greet out of respect between paragraphs; Rabbi Abba: the prohibition is for one who rises early to go to another's door

Tending to your affairs first

  • Rabbi Yona in Rabbi Zeira's name: as if he built an altar — when asked 'altar?', he corrected: 'I said it is simply prohibited'
  • Rav Idi bar Avin in Rav Yitzḥak bar Ashyan's name: forbidden, as it says 'Righteousness shall go before Him, and shall set His footsteps on the way' (Ps. 85:14)

Praying first, then setting out

  • The same verse read positively: whoever prays and then goes on his way — the Holy One tends to his affairs for him
  • Rabbi Zeira also: seven nights without a dream, one is called 'evil' — read 'satisfied' (שָׂבֵעַ) as 'seven' (שֶׁבַע) in Prov. 19:23
  • Rabbi Yoḥanan: whoever fills himself with Torah and sleeps is not told evil tidings — 'he who lies satisfied is not visited by evil'

✅ 'The Lord Your God is True' — Once or Twice?

Rabbi Yoḥanan rules like Rabbi Yehuda; the Amoraim then argue about the word Emet and the tzitzit portion at night
R' Yoḥanan
The halakha follows Rabbi Yehuda: between 'your God' and 'True and Firm' one may not interrupt — because it is written וַה׳ אֱלֹהִים אֱמֶת, 'And the Lord God is True' (Jer. 10:10).
R' Yoḥanan
And one goes back and says Emet again when beginning the blessing.
Rabba
One does not repeat Emet. When a prayer leader before Rabba said 'Emet, Emet', Rabba remarked: 'every Emet-Emet has seized this one'.
Rav Yosef
How fine is the tradition of Rav Shmuel bar Yehuda: in the West, at night they say only 'Speak to the children of Israel and say to them… I am the Lord your God — True'.
Abaye
What is fine about it? Rav Kahana said in Rav's name: one should not begin the tzitzit portion at night, but if he began, he completes it. And 'say to them' is a beginning (Rav Shmuel bar Yitzḥak in Rav's name).
Rav Pappa
In the West they hold it is not a beginning until one says 'and they shall make for themselves fringes'.
Abaye
Therefore: we begin, since they begin in the West — and once we begin, we complete, per Rav.
Ḥiyya bar Rav: If he said 'I am the Lord your God' he must say Emet; if not, he need not. But the Exodus must be mentioned at night! — He says instead: 'We thank You… Who took us out of Egypt and redeemed us from the house of bondage, and did miracles for us at the sea, and we sang to You'.

📚 Why Shema, Then VeHaya im Shamoa, Then VaYomer?

Rabbi Yehoshua ben Korḥa's reason and Rabbi Shimon ben Yoḥai's — 'one reason and another'
PortionR' Yehoshua ben KorḥaR' Shimon ben Yoḥai (as corrected)
Shema (Deut. 6)First accept the yoke of the kingdom of HeavenContains learning, teaching and doing — 'teach them', 'bind them', 'write them'
VeHaya im Shamoa (Deut. 11)Then accept the yoke of mitzvot; applies day and nightContains teaching and doing
VaYomer — tzitzit (Num. 15)Applies only by dayContains doing alone
The correction: Rabbi Shimon ben Yoḥai first said Shema is 'to learn' and VeHaya 'to teach'. The Gemara objects that Shema also says 'teach them', 'bind', 'write', and VeHaya also says 'bind' — so his statement is reread as counting how many directives each portion holds. He does not disagree with Rabbi Yehoshua ben Korḥa; he adds a second reason.

📿 Rav Recited Shema Before Donning Tefillin — How?

A baraita, two answers that fail, and the messenger who was late
1️⃣ Rav washed his hands, recited Shema, then donned tefillin and prayed
↓
2️⃣ Baraita: one digging a burial niche is exempt from Shema, prayer, tefillin and all mitzvot; when Shema's time arrives he comes up, washes, dons tefillin, recites Shema and prays — tefillin first!
↓
3️⃣ (Its own contradiction — exempt, then obligated — is resolved: the end speaks of two diggers, the beginning of one alone)
↓
4️⃣ Answer: Rav follows Rabbi Yehoshua ben Korḥa — yoke of Heaven first. Rejected: he spoke of recitation before recitation, not recitation before action
↓
5️⃣ And does Rav even hold that? Rav Ḥiyya bar Ashi saw Rav wash, bless, teach the lesson, don tefillin and only then recite Shema. (That testimony stands to teach that even Mishna needs a blessing)
↓
✅ The messenger was at fault (שְׁלוּחָא הוּא דְּעַוֵּית) — he brought the tefillin late, so Rav recited Shema at its time and donned them afterward
↓
⚠️ Ulla: Shema without tefillin is like bearing false testimony against oneself. Rabbi Yoḥanan: like a burnt-offering without its meal-offering, a sacrifice without libations
Where the daf ends: Rabbi Yoḥanan begins to describe how one accepts the complete yoke of the kingdom of Heaven — continued on the next daf.
