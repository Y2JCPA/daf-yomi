QUESTION 1 OF 5
What is the practical difference between Havineinu and the brief prayer recited in a place of danger?
A. Havineinu is said only by scholars; the brief prayer by everyone
B. Havineinu requires the first and last three blessings and need not be repeated at home; the brief prayer requires neither but must be repeated
C. The brief prayer must be said standing; Havineinu may be said walking
D. There is no difference — both replace the Amida
Correct: B — Havineinu has the status of an Amida: it is framed by the six fixed blessings, exempts one from praying again, and is said standing. The brief prayer is a stopgap said standing or walking, and one must pray again on arriving home.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
A rider on a donkey reaches the time for prayer. What does Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi rule, and is that the halakha?
A. He must dismount in all cases; the halakha follows him
B. He sits and prays in any case, since his mind is not settled; Rava rules the halakha follows him
C. He dismounts only if someone can hold the donkey; the halakha follows him
D. He waits until he arrives; the halakha does not follow him
Correct: B — The Tosefta distinguishes whether someone can hold the donkey; Rabbi says to stay seated either way because the interruption of the journey would disturb his concentration. Rava, or Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi, rules like Rabbi.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
What is the practical difference between Rabbi Elazar ben Azarya's opinion as cited by the first tanna and as cited by Rabbi Yehuda?
A. Whether Mussaf may be recited after seven hours
B. Whether a ḥever ir requires ten or thirteen people
C. The case of an individual where there is no ḥever ir: exempt per the first tanna, obligated per Rabbi Yehuda
D. Whether the prayer leader fulfills the obligation of others
Correct: C — Both agree an individual is exempt where a quorum exists. Where there is no ḥever ir, the first tanna's version exempts him (Mussaf was instituted only for a quorum), while Rabbi Yehuda's version obligates him — as Shmuel did on the day the army came to Neharde'a.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
Rav Sheshet held that the traveler's prayer may be said even while walking. Why did he nonetheless ask his servant to stand him up?
A. He changed his mind and agreed with Rav Ḥisda
B. Because standing was required for a blind person
C. 'From being good, do not be called bad' — since Rav Ḥisda had stopped anyway, there was no reason not to do better
D. Because the servant misunderstood his instructions
Correct: C — Rav Sheshet did not retract; he held standing is not required. But since Rav Ḥisda had already stopped to pray, standing cost nothing, and one should do the better thing when it is possible.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
Which verse does the Gemara finally accept as the source that one must begin prayer from gravity (koved rosh)?
A. Hannah's 'she was bitter of soul and prayed' (I Samuel 1:10)
B. David's 'I will bow toward Your holy Temple in fear of You' (Psalms 5:8)
C. 'Bow to the Lord in the beauty of holiness' read as 'in trembling' (Psalms 29:2)
D. 'Serve the Lord in fear and rejoice with trembling' (Psalms 2:11)
Correct: D — The first three proofs are each rejected — Hannah and David may be special cases, and 'beauty' may be literal, as with Rav Yehuda who adorned himself. Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak's verse from Psalms 2:11 is accepted and also yields 'where there is rejoicing, let there be trembling'.
