📖 Overview: Does Sprinkling Work After the Meat Already Left?

Meilah 6a–6b
בְּשַׂר קׇדְשֵׁי קָדָשִׁים שֶׁיָּצָא לִפְנֵי זְרִיקַת דָּמִים
The scene: The Gemara continues testing baraitot against the 'fitness of sprinkling' vs. 'fitness of eating' debate, refining two of Rabbi Shimon's teachings — on notar and on piggul — to mean 'fit for sprinkling' rather than 'actually sprinkled.' A final round of testing yields a broader principle: sprinkling that would bring an offering INTO me'ilah must be proper, while sprinkling that REMOVES an offering from me'ilah works even if performed improperly. The daf then turns to a new mishna: meat of the most sacred offerings that left the Temple courtyard before its blood was sprinkled, then came back in — do we say the sprinkling still works to protect it from me'ilah? Rabbi Eliezer says no; Rabbi Akiva says yes, bringing a striking proof from the case of two sin offerings, one lost and one designated in its place.

🌙 Refining Rabbi Shimon's Baraita on Notar

'Before sprinkling' vs. 'after sprinkling' turns out to mean something more precise
1️⃣ Rabbi Shimon: meat eaten before the blood's sprinkling → me'ilah; after sprinkling → no me'ilah
↓
2️⃣ Challenge: if there was still time in the day to sprinkle the collected blood, yet me'ilah still applies — doesn't that prove only ACTUAL sprinkling (not mere fitness) removes me'ilah?
↓
3️⃣ Gemara deflects: no, the case is blood collected just before sunset, with NO time left to sprinkle that day
↓
4️⃣ So Rabbi Shimon's real distinction is: before the blood was fit for sprinkling vs. after it became fit for sprinkling
Same pattern repeats: A parallel baraita about piggul ('there is piggul subject to me'ilah and piggul that is not') is tested and refined the identical way — proving nothing new, but reinforcing that 'fit for sprinkling' is the operative standard.

🚫 Notar and Piggul: The Same Structure, The Same Refinement

Two baraitot, one underlying test

Notar Baraita

  • 'There is notar subject to me'ilah, and notar that is not'
  • Before fit-for-sprinkling → me'ilah applies
  • After fit-for-sprinkling → me'ilah does not apply

Piggul Baraita

  • 'There is piggul subject to me'ilah, and piggul that is not'
  • Identical before/after structure
  • Identical refinement to 'fit for sprinkling'
Why cite both? Each attempted proof for 'fitness of eating' is independently deflected in the same way — the Gemara shows the parallel doesn't settle the Ḥizkiya/Rabbi Yoḥanan debate either way.

📐 A Governing Principle Emerges

When must sprinkling be performed properly to matter?
כֹּל לְאֵיתוֹיֵי לִידֵי מְעִילָה, זְרִיקָה כְּתִיקְנָהּ מַיְיתֵי לִידֵי מְעִילָה. כֹּל לְאַפּוֹקֵי מִידֵי מְעִילָה, אֲפִילּוּ שֶׁלֹּא כְּתִיקְנָהּ נָמֵי מַפְּקָא
The rule: Testing yet another version of 'the piggul is always subject to me'ilah,' the Gemara extracts a broader principle: wherever sprinkling's role is to bring an offering INTO me'ilah — like the sacrificial portions of offerings of lesser sanctity — only a proper sprinkling has that effect. But wherever sprinkling's role is to remove an offering FROM me'ilah — like the meat of offerings of the most sacred order — even an improper sprinkling achieves that removal. Notably, this conclusion is not in accordance with Rav Giddel's stricter rule from the previous dafim.

🔄 New Mishna: Meat That Left the Courtyard Before Sprinkling

Rabbi Eliezer vs. Rabbi Akiva on whether sprinkling still protects it
R' Eliezer
The sprinkling doesn't help — the meat already left before the blood was sprinkled, so it's subject to me'ilah, but exempt from piggul, notar, and tamei violations (since it never became properly permitted).
R' Akiva
The sprinkling IS effective even though the meat already left — so it's exempt from me'ilah, but now DOES carry liability for piggul, notar, and tamei (since it's treated as properly permitted meat).
R' Akiva's proof
Consider: a man loses his sin offering, designates a replacement, then the first is found — both stand fit. If he sprinkles one animal's blood, that exempts the OTHER animal's meat too, since either could have been chosen.
Kal vaChomer
If sprinkling one animal's blood can exempt an entirely different animal's meat from me'ilah, all the more so it should exempt its OWN meat — even meat that left the courtyard!
Same dispute, opposite offerings: For sacrificial portions of offerings of LESSER sanctity that left before sprinkling, the mishna flips the positions: Rabbi Eliezer says sprinkling is ineffective (no me'ilah, no piggul/notar/tamei), Rabbi Akiva says it IS effective (me'ilah applies, piggul/notar/tamei apply).

⚖️ Why the Mishna Needs Both Cases — and Rabbi Yoḥanan's Limit

Kodshei kodashim and kodashim kalim each teach something the other could not
If Only This Case Were Taught...The Mistaken Inference
Only kodshei kodashim (most sacred order)I'd think Rabbi Eliezer requires PROPER sprinkling only to remove me'ilah — but concedes improper sprinkling still ADDS me'ilah for lesser offerings
Only kodashim kalim (lesser sanctity)I'd think Rabbi Akiva requires only that sprinkling ADDS me'ilah even if improper — but concedes it takes PROPER sprinkling to remove me'ilah for sacred-order meat
Both cases togetherShows the tanna'im truly disagree on both fronts — neither concedes to the other in either direction
Rabbi Yoḥanan's qualification of R' AkivaSprinkling exempts meat that left ONLY if part of the offering remained inside the courtyard — if the ENTIRE offering left, Rabbi Akiva agrees sprinkling does not help
