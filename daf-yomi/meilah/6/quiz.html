QUESTION 1 OF 5
How does the Gemara refine Rabbi Shimon's statement that 'there is notar subject to me'ilah and notar that is not'?
A. The distinction is whether the animal was male or female
B. The real distinction is whether the blood was 'fit for sprinkling' (sunset had not yet come) or not, not whether it was actually sprinkled
C. The distinction is only about sin offerings
D. There is no valid distinction; the baraita is rejected entirely
Correct: B — After challenge and refutation, the Gemara concludes Rabbi Shimon's true distinction is between blood that had not yet become fit for sprinkling (collected just before sunset) and blood that had become fit.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
What general principle does the Gemara extract about when sprinkling must be performed properly to have an effect on me'ilah?
A. Sprinkling always needs to be proper, no exceptions
B. Proper sprinkling is required to bring an offering INTO me'ilah, but even improper sprinkling can REMOVE an offering from me'ilah
C. Sprinkling never affects me'ilah status
D. Only the time of day sprinkling occurs matters, not its validity
Correct: B — The Gemara concludes that where sprinkling's role is to add an offering to the category of me'ilah, it must be proper; where its role is to remove an offering from me'ilah, even an improper sprinkling suffices.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
In the new mishna, what do Rabbi Eliezer and Rabbi Akiva disagree about regarding meat of the most sacred offerings that left the courtyard before the blood was sprinkled?
A. Whether the meat may be eaten by non-priests
B. Whether the sprinkling, performed after the meat already left, is effective to remove the meat from me'ilah and subject it to piggul/notar/tamei liability instead
C. Whether the blood itself may be sprinkled at all
D. Whether the animal needs to be slaughtered again
Correct: B — Rabbi Eliezer holds the sprinkling is ineffective for meat that already left (still me'ilah, no piggul/notar/tamei); Rabbi Akiva holds it is effective (no me'ilah, but piggul/notar/tamei apply).
QUESTION 4 OF 5
What proof does Rabbi Akiva bring from the case of a lost sin offering and its replacement?
A. That two sin offerings can never be sacrificed together
B. That sprinkling one animal's blood exempts even the OTHER animal's meat from me'ilah, so a fortiori it should exempt its own meat that merely left the courtyard
C. That lost animals are always disqualified
D. That sin offerings are never subject to me'ilah
Correct: B — Since sprinkling one animal's blood can exempt a completely different animal's meat (because either could have been chosen), Rabbi Akiva argues by kal vaḥomer that it certainly exempts its own meat, even meat that left the courtyard.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to Rabbi Yoḥanan, when does Rabbi Akiva agree that sprinkling is effective to remove meat that left the courtyard from me'ilah?
A. Only when the entire offering left the courtyard
B. Only when part of the offering left the courtyard while part remained inside
C. Never — Rabbi Yoḥanan says Rabbi Akiva always requires the meat to stay inside
D. Only for offerings of lesser sanctity
Correct: B — Rabbi Yoḥanan limits Rabbi Akiva's ruling to a case where only part of the offering left the courtyard; if the entire offering left, Rabbi Akiva agrees the sprinkling does not remove it from me'ilah.
