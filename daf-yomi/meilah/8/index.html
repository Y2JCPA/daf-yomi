📖 Overview: A New Perek Begins — Chatat HaOf

Meilah 8a–8b
חַטַּאת הָעוֹף מוֹעֲלִין בָּהּ מִשֶּׁהוּקְדְּשָׁה
The scene: The first chapter closes with a final concession — even Rabbi Akiva agrees that sprinkling never actually permits eating meat that left the courtyard, only removes it from me'ilah. The second chapter opens with a new mishna tracing a bird sin offering through three stages: subject to me'ilah from consecration, susceptible to disqualification (but not to transmitting impurity) once its neck is pinched, and subject to piggul/notar/tamei but no longer me'ilah once its blood is sprinkled. The Gemara analyzes precisely whose opinion the mishna follows regarding tevul yom, why a leftover prohibition survives sprinkling, and then pivots to a dispute between Rav Huna and Rav Adda bar Ahava about whether squeezing out the bird's remaining blood is essential to the offering's validity — tracked through two different manuscript traditions of the mishna and a close reading of Leviticus 5:9.

🐦 The Bird Sin Offering's Three Stages

What applies to it at each point in its sacrificial life
StageMe'ilah?Other Halakhot
From consecrationYes — fully subject to me'ilah—
After the nape of its neck is pinched (מְלִיקָה)Yes, still subjectNow susceptible to disqualification via tevul yom, mechusar kippurim, or leaving the blood overnight (linah)
After its blood is sprinkled (הַזָּיָה)No — no longer subject to me'ilahNow carries karet liability for piggul, notar, and eating while ritually impure
The mishna's structure: Susceptibility to disqualification and liability to karet-level prohibitions both begin only after prior stages are complete — mirroring the 'period of fitness' logic from the first chapter.

🚫 Susceptible to Disqualification — But Can It Transmit Impurity?

A precise inference from the mishna's wording
❓ Mishna says the pinched bird is 'susceptible to disqualification' via tevul yom — does this mean it does NOT transmit impurity onward to other items?
↓
🤔 Whose opinion is this? Abba Shaul: a tevul yom is like first-degree impurity for sacred food; Rabbi Meir: like second-degree; the Rabbis: only disqualifies, doesn't transmit at all
↓
📏 The mishna's wording ('disqualifies' only) fits the Rabbis' view — not Abba Shaul's or Rabbi Meir's
↓
✅ Rava reconciles all views: even Abba Shaul/Rabbi Meir agree that by Torah law a tevul yom merely disqualifies; the Sages added a stringency ('מַעֲלָה') specifically for consecrated items, treating him AS IF first- or second-degree impure
Net effect: All three tanna'im can agree with the mishna's basic Torah-law statement; their dispute is really about a rabbinic stringency layered on top.

🍖 No Me'ilah, But Still Forbidden to Eat

Rabbi Ḥanina resolves the same puzzle seen on the previous daf
לַיּוֹצְאִין, וְרַבִּי עֲקִיבָא הִיא, דְּאָמַר זְרִיקָה מוֹעֶלֶת לַיּוֹצֵא, דְּלָאו בַּת אֲכִילָה הִיא
The resolution: The mishna says that after sprinkling, one is no longer liable for me'ilah — implying, if read strictly, that the meat is now simply permitted, like ordinary priestly property. But an unstated prohibition on eating it remains. Rabbi Ḥanina explains: this is meat that left the permitted eating area before the blood was sprinkled. Per Rabbi Akiva's rule, sprinkling still removes such meat from me'ilah, but it never actually becomes fit to eat — sprinkling protects it from misuse without restoring its edibility.

🩸 Rav Huna vs. Rav Adda bar Ahava: Is Squeezing Essential?

Two versions of the mishna's final phrase drive the dispute
Rav Huna
Failure to squeeze out the bird sin offering's remaining blood (מִיצּוּי) does NOT invalidate the offering — his version of the mishna reads 'once its blood was sprinkled' (הוּזָּה דָּמָהּ).
Rav Adda bar Ahava
Squeezing out the remaining blood DOES invalidate the offering if omitted — his version reads 'once its blood was squeezed out' (מִיצָּה דָּמָהּ).
Challenge
Leviticus 5:9 states: 'the remainder of the blood shall be squeezed out at the base of the altar, it is a sin offering' — this phrasing seems to make squeezing essential, supporting Rav Adda bar Ahava.
Rav Huna's answer
Per the school of Rabbi Yishmael: the verse means only if blood remains, squeeze it out — not that blood must remain to be squeezed. 'It is a sin offering' refers back to the sprinkling clause, not the squeezing clause.
Stakes: If squeezing is not essential, a bird sin offering is valid — and exits me'ilah — from the moment of sprinkling alone.

📜 Testing the Verse — and a Parallel From the Meal Offering

Does 'the remainder shall be squeezed out' set a requirement or just a contingency?
1️⃣ Leviticus 5:9: 'the remainder of the blood shall be squeezed out at the base of the altar, it is a sin offering'
↓
2️⃣ Rav Huna reads it (via Beit Rabbi Yishmael) as conditional: IF blood remains, squeeze it — no requirement that blood must remain
↓
3️⃣ Rav Aḥa, son of Rava, challenges Rav Ashi: doesn't the same logic apply to the meal offering's leftover verse — 'that which is left (וְהַנּוֹתֶרֶת) shall be Aaron's' (Lev. 2:3)?
↓
4️⃣ Should that too mean only 'IF something remains, it goes to the priests' — with no requirement that anything remain at all?
To be continued: This parallel challenge about the meal offering's leftovers is the question that carries directly into the next daf.
