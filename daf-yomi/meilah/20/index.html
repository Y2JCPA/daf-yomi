📖 Overview: Agency, Attachment, and the Limits of Liability

Meilah 20a–20b
הַשָּׁלִיחַ שֶׁעָשָׂה שְׁלִיחוּתוֹ – בַּעַל הַבַּיִת מָעַל. לֹא עָשָׂה שְׁלִיחוּתוֹ – הַשָּׁלִיחַ מָעַל
The scene: The daf works through several scenarios testing exactly when benefit from consecrated property becomes me'ilah: giving a consecrated stone to another (only the giver is liable), building it into a wall (no liability until one lives beneath it), and paying a bathhouse attendant with consecrated money (liable immediately, even without bathing, since the option itself has value). The Gemara explores why a Temple treasurer is exempt while others aren't, and debates whether an item detached and then reattached to the ground still counts as 'detached' for me'ilah. A new mishna then opens a chapter on agency: if an agent properly carries out his errand, the one who sent him bears liability; if he deviates, the agent himself does — illustrated through a memorable case of swapped meat and liver at a dinner party.

The mishna's chain of scenarios

🏦 Shmuel's Explanation: Why the Treasurer Is Different

Resolving why keeping an item is exempt but giving it away is not
בְּגִזְבָּר, הַמְּסוּרוֹת לוֹ, עָסְקִינַן
The answer: Why does simply taking the stone create no liability, while giving it to someone else does? Shmuel explains: the mishna's first clause describes a Temple TREASURER, to whom consecrated property is legitimately entrusted. Since holding the item is part of his job, mere possession isn't misuse — only once he TRANSFERS it beyond his official custody, to someone with no right to it, does me'ilah occur.

🪟 Rav's Windowsill and the Attached/Detached Debate

Does building consecrated material into a structure change its status?
❓ Why does the mishna require actually LIVING beneath the beam? Doesn't shaping it to fit the building already count as 'change' (like idol worship requires)?
↓
🔧 Rav: the case is a beam simply PLACED on a windowsill, unshaped — no 'change' occurred, so liability waits until real benefit (residing under it)
↓
🏠 Does this support Rav's separate ruling that a house, though attached to the ground, still counts as 'detached' for idol-worship purposes (since it was built from detached materials)?
↓
🔄 Rav Aḥa's alternative: even if attached material reverts to 'attached' status, misuse still applies here for a DIFFERENT reason — the Torah forbids any benefit 'visible to the eye,' regardless of attachment
↓
✅ Reish Lakish's clarification of a supporting baraita: a house built from ALREADY-CONSECRATED materials stays subject to me'ilah; a house built first and consecrated AFTERWARD would not be (like the mishna's cave-dwelling exemption)

🍖 New Mishna: The Agent and the Swapped Dish

Proper agency shifts liability to the homeowner; deviation shifts it to the agent
InstructionWhat HappenedWho's Liable?
'Give the guests meat'Agent gave liver insteadThe AGENT — he deviated from his instructions, so he acts on his own, not as an agent
'Give the guests liver'Agent gave meat insteadThe AGENT — same principle, reversed
'Give each guest one piece'Agent said 'take two,' and guests took threeAll three parties: homeowner (1st piece — instructions followed), agent (2nd piece — his own addition), guests (3rd piece — took beyond even the agent's word)
Why meat vs. liver counts as deviation: Rav Ḥisda: this follows Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, who treats liver, innards, and even bird meat as NOT the same category as ordinary meat — so substituting one for the other is a real deviation, not a minor adjustment an agent could reasonably make on his own.

🐟 A Detour: Why Are Birds Included but Fish Excluded?

Working out exactly which substitutions count as reasonable agency
Question
If a person naturally says 'I couldn't find animal meat, so I brought bird meat instead' (making birds an acceptable substitution an agent could make on his own) — wouldn't the same logic apply to fish?
Rav Pappa (1st attempt)
The case is the day of bloodletting, when people avoid fish for health reasons — so an agent would never even consider it.
Challenge
But bloodletting also makes bird meat and salted meat inadvisable, per Shmuel and a baraita — so that reasoning proves too much!
Rav Pappa (revised)
Rather, the case is a day when the person's EYES hurt — fish are considered harmful for the eyes specifically, so an agent naturally wouldn't buy fish, while birds remain a sensible substitute.
Final mishna note: Rav Sheshet reads the 'take two pieces' case narrowly: the agent explicitly said 'one piece per the homeowner's word, one per my own' — meaning the extra piece was clearly marked as the agent's OWN addition, not proof that any addition automatically counts as valid agency.
