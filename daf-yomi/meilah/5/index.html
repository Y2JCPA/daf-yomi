📖 Overview: Pinpointing 'A Period of Fitness'

Meilah 5a–5b
לָא דַּיְיקָא מַתְנִיתִין דְּלָא כְּחִזְקִיָּה וּדְלָא כְּרַבִּי יוֹחָנָן
The scene: Building on bar Kappara's question from the end of the last daf, the Gemara records the actual dispute: Ḥizkiya says Rabbi Yehoshua's 'period of fitness for the priests' means fitness of slaughtering, while Rabbi Yoḥanan says it means fitness of actually eating the offering's meat. Rabbi Zeira observes that a careful reading of the mishna fits neither view precisely, and the Gemara spends the daf testing the mishna's cases — the overnight case, blood collected or sprinkled by unfit priests — against a third possibility: fitness of sprinkling. Along the way Rav Asi, Rav Yosef, and a related dispute between Reish Lakish and Rabbi Yoḥanan about when an invalid sprinkling renders the remaining blood 'leftovers' all get folded into the analysis, which circles back to confirm Rav Giddel's rule about piggul and me'ilah.

⏳ Three Candidate Readings of 'A Period of Fitness'

When exactly does an offering stop being subject to me'ilah?

Ḥizkiya: Fitness of Slaughter

  • Once properly slaughtered, later disqualification does not bring back me'ilah
  • The earliest possible cutoff point

Fitness of Sprinkling (floated)

  • Once the blood is ready/fit to be sprinkled — even if not yet actually sprinkled
  • A middle position tested throughout the daf

Rabbi Yoḥanan: Fitness of Eating

  • Only once the blood was actually, properly sprinkled and the meat became permitted to priests
  • The latest possible cutoff point
Rabbi Zeira's observation: A precise reading of the mishna's own cases doesn't line up cleanly with either Ḥizkiya's or Rabbi Yoḥanan's position — prompting the Gemara to test the 'sprinkling' reading against the text.

🌙 Did the Blood or the Meat Remain Overnight?

Testing the mishna's 'notar' case for a hidden proof
❓ Mishna: an offering 'that remained overnight' had a period of fitness — no me'ilah. Does 'remained overnight' mean the blood, still unsprinkled?
↓
💡 If so: slaughter + collection were proper, but sprinkling never happened — yet still no me'ilah!
↓
📊 That would prove: fitness of SPRINKLING is what Rabbi Yehoshua meant — against both Ḥizkiya and Rabbi Yoḥanan
↓
🔄 Gemara rejects this: the case means the MEAT remained overnight — the blood was already properly sprinkled
↓
✅ So this case actually supports Rabbi Yoḥanan: fitness of EATING (sprinkling was completed, meat was permitted, then it became notar)
Round one: The attempted proof for 'fitness of sprinkling' fails — the overnight case is reinterpreted to fit Rabbi Yoḥanan instead.

🧺 Which Invalid Sprinklings Create 'Leftover' Blood?

Reish Lakish questions Rabbi Yoḥanan
Reish Lakish
If an unfit priest sprinkles some of the blood, does that render the remaining blood 'leftovers' (שירים), so a later valid sprinkling accomplishes nothing?
R' Yoḥanan
Only a sprinkling done with piggul intent — chutz lizmano or chutz limkomo — creates leftovers, since such a sprinkling still 'accepts' the offering for piggul purposes.
Gemara asks
Does that exclude every other unfit sprinkling, including one by a ritually impure priest?
Clarified
No — an impure priest IS included, since impurity does not disqualify him from serving when most of the community is impure (offerings of the community override tumah). Only sprinklings that could NEVER count for the community — like a blemished priest's — fail to create leftovers.
Net result: Piggul-intent sprinklings and an impure priest's sprinkling both render remaining blood 'leftovers'; other invalid sprinklings (e.g., by a blemished priest) do not.

🔁 Back to 'The Piggul Is Always Subject to Me'ilah'

A final test — and confirmation of Rav Giddel's rule
זְרִיקַת פִּיגּוּל – אֵינוֹ מוֹצִיא מִידֵי מְעִילָה, וְאֵינוֹ מֵבִיא לִידֵי מְעִילָה
The last round: The Gemara reconsiders the baraita 'a piggul offering is always subject to me'ilah.' If it refers to blood collected but not yet sprinkled, it would support 'fitness of sprinkling.' But the Gemara instead reads it as referring to blood already sprinkled with piggul intent — meaning even after sprinkling, sacred-order piggul offerings remain subject to me'ilah. The word 'always' (לְעוֹלָם) is there specifically to teach Rav Giddel's rule: piggul sprinkling neither removes sacred-order offerings from me'ilah nor brings offerings of lesser sanctity into it.
