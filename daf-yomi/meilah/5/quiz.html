5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
What do Ḥizkiya and Rabbi Yoḥanan each say Rabbi Yehoshua meant by 'a period of fitness for the priests'?
A. Ḥizkiya: fitness of eating; Rabbi Yoḥanan: fitness of slaughtering
B. Ḥizkiya: fitness of slaughtering; Rabbi Yoḥanan: fitness of eating the meat
C. Both agree it means fitness of sprinkling
D. Ḥizkiya: fitness of sprinkling; Rabbi Yoḥanan: fitness of slaughtering
Correct: B — Ḥizkiya holds that a properly performed slaughter alone starts the 'period of fitness'; Rabbi Yoḥanan holds it requires that the meat actually became permitted for priestly consumption.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
How does the Gemara ultimately explain the mishna's case of an offering 'that remained overnight' (notar)?
A. The blood remained overnight, unsprinkled
B. The meat remained overnight, after the blood had already been properly sprinkled
C. Both the blood and the meat remained overnight
D. The mishna is not discussing notar at all
Correct: B — The Gemara rejects reading it as unsprinkled blood and instead explains it as meat left overnight after valid sprinkling — supporting Rabbi Yoḥanan's 'fitness of eating' reading in that round.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
Why does Rav Asi object to saying that the Zevaḥim mishna about a sin offering's blood is simply worded imprecisely (לאו דווקא)?
A. Because imprecise wording is never used in the Mishna
B. Because if that mishna is imprecise, and our Meilah mishna uses the same style, we'd have no reason to think either is precise — but the Meilah mishna should be exact
C. Because sin offerings are never discussed imprecisely
D. Because the verse in Leviticus contradicts the mishna entirely
Correct: B — Rav Asi reasons that since both mishnayot use identical phrasing, it's implausible that both are imprecise; the conclusion is that the Meilah mishna is precise and the Zevaḥim one is not.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Rabbi Yoḥanan's answer to Reish Lakish, which invalid sprinkling(s) render the remaining blood 'leftovers' (shirayim)?
A. Any sprinkling by any unfit priest, without exception
B. Only sprinkling done with piggul intent (and, as clarified, sprinkling by an impure priest in communal offerings)
C. No invalid sprinkling ever creates leftovers
D. Only sprinkling performed by a blemished priest
Correct: B — Rabbi Yoḥanan limits this to piggul-intent sprinklings (which still 'accept' the offering) and, as the Gemara clarifies, an impure priest serving when tumah is overridden for the community — but not other invalid sprinklers like a blemished priest.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
How does the Gemara's final analysis of 'the piggul is always subject to me'ilah' connect to Rav Giddel's teaching?
A. It contradicts Rav Giddel and proves 'fitness of sprinkling' instead
B. It is read as referring to blood already sprinkled with piggul intent, confirming that sprinkling with piggul intent does not remove sacred-order offerings from me'ilah
C. It proves that piggul offerings are never subject to me'ilah
D. It has no connection to Rav Giddel's teaching
Correct: B — The baraita's word 'always' is understood to mean that even after a piggul-intent sprinkling, offerings of the most sacred order remain subject to me'ilah — exactly Rav Giddel's rule.
