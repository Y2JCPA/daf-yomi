QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to the new mishna, what is unique about items consecrated for the altar compared to items consecrated for Temple maintenance, regarding combination?
A. Altar items never combine with anything
B. Altar items combine for both me'ilah AND piggul/notar/tamei liability, while maintenance items combine only for me'ilah
C. Maintenance items combine for piggul but altar items do not
D. There is no difference between the two
Correct: B — The mishna specifies that only altar-consecrated items combine to reach the olive-bulk threshold for piggul, notar, and tamei liability, since those concepts don't apply to Temple-maintenance items.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to Rabbi Yannai's original claim, which categories are subject to me'ilah by Torah law?
A. All consecrated items equally
B. Only items consecrated for Temple maintenance and the burnt offering, since only these are 'uniquely for the Lord'
C. Only sin and guilt offerings
D. Nothing is subject to Torah-level me'ilah
Correct: B — Rabbi Yannai derives from Leviticus 5:15 that only items with no portion for priests or owners — Temple maintenance items and the burnt offering — carry Torah-level me'ilah.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
How does the Gemara respond to the challenge from Ulla (citing Rabbi Yoḥanan) that dead sacrificial animals lose me'ilah 'by Torah law'?
A. By rejecting Ulla's statement entirely
B. By revising Rabbi Yannai's position: altar-consecrated animals DO have Torah-level me'ilah, derived from a different verse (Lev. 22:14 or Lev. 3:16) than the one for maintenance items (Lev. 5:15)
C. By saying Ulla was mistaken about Rabbi Yoḥanan's view
D. By concluding there is no Torah-level me'ilah anywhere
Correct: B — The Gemara resolves the difficulty by clarifying that Rabbi Yannai's school actually holds there are two separate Torah sources — one for maintenance items and a different one for altar-consecrated animals.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Rabbi Yehoshua's rule, when do the meat and fat of a burnt offering combine to permit sprinkling the blood, even if only half an olive-bulk of each remains?
A. Never — a full olive-bulk of each is always required
B. Always for a burnt offering, since both meat and fat are equally consumed on the altar, unlike other offerings where meat is eaten and fat is burned
C. Only for peace offerings
D. Only when the priest is the High Priest
Correct: B — Because a burnt offering is entirely consumed on the altar, its meat and fat serve the identical function, so even half an olive-bulk of each combines to permit sprinkling — unlike other offerings, where they serve different purposes and don't combine that way.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
Why do teruma, teruma of the tithe, teruma of demai-tithe, ḥalla, and bikkurim all combine to reach the requisite measure?
A. Because they are all grown in the same season
B. Because all of them are called 'teruma' somewhere in the Torah
C. Because they are all consecrated to the altar
D. Because they share the same olive-bulk measure as meat offerings
Correct: B — The Gemara explains that the shared Torah terminology — each of these items is called 'teruma' in some verse — is what allows them to combine together for the requisite measure.
