📖 Overview: Combining Amounts, and How Far Torah-Law Me'ilah Reaches

Meilah 15a–15b
מְחַוַּורְתָּא, אֵין חַיָּיבִין מִשּׁוּם מְעִילָה אֶלָּא עַל קׇדְשֵׁי בֶּדֶק הַבַּיִת וְעוֹלָה בִּלְבַד
The scene: A new mishna opens by establishing which consecrated items 'join together' (מצטרפין) to reach the minimum measures for liability — one peruta for me'ilah, an olive-bulk for piggul/notar/tamei. Rabbi Yannai then makes a striking claim: Torah-level me'ilah applies only to items consecrated for Temple maintenance and to the burnt offering, since only these are 'uniquely for the Lord' — other altar offerings have portions belonging to priests or owners. The Gemara tests this against mishna after mishna that seems to show broader me'ilah, deflecting each with 'that's only by rabbinic law,' before finally locating two separate Torah verses that anchor Torah-level me'ilah for the two categories. The daf then turns to which specific components of an offering combine to reach the required olive-bulk, and closes with items that share a common Torah name.

🔗 What Combines to Reach the Threshold?

The new mishna's three categories
CategoryCombines For...Combines With
Items consecrated for the altarMe'ilah (1 peruta) AND piggul/notar/tamei (olive-bulk)Other altar-consecrated items
Items consecrated for Temple maintenanceMe'ilah only (1 peruta)Other Temple-maintenance items
Altar items + Temple-maintenance items togetherMe'ilah only (1 peruta)Each other, despite having different types of sanctity
Why state the first case separately? Even though altar items already combine with maintenance items for me'ilah, the mishna needs a separate first clause because ONLY altar items — never maintenance items — combine for piggul, notar, and tamei liability.

📜 The Final Resolution: Two Verses, Two Categories

Rabbi Yannai's school clarifies what each verse actually teaches
מֵהַאי קְרָא קׇדְשֵׁי בֶּדֶק הַבַּיִת שָׁמְעִין מִינַּהּ, קׇדְשֵׁי מִזְבֵּחַ לָא שָׁמְעִין מִינַּהּ
The correction: A challenge from Ulla (citing Rabbi Yoḥanan, that dead offerings lose me'ilah 'by Torah law') forces a revision: Rabbi Yannai must actually hold that altar-consecrated animals DO carry Torah-level me'ilah while alive — otherwise Ulla's statement makes no sense. The resolution: Leviticus 5:15 ('the sacred items of the Lord') is the source specifically for Temple-maintenance items; altar-consecrated items get their Torah-level me'ilah from a DIFFERENT verse — either 'if a man eat of the sacred items' (Lev. 22:14) or 'all the fat is the Lord's' (Lev. 3:16). Two categories, two independent sources — not one verse stretched to cover everything.

🥩 What Joins Together to Form an Olive-Bulk?

New mishna: components of a burnt offering vs. a thanks offering
OfferingComponents That Combine
Burnt offering (olah)Meat, fat, fine flour, wine, oil — five items (Rav Huna's mistaken teaching 'in the world' was corrected by Rava to 'in the burnt offering')
Thanks offering (todah)The same five, plus the accompanying loaves — six items total
Meat + fat for sprinkling the blood (R' Yehoshua's rule, most offerings)Must together reach a full olive-bulk — half an olive-bulk of each does NOT suffice, since meat and fat serve different purposes
Meat + fat for a BURNT offering specificallyEven half an olive-bulk of each suffices — since both are equally consumed on the altar, unlike offerings where meat is eaten and fat is burned
Chain effect: Whatever combination is enough to permit sprinkling the blood also becomes the threshold for later piggul, notar, and tamei liability — but peace offerings' meat and sacrificial portions never combine for sprinkling, so they never combine for those liabilities either.

🌾 Two More Combining Groups: A Shared Name, and Shared Repugnance

Closing mishnayot of the chapter

The Teruma Family

  • Teruma, teruma of the tithe, teruma of demai-tithe, ḥalla, and bikkurim all combine
  • Reason: ALL are called 'teruma' somewhere in the Torah
  • Ḥalla: 'you shall set apart teruma' (Num. 15:20); bikkurim: 'the offering of your hand' (Deut. 12:17)

Forbidden Foods

  • All animal carcasses (nevelot) combine with each other
  • All repugnant creatures (shekatzim) combine with each other
  • Each category reaches its own olive-bulk (or lentil-bulk, for the eight creeping animals) threshold independently
