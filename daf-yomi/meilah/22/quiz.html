Me'ilah 22 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to Rabbi Meir, how may a person permissibly drink wine purchased from Samaritans before separating teruma and tithes?
A. He may never drink it under any circumstances
B. By declaring in advance a specific future separation (e.g., 'two log I will separate later are teruma') and relying on that designation to drink immediately
C. By drinking only after actually removing all teruma and tithes
D. By asking a priest for special permission
Correct: B — Rabbi Meir permits designating specific future portions as teruma and tithes and relying on that declaration to drink the wine right away, before physically performing the separation.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
Which Sages disagree with Rabbi Meir's method of retroactive designation for the Samaritan wine, and what is their basic objection?
A. No one disagrees; it is universally accepted
B. Rabbi Yehuda, Rabbi Yosei, and Rabbi Shimon prohibit it, rejecting reliance on a designation that has not yet actually been carried out
C. Only Rabbi Akiva disagrees, for unrelated reasons
D. The Rabbis agree but require a different order of separation
Correct: B — The mishna in Demai records that Rabbi Yehuda, Rabbi Yosei, and Rabbi Shimon all forbid relying on a future, not-yet-performed act of separation.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
How does Rabbi Yoḥanan resolve the apparent contradiction between Rabbi Meir's wine ruling and the purse mishna's requirement to spend the entire purse before certainty of me'ilah?
A. He says the two cases are unrelated and cannot be compared at all
B. He explains that the purse mishna's stricter clause refers to someone who said 'this purse will not be exempt from consecration,' meaning he specifically intended the LAST coin, similar to naming a specific future portion
C. He rules that Rabbi Meir's opinion is rejected entirely
D. He says the purse case always follows the lenient Rabbi Meir approach
Correct: B — Rabbi Yoḥanan consistently resolves each version of this challenge by pointing to the specific wording 'this purse will not be exempt,' which identifies a definite final coin, paralleling Rabbi Meir's defined future portion.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
What is the crucial difference between Rabbi Meir's wine declaration and the basic case of 'a peruta IN this purse is consecrated' (where the Rabbis require spending the whole purse)?
A. There is no real difference between them
B. Rabbi Meir's declaration names a specific, defined future act of separation, while 'a peruta in this purse' does not identify any particular coin, leaving every expenditure uncertain
C. The wine case involves a much larger sum of money
D. The purse case only applies to gold coins
Correct: B — Rabbi Meir's method relies on a concrete, defined future action, whereas the basic purse declaration leaves the identity of the consecrated coin genuinely unknown until the entire purse is exhausted.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
What Hebrew formula marks the conclusion of tractate Meilah on this daf?
A. 'Baruch Hashem le'olam, amen ve'amen'
B. 'הַדְרָן עֲלָךְ הַשָּׁלִיחַ שֶׁעָשָׂה שְׁלִיחוּתוֹ וּסְלִיקָא לַהּ מַסֶּכֶת מְעִילָה' — 'We shall return to you, [the agent who performed his agency], and thus concludes tractate Meilah'
C. 'Siyum masechet Menachot'
D. There is no closing formula; the tractate simply stops
Correct: B — The daf ends with the traditional 'hadran' formula naming the final chapter's opening words, followed by the explicit statement that tractate Meilah has concluded.
