QUESTION 1 OF 5
How does Rava resolve the apparent contradiction between a baraita (piggul intent applies to 'lost or burned' portions) and Rabbi Yoḥanan's rule that sprinkling only helps when part of an offering remains?
A. He says the baraita is entirely false
B. He emends the baraita to mean portions that are 'about to be' lost or burned, not yet actually destroyed
C. He says piggul never applies to lost portions
D. He says Rabbi Yoḥanan's rule is incorrect
Correct: B — Rava reinterprets the baraita's wording so it refers to portions still present but destined for loss or burning — resolving the contradiction while preserving Rabbi Yoḥanan's limit.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
According to Rav Pappa, when is Rabbi Akiva's rule that sprinkling exempts meat that left the courtyard actually effective?
A. Only if the meat left while the blood remained inside and was properly sprinkled — not if the blood itself left
B. In every case, regardless of where the blood was
C. Only for offerings of lesser sanctity
D. Only if the meat was returned before sunset
Correct: A — Rav Pappa teaches that sprinkling rescues meat that left only when the blood stayed inside the courtyard; if the blood itself left, sprinkling accomplishes nothing even if the blood is later brought back and sprinkled.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
What limitation does Rabbi Elazar place on Rabbi Akiva's kal vaḥomer proof from the two sin offerings?
A. It only applies to burnt offerings
B. It only works if both animals were slaughtered at the same time, not one after the other
C. It only applies to guilt offerings
D. There is no limitation; it always applies
Correct: B — Rabbi Elazar restricts the proof to a case of simultaneous slaughter, treating the two animals as one body; sequential slaughter means the second is already unfit before the first's blood is even sprinkled.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
What criticism does Reish Lakish (citing Rav Oshaya) raise against Rabbi Akiva's answer to Rabbi Shimon in the Kfar Pani story, and how does Rabbi Yoḥanan respond?
A. Reish Lakish says the story never happened; Rabbi Yoḥanan agrees
B. Reish Lakish calls it a 'stolen answer' since the timing of slaughter shouldn't matter if the second animal is unfit regardless; Rabbi Yoḥanan shows Reish Lakish himself relies on the same simultaneous/sequential distinction elsewhere
C. Reish Lakish says Rabbi Akiva contradicts himself about piggul; Rabbi Yoḥanan agrees
D. Reish Lakish says the old man in Kfar Pani was not a valid witness
Correct: B — Reish Lakish calls the answer deceptive because it doesn't explain why timing matters; Rabbi Yoḥanan silences him by showing that even Reish Lakish accepts a simultaneous-vs-sequential distinction in the case of two guarantee guilt offerings.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to the new mishna, what is unique about the sprinkling of blood for offerings of the most sacred order compared to offerings of lesser sanctity?
A. Sprinkling is only a stringency for both categories
B. For sacred-order offerings, sprinkling brings both leniency (removing meat from me'ilah) and stringency (piggul/notar/tamei liability); for lesser-sanctity offerings, sprinkling brings only stringency
C. Sprinkling has no effect on either category
D. Sprinkling only matters for offerings of lesser sanctity
Correct: B — The mishna states that sprinkling for sacred-order offerings is both a leniency (exempting the meat from me'ilah) and a stringency (creating piggul/notar/tamei liability), while for lesser-sanctity offerings it is entirely a stringency, since those offerings were never subject to me'ilah before sprinkling.
