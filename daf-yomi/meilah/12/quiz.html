QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to Rabbi Elazar, how is the location for burying the inner altar's ashes derived from the Torah?
A. It is not derived at all; it's a purely rabbinic enactment
B. From the phrase 'in the place of the ashes' in the verse about the bird burnt offering (Lev. 1:16), which would otherwise be unnecessary
C. From a direct verse naming the inner altar specifically
D. From an oral tradition with no scriptural basis
Correct: B — Rabbi Elazar reasons that since the phrase is unnecessary for the outer altar (already taught elsewhere), it must be teaching a new halakha — that the inner altar's ashes go to the same place.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
Why does Rabbi Shimon hold that doves too young for sacrifice ARE subject to me'ilah, unlike the Rabbis' view?
A. Because he holds all birds are always subject to me'ilah
B. Because it fits his general principle that consecrating an item before its proper time creates only a prohibition (not karet), implying the consecration itself does take effect
C. Because young doves are worth more money
D. Because he disagrees with the entire concept of me'ilah
Correct: B — Rabbi Shimon's broader position is that early consecration is effective (creating a prohibition), which is consistent with holding that premature doves retain consecrated status and are subject to me'ilah.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
How does Ulla resolve Rav Ḥisda's second challenge — that blood seems to be a precedent for something starting without me'ilah and later acquiring it, contradicting the birds' rabbinic-only me'ilah?
A. He admits defeat and retracts his opinion
B. He points out that blood actually has Torah-level me'ilah from the start, since letting blood from a living sacrificial animal is already forbidden and subject to misuse by Torah law
C. He says blood is never subject to me'ilah at any point
D. He says the challenge is irrelevant to his original rule
Correct: B — Ulla (or the Gemara on his behalf) explains that blood is not a true precedent, since it already carries Torah-level me'ilah while the animal is alive — unlike the birds, whose initial state truly lacks any me'ilah.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
Why is blood subject to me'ilah while milk is not, according to Rav's explanation to Rav Hamnuna?
A. Because milk is never consumed by anyone
B. Because the animal cannot exist without blood, making it part of the animal itself, while the animal can exist without milk
C. Because blood is more valuable than milk
D. Because milk belongs exclusively to the owner, not the Temple
Correct: B — Rav explains that blood is inseparable from the animal's existence, so it shares the animal's consecrated status, whereas milk is a separable byproduct the animal can live without.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to Rav Pappa's clarification of the new mishna, when ARE the milk and eggs of a consecrated animal subject to me'ilah?
A. Never, under any circumstances
B. Only when the animal was consecrated with inherent sanctity (kedushat haguf) specifically for the altar
C. When the animal was consecrated with sanctity that inheres in its value (kedushat damim) for the altar, or for Temple maintenance — in both cases treated as one unit with the animal
D. Only for donkeys, never for hens
Correct: C — Rav Pappa explains that milk/eggs escape me'ilah only when the animal itself was consecrated with inherent sanctity for direct sacrifice; when consecrated for its value or for Temple maintenance, the animal and its produce are treated as a single consecrated unit, both subject to me'ilah.
