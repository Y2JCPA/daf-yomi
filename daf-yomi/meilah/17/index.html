📖 Overview: From Measures to Miracles

Meilah 17a–17b
כֹּל שֶׁטּוּמְאָתוֹ וְשִׁיעוּרוֹ שָׁוִין – מִצְטָרְפִין
The scene: Rava closes yesterday's question by clarifying that the Torah's juxtaposition of kosher/non-kosher animals with creeping things applies only to the prohibition of 'you shall not make yourselves detestable,' not to their measures — so there is no basis to split kosher and non-kosher carcasses into different impurity-measures. A new mishna then states Rabbi Yehoshua's principle: only items sharing both the same TYPE of impurity and the same MEASURE combine. This leads into a striking historical narrative — Rabbi Matya ben Ḥarash's question about the source for blood-of-creeping-animals impurity, traced back to Rabbi Elazar bar Rabbi Yosei, and the story of how Roman decrees against Shabbat, circumcision, and family purity were nullified, first cleverly by Rabbi Reuven ben Isterobeli, and then — after the decrees were reinstated — through Rabbi Shimon bar Yoḥai's journey to Rome, aided by a demon named ben Temalyon.

⚖️ Rava's Answer: One Prohibition, Not Different Measures

Resolving Rav Adda bar Ahava's challenge from the end of yesterday's daf
כִּי קָא מַקֵּישׁ רַחֲמָנָא – לְבַל תְּשַׁקְּצוּ, אֲבָל לְשִׁיעוּרִין – לָא
The resolution: When the Torah (Leviticus 20:25) juxtaposes kosher and non-kosher animals with creeping things, that comparison teaches only that all three share the single prohibition 'you shall not make yourselves detestable.' It does NOT mean their MEASURES for impurity must also be compared or split apart. So unlike the eight special creeping animals (which the Torah does textually distinguish for measure purposes), kosher and non-kosher animal carcasses share the identical olive-bulk measure for impurity — there is no room to invent a separate, larger measure for non-kosher carcasses.

🏛️ Rabbi Reuven ben Isterobeli's Clever Strategy

Nullifying three Roman decrees with a single line of reasoning, three times
1️⃣ Rome decreed: no Shabbat observance, no circumcision, no family purity laws. Rabbi Reuven ben Isterobeli disguised himself and sat among the Roman officials
↓
2️⃣ On Shabbat: 'Doesn't an enemy's poverty please you? Then let the Jews rest and not work — they'll grow poor!' They agreed and canceled the decree
↓
3️⃣ On circumcision: 'Doesn't an enemy's weakness please you? Then let them circumcise their sons and weaken them!' Canceled again
↓
4️⃣ On family purity: 'Doesn't an enemy's decline please you? Then let them refrain from their wives when impure, so they won't multiply!' Canceled a third time
↓
5️⃣ Twist: the Romans then recognized he was a Jew and reinstated all three decrees — setting up the need for a new mission to Rome

✡️ Rabbi Shimon bar Yoḥai's Mission to Rome

A dangerous journey, a demon's help, and torn decrees
🕊️ Rabbi Shimon bar Yoḥai, 'accustomed to miracles,' volunteers to go to Rome with Rabbi Elazar bar Rabbi Yosei, despite the danger
↓
😔 En route, Rabbi Elazar whispers the answer to a Torah question in his teacher's presence — a breach of etiquette. Rabbi Shimon, upset, says he won't return home
↓
👻 A demon, ben Temalyon, offers to help by possessing the emperor's daughter in advance. Rabbi Shimon tearfully accepts, noting even a demon's help is a form of divine kindness
↓
🗣️ Arriving in Rome, Rabbi Shimon commands: 'Ben Temalyon, emerge!' — the demon leaves the emperor's daughter, healing her instantly
↓
🎁 The grateful emperor offers any reward and opens his treasury. In it, they find the decree — and tear it up, nullifying it for good
