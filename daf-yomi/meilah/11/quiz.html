5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
Why is there no liability for me'ilah when one derives benefit from a nazirite's undesignated offering money?
A. Because nazirite offerings are never consecrated
B. Because all the money is fit to purchase the peace offering, which is subject to me'ilah only after its blood is sprinkled
C. Because nazirites are exempt from all Temple obligations
D. Because the money technically belongs to the priests
Correct: B — The mishna explains that since the undesignated money could always be used for the peace offering — subject to me'ilah only after sprinkling — there's no definite me'ilah liability before that point.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
How does Rava respond to Reish Lakish's challenge that the mishna should also exempt undesignated money for bird offerings (nests)?
A. He agrees and says the mishna is incomplete
B. He distinguishes the cases: the Torah explicitly directs nazirite money toward a valid peace offering, but bird money would only buy birds unfit for the altar (too young or too old)
C. He says bird offerings are never subject to me'ilah regardless
D. He says nazirites never bring bird offerings
Correct: B — Rava explains that unlike the nazirite's money, which can always fund a fit peace offering, money set aside for unspecified bird nests could only purchase birds that are unfit for sacrifice — not a comparable exemption.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to Rabbi Shimon's mishna, how do blood and libation wine differ in their me'ilah timeline?
A. Both follow identical rules
B. Blood starts lenient (no me'ilah) and becomes strict (me'ilah applies) after flowing to the Kidron Valley; libation wine starts strict (me'ilah applies) and becomes lenient (no me'ilah) once it reaches the drainpipes
C. Blood is never subject to me'ilah at any point
D. Libation wine is always subject to me'ilah
Correct: B — Rabbi Shimon describes a mirror-image pattern: blood moves from no-me'ilah to me'ilah, while libation wine moves from me'ilah to no-me'ilah, as their sanctity 'expires' at different points.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
What principle does the Gemara use to explain why the removal of the ashes (trumat hadeshen) — subject to me'ilah even after its mitzva is completed — cannot prove that blood should likewise remain subject to me'ilah after atonement?
A. The principle that ashes are never truly consecrated
B. The principle that 'two verses that come as one' (two textually linked exceptional cases) do not generalize to teach other cases
C. The principle that only blood-related halakhot can be compared to each other
D. There is no such principle; the Gemara simply asserts it as fact
Correct: B — The Gemara identifies trumat hadeshen as paired with another exceptional case (like the scapegoat's limbs) under the rule that two verses sharing a halakha in common do not extend that halakha elsewhere.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
What does Rabbi Elazar bar Rabbi Tzadok describe happening once every seventy years at the altar?
A. The altar was completely rebuilt
B. Young priests removed congealed libation wine from the drainpipes and burned it in sanctity
C. The Temple was reconsecrated
D. All unused sacrificial blood was collected and buried
Correct: B — He describes how congealed wine, resembling pressed fig-cakes, accumulated in the altar's drainpipes over decades and was periodically removed and burned in sanctity by young priests.
