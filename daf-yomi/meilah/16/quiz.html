QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to Rav, does the mishna's rule that 'all carcasses combine' apply to the prohibition of eating them?
A. Yes, kosher and non-kosher carcasses always combine for eating
B. No — that rule applies only to ritual impurity; for eating, kosher carcasses combine only with kosher, and non-kosher only with non-kosher
C. The mishna doesn't discuss eating at all
D. Rav says carcasses never combine for any purpose
Correct: B — Rav limits the mishna's combination rule to ritual impurity, holding that the eating prohibition treats kosher and non-kosher carcasses as separate categories.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
How does Rav Asi resolve the apparent contradiction between two clauses of a baraita about combining cow and camel flesh?
A. He rejects the baraita as unreliable
B. He explains that the contradictory clause follows a view that a prohibition CAN take effect on an already-prohibited item, while he himself holds it cannot — so the cases are governed by different, non-combining prohibitions
C. He says cow and camel can never be compared
D. He says the baraita is discussing a different topic entirely
Correct: B — Rav Asi attributes the seemingly contradictory ruling to a tanna who holds that a new prohibition can take effect atop an existing one, which is not his own position — so the baraita doesn't actually contradict his view.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
What is Rav's rule for the minimum amount of creeping animals (sheratzim) one must eat to be liable for lashes?
A. A lentil-bulk
B. An olive-bulk, since the term 'eating' in the Torah always implies that measure
C. An egg-bulk
D. There is no minimum measure
Correct: B — Rav Yehuda cites Rav's rule that eating creeping animals requires an olive-bulk for lashes, based on the general principle that scriptural 'eating' implies that specific measure.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
How does Rava ultimately reconcile Rabbi Yoḥanan's apparent endorsement of a lentil-bulk measure for eating creeping animals with Rav's olive-bulk rule?
A. He says Rabbi Yoḥanan was mistaken
B. He limits the lentil-bulk rule to only the eight specific creeping animals the Torah singles out; all other creeping animals still require an olive-bulk
C. He says both measures apply equally to all creeping animals
D. He says the lentil-bulk rule applies only to impurity, never to eating, with no exceptions
Correct: B — Rava's resolution restricts Rabbi Yoḥanan's lentil-bulk ruling to the eight creeping animals explicitly enumerated in the Torah, preserving Rav's olive-bulk rule for all others.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
What does Rav Adda bar Ahava suggest as a parallel implication of Rava's 'eight special creeping animals' principle?
A. That all creeping animals should have identical measures
B. That kosher and non-kosher animal carcasses might likewise need different measures for imparting impurity or triggering the eating prohibition, since they too are distinguished by the Torah
C. That impurity laws should be abolished for animals
D. That Rava's principle applies only to birds, not animals
Correct: B — Rav Adda bar Ahava extends Rava's logic — since the Torah juxtaposes kosher/non-kosher animals with creeping animals in the same verse — to ask whether kosher and non-kosher carcasses should likewise have distinct measures.
