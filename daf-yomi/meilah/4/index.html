📖 Overview: Sharpening Rav Giddel's Rule on Piggul and Sprinkling

Meilah 4a–4b
כׇּל שֶׁהָיְתָה לָהּ שְׁעַת הֶיתֵּר לַכֹּהֲנִים – אֵין מוֹעֲלִין בָּהּ
The scene: The Gemara continues testing Rav Giddel's rule that piggul intent at sprinkling does not remove offerings of the most sacred order from me'ilah, and does not create me'ilah for offerings of lesser sanctity. Ravina raises a challenge from Ilfa's explanation of a mishna about two consecutive improper intentions, which Rav Ashi resolves by noting that piggul status is only revealed once the blood is sprinkled. The Gemara then works through several rounds of a baraita, probing each clause — including a case where blood is left overnight and sprinkled the next day — to see whether it supports or contradicts Rav Giddel. The daf closes by returning to Rabbi Yehoshua's mishna and bar Kappara's question to his nephew bar Pedat about what exactly 'a period of fitness for the priests' means.

🍞 What Does 'The Loaves Are Sanctified' Actually Mean?

Resolving the thanks-offering difficulty
מַאי ״קָדַשׁ״ – לִיפָּסֵל, דְּבָעֵי שְׂרֵיפָה
The answer: When the mishna says the loaves of a thanks offering slaughtered chutz lizmano or chutz limkomo are 'sanctified,' it does not mean they become subject to full me'ilah. It means only that they are sanctified enough to become disqualified — requiring burning rather than simply being treated as ordinary bread. This softer meaning of 'sanctified' removes the difficulty for Rav Giddel: slaughter alone with piggul intent can cause this lesser consequence without yet creating full me'ilah, which still awaits the sprinkling.

🌙 Overnight Blood vs. Piggul Intent

Why an action (linah) and a thought (piggul) may behave differently

Blood Left Overnight (לינה)

  • A physical failure to act properly
  • Later sprinkling does NOT remove the offering from me'ilah
  • Applies clearly to a sin offering, whose sprinkling would otherwise have permitted priestly consumption

Piggul Intent (מַחְשָׁבָה)

  • A disqualifying thought, not a physical defect
  • Sprinkling MAY be effective to remove the offering from me'ilah
  • This is exactly Rav Giddel's position for offerings of the most sacred order
Why it matters: The Gemara uses this distinction to explain why the overnight-blood baraita does not automatically prove or disprove Rav Giddel's rule about piggul.

📏 Bar Kappara's Homework Question for Bar Pedat

What did Rabbi Yehoshua mean by 'a period of fitness for the priests'?
Bar Kappara
My sister's son, think carefully about tomorrow's question in the study hall: Rabbi Yehoshua taught that an offering with 'a period of fitness for the priests' is exempt from me'ilah once disqualified.
Bar Kappara
Did we learn this about fitness of the slaughter alone — that as long as it was slaughtered properly, later disqualification exempts it from me'ilah?
Setup
The mishna's examples — notar, impurity, leaving the courtyard — all occur only after the offering was already fully and properly sacrificed, permitted to the priests.
Open question
Or does 'a period of fitness' require that the entire sacrificial process, through sprinkling, was completed properly — not slaughter alone?
Why it matters: This distinction is exactly what's at stake in the piggul cases just discussed — whether a valid slaughter alone can start the clock on 'fitness for the priests,' even if the blood is later sprinkled with disqualifying intent.
