Me'ilah 19 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
Why is using Temple treasury funds to purchase bird nests for a zav's obligatory offering considered me'ilah, even though the birds serve a sacred purpose?
A. Because bird nests are never valid offerings
B. Because such offerings must be purchased with the individual's own private funds, not Temple funds, even though the purpose itself is sacred
C. Because zavim cannot bring bird offerings at all
D. Because the birds themselves are non-kosher
Correct: B — The baraita explains that although the money is used for a sacred purpose, using Temple funds instead of one's own personal funds still constitutes deriving improper benefit from consecrated property.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
What is the dispute between Rabbi Shimon and Rabbi Yehuda regarding spending consecrated money on one's own sin or guilt offering?
A. Whether such offerings are valid at all
B. Whether me'ilah occurs the moment the money is spent (Rabbi Shimon) or only once the blood is sprinkled (Rabbi Yehuda)
C. Whether the offering must be a specific animal
D. Whether priests or laypeople are liable
Correct: B — Rabbi Shimon holds liability begins immediately upon spending the funds; Rabbi Yehuda holds it begins only when atonement is achieved through the blood's sprinkling.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to the Gemara's three-way comparison, what does the analogy to TERUMA teach about me'ilah?
A. That damage alone, without any benefit, does NOT create liability for me'ilah
B. That benefit alone is always sufficient for liability
C. That only priests can commit me'ilah
D. That me'ilah requires public knowledge of the act
Correct: A — The teruma comparison excludes a case of pure damage without benefit (like spilling teruma on the ground) from liability — the same principle applies to consecrated items generally.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Rav Zevid's response to Rav Kahana, does gold ever sustain damage?
A. No, gold never sustains damage under any circumstances
B. Gold may not be damaged immediately through use, but it can still sustain gradual damage over a long period, and apparent deterioration may also result from careless handling
C. Gold is more prone to damage than any other metal
D. Rav Zevid agrees gold cannot be used as an example in the mishna
Correct: B — Rav Zevid concedes gold isn't damaged instantly by ordinary use (unlike wool-bearing clothing), but points out it can still deteriorate gradually, or through careless treatment, as with Nun's daughter-in-law's jewelry.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
According to the new mishna, which categories of consecrated items are subject to 'misuse after misuse,' where multiple people can each independently commit me'ilah on the same item?
A. Only stones and beams
B. Animals and service vessels — since their sanctity is not depleted by a single act of misuse
C. Only gold jewelry
D. No items are ever subject to misuse after misuse
Correct: B — The mishna teaches that only sacrificial animals and service vessels retain their sanctity through repeated acts of misuse, allowing successive individuals to each be independently liable; Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi adds any non-redeemable item, such as Temple firewood.
