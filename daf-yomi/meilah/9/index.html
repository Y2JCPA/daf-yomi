📖 Overview: A Master List of Every Offering's Me'ilah Timeline

Meilah 9a–9b
מוֹעֲלִין בָּהֶן מִשֶּׁהוּקְדְּשׁוּ
The scene: The daf first resolves the squeezing-of-blood dispute from the previous daf by reading the Zevaḥim mishna 'disjunctively' — the two disqualifications it lists apply to two different bird offerings. A sweeping new mishna then catalogs when me'ilah begins and ends for the bird burnt offering, burned bulls and goats, the burnt offering, sin/guilt/communal peace offerings, the two loaves of Shavuot, the shewbread, and meal offerings — showing that some parts (hides, remainder-meat) exit me'ilah while other parts (sacrificial portions, the handful) remain subject to it until actually burned. The Gemara then turns to two related disputes: whether ash on the altar's heap is still subject to me'ilah after the daily ash-removal, and where the money paid to atone for misuse of consecrated property is directed.

🩸 Resolving Yesterday's Squeezing Dispute

One mishna, read as describing two different bird offerings
1️⃣ Shmuel's father objects to Rav Huna: a Zevaḥim mishna says bird offerings are disqualified if pinched OR their blood squeezed with improper intent — 'squeezed' implies squeezing is essential!
↓
2️⃣ He resolves his own objection: the mishna is taught לִצְדָדִין — disjunctively, about two different birds
↓
3️⃣ Pinching (מְלִיקָה) applies to BOTH the bird sin offering and the bird burnt offering
↓
4️⃣ Squeezing (מִיצּוּי) matters only for the bird BURNT offering, whose blood is never sprinkled on the altar — so squeezing intent is what counts there
Bottom line: The Gemara concludes there are simply two tanna'im who disagree, both following the school of Rabbi Yishmael, about whether missing the squeezing step invalidates a bird sin offering.

📋 Every Offering's Me'ilah Timeline

The new mishna's sweeping survey
OfferingMe'ilah Ends On...Notes
Bird burnt offeringNever — subject to me'ilah until it leaves for the ash pitFully consumed on the altar; nothing is ever eaten
Burned bulls and goats (parim/se'irim hanisrafim)Never — subject even at the ash pit, until the flesh is fully scorchedTheir disposal itself remains part of their sanctity
Burnt offering (olah)Hides exit me'ilah once flayed; flesh stays subject until the ash pitOnly the hide is ever given to priests
Sin/guilt offerings, communal peace offeringsMeat exits me'ilah (eaten by priests); sacrificial portions (eimurim) stay subject until the ash pitMeat is edible, portions are altar-only
Two loaves (Shavuot) / meal offeringsExit me'ilah once the accompanying sacrifice's blood is sprinkled / once the handful is offeredThe remainder (שיריים) becomes ordinary priestly food
The pattern: Whatever is ultimately eaten by priests or owners exits me'ilah once permitted; whatever is entirely consumed by fire on the altar remains subject to me'ilah for its entire journey, right up to actual combustion.

🔥 Is the Altar's Ash Pile Still Subject to Me'ilah?

Rav vs. Rabbi Yoḥanan, after the daily removal of ashes
Rav
Once the mitzva of תְּרוּמַת הַדֶּשֶׁן (removing a shovelful of ash each morning) has been performed, the remaining ash pile is no longer subject to me'ilah — its mitzva is complete.
R' Yoḥanan
It remains subject to me'ilah — the verse requires priestly garments even for carrying the ash outside the camp afterward (Lev. 6:3–4), proving the ash retains its consecrated status that whole time.
Agreed by both
Before the daily removal ever happens, everyone agrees the ash pile is fully subject to me'ilah — the dispute is only about the status AFTER that mitzva is done.
Why it matters: This dispute over 'has the mitzva been completed' recurs across many areas of consecrated property — once an item has served its purpose, does sanctity linger or lapse?

🪨 Ember vs. Ash: Does 'Substance' Matter?

Testing both opinions against a mishna about dislodged material
❓ Mishna (Zevaḥim 86a): an ember dislodged from the altar is NOT restored; implies one still ON the altar WOULD be restored
↓
✅ For Rabbi Yoḥanan: fits perfectly — the altar's contents (even after ash removal) retain sanctity
↓
⚡ For Rav: difficult! Rav answers: an ember is different — it still has מְשָׁשָׁא (substance, still burnable), unlike plain ash
↓
🔄 Reversed reading: maybe ONLY an ember (with substance) is restored — plain ash, lacking substance, is NEVER subject to me'ilah, even on the altar. Now Rabbi Yoḥanan is challenged!
↓
✅ R' Yoḥanan's answer: ash too must be restored if dislodged; the mishna mentions 'ember' specifically to teach the (bigger) novelty that even an ember with substance is NOT restored once dislodged

💰 Where Does the Me'ilah Payment Go?

Rav vs. Levi, harmonized with a baraita about Rabbi Shimon
מַה שֶׁנֶּהֱנָה – יִפְּלוּ לִנְדָבָה. וְלֵוִי אָמַר: יָבִיא דָּבָר שֶׁכּוּלּוֹ לַמִּזְבֵּחַ
The dispute: For one who derives benefit from sacred-order meat before sprinkling, or from lesser-sanctity portions after sprinkling, Rav says the repaid value is allocated to communal gift offerings (nedava); Levi says one must instead bring an item entirely burned on the altar, such as incense. Baraitot support each view. A further baraita (following Rav) adds nuance: benefiting from money set aside for a sin/guilt offering before it was sacrificed requires adding to the fund and buying a costlier animal; after the offering was already brought, the money goes to the Dead Sea (for a sin offering, per Rabbi Shimon's rule that an atoned-for sin offering is left to die) or to communal gifts (for a guilt offering, which has no such rule).
