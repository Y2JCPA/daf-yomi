Me'ilah 13 Quiz

5 questions · Self-graded · Test your learning!
QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to the mishna, if one consecrates an empty cistern and it is later filled with water, what is the halakha?
A. Me'ilah applies to both the cistern and the water
B. Me'ilah applies only to the cistern, not to the water that filled it afterward
C. Me'ilah applies only to the water, not the cistern
D. Neither the cistern nor the water is subject to me'ilah
Correct: B — The mishna distinguishes items present at the time of consecration (fully subject to me'ilah) from items that arrive afterward (only the original vessel/land remains subject to me'ilah).
QUESTION 2 OF 5
How does the Gemara derive that the milk of a sacrificial animal's mother is prohibited to her not-yet-consecrated offspring?
A. From a direct verse stating this explicitly
B. Through a verbal analogy ('its mother') linking the sacrificial animal to the firstborn offering, which is subject to me'ilah in its entirety
C. From the laws governing tithed produce only
D. There is no derivation; it is a rabbinic decree with no source
Correct: B — The Gemara uses the shared phrase 'its mother' between the sacrificial animal verse and the firstborn offering verse to extend full me'ilah — including the milk — to the sacrificial mother.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
Why does a threshing cow need a separate verse to be excluded from eating consecrated vetch, while a laborer needs no separate derivation to be excluded from eating consecrated figs?
A. Because cows have no legal status in Jewish law
B. Because the laborer's verse ('your neighbor's vineyard') naturally excludes Temple property, while the cow's exclusion requires the specific phrase 'its treading' from 'do not muzzle an ox'
C. Because consecrated produce is never edible by animals
D. Because the mishna does not actually discuss the cow's case
Correct: B — The laborer's exclusion follows automatically from the wording 'your neighbor's,' but the ox-muzzling verse needed the added word 'its' to specifically exclude a consecrated field from the general rule.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to the new mishna, when does one become liable for misusing water drawn for the Sukkot water libation in the golden jug?
A. From the moment it is drawn from the Siloam pool
B. Only once it is poured into the service-vessel flask
C. It is never subject to me'ilah
D. Only after the libation is poured on the altar
Correct: B — The mishna teaches that the water in the golden jug (not yet a service vessel) carries no me'ilah liability; only once transferred into the flask, a consecrated service vessel, does me'ilah apply.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
How is the dispute between Reish Lakish and Rabbi Yoḥanan about the water libation's measure ultimately resolved?
A. Rabbi Yoḥanan is proven correct with no further discussion
B. Reish Lakish's apparent inconsistency is resolved: he does hold there is a fixed measure of three log, matching wine; his 'no measure' statement was only describing Rabbi Menaḥem Yodafa's explanation of Rabbi Elazar's view, not his own position
C. The Gemara declares the question unresolved (teiku)
D. Both agree there is no measure for water at all
Correct: B — The Gemara clarifies that Reish Lakish's own view holds there IS a fixed three-log measure for the water libation, paralleling wine; his earlier statement was made only in the context of explaining someone else's position.
