QUESTION 1 OF 5
According to Reish Lakish, why does an idol that breaks by itself become permitted to derive benefit from?
A. Because breaking always constitutes formal nullification
B. Because a gentile witnessing the break would presumably reason that if the idol couldn't save itself, it can't save him — and that realization itself nullifies it
C. Because idols have no legal status once damaged
D. Because Jewish law never recognizes idol prohibitions
Correct: B — Reish Lakish's reasoning is that the gentile owner's presumed reaction — realizing the idol's powerlessness — functions as an act of nullification, even without physically breaking it himself.
QUESTION 2 OF 5
How does Rabbi Abbahu, citing Rabbi Yoḥanan, ultimately explain the phrase 'dislodge with a pole' regarding a nest atop a tree worshipped as idolatry?
A. It means dislodging the entire nest by hand
B. It means dislodging the chicks, not the nest, because reaching the chicks requires climbing the tree and risking real benefit from it
C. It means burning the nest
D. It refers to breaking the idol itself
Correct: B — This final explanation resolves the difficulty about why a pole is needed at all: since the nest itself was built from branches brought from elsewhere, the real concern is having to climb to get the chicks.
QUESTION 3 OF 5
According to the new mishna, why are sawdust and fallen leaves from the treasurers' purchased logs NOT subject to me'ilah?
A. Because sawdust and leaves are never consecrated substances
B. Because the treasurers only purchased the finished logs themselves — sawdust and leaves were never part of what was acquired
C. Because they fall outside the Temple courtyard
D. Because Temple treasurers are exempt from misuse laws
Correct: B — The mishna reasons that the treasurers bought specifically wood fit for construction; byproducts like sawdust and shed leaves were never the object of the purchase.
QUESTION 4 OF 5
According to Shmuel, why does Temple construction use non-sacred materials that are only consecrated once the structure is complete?
A. Because Temple materials are never consecrated
B. Because the donor's consecrated money is directed to take effect on the completed structure rather than on the raw materials or labor, avoiding constant me'ilah violations during construction
C. Because non-sacred materials are cheaper
D. Because only the High Priest may handle consecrated construction materials
Correct: B — Shmuel explains that this method allows builders and suppliers to work with and be paid for non-sacred materials throughout construction, with sanctity only transferring to the finished structure.
QUESTION 5 OF 5
How does Rav Pappa explain why the same 'people need to rest' concern doesn't prevent the treasurers' same-day-use wood from being consecrated immediately, per the mishna?
A. Because that wood is never actually consecrated
B. Because wood purchased for use on that same day presents little realistic risk of anyone sitting on it for an extended period, unlike long-term construction materials
C. Because same-day wood is always guarded by priests
D. Because Rav Pappa disagrees entirely with the mishna's ruling
Correct: B — Rav Pappa distinguishes materials meant for immediate use (low risk of resting upon them) from ongoing construction materials (real risk over time), explaining why only the latter are delayed in consecration.
